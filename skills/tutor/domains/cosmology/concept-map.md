# Cosmology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Cosmological redshift** — wavelength stretching due to cosmic expansion
2. **Hubble's law** — linear relationship between distance and recession velocity. Depends on: cosmological redshift
3. **Scale factor** — dimensionless measure of cosmic expansion. Depends on: Hubble's law
4. **Friedmann equations** — differential equations governing cosmic dynamics. Depends on: scale factor
5. **Early universe timeline** — chronology from Planck time to present
6. **Big Bang nucleosynthesis** — formation of light elements in first minutes. Depends on: early universe timeline
7. **Thermal history** — temperature evolution and particle decoupling. Depends on: scale factor, early universe timeline
8. **Inflation** — exponential expansion solving horizon and flatness problems. Depends on: Friedmann equations
9. **Recombination** — formation of neutral atoms allowing photon decoupling. Depends on: thermal history
10. **Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)** — relic radiation from recombination. Depends on: recombination, thermal history
11. **CMB anisotropies** — temperature fluctuations from density perturbations. Depends on: CMB, inflation
12. **Acoustic oscillations** — sound waves in baryon-photon plasma. Depends on: CMB anisotropies, thermal history
13. **CMB power spectrum** — statistical description of anisotropies. Depends on: acoustic oscillations
14. **Gravitational instability** — growth of density perturbations. Depends on: Friedmann equations
15. **Large-scale structure** — cosmic web of galaxies, filaments, voids. Depends on: gravitational instability
16. **Dark matter** — non-luminous matter dominating gravitational dynamics. Depends on: large-scale structure, CMB power spectrum
17. **Structure formation** — from quantum fluctuations to galaxies. Depends on: inflation, gravitational instability, dark matter
18. **Cosmic acceleration** — accelerating expansion from dark energy. Depends on: Hubble's law, Friedmann equations
19. **Dark energy** — mysterious component driving acceleration. Depends on: cosmic acceleration
20. **Cosmological constant** — Einstein's constant or vacuum energy. Depends on: dark energy

## Dependencies

### Expansion and Dynamics
- **Hubble's law** requires understanding **cosmological redshift** because the redshift-distance relation is the primary observational evidence for expansion
- **Scale factor** builds on **Hubble's law** because it formalizes the expansion rate into a time-dependent quantity
- **Friedmann equations** depend on **scale factor** because they describe how the scale factor evolves based on energy content

### Early Universe
- **Big Bang nucleosynthesis** requires the **early universe timeline** because nuclear reactions only occurred during a specific temperature window
- **Thermal history** depends on both **scale factor** and **early universe timeline** because temperature scales inversely with expansion
- **Inflation** builds on **Friedmann equations** because it requires modifying the dynamics to achieve exponential expansion

### CMB and Perturbations
- **Recombination** depends on **thermal history** because it occurs when the universe cooled enough for atoms to form
- **CMB** depends on **recombination** because photons decoupled at that epoch
- **CMB anisotropies** require understanding both **CMB** and **inflation** because inflation seeds the initial perturbations
- **Acoustic oscillations** build on **CMB anisotropies** and **thermal history** because they describe how pressure and gravity competed before recombination
- **CMB power spectrum** depends on **acoustic oscillations** because the peaks encode the oscillation modes

### Structure Formation
- **Gravitational instability** requires **Friedmann equations** because perturbation growth depends on the background expansion
- **Large-scale structure** emerges from **gravitational instability** through billions of years of growth
- **Dark matter** is inferred from **large-scale structure** (rotation curves, cosmic web) and **CMB power spectrum** (gravitational effects)
- **Structure formation** synthesizes **inflation** (initial conditions), **gravitational instability** (growth mechanism), and **dark matter** (dominant mass component)

### Dark Sector
- **Cosmic acceleration** builds on **Hubble's law** and **Friedmann equations** because supernova data showed unexpected evolution
- **Dark energy** is the theoretical framework explaining **cosmic acceleration**
- **Cosmological constant** is the simplest **dark energy** model, equivalent to vacuum energy

## Critical Bottlenecks

### 1. Scale Factor
The **scale factor** is the single most important concept — it unifies redshift observations, Friedmann dynamics, and thermal history. Students must be comfortable with it before proceeding to advanced topics.

### 2. Friedmann Equations
Without understanding the **Friedmann equations**, students cannot grasp:
- Why different epochs are dominated by different components (radiation, matter, dark energy)
- How expansion rate changes over time
- The role of curvature and energy density

### 3. CMB Physics
Understanding the **CMB** and its **power spectrum** is essential for:
- Connecting early universe theory to observations
- Understanding how we measure cosmological parameters
- Appreciating the evidence for inflation, dark matter, and dark energy

### 4. Dark Matter
**Dark matter** is required for:
- Understanding why structure formation works
- Explaining CMB acoustic peaks
- Making sense of modern cosmological models

Without accepting dark matter's existence (even if composition is unknown), later topics become confusing.

## Prerequisite Topics

### Required External Knowledge
- **Calculus** — needed for Friedmann equations, perturbation growth equations
- **Special relativity** — needed for understanding redshift, time dilation, relativistic particles in early universe
- **Thermodynamics** — needed for thermal history, blackbody radiation, particle equilibrium
- **Basic physics** — needed for understanding gravity, electromagnetic radiation, nuclear physics

### Helpful Background (not required)
- **General relativity basics** — helps with understanding spacetime curvature, but not essential at intermediate level
- **Statistical mechanics** — helps with partition functions and thermal equilibrium
- **Quantum mechanics** — helps with understanding inflation's quantum fluctuations

## Common Interconnections

### Inflation ↔ CMB ↔ Structure
These three form a coherent story:
1. **Inflation** generates quantum fluctuations
2. **CMB** preserves the imprint of these fluctuations
3. **Structure formation** amplifies them into the cosmic web

This connection is pedagogically powerful — it shows how we trace structures from t ~ 10⁻³⁵ s to today.

### Dark Matter ↔ Dark Energy
While distinct phenomena, both are "dark" (non-luminous) and together comprise 95% of cosmic energy:
- **Dark matter** (27%) — clusters gravitationally, drives structure formation
- **Dark energy** (68%) — uniform, drives acceleration
- Ordinary matter (5%) — what we're made of

This contrast helps students appreciate the strange composition of our universe.

### Observations ↔ Theory
Every theoretical concept has observational counterparts:
- **Hubble's law** ↔ galaxy redshift surveys
- **Nucleosynthesis** ↔ primordial element abundances
- **CMB** ↔ Planck/WMAP satellite data
- **Dark matter** ↔ rotation curves, lensing, bullet cluster
- **Dark energy** ↔ Type Ia supernovae

Emphasizing this interplay prevents cosmology from feeling purely theoretical.
