# Cosmology Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. The Expanding Universe
- Hubble's law and cosmic expansion
- Redshift and distance measurements
- Scale factor and cosmic time
- Friedmann equations (simplified for intermediate level)

### 2. The Hot Big Bang
- Early universe timeline (from Planck time to recombination)
- Nucleosynthesis and light element abundance
- Thermal history and particle decoupling
- Inflation and horizon/flatness problems

### 3. Cosmic Microwave Background
- Discovery and significance
- CMB temperature fluctuations and power spectrum
- Acoustic oscillations
- Constraints on cosmological parameters

### 4. Structure Formation
- Gravitational instability and growth of perturbations
- From quantum fluctuations to galaxies
- Large-scale structure surveys
- Cosmic web formation

### 5. Dark Matter
- Evidence from rotation curves, lensing, CMB
- Particle candidates (WIMPs, axions)
- Direct and indirect detection efforts
- Role in structure formation

### 6. Dark Energy and Cosmic Acceleration
- Supernova observations (1998 discovery)
- Cosmological constant vs. dark energy models
- Future of the universe
- Current observational constraints

## Notes on Intermediate Level Approach

At intermediate level, students should:
- Be comfortable with calculus and basic differential equations
- Have some exposure to special relativity
- Understand basic thermodynamics and statistical mechanics
- Be ready for order-of-magnitude calculations and dimensional analysis

The curriculum should balance:
- Observational evidence (what we measure)
- Theoretical framework (why it works)
- Historical development (how we learned it)
- Open questions (what we don't know)

Avoid full general relativity tensor formalism, but don't shy away from Friedmann equations in their standard form. Use analogies and visualizations extensively.
