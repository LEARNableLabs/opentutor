# Cosmology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

**Intermediate Level (Target)**
- **Barbara Ryden, "Introduction to Cosmology" (2nd ed., Cambridge University Press)** — The standard undergraduate textbook. Excellent balance of observation and theory, clear derivations, good problem sets. Perfect for intermediate students with calculus background. Covers all major topics: expansion, thermal history, CMB, structure formation, dark matter, dark energy.

- **Andrew Liddle, "An Introduction to Modern Cosmology" (3rd ed., Wiley)** — Shorter and more accessible than Ryden. Good for quick overviews. Less mathematical depth but covers the essentials clearly.

**Advanced Reference**
- **Scott Dodelson & Fabian Schmidt, "Modern Cosmology" (2nd ed., Academic Press)** — Graduate-level text with rigorous treatment of perturbation theory, CMB physics, and structure formation. Use for deeper dives on specific topics (e.g., Boltzmann equation).

- **Steven Weinberg, "Cosmology" (Oxford University Press)** — Comprehensive and authoritative. Very technical. Good for looking up detailed derivations.

**Popular Science Background**
- **Brian Greene, "The Fabric of the Cosmos"** — Excellent for building intuition about spacetime and quantum cosmology before diving into mathematics.

- **Sean Carroll, "The Big Picture"** — Connects cosmology to thermodynamics, entropy, and the arrow of time. Philosophical and accessible.

### Online Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 8.286: The Early Universe (Fall 2013)**  
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/physics/8-286-the-early-universe-fall-2013/  
  Professor Alan Guth (co-discoverer of inflation). Covers inflation, perturbations, nucleosynthesis. Full lecture videos, notes, problem sets.

- **Leonard Susskind's Stanford Lectures on Cosmology (Winter 2013)**  
  https://theoreticalminimum.com/courses/cosmology/2013/winter  
  Part of "The Theoretical Minimum" series. Susskind's clear, physical approach. Covers expansion, Friedmann equations, inflation, dark energy.

- **Perimeter Institute's PSI Lectures on Cosmology**  
  https://www.perimeterinstitute.ca/  
  Graduate-level course materials. Includes video lectures from top researchers. Search their video archive for cosmology content.

- **Coursera: "The Evolving Universe" (Caltech)**  
  Intermediate-level course covering galaxies, cosmology, and observational techniques.

### Lecture Notes

- **Sean Carroll's Spacetime and Geometry Lecture Notes**  
  https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019  
  Free GR lecture notes. Good for understanding the geometric foundation of cosmology.

- **Daniel Baumann's Cosmology Lecture Notes (Cambridge)**  
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.5424  
  Comprehensive graduate-level notes covering ΛCDM model, inflation, perturbations, CMB.

### Interactive Tools

**CMB Power Spectrum Calculator (NASA LAMBDA)**  
https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/toolbox/tb_cmbfast_ov.cfm  
Adjust cosmological parameters and see how the CMB power spectrum changes. Essential for understanding parameter constraints.

**Planck Mission Website**  
https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/planck  
Access to CMB temperature and polarization maps. Science results, papers, educational materials. The legacy archive (https://pla.esac.esa.int/) has downloadable data.

**NASA WMAP Mission Site**  
https://map.gsfc.nasa.gov/  
Earlier CMB satellite. Excellent educational resources, clearer explanations than Planck (aimed at broader audience). Images, videos, FAQs.

**Illustris Simulation**  
https://www.illustris-project.org/  
Interactive exploration of cosmological simulations. Visualize structure formation from early universe to present. Movies, images, data downloads.

**Eagle Simulation**  
http://icc.dur.ac.uk/Eagle/  
Another large-scale structure simulation. Includes galaxy formation. Complementary to Illustris.

**Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS)**  
https://www.sdss.org/  
Access galaxy survey data. Interactive sky viewer (SkyServer). Educational resources on large-scale structure, galaxy evolution.

**Hubble Ultra Deep Field Interactive**  
https://hubblesite.org/science/universe  
Zoom into deep field images, learn about distant galaxies. Hubble's science pages cover cosmology topics with stunning images.

**Cosmic Microwave Background Polarization Viewer (Planck)**  
Interactive tool for exploring E-mode and B-mode polarization.

### Code and Simulations

**CAMB (Code for Anisotropies in the Microwave Background)**  
https://camb.info/  
Python interface for computing CMB power spectra and matter power spectra. Used by researchers; students can experiment with parameters.

**CLASS (Cosmic Linear Anisotropy Solving System)**  
http://class-code.net/  
Alternative to CAMB. Python wrapper available. Good for learning how cosmological codes work.

**nbodykit**  
https://nbodykit.readthedocs.io/  
Python toolkit for analyzing large-scale structure simulations. Can compute correlation functions, power spectra.

**cosmolopy**  
https://github.com/roban/cosmolopy  
Python package for cosmological calculations: distances, ages, densities. Useful for homework problems.

## Unexpected Connections

### Thermodynamics and Entropy
Cosmology is deeply connected to the second law of thermodynamics. Why was the early universe low-entropy? What does the future heat death mean for entropy? Sean Carroll's "From Eternity to Here" explores this.

### Particle Physics
The early universe was a particle physics experiment at energies we can't reach in accelerators. Neutrino physics, baryogenesis, phase transitions all connect cosmology to the Standard Model and beyond.

### Quantum Mechanics
Inflation converts quantum vacuum fluctuations into classical density perturbations. This is the only place where quantum mechanics affects macroscopic structures (galaxies). Philosophical implications for measurement and decoherence.

### Information Theory
Black hole information paradox connects to cosmological horizons. Does information survive across horizons? Holographic principle links entropy to area, not volume.

### Philosophy
Anthropic reasoning, multiverse interpretations, the nature of time, and whether the universe had a beginning touch on fundamental philosophical questions. Cosmology is where physics meets metaphysics.

### Mathematics
Cosmology uses differential geometry (GR), Fourier analysis (power spectra), statistical mechanics (thermal history), and stochastic processes (inflation). It's a showcase for applied mathematics.

## Historical Papers (for context)

- **Hubble (1929), "A Relation Between Distance and Radial Velocity Among Extra-Galactic Nebulae"** — The original expansion discovery
- **Penzias & Wilson (1965), "A Measurement of Excess Antenna Temperature at 4080 Mc/s"** — CMB discovery (accidental)
- **Guth (1981), "Inflationary Universe: A Possible Solution to the Horizon and Flatness Problems"** — Inflation's birth
- **Riess et al. (1998), "Observational Evidence from Supernovae for an Accelerating Universe"** — Dark energy discovery
- **Planck Collaboration (2020), "Planck 2018 results"** — Latest CMB parameter constraints

Access these via NASA ADS: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/

## Experimental Facilities (for wild cards)

- **Large Hadron Collider (CERN)** — Searches for dark matter particles, studies early universe conditions
- **IceCube Neutrino Observatory** — Detects high-energy neutrinos, probes cosmic accelerators
- **LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA** — Gravitational wave detectors, can probe early universe through primordial GW background
- **Simons Observatory** — Next-generation CMB telescope (Chile)
- **CMB-S4** — Future CMB experiment with unprecedented sensitivity
- **Euclid Mission** — ESA satellite for mapping dark energy and structure (launched 2023)
- **Vera Rubin Observatory (LSST)** — Wide-field telescope for large-scale structure, dark energy (Chile, first light 2025)

## Final Note

Cosmology is a rapidly evolving field. Check arXiv regularly, follow researchers on Twitter/X (many are active in science communication), and watch for press releases from major observatories. The Hubble tension, primordial gravitational waves, and dark matter detection are all areas where breakthroughs could happen any time. Stay curious!
