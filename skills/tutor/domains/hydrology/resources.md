# Hydrology and Water Cycles — Resources

## Primary Textbooks (for lesson content)

### Core Texts for Intermediate Level

- **Applied Hydrology** by Ven Te Chow, David R. Maidment, and Larry W. Mays (McGraw-Hill, 1988)
  - *What it covers:* Comprehensive treatment of surface water hydrology, rainfall-runoff modeling, hydrograph analysis, flood routing, design applications
  - *Why it's good:* Industry standard reference, balances theory and practice, excellent worked examples, appropriate mathematical level for intermediate students
  - *Best for:* Surface water hydrology modules (lessons 11-16, 23-26)

- **Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis** by Philip B. Bedient, Wayne C. Huber, and Baxter E. Vieux (Pearson, 6th edition 2024)
  - *What it covers:* Modern treatment with GIS integration, urban hydrology, modeling software (HEC-HMS, HEC-RAS), climate change impacts
  - *Why it's good:* Updated with current tools and methods, strong on computational approaches, real case studies
  - *Best for:* Modeling modules (lessons 23-26), urban impacts (lesson 28)

- **Physical Hydrology** by S. Lawrence Dingman (Waveland Press, 3rd edition 2015)
  - *What it covers:* Process-based approach to all components of the hydrologic cycle, strong on atmospheric processes, evapotranspiration, snowmelt
  - *Why it's good:* Excellent conceptual explanations, thorough treatment of energy balance, good for understanding mechanisms
  - *Best for:* Water cycle fundamentals (lessons 1-9), evapotranspiration (lesson 9)

- **Groundwater** by R. Allan Freeze and John A. Cherry (Prentice Hall, 1979)
  - *What it covers:* Comprehensive groundwater physics, flow equations, aquifer characterization, well hydraulics, contaminant transport
  - *Why it's good:* Definitive groundwater textbook, rigorous but readable, thorough coverage of fundamental concepts
  - *Best for:* Groundwater modules (lessons 17-22)

### Supplementary Texts

- **Introduction to Hydrology** by Warren Viessman Jr. and Gary L. Lewis (Pearson, 5th edition 2003)
  - Accessible introductory text, good for review or students needing extra support

- **Handbook of Hydrology** edited by David R. Maidment (McGraw-Hill, 1993)
  - Reference handbook with chapters by leading experts, good for deep dives on specific topics

### Modeling Software

- **HEC-HMS (Hydrologic Modeling System)**
  - https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-hms/
  - *Content:* Free rainfall-runoff modeling software from US Army Corps of Engineers
  - *Best for:* Surface water modeling (lessons 13, 23-26)
  - *Download:* Free, Windows-based

- **HEC-RAS (River Analysis System)**
  - https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/software/hec-ras/
  - *Content:* Free hydraulic modeling software for river flow and flooding
  - *Best for:* Channel hydraulics, flood mapping
  - *Download:* Free, Windows-based

- **SWAT (Soil and Water Assessment Tool)**
  - https://swat.tamu.edu/
  - *Content:* Watershed-scale model for water, sediment, nutrient transport
  - *Best for:* Integrated watershed modeling (lessons 23-26)
  - *Download:* Free, requires GIS interface

- **MODFLOW**
  - https://water.usgs.gov/ogw/modflow/
  - *Content:* Industry-standard groundwater flow modeling software
  - *Best for:* Groundwater modeling (lessons 18-21)
  - *Download:* Free, various GUI interfaces available (ModelMuse, GMS, Visual MODFLOW)

- **Online Groundwater Flow Simulator**
  - http://www.groundwatermodels.com/
  - *Content:* Web-based simple groundwater flow simulations
  - *Best for:* Conceptual understanding without software installation

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

- **Hydrology and Music** — River flow can be sonified; hydrographs as musical scores
  - Example: "A Year Along the Gila River" data sonification project

- **Hydrology and Art** — Maya Lin's "What is Missing?" memorial on species extinction includes water loss
  - https://www.whatismissing.org/

- **Hydrology and Economics** — Water markets, virtual water trade, water footprint of products
  - Resource: Water Footprint Network (https://waterfootprint.org/)

- **Hydrology and Archeology** — Ancient water harvesting systems (qanats, acequias), past civilizations and water
  - Interesting case: Angkor Wat's sophisticated hydrology

- **Hydrology and Sports** — Whitewater kayaking and river hydraulics, surfing and wave mechanics
  - Connection: River hydraulics at play

- **Hydrology and Literature** — "Cadillac Desert" by Marc Reisner on Western US water politics
  - Essential reading for understanding water conflicts

- **Astrobiology** — Search for water on Mars, Europa, Enceladus—where there's water, there might be life
  - Connection: Hydrology principles apply beyond Earth

## Wild Cards (Fascinating Case Studies)

1. **The Aral Sea disaster** — human-caused hydrologic catastrophe, shrinking due to irrigation diversions
2. **Saudi Arabia's fossil aquifer depletion** — mining 10,000-year-old groundwater for agriculture
3. **Florida's disappearing lakes** — sinkholes drain lakes overnight in karst terrain
4. **The Ogallala Aquifer** — vast aquifer under Great Plains, being depleted faster than recharge
5. **Bangladesh's arsenic crisis** — natural arsenic in groundwater affecting millions
6. **California's sinking Central Valley** — land subsidence from groundwater pumping
7. **China's South-North Water Transfer Project** — massive inter-basin water transfer
8. **The Netherlands' water management** — living below sea level through engineering
9. **Beavers as watershed engineers** — ecosystem restoration through dam building
10. **Atmospheric rivers on West Coast** — narrow vapor corridors delivering extreme precipitation
