# Algebraic Geometry — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Classical Algebraic Geometry
- **Affine varieties** — zero sets of polynomials, coordinate rings, Zariski topology
- **Projective varieties** — homogeneous ideals, projective space, projective closure
- **Morphisms and rational maps** — regular functions, dominant maps, birational equivalence
- **Dimension theory** — Krull dimension, transcendence degree

### Algebraic Curves
- **Plane curves** — affine and projective curves, degree, singularities
- **Divisors on curves** — effective divisors, linear equivalence, Picard group
- **Riemann-Roch theorem** — dimension of spaces of rational functions with prescribed poles
- **Elliptic curves** — genus 1 curves with marked point, group structure

### Sheaf Theory and Schemes
- **Presheaves and sheaves** — sheafification, stalks, sections
- **Affine schemes** — Spec(R), structure sheaf, functoriality
- **General schemes** — gluing affine schemes, morphisms of schemes
- **Properties of schemes** — reducedness, irreducibility, dimension
- **Morphisms of schemes** — proper, finite, quasi-finite, separated

### Advanced Topics (Brief Introduction)
- **Coherent sheaves** — locally free sheaves, vector bundles
- **Cohomology** — Čech cohomology, derived functors
- **Differentials** — Kähler differentials, canonical sheaf

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Hartshorne's "Algebraic Geometry"** (Springer GTM 52) — canonical graduate-level text, comprehensive but demanding
- **Vakil's "The Rising Sea"** (Princeton 2024) — modern, example-driven, extensive exercises
- **Mumford's "Red Book of Varieties and Schemes"** — classic text with motivation
- **Eisenbud & Harris "The Geometry of Schemes"** — geometric intuition for schemes
- **Shafarevich "Basic Algebraic Geometry" (2 vols)** — gentler classical introduction

### Online Courses
- **MIT 18.725** (Introduction to Algebraic Geometry) — varieties over algebraically closed fields, introduction to schemes
- **MIT 18.726** (Algebraic Geometry) — continuation with schemes, sheaves, cohomology
- **Stanford Math 216** (Vakil) — foundations of algebraic geometry in modern language
- **Berkeley Math 256A-B** — two-semester schemes-based introduction

### Prerequisites
- **Commutative algebra** — rings, ideals, localization, Noetherian rings, dimension theory
- **Abstract algebra** — groups, fields, Galois theory (basic)
- **Point-set topology** — compactness, connectedness, basic properties
- **Category theory** (helpful but not essential) — functors, universal properties

## Teaching Path

For intermediate students, the optimal path is:
1. **Classical foundation** — affine and projective varieties (3-4 weeks)
2. **Curves in depth** — detailed study of one-dimensional varieties (2-3 weeks)
3. **Sheaf theory** — abstract machinery with geometric motivation (2 weeks)
4. **Schemes** — modern perspective, connecting classical and abstract (3-4 weeks)
5. **Synthesis** — see classical results reinterpreted in scheme language (1 week)

This balances geometric intuition (curves) with abstract power (schemes), ensuring students understand WHY schemes matter, not just HOW they work.
