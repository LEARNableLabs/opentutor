# Algebraic Geometry — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Vakil, "The Rising Sea: Foundations of Algebraic Geometry"** (Princeton, 2024) — Modern, example-driven, extensive exercises. Best match for intermediate students. Over 800 pages with careful motivation. Free early versions available online.
  - https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/216blog/FOAGnov1817public.pdf

- **Hartshorne, "Algebraic Geometry"** (Springer GTM 52, 1977) — Canonical graduate text. Dense, comprehensive, proof-heavy. Not for beginners, but essential reference. Chapters I-II cover varieties and schemes; Chapter III covers cohomology; Chapter IV covers curves.
  - https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~kamenova/homepage_files/Hartshorne_engl.pdf
  - https://www.amazon.com/Algebraic-Geometry-Graduate-Texts-Mathematics/dp/0387902449

- **Mumford, "The Red Book of Varieties and Schemes"** (Springer LNM 1358, 1999) — Classic introduction with geometric intuition. Emphasizes motivation and examples. Good companion to Hartshorne.

- **Eisenbud & Harris, "The Geometry of Schemes"** (Springer GTM 197, 2000) — Geometric perspective on schemes. Excellent for building intuition. Accessible examples and pictures.

- **Shafarevich, "Basic Algebraic Geometry" (2 volumes)** (Springer, 2013) — Gentler introduction. Volume 1 covers varieties, Volume 2 covers schemes and complex geometry. Good for building classical intuition before schemes.

- **Gathmann, "Algebraic Geometry" (Lecture Notes)** — Free, well-written lecture notes. Covers varieties and schemes with good examples.
  - https://www.mathematik.uni-kl.de/~gathmann/class/alggeom-2019/alggeom-2019.pdf

### Commutative Algebra Prerequisites

- **Eisenbud, "Commutative Algebra with a View Toward Algebraic Geometry"** (Springer GTM 150, 1995) — Comprehensive, with geometric motivation. The standard reference.

- **Atiyah & MacDonald, "Introduction to Commutative Algebra"** (Addison-Wesley, 1969) — Concise, elegant, classic. Good for quick review or learning on the fly.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### 1. String Theory and Mirror Symmetry
Algebraic geometry (Calabi-Yau varieties, moduli spaces) is central to string theory. Mirror symmetry relates complex and symplectic geometry in surprising ways, leading to new mathematical conjectures and theorems.

- **Introduction:** "Mirror Symmetry" by Hori, Katz, et al. (Clay Mathematics Monographs)
- **For physicists:** Brian Greene's "The Elegant Universe" (popular science, mentions Calabi-Yau spaces)

### 2. Cryptography and Elliptic Curves
Elliptic curves over finite fields underpin modern cryptography (elliptic curve cryptography, ECC). The group structure on elliptic curves makes them ideal for secure key exchange.

- **Introduction:** Washington, "Elliptic Curves: Number Theory and Cryptography" (CRC Press)
- **Application:** Bitcoin and blockchain use elliptic curve signatures (ECDSA)

### 3. Coding Theory and Algebraic Geometry Codes
Algebraic geometry codes (AG codes) use curves over finite fields to construct error-correcting codes with good parameters. Goppa codes and Reed-Solomon codes are special cases.

- **Introduction:** Stichtenoth, "Algebraic Function Fields and Codes" (Springer)

### 4. Robotics and Configuration Spaces
The configuration space of a robot is often an algebraic variety. Motion planning and kinematics use algebraic geometry to study feasible motions.

- **Introduction:** "Algebraic Geometry and Kinematics" (papers by Selig, Hegedus, et al.)

### 5. Computer Vision and Multiview Geometry
Reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images involves algebraic geometry (Grassmannians, projective geometry, fundamental matrices). Structure from motion uses varieties.

- **Introduction:** Hartley & Zisserman, "Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision" (Cambridge)

### 6. Phylogenetics and Tropical Geometry
Evolutionary trees (phylogenetic trees) can be studied using tropical algebraic geometry. Tropical varieties model tree spaces and evolutionary distances.

- **Introduction:** Pachter & Sturmfels, "Algebraic Statistics for Computational Biology" (Cambridge)

### 7. Algebraic Statistics
Statistical models (Gaussian mixtures, hidden Markov models, graphical models) often correspond to algebraic varieties. Algebraic geometry provides tools for inference and identifiability.

- **Introduction:** Drton, Sturmfels, Sullivant, "Lectures on Algebraic Statistics" (Birkhäuser)

### 8. Quantum Computing and Stabilizer Codes
Quantum error-correcting codes (stabilizer codes) use symplectic geometry and algebraic varieties over finite fields.

- **Introduction:** Nielsen & Chuang, "Quantum Computation and Quantum Information" (Cambridge, Chapter 10)

## Recommended Reading Path

### For this curriculum (intermediate level)

1. **Start:** Vakil Chapters 1-10 (varieties) or Shafarevich Vol. 1 (classical algebraic geometry)
2. **Curves:** Hartshorne Chapter I (varieties) + Chapter IV (curves), or Miranda "Algebraic Curves and Riemann Surfaces"
3. **Sheaves and Schemes:** Vakil Chapters 11-20 or Hartshorne Chapter II (schemes)
4. **Exercises:** Hartshorne problems, Vakil exercises, problem sets from MIT/Stanford courses
5. **Computation:** SageMath or Macaulay2 tutorials for hands-on work

### Next steps (after this curriculum)

- **Cohomology:** Hartshorne Chapter III, or Vakil Chapters 21-30
- **Intersection theory:** Fulton "Intersection Theory," or Eisenbud & Harris "3264 and All That"
- **Birational geometry:** Kollár & Mori "Birational Geometry of Algebraic Varieties"
- **Arithmetic geometry:** Silverman "The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves," or Hartshorne "Algebraic Geometry" Appendix C
- **Stacks and moduli:** Stacks Project (https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/), or Alper "Notes on Algebraic Stacks"
