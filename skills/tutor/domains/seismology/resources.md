# Seismology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Introduction to Seismology" by Peter M. Shearer (Cambridge, 2009)**
  - Comprehensive intermediate-level textbook
  - Excellent balance of theory and application
  - Clear explanations of wave propagation, Earth structure, and source theory
  - Problem sets at end of each chapter
  - **Why it's good for intermediate:** Math is accessible (calculus/linear algebra), focuses on physical intuition alongside formalism
  - Available in most university libraries; ~$60-80 used

- **"Modern Global Seismology" by Lay and Wallace (Academic Press, 1995)**
  - Encyclopedic coverage of Earth structure and seismology
  - Strong on global seismology and Earth models
  - More advanced than Shearer but excellent reference
  - **Use for:** Earth structure module (lessons 7-12)

- **"Quantitative Seismology" by Aki and Richards (University Science Books, 2002)**
  - The classic advanced reference
  - Rigorous mathematical treatment
  - **Use for:** Students who want deeper theory, not as primary text for intermediate level

- **"Earthquakes" by Bruce Bolt (W.H. Freeman, 2003)**
  - More accessible, less mathematical
  - Excellent for earthquake science and hazards
  - **Use for:** Hazards module (lessons 24-27) and general context

### Interactive Tools

- **IRIS Earthquake Browser (IEB)**
  - URL: https://ds.iris.edu/ieb/
  - **Critical tool** — explore real earthquakes, visualize seismograms, filter data
  - Use: lessons 5, 11, 17, 22, 27
  - Free account, web-based

- **IRIS Wilber 3**
  - URL: https://ds.iris.edu/wilber3/find_event
  - Request seismic waveforms for any event
  - More advanced than IEB
  - Use: lesson 22 (data access)

- **TauP Toolkit**
  - URL: https://www.seis.sc.edu/software/TauP/
  - Calculate travel times and ray paths through Earth models
  - Java application (requires download)
  - Use: lessons 9, 11
  - **Alternatives:** Web-based tools at IRIS (https://ds.iris.edu/ds/products/taup/)

- **Global CMT Catalog**
  - URL: https://www.globalcmt.org/
  - Centroid Moment Tensor solutions (focal mechanisms) for earthquakes
  - Interactive map and search
  - Use: lessons 16, 17

- **USGS Earthquake Map**
  - URL: https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/map/
  - Real-time earthquake monitoring
  - Filter by magnitude, time, location
  - Use: **daily habit** — check recent earthquakes; lessons 22, 27

- **Seismic Sound Lab (SeisSound)**
  - URL: http://www.seissound.org/
  - Convert seismograms to audio
  - Use: fun engagement, shows frequency content

- **AmaSeis (educational seismology software)**
  - URL: https://www.iris.edu/hq/programs/education_and_outreach/amaseis
  - Free software for visualizing and analyzing seismic data
  - Windows-based
  - Use: lessons 5, 21, 22

- **Focal Mechanism Tools**
  - **USGS Moment Tensor Calculator:** https://earthquake.usgs.gov/research/cmt/
  - **IRIS SPUD Moment Tensor:** https://ds.iris.edu/spud/momenttensor
  - Interactive beach ball viewers
  - Use: lessons 16, 17

  - URL: https://www.obspy.org/

  - Read seismic data, filter, analyze, visualize
  - Extensive documentation and tutorials
  - Use: for students with Python background, lessons 21-22

- **SAC (Seismic Analysis Code)**
  - URL: https://ds.iris.edu/ds/nodes/dmc/software/downloads/sac/

  - Command-line interface
  - Use: more advanced analysis, professional standard

- **Jupyter Notebooks for Seismology**

  - Use: if student is comfortable with coding

- **IRIS Web Services**
  - URL: https://service.iris.edu/irisws/
  - RESTful API for accessing seismic data programmatically
  - Use: advanced data access for computational students

## People (Researchers & Practitioners to Look Up)

### Historical Figures

- **John Milne** — invented the modern seismograph (1880s), "father of seismology"
- **Charles Richter** — developed the Richter magnitude scale (1935)
- **Beno Gutenberg** — Gutenberg-Richter law, Earth's core discovery
- **Inge Lehmann** — discovered the Earth's inner core (1936)
- **Keiiti Aki** — moment tensor theory, seismic source characterization
- **Harry Fielding Reid** — elastic rebound theory of earthquakes (1910)

### Contemporary Researchers (Active in Modern Seismology)

- **Hiroo Kanamori** (Caltech) — moment magnitude scale, earthquake physics
- **Seth Stein** (Northwestern) — seismic hazard, plate tectonics
- **Thorne Lay** (UC Santa Cruz) — deep earthquakes, Earth structure
  - Co-author of "Modern Global Seismology"
- **Susan Hough** (USGS) — earthquake hazards, historical seismology
  - Also an excellent science writer
- **Jeroen Tromp** (Princeton) — computational seismology, full waveform inversion
- **Richard Allen** (UC Berkeley) — earthquake early warning, ShakeAlert
- **Lucy Jones** (formerly USGS) — earthquake hazards communication, public outreach
  - Very active on social media, great for seeing seismology in action

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Discipline)

### Mathematics

- **Inverse Problems**
  - Seismic tomography is a classic inverse problem
  - Connects to medical imaging (CT/MRI), geophysical exploration
  - Regularization, resolution, non-uniqueness apply broadly

- **Signal Processing**
  - Fourier analysis, filtering, spectral methods
  - Used in audio engineering, telecommunications, finance

- **Statistics & Stochastic Processes**
  - Earthquake occurrence modeling (Poisson process, ETAS models)
  - Connects to insurance, risk assessment, reliability engineering

### Physics

- **Wave Phenomena**
  - Electromagnetic waves, quantum mechanics (wave-particle duality)
  - Acoustics, optics — same wave equation, different media

- **Phase Transitions**
  - Core-mantle boundary, inner-outer core boundary
  - Analogous to material phase changes (solid-liquid-gas)

### Engineering

- **Earthquake Engineering**
  - Structural dynamics, vibration analysis
  - Building codes, base isolation, tuned mass dampers
  - Connect seismology to civil/mechanical engineering

- **Exploration Geophysics**
  - Seismic reflection/refraction for oil & gas, groundwater
  - Same physics, different scales and frequencies

### Computer Science

- **Machine Learning for Seismology**
  - Automated phase picking using neural networks
  - Earthquake detection in continuous data (deep learning)
  - Example: Stanford ML group (https://github.com/interseismic/generalized-phase-detection)

- **Big Data & Cloud Computing**
  - IRIS data archive is petabyte-scale
  - Distributed processing for global tomography

### Social Sciences

- **Disaster Preparedness & Communication**
  - How to communicate probabilistic hazard to public?
  - Social vulnerability and resilience
  - Earthquake insurance and policy

- **Historical Seismology**
  - Using historical records to extend earthquake catalogs
  - Paleoseismology — reading earthquake history from geology
  - Example: New Madrid 1811-1812 from newspaper accounts

### Art & Culture

- **Seismology-Inspired Art**
  - Seismogram visualizations as aesthetic objects
  - Sound art using earthquake audio (SeisSound)

- **Mythology & Earthquakes**
  - Namazu (Japanese earthquake catfish)
  - Poseidon (Earth-shaker)
  - Cultural responses to seismic hazards

## Recommended Learning Path Through Resources

### Weeks 1-2: Wave Fundamentals (Lessons 1-6)
- Start with **IRIS animations** for wave types
- Read **Shearer Chapter 1-2** (introduction, wave propagation)
- Use **AmaSeis** or **IRIS IEB** to see real seismograms
- Watch IRIS animation on reflection/refraction

### Weeks 3-4: Earth Structure (Lessons 7-12)
- Read **Shearer Chapter 3-4** (Earth structure, travel times)
- Explore **TauP toolkit** for ray tracing
- Use **IRIS Earth Model Collaboration** to compare models
- Read about Inge Lehmann's inner core discovery

### Weeks 5-6: Earthquake Sources (Lessons 13-17)
- Read **Shearer Chapter 5** (source theory)
- Explore **Global CMT catalog** for focal mechanisms
- Practice beach ball interpretation with interactive tools
- Read about elastic rebound theory (Harry Fielding Reid)

### Weeks 7-8: Instrumentation & Data (Lessons 18-22)
- Read **Shearer Chapter 6** (instrumentation)
- Hands-on: **IRIS Wilber 3** — request data for recent earthquake
- If coding: **ObsPy tutorial** (https://docs.obspy.org/tutorial/)
- Explore **USGS Earthquake Catalog**

### Weeks 9-10: Hazards & Applications (Lessons 24-27)
- Read **Bruce Bolt book** chapters on hazards
- Explore **USGS Hazard Maps** (https://earthquake.usgs.gov/hazards/)
- Watch **ShakeAlert videos**
- Follow **Lucy Jones on Twitter/X** (@DrLucyJones) for earthquake communication examples
