# Seismology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Seismic Waves and Wave Propagation
- Body waves (P-waves, S-waves) and surface waves (Love, Rayleigh)
- Wave equation and elastic wave theory
- Attenuation, dispersion, and anelasticity
- Reflection and refraction at interfaces

### 2. Earth Structure and Seismic Tomography
- Layered structure: crust, mantle, outer core, inner core
- Velocity models (PREM, AK135)
- Travel-time curves and ray tracing
- Seismic tomography and imaging

### 3. Earthquake Source Mechanisms
- Elastic rebound theory
- Fault mechanics and stress
- Moment tensor and focal mechanisms
- Magnitude scales (Richter, moment magnitude)
- Earthquake statistics and Gutenberg-Richter law

### 4. Earthquake Hazards and Risk
- Ground motion prediction
- Seismic hazard assessment
- Liquefaction and site effects
- Early warning systems

### 5. Instrumentation and Data Analysis
- Seismometers and accelerometers
- Network design and data acquisition
- Signal processing and filtering
- Phase picking and event location

## Key Educational Sources

### Textbooks
- **"Introduction to Seismology" by Peter Shearer** — comprehensive, accessible at intermediate level, excellent for fundamentals and wave theory
- **"Quantitative Seismology" by Aki and Richards** — advanced but foundational for understanding wave propagation
- **"Modern Global Seismology" by Lay and Wallace** — comprehensive coverage of Earth structure

### Online Courses
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Introduction to Seismology** — lecture notes, problem sets, and reading materials
- **IRIS Education Resources** — interactive tools, animations, tutorials for wave propagation and earthquake concepts
- **Coursera: Earthquakes (UBC)** — accessible introduction with practical applications

### Interactive Tools and Data
- **IRIS Earthquake Browser** (https://ds.iris.edu/ieb/) — explore real earthquake data
- **USGS Earthquake Catalog** (https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/search/) — current and historical earthquake data
- **IRIS Station Book** — global seismic network visualization
- **SeisSound** — audio representations of seismic waves
- **JAmaSeis** — educational seismology software

### Visualization Resources
- **IRIS Animations** (https://www.iris.edu/hq/inclass/animations) — wave propagation, Earth structure, fault motion
- **UNAVCO GPS visualizations** — crustal deformation and plate tectonics
- **Seismic Waves JavaScript visualizations** — interactive wave demonstrations

## Research Gaps Identified

- Need for more interactive tools bridging basic wave concepts to actual seismogram interpretation
- Limited resources connecting mathematical formalism to physical intuition at intermediate level
- Opportunity to integrate real-time data analysis into learning progression
