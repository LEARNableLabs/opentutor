# Mechanical Watch Repair — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Energy flow** — How a mainspring stores energy and releases it through the gear train to the escapement
2. **Escapement** — The mechanism that converts continuous rotary motion into controlled oscillations
3. **Balance assembly** — The timekeeping heart: balance wheel, hairspring, and impulse jewel working together
4. **Movement types** — Hand-wound vs. automatic, different calibers and their characteristics. Depends on: energy flow
5. **Essential tools** — Screwdrivers, tweezers, loupes, case openers, specialized repair tools
6. **Workspace organization** — Dust control, lighting, parts management, preventing loss of tiny components
7. **Disassembly sequence** — Systematic removal of components in correct order to avoid damage. Depends on: movement types, essential tools
8. **Documentation** — Photographing, organizing parts, note-taking for complex repairs. Depends on: disassembly sequence
9. **Component handling** — Proper tweezers technique, avoiding pivot damage, protecting delicate parts. Depends on: essential tools
10. **Ultrasonic cleaning** — Chemical cleaning of movement parts, basket technique, drying. Depends on: disassembly sequence
11. **Watch oils and greases** — Different lubricants for different applications, viscosity, aging. Depends on: energy flow, escapement
12. **Lubrication technique** — Applying microscopic amounts of oil precisely, avoiding over-lubrication. Depends on: watch oils and greases, component handling
13. **Reassembly sequence** — Putting everything back together systematically. Depends on: disassembly sequence, lubrication technique
14. **Timing machine operation** — Measuring rate, amplitude, beat error, positional variance. Depends on: balance assembly
15. **Beat error** — Understanding and correcting unequal tick-tock intervals. Depends on: balance assembly, timing machine operation
16. **Rate adjustment** — Fine-tuning the watch to run at exactly 86,400 beats per day. Depends on: balance assembly, timing machine operation
17. **Functional testing** — Verifying all operations before declaring a service complete. Depends on: reassembly sequence, rate adjustment
18. **Mainspring replacement** — Safely removing and installing mainsprings. Depends on: energy flow, component handling
19. **Pivot work** — Assessing and replacing damaged pivots, basic lathe operations. Depends on: component handling, essential tools
20. **Jewel work** — Removing and installing jewels, managing endshake. Depends on: component handling, essential tools
21. **Balance staff replacement** — The most delicate repair, involving roller table and impulse jewel. Depends on: balance assembly, pivot work, component handling
22. **Swiss lever escapement geometry** — Understanding lock, drop, impulse in the escapement. Depends on: escapement
23. **Pallet stone replacement** — Replacing worn or chipped pallet stones. Depends on: Swiss lever escapement geometry, jewel work
24. **Balance truing and poising** — Correcting balance wheel deformation and achieving perfect dynamic balance. Depends on: balance assembly, component handling
25. **Hairspring manipulation** — Working with the most delicate component without permanent damage. Depends on: balance assembly, component handling

## Dependencies

### Critical Dependencies
- **Balance assembly** is foundational for timing work — you cannot adjust beat error or rate without understanding how the balance, hairspring, and impulse jewel interact
- **Component handling** is required before attempting ANY repair — improper tweezers technique will damage pivots and destroy parts
- **Disassembly sequence** must be mastered before cleaning and lubrication — random disassembly leads to lost parts and damaged components
- **Energy flow** understanding is necessary for mainspring work and troubleshooting power delivery problems
- **Escapement** understanding underpins all advanced work — it's where energy becomes timekeeping

### Bottleneck Concepts
These must be solid before progressing:
- **Component handling** — bad technique here ruins everything downstream
- **Lubrication technique** — over-oiling causes as many problems as under-oiling; must be precise
- **Balance assembly understanding** — all timing and advanced work depends on this
- **Documentation** — without systematic documentation, reassembly becomes guesswork

### Mind-Blowing Moments
- **Escapement function** — the "aha!" moment when students realize how the pallet fork converts smooth rotation into precise oscillation
- **Beat error correction** — discovering you can make tick-TOCK become tick-tick by moving the hairspring collet a fraction of a degree
- **Balance poising** — realizing that a microscopic imbalance causes massive positional variance in timekeeping
- **Hairspring resilience** — the paradox that the most fragile-looking part can often be carefully straightened and restored

### Progressive Complexity
1. **Foundations** (lessons 1-5) — Build mental model and basic skills
2. **Service fundamentals** (lessons 6-17) — Master complete service cycle from disassembly through timing
3. **Repair skills** (lessons 18-22) — Handle component failures that occur during service
4. **Advanced techniques** (lessons 23-27) — Work on precision adjustments and delicate assemblies

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"You need expensive tools to start"** — While quality tools help, beginners often over-buy. Start with basics: good screwdrivers, tweezers, loupe, movement holder
2. **"Mechanical watches are just tiny clocks"** — Watches face different challenges: portability, positional variance, size constraints, power reserve limitations
3. **"More oil is safer than less"** — Over-lubrication is a primary cause of problems; oil migrates and gums up the movement
4. **"The balance spring and hairspring are different parts"** — They're the same component with two names
5. **"You can clean parts with anything that evaporates"** — Wrong chemistry leaves residues or damages components; use proper watch cleaning solutions
6. **"Magnetization is rare"** — Modern life is full of magnets; it's one of the most common problems
7. **"If it runs, the service was successful"** — A watch can run but still have poor amplitude, beat error, or positional variance
8. **"Swiss movements are always better than Asian"** — Modern Asian movements (especially Seiko, Miyota) are often excellent; quality varies within all origins

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic mechanical understanding** — Needed for: energy flow, gear trains, spring mechanics
- **Fine motor skills and hand-eye coordination** — Needed for: all hands-on work, especially component handling
- **Comfort with precision tools** — Needed for: using specialized watchmaking tools effectively
- **Basic understanding of gears and springs** — Needed for: understanding the gear train and mainspring
- **Patience and attention to detail** — Needed for: every aspect of watch repair

## Rabbit Holes and Extensions

- **Chronograph mechanisms** — Modular complications add fascinating complexity
- **Calendar mechanisms** — Day, date, month, moon phase complications
- **Historical movements** — Pocket watches, vintage calibers, different escapement types (cylinder, duplex, chronometer)
- **Materials science** — Why certain alloys are used for hairsprings, why jewels reduce friction
- **Micro-engineering** — How tolerances of microns are achieved in mass production
- **Finishing techniques** — Geneva stripes, perlage, anglage, polishing
- **Restoration vs. repair** — Different philosophies for vintage vs. modern watches
