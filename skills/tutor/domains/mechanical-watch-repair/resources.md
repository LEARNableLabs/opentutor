# Mechanical Watch Repair — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks — Intermediate Level

- **"Maintaining and Repairing Mechanical Watches: A Practical Guide" by Mark W. Wiles (2016)**
  - Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Maintaining-Repairing-Mechanical-Watches-Practical/dp/1785001558
  - Modern comprehensive guide by practitioner with 20+ years experience
  - Covers basics, how watches work, servicing/cleaning, common repairs, different mechanism types
  - Excellent for intermediate students; practical focus rather than theoretical
  - Well-illustrated with clear photography

- **"Practical Watch Repairing" by Donald de Carle**
  - Classic reference, still in print
  - Based on decades of practical experience
  - More dated but sound fundamentals
  - Good for historical perspective and traditional techniques

- **"The Watch Repair Course and Introduction To Watch Repair" by John Tope**
  - Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Watch-Repair-Course-Introduction/dp/1945014075
  - Structured beginner-to-intermediate course format
  - Systematic approach with clear progressions

### Course Materials

- **The Complete Watch Repair Course with Diagrams** (PDF)
  - Download: http://www.ticktockpro.com/The%20Complete%20Watch%20Repair%20Course%20with%20diagrams.pdf
  - Detailed illustrated course covering all major topics
  - Excellent diagrams for understanding mechanisms
  - Free resource, comprehensive scope

- **Wisconsin Institute of Horology Home Study Course**
  - Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Repair-Wisconsin-Institute-Horology-Hardcover/dp/B008NS4HN2
  - 23 lesson booklets covering filing, lathe work, pivoting, jeweling, watch repair
  - Structured progression from basics to advanced
  - Historical institution with proven curriculum

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Materials Science
- **Metallurgy of hairsprings** — Nivarox, Elinvar, silicon: temperature compensation and anti-magnetic properties
- **Jewel engineering** — Synthetic ruby bearings, friction reduction, why not use diamonds

### Physics
- **Harmonic oscillation** — Balance and hairspring as simple harmonic oscillator
- **Isochronism** — Why oscillation period must remain constant regardless of amplitude
- **Positional effects** — Center of gravity, moment of inertia, and timekeeping in different positions

### Engineering
- **Micro-tolerances** — Achieving micron-level precision in mass production
- **Tribology** — Science of friction, wear, and lubrication at microscopic scales
- **Escapement geometry** — Mathematical optimization of lock, drop, and impulse angles

### Computer Science
- **State machines** — Escapement as mechanical state machine (locked → impulse → locked)
- **Feedback control** — Balance and escapement as feedback control system maintaining constant frequency

### Art & Craftsmanship
- **Finishing techniques** — Geneva stripes (Côtes de Genève), perlage, anglage, hand-polishing
- **Guilloche** — Decorative engine-turning patterns on dials
- **Restoration ethics** — Preservation vs. restoration, patina value

### Economics
- **Value appreciation** — Why some vintage movements appreciate, market for watch collecting
- **Planned obsolescence** — Modern quartz vs. mechanical, serviceable vs. disposable design

### History
- **Navigation and longitude** — Marine chronometers and John Harrison's sea clocks
- **Industrialization** — American mass production vs. Swiss craftsmanship
- **Military horology** — WWII navigator watches, military specifications

## Recommended Learning Path Through Resources

### Phase 1: Visual Understanding (Before hands-on work)
1. Bartosz Ciechanowski's interactive article — understand how everything moves
2. Watch Repair Tutorials YouTube — first 5 videos for overview
3. "The Complete Watch Repair Course with Diagrams" PDF — read Part I

### Phase 2: First Service (Lessons 1-17)
1. Watch Repair Tutorials — full service series
2. Mark Wiles textbook — Parts II-IV
3. Interactive tools for reference when confused

### Phase 3: Repairs (Lessons 18-22)
1. Watch Repair Tutorials — specific repair videos
2. AWCI course materials if pursuing professional path
3. Forums for troubleshooting specific problems

### Phase 4: Advanced (Lessons 23-27)
1. AWCI advanced courses (240, 230)
2. Donald de Carle for traditional techniques
3. Community forums for rare/complex repairs

## Practice Movements Recommendation

- **ETA/Unitas 6497 or 6498** — Large, hand-wound, robust, cheap, parts available
- **Seagull ST36 (6497 clone)** — Even cheaper, good for practice
- **ETA 2824 or Miyota 8215** — Automatic movements, next step up
- **Vintage pocket watch movements** — Often available cheaply, large and easier to see

Start with damaged/broken movements for practice, then progress to working movements for complete service.
