# Music Theory Research Summary

## Topic Overview
Music theory — harmony, counterpoint, form at the intermediate level covers the structural principles and compositional techniques of Western tonal music (circa 1650-1900), including diatonic and chromatic harmony, voice leading, species counterpoint, and musical forms.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Harmony
- Diatonic harmony (triads, seventh chords, progressions)
- Voice leading principles and part writing
- Chromatic harmony (secondary dominants, modulation, borrowed chords)
- Tones of figuration and embellishment
- Functional harmony and Roman numeral analysis

### 2. Counterpoint
- Species counterpoint (first through fifth species)
- Two-part and multi-part writing
- Melodic design and independence
- Imitative techniques (canon, fugue)
- Contrapuntal elaboration

### 3. Form
- Phrase structure and periodicity
- Binary and ternary forms
- Rondo and variation forms
- Sonata form
- Small forms and compound structures

## Key Sources Found

### University Courses
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** — Harmony and Counterpoint I & II: comprehensive course covering diatonic harmony, simple counterpoint, chromatic harmony, and forms. Combines composition, analysis, listening, and keyboard work.
- **Lawrence University** — Music Theory sequence: structural principles of tonal music with emphasis on harmony, counterpoint, and form analysis.
- **Amherst College** — Tonal Harmony and Counterpoint: technical writing and analysis in the Western tonal tradition.
- **University of Illinois** — Fundamental tonal theory including species counterpoint, part writing, phrase structure, and form.

### Textbooks
- **Arnold Schoenberg** — Theory of Harmony (1911): influential foundational text on harmonic theory
- **Arnold Schoenberg** — Structural Functions of Harmony: practical introduction to harmonic analysis
- **Arnold Schoenberg** — Fundamentals of Musical Composition: form and compositional techniques
- **Walter Piston** — Harmony (1959): standard textbook for university programs
- **Walter Piston** — Counterpoint (1970): broader view with examples from Baroque through Romantic periods
- **Johann Joseph Fux** — Gradus ad Parnassum: classical treatise on species counterpoint

### Online Interactive Tools
- **MusicTheory.net** — interactive lessons and exercises on intervals, chords, scales
- **Teoria** — tutorials, exercises, and quizzes covering harmony, counterpoint, and analysis
- **Hooktheory** — interactive visualization of harmonic progressions and voice leading
- **Musical Harmony online tool** — interactive keyboard for finding keys, chords, and harmonies
- **MuseScore** — open-source notation software with analysis plugins

### Video Resources
- **YouTube** — extensive tutorial content from beginner to advanced
- **Dr. Kati Meyer** — music theory curriculum videos
- **Adam Neely** — video essays on music theory and analysis

### Archive Resources
- **Internet Archive** — digital copies of classic theory texts including works by Schoenberg, Piston, and Fux

## Pedagogical Approach for Intermediate Level
Based on course syllabi, intermediate music theory combines:
- Written exercises (part writing, harmonization)
- Analysis of repertoire examples
- Composition projects applying theoretical principles
- Aural skills integration
- Keyboard realization

Students at this level should have basic music reading, intervals, scales, and triadic knowledge. The curriculum builds from diatonic foundations through chromatic harmony and formal analysis.

## Available Educational Resources
Rich ecosystem of textbooks, online tools, video lectures, and course materials. MIT OCW provides full course materials. Interactive tools like MusicTheory.net and Hooktheory enable hands-on practice. Classical texts remain foundational while modern resources add accessibility.
