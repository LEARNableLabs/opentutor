# Music Theory — Harmony, Counterpoint, Form — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 13 lessons (45%)
- **Socratic questions** — 6 lessons (21%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 2 lessons (7%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 31% accessible (1-2), 38% standard (3), 31% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 13: "What makes a voice leading solution smooth versus awkward?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 14: "Can you write a four-part chorale phrase?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 19: "Can you write a simple two-part invention?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 20: "How do secondary dominants borrow from other keys?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "Why does modal mixture add color and emotion?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers music theory — harmony, counterpoint, form, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: functional harmony, tonic-dominant axis, harmonic rhythm, melodic motion, conjunct vs disjunct, melodic contour, seventh chords, chord quality, tendency tones, harmonization, chord selection, cadences, primary triads, tonal center, cadential motion (and 72 more).
