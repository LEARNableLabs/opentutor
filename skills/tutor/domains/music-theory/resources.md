# Music Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (Textbooks & Course Materials)

### MIT OpenCourseWare
- **[Harmony and Counterpoint I](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21m-301-harmony-and-counterpoint-i-spring-2005/)** — Diatonic harmony, simple counterpoint in two parts, tones of figuration. Includes lecture notes, assignments, and listening examples. Perfect for lessons 1-15.
- **[Harmony and Counterpoint II](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21m-302-harmony-and-counterpoint-ii-spring-2005/)** — Chromatic harmony, modulation, and musical form. Classical idiom (ca. 1750-1825). Ideal companion for lessons 16-30.

### Classic Textbooks
- **[Arnold Schoenberg — Theory of Harmony (Amazon)](https://www.amazon.com/Theory-Harmony-ANNIVERSARY-Arnold-Schoenberg/dp/0520266080)** — Foundational text from 1911, still profoundly influential. Deep dive into harmonic theory. Dense but brilliant.
- **[Arnold Schoenberg — Fundamentals of Musical Composition (Amazon)](https://www.amazon.com/Fundamentals-Musical-Composition-Arnold-Schoenberg/dp/0571196586)** — Form, phrase structure, and compositional techniques. More accessible than Theory of Harmony.
- **Walter Piston — Harmony** — Standard university textbook. Clear, systematic, comprehensive. (Referenced in search results but specific link not provided; widely available in libraries and online booksellers.)
- **Walter Piston — Counterpoint** — Broader historical view, quotes examples from Baroque through Romantic periods.
- **[Johann Joseph Fux — Gradus ad Parnassum](https://archive.org/details/BooksOnMusicTheoryHarmonyCounterpointOrchestrationinEnglishJazzToo)** — The classical treatise on species counterpoint. Available in Internet Archive collection.

### Internet Archive Collection
- **[Books On Music Theory (Harmony, Counterpoint, Orchestration)](https://archive.org/details/BooksOnMusicTheoryHarmonyCounterpointOrchestrationinEnglishJazzToo)** — Free digital library of classic theory texts including Schoenberg, Piston, Fux, and many others. Invaluable resource.

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Mathematics & Music Theory
**Fourier Analysis & Harmonic Series** — The mathematical decomposition of sound waves into sine components directly explains why certain intervals sound consonant (simple frequency ratios) vs dissonant (complex ratios). Acoustics is applied physics.

**Group Theory & Transformational Analysis** — Modern music theory uses abstract algebra (group theory) to model transformations between chords. Neo-Riemannian theory analyzes chromatic harmony using geometric transformations. Bridge to higher mathematics.

**Fibonacci Sequence & Golden Ratio in Form** — Some composers (Bartók, Debussy) structured phrases and sections using Fibonacci proportions. The golden ratio appears in formal design.

### Cognitive Science & Perception
**Gestalt Principles in Musical Phrasing** — How we perceive musical grouping (phrases, sections) follows Gestalt psychology principles: proximity, similarity, continuity. Music cognition research explains why certain formal structures feel coherent.

**Working Memory & Harmonic Expectations** — Cognitive psychology explains why deceptive cadences surprise: our working memory predicts V→I based on learned patterns. Chromatic harmony plays with these expectations.

### Linguistics & Music
**Syntax & Musical Grammar** — Generative linguistics (Chomsky) inspired generative theory of tonal music (Lerdahl & Jackendoff). Hierarchical structure in language mirrors hierarchical structure in music.

**Prosody & Musical Phrasing** — The rise and fall of speech (prosody) parallels melodic contour and phrase structure. Composers consciously set text to match linguistic stress patterns.

### Architecture & Musical Form
**Classical Architecture & Sonata Form** — Symmetry, proportion, and balance in Classical architecture (columns, pediments) parallel exposition-development-recapitulation balance in sonata form. Both reflect Enlightenment aesthetics.

**Gothic Cathedrals & Polyphony** — The vertical soaring of Gothic architecture emerged contemporaneously with complex polyphonic music (organum, motet). Both emphasize vertical dimension and spatial/sonic grandeur.

### Physics & Temperament Systems
**Harmonic Series (Overtones)** — Every pitched sound naturally contains overtones at specific frequency ratios. The first overtones are octave (2:1), fifth (3:2), fourth (4:3) — the most consonant intervals. Consonance is rooted in physics.

**Equal Temperament Trade-offs** — Modern pianos use equal temperament (12 equal semitones), slightly detuning all intervals except octaves to enable modulation to all keys. Just intonation uses pure ratios but can't modulate freely. Music theory evolved alongside tuning technology.

### Philosophy & Aesthetics
**Schopenhauer's Metaphysics of Music** — Philosopher Arthur Schopenhauer argued music is the direct objectification of the will (unlike other arts which represent ideas). Music bypasses conceptual thought and expresses pure emotion. Music theory describes the "how," philosophy tackles the "why."

**Hanslick's Formalism** — Critic Eduard Hanslick argued music's beauty lies in its formal structures, not extramusical associations. This formalist view positions music theory as the study of autonomous musical beauty.

## Supplementary Reading

### Music History Context
- Understanding Baroque (Bach, Handel), Classical (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven), and Romantic (Chopin, Brahms, Wagner) style periods enriches theoretical study. Form and harmony evolved across these eras.

### Performance Practice
- How performers interpret harmonic tension, voice leading, and form in real time. Theory informs performance decisions (phrasing, dynamics, tempo).

### Jazz Theory Extensions
- Mark Levine's "The Jazz Theory Book" — How functional harmony extends into jazz idiom (chord-scale theory, upper extensions, substitutions).

### 20th Century & Beyond
- After mastering common-practice tonality (this curriculum's scope), explore Impressionism (Debussy, Ravel), atonality (Schoenberg, Webern), minimalism (Reich, Glass), and contemporary techniques. Music theory didn't stop in 1900.

## For the Curious: Deep Dives

- **Schenkerian Analysis** — Advanced analytical method reducing music to fundamental structures. Complex but reveals deep formal coherence.
- **Neo-Riemannian Theory** — Geometric approach to chromatic harmony using transformational operations. Modern theoretical frontier.
- **Partimenti** — Historical improvisation method using figured bass. Pedagogical approach that complements species counterpoint.
- **Modal Counterpoint (Renaissance)** — Pre-tonal polyphony (Palestrina, Josquin). Different rules than common-practice, emphasizes intervallic control.
- **Set Theory & Serialism** — Post-tonal analysis (Schoenberg's 12-tone method, Webern's pointillism). Requires solid tonal foundation first.

## Final Thoughts

Music theory is a vast landscape — this curriculum covers core common-practice tonality (harmony, counterpoint, form). Think of it as the foundation for exploring any direction: jazz, film scoring, contemporary classical, analysis, composition, or just deeper listening. The resources listed here provide pathways into each of those territories.

The best resource is always **real music**. Listen to Bach chorales, Mozart sonatas, Chopin preludes, Beatles songs, film scores. Theory explains patterns found in music you love. Never let theory study replace the joy of listening and playing.
