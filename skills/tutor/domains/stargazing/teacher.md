## Domain Hooks
- **History of telescope development** — Galileo's first observations, Herschel's giant reflectors, the leap to adaptive optics. Drop in when discussing refractor vs reflector trade-offs (lesson 9).

- **Atmospheric optics** — why stars twinkle but planets don't, green flash, airglow, zodiacal light. Introduce when discussing seeing conditions (lesson 24).

- **Eye physiology** — Purkinje shift (color perception changes in low light), scotopic vs photopic vision, why averted vision works. Relevant during dark adaptation lessons (5, 17).

- **The cosmic distance ladder** — how we know distances to stars and galaxies. Beautiful context when observing Andromeda Galaxy (2.5 million light-years away).

- **Light pollution activism** — International Dark-Sky Association, dark sky preserves, lighting ordinances. Tie into lesson 5 on light pollution.

- **Messier's legacy** — why 18th-century comet hunter Charles Messier cataloged "fuzzy objects" that became the most popular deep sky targets. 

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"More magnification = better view"**
   - Students often assume higher magnification always reveals more detail. In reality, magnification is limited by aperture, atmospheric seeing, and optical quality. Pushing beyond ~50× per inch of aperture typically produces dim, blurry images.
   - **Correction**: Emphasize that aperture (light gathering power) matters far more than magnification. Demonstrate how excessive magnification spreads limited light too thin. Use the "exit pupil" concept to show why dim views result from small exit pupils.

2. **"Deep sky objects look like Hubble photos"**
   - The "Hubble disappointment" — students expect colorful, detailed nebulae and galaxies but see faint, gray smudges through eyepieces.
