# Orchestration and Instrumentation — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 11 lessons (41%)
- **Socratic questions** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 2 lessons (7%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (7%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 48% accessible (1-2), 37% standard (3), 15% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 17: "How do you balance brass against the rest of the orchestra?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 20: "How does harp pedaling limit what you can write?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 23: "How do you orchestrate a piano piece for full orchestra?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 26: "What does your ideal ensemble arrangement reveal about orchestration?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers orchestration and instrumentation, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: timbre, acoustics, instrument families, overtones, transposition, concert pitch, written pitch, transposing instruments, violin range, bowing techniques, articulations, vibrato, comfortable ranges, extreme registers, viola and cello ranges (and 94 more).
