# The Physics of Musical Instruments — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"The Physics of Musical Instruments" (2nd ed.)** by Neville H. Fletcher & Thomas D. Rossing (Springer, 1998)
  - Comprehensive graduate-level treatment. Covers all families (strings, winds, percussion, electronic). Excellent for deep dives but requires comfort with PDEs and special functions.
  - Best for: Instructor reference, advanced tangents
  - https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-21603-4

- **"The Science of Sound" (3rd ed.)** by Thomas D. Rossing, F. Richard Moore, Paul A. Wheeler (Addison-Wesley, 2001)
  - Undergraduate-friendly. Balances math with intuition. Strong on psychoacoustics and perception.
  - Best for: Core reading at intermediate level
  - ISBN: 978-0805385656

- **"Musical Acoustics" (3rd ed.)** by Donald E. Hall (Brooks/Cole, 2001)
  - Practical, instrument-focused. Less rigorous math but excellent explanations and real-world examples.
  - Best for: Contextual examples, instrument-specific details
  - ISBN: 978-0534377281

- **"Acoustics and Psychoacoustics" (5th ed.)** by David M. Howard & Jamie A. Angus (Routledge, 2017)
  - Strong on perception, room acoustics, and recording. Good for connecting physics to listening experience.
  - Best for: Timbre, perception, recording technology
  - https://www.routledge.com/Acoustics-and-Psychoacoustics/Howard-Angus/p/book/9781138855885

### Online Courses & Lecture Notes

- **UNSW Music Acoustics** (Joe Wolfe, University of New South Wales)
  - Outstanding free resource. Clear explanations, animations, instrument-specific pages.
  - Covers: strings, winds, brass, percussion, voice
  - https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/

- **Dan Russell's Acoustics Animations** (Penn State)
  - Superb visualizations of wave phenomena, standing waves, modes, interference
  - https://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/demos.html

- **Stanford CCRMA** (Center for Computer Research in Music and Acoustics)
  - Physical modeling, digital synthesis, signal processing
  - Julius O. Smith's online books (free): "Physical Audio Signal Processing"
  - https://ccrma.stanford.edu/
  - https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/

- **MIT OpenCourseWare — 8.03 Vibrations and Waves**
  - General wave mechanics course; musical instruments appear as examples
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/

- **Coursera: "Introduction to Acoustics" (KAIST)**
  - Free online course covering fundamentals through applications
  - https://www.coursera.org/learn/intro-to-acoustics

### Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Architecture & Concert Hall Acoustics**
  - Sabine's equation, reverberation time, diffusion
  - Connects instrument physics to performance spaces
  - Resource: https://www.acoustics.salford.ac.uk/

- **Metamaterials & Acoustic Cloaking**
  - Engineered materials with exotic properties (negative index, band gaps)
  - Future directions for instrument design
  - Resource: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_metamaterial

- **Biomechanics of Voice Production**
  - Vocal folds as self-oscillating reeds
  - Connects wind instrument physics to human voice
  - Resource: https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/voice.html

- **Earthquake Waves & Seismology**
  - Same wave mechanics, different scale
  - P-waves (longitudinal) vs S-waves (transverse), dispersion
  - Resource: https://www.iris.edu/hq/

- **Quantum Mechanics & Wave Functions**
  - Schrödinger equation has same structure as wave equation
  - Particle in a box = string with fixed ends
  - Rabbit hole: "The quantum guitar string"

- **Nonlinear Dynamics & Chaos in Reeds**
  - Clarinet multiphonics, saxophone altissimo involve chaotic regimes
  - Resource: https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/multiphonics.html

- **3D Printing Custom Instruments**
  - Additive manufacturing enables novel geometries
  - Test theoretical designs easily
  - Resource: https://www.thingiverse.com/search?q=musical+instrument

## Recommended Learning Path Through Resources

### For lesson preparation (as tutor):
1. Start with **UNSW Music Acoustics** for instrument-specific deep dives
2. Use **Dan Russell's animations** for wave phenomena visualizations
3. Reference **Fletcher & Rossing** for theoretical rigor and derivations
4. Browse **CCRMA resources** for synthesis and modern techniques

### For student exploration (intermediate level):
1. **Explore interactively:** PhET sims, Chrome Music Lab, Circles Sines Signals
2. **Watch:** Sixty Symbols, Physics Girl for engaging explanations
3. **Read:** "Science of Sound" (Rossing et al.) as primary text
4. **Experiment:** Record with Audacity, analyze spectra, vary instrument parameters
5. **Code (optional):** Implement Karplus-Strong, experiment with Tone.js

### For going deeper (rabbit holes):
1. **Physical modeling:** Julius Smith's online books + code
2. **Specific instruments:** UNSW pages for violin, flute, trumpet, etc.
3. **Perception:** Howard & Angus "Acoustics and Psychoacoustics"
4. **Research frontier:** Browse JASA, ISMA proceedings, NIME papers

## Tools for Hands-On Learning

Students should be encouraged to:
- **Measure wave speed on strings:** Vary tension, length, mass; predict and verify
- **Record and analyze spectra:** Compare violin, flute, clarinet on same pitch
- **Build simple instruments:** Straw oboe, PVC pipe flute, shoebox guitar
- **Experiment with tone holes:** Drill holes in tubes, measure frequency shifts
- **Visualize modes:** Chladni plates (sand on vibrating metal), water surface waves
- **Code synthesis:** Implement Karplus-Strong in Python or JavaScript

These experiments bridge theory and experience, making abstract concepts concrete.
