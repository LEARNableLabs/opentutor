# Competitive Rock Climbing — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

### Route Reading Cluster
1. **Visual scanning patterns** — systematic approach to reading routes from bottom to top, identifying key features
2. **Hold identification** — recognizing hold types (crimps, slopers, pinches, pockets) and grip strategies
3. **Rest position spotting** — identifying where energy can be recovered during a route
4. **Sequence chunking** — breaking routes into manageable sections between rest positions. Depends on: rest position spotting
5. **Energy management** — distributing effort across a route to avoid failure. Depends on: sequence chunking, rest position spotting
6. **Body position analysis** — understanding how hip orientation and center of gravity affect movement efficiency
7. **Movement efficiency** — optimizing sequences to minimize energy expenditure. Depends on: body position analysis
8. **Onsight strategy** — approach for climbing routes without prior knowledge or practice
9. **Beta refinement** — process of improving movement sequences through iteration
10. **Observation time management** — using preview time effectively in competition settings

### Training Science Cluster
11. **Energy systems** — understanding ATP-CP, anaerobic, and aerobic pathways for different climbing durations
12. **Supercompensation** — training adaptation cycle where performance improves after recovery
13. **Recovery adaptation** — physiological processes that occur during rest periods. Depends on: supercompensation
14. **Overtraining syndrome** — state of accumulated fatigue from inadequate recovery
15. **Periodization** — structured approach to organizing training into phases with specific goals
16. **Training blocks** — focused training periods targeting specific qualities. Depends on: periodization
17. **Tapering** — strategic reduction in training volume before competition. Depends on: periodization
18. **Peaking** — arriving at maximum performance at the right time. Depends on: tapering, periodization

### Physical Training Cluster
19. **Recruitment vs. capacity** — distinction between maximal force production and sustained effort ability
20. **Strength-endurance continuum** — spectrum of training from pure strength to pure endurance
21. **Fingerboard protocols** — structured hanging routines for finger strength development
22. **Power training readiness** — prerequisites for safe campus board and dynamic training
23. **Campus board protocols** — specific exercises for power development. Depends on: power training readiness
24. **Limit bouldering** — training at maximum difficulty to develop recruitment. Depends on: recruitment vs. capacity
25. **Volume training** — accumulating climbing time to build capacity. Depends on: recruitment vs. capacity
26. **Core tension** — ability to maintain body position on steep terrain
27. **Antagonist training** — strengthening opposing muscle groups to prevent injury and maintain balance
28. **Mobility requirements** — flexibility needed for specific movement patterns
29. **Functional flexibility** — range of motion that directly improves climbing performance

### Mental Training Cluster
30. **Fight-or-flight response** — physiological arousal that can help or hinder performance
31. **Performance anxiety** — psychological stress in competitive or challenging situations
32. **Physiological arousal** — activation level of the nervous system. Relates to: fight-or-flight response
33. **Mental rehearsal** — visualizing movement sequences before execution
34. **Motor imagery** — creating mental representations of physical movements. Depends on: mental rehearsal
35. **Visualization protocols** — structured approaches to mental training
36. **Isolation zone management** — psychological strategies for competition waiting areas
37. **Flow state** — optimal performance state characterized by full engagement and automaticity
38. **Optimal arousal** — activation level that maximizes performance. Relates to: physiological arousal, flow state
39. **Performance zone** — range of arousal levels where performance is optimal

### Competition Tactics Cluster
40. **Warm-up structure** — organized preparation before competition efforts
41. **Activation without fatigue** — achieving readiness without depleting energy. Depends on: warm-up structure
42. **Time management** — allocating effort across multiple problems or routes in competition
43. **Effort distribution** — deciding how much energy to invest in each attempt. Depends on: time management, energy management
44. **Strategic rest** — choosing when to recover during competition. Depends on: effort distribution
45. **Problem selection** — deciding which routes to attempt and in what order

### Injury Prevention Cluster
46. **Pulley anatomy** — understanding finger tendon structure and injury mechanisms
47. **Load management** — controlling training volume and intensity to prevent injury
48. **Progressive overload** — gradually increasing training stress. Depends on: load management
49. **Pain vs. injury signals** — distinguishing between productive discomfort and damage
50. **Autoregulation** — adjusting training based on readiness and response
51. **Recovery monitoring** — tracking markers to guide training decisions. Depends on: autoregulation
52. **Training load management** — systematically controlling cumulative training stress. Depends on: load management, recovery monitoring

## Dependencies

### Critical Foundations
- **Rest position spotting** is foundational for both route reading and competition tactics — without identifying rest positions, sequence chunking and effort distribution become inefficient
- **Energy systems** knowledge is essential before understanding periodization, tapering, and warm-up protocols — the "why" behind timing and intensity choices
- **Recruitment vs. capacity** distinction underlies all physical training method selection — without this, training becomes random

### Sequential Build-ups
- **Sequence chunking** builds on **rest position spotting** — you can't chunk efficiently until you know where you can recover
- **Movement efficiency** depends on **body position analysis** — optimizing sequences requires understanding how positioning affects energy cost
- **Beta refinement** requires understanding **onsight strategy** first — you need baseline approach before iteration
- **Tapering** and **peaking** require **periodization** foundation — you can't time peak performance without structured phases
- **Campus board protocols** require **power training readiness** — jumping straight to campus without strength baseline increases injury risk
- **Effort distribution** integrates **time management** and **energy management** — competition tactics synthesize route reading and training science

### Cross-Cluster Connections
- **Energy management** (route reading) connects to **energy systems** (training science) — understanding physiology improves tactical decisions
- **Optimal arousal** (mental) connects to **performance anxiety** and **flow state** — managing arousal is key to both reducing anxiety and achieving flow
- **Load management** (injury prevention) connects to **periodization** (training science) — systematic training planning prevents overuse
- **Visualization protocols** (mental) enhance **beta refinement** (route reading) — mental rehearsal improves movement sequence optimization

## Bottlenecks

### Conceptual Bottlenecks
1. **Energy systems** — if not understood, periodization, training method selection, and competition warm-ups lack foundation
2. **Recruitment vs. capacity** — confusion here leads to ineffective training and mixing incompatible stimuli
3. **Rest position spotting** — weak skill here cascades through entire route reading skillset

### Skill Bottlenecks
1. **Sequence chunking** — difficulty here makes competition time management nearly impossible
2. **Autoregulation** — without this, load management becomes arbitrary and injury risk increases
3. **Body position analysis** — weak analysis leads to inefficient movement, limiting both performance and learning

## Common Misconceptions

### Route Reading
- **"Reading means memorizing hold sequences"** — actually about identifying patterns, rest positions, and critical decision points, not rote memorization
- **"More observation time is always better"** — diminishing returns; efficient reading > long reading

### Training Science
- **"Training harder is always better"** — ignores supercompensation and recovery; leads to overtraining
- **"Periodization is only for advanced climbers"** — beginners benefit from structured phases; prevents burnout
- **"Strength and endurance are separate"** — they're a continuum; training one doesn't exclude the other

### Physical Training
- **"Fingerboard training will injure your fingers"** — proper progressive protocol is safer than random hard climbing
- **"Campus board is the fastest way to get stronger"** — it's power-specific; won't help everyone and requires readiness
- **"Flexibility isn't important for climbing"** — functional flexibility unlocks movement options on steep terrain

### Mental Training
- **"Fear is weakness"** — fear is physiological; managing it is skill, not character
- **"Flow state can't be trained"** — trigger conditions can be identified and cultivated
- **"Competition anxiety means you're not ready"** — anxiety is normal; managing it is the skill

### Injury Prevention
- **"Pain means stop immediately"** — need to distinguish productive discomfort from injury signals
- **"Injuries are random bad luck"** — most are preventable through load management
- **"More rest is always better for injury"** — active recovery and progressive loading aid healing

## Prerequisite Topics

### Essential Prerequisites
- **Basic climbing technique** — needed for: body position analysis, movement efficiency, route reading
- **Climbing grades and styles** — needed for: difficulty assessment, training selection, competition understanding
- **Consistent practice experience** — needed for: autoregulation, understanding personal response to training

### Helpful But Not Required
- **Basic anatomy knowledge** — helps with: pulley anatomy, antagonist training, injury prevention
- **General fitness concepts** — helps with: energy systems, supercompensation, recovery
- **Competition observation** — helps with: isolation zone management, competition tactics
