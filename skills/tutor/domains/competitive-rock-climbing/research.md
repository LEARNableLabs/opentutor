# Competitive Rock Climbing — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Route Reading and Beta Analysis
- Movement pattern recognition (body positioning, footwork sequences, rest positions)
- Sequence planning and beta optimization
- Hold type identification and grip strategies
- Visual scanning techniques for onsight climbing
- Competition-specific route reading under time pressure

### 2. Training Science Fundamentals
- Energy system training (aerobic base, anaerobic capacity, ATP-CP system)
- Periodization models for climbing (linear, block, conjugate)
- Strength vs. power vs. endurance continuum
- Training adaptation and recovery science
- Tapering for competition peaks

### 3. Physical Training Methods
- Fingerboard/hangboard protocols (max hangs, repeaters, recruitment)
- Campus board training for power development
- System board and limit bouldering
- Core and antagonist training
- Flexibility and mobility for movement efficiency

### 4. Mental Training and Competition Psychology
- Fear management and fall practice
- Visualization and mental rehearsing
- Competition anxiety and performance under pressure
- Isolation zone strategies
- Flow state cultivation

### 5. Competition Tactics
- Onsight vs. redpoint strategies
- Time management in comp formats (boulder, lead, speed)
- Reading the field and strategic rest
- Warm-up protocols
- Recovery between rounds

### 6. Injury Prevention and Recovery
- Common climbing injuries (finger pulleys, elbows, shoulders)
- Load management and autoregulation
- Active recovery techniques
- Movement quality and technique as injury prevention

## Key Sources

### Textbooks and Training Manuals
- **Training for Climbing** by Eric J. Hörst (3rd edition) — comprehensive training science, intermediate-friendly
- **The Rock Climber's Training Manual** by Michael L. Anderson and Mark L. Anderson — systematic periodization approach
- **9 out of 10 Climbers Make the Same Mistakes** by Dave MacLeod — technique and mental training
- **Performance Rock Climbing** by Dale Goddard and Udo Neumann — sport science foundation

## Gaps and Challenges

- Route reading is highly experiential — limited formal pedagogy exists
- Training science is well-documented but needs adaptation for individual response
- Mental training resources are scattered; few systematic approaches
- Competition tactics often learned through experience rather than formal instruction
- Injury prevention research is growing but still limited compared to other sports
