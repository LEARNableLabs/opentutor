## Domain Hooks
- **Glass stress visualization with polarized light** — Strain patterns become visible with crossed polarizers. Drop after lesson 3 when discussing annealing. Visually striking and directly demonstrates stress.

- **Spectroscopy history** — Spectral line discovery (Fraunhofer, Kirchhoff, Bunsen) led to quantum mechanics. Connect to lesson 10. Shows how craft observation (colored flames) drove fundamental physics.

- **Crookes tubes and cathode rays** — Historical connection between gas discharge tubes and the electron's discovery. Drop during lesson 13 (cathode fall region). Shows how neon physics connects to foundational science.

- **Uranium glass and fluorescence** — Before phosphors, uranium glass created green glow. Interesting historical parallel and introduces fluorescence concept before lesson 15.

- **Plasma physics connections** — Glow discharge is a low-temperature plasma; same physics governs fusion research and spacecraft propulsion. Drop during lesson 12-13 for students i

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Glass has a melting point like ice"** — Students expect a sharp solid-to-liquid transition. Glass is an amorphous solid with a gradual glass transition temperature (Tg). Corrected by showing viscosity curves and emphasizing the working range concept (lesson 2).

2. **"You can make green neon by mixing blue and yellow gases"** — Spectral lines don't mix like pigments; you see discrete wavelengths simultaneously, not blended hues. Corrected by showing emission spectra and explaining additive color vs. spectral superposition (lesson 16).

3. **"Thicker glass is stronger for tight bends"** — Thicker walls increase rigidity, making tight bends *harder* without collapse. Thin-walled tubing allows tighter radii. Corrected by discussing wall thickness vs. bend radius relationships (lesson 6).
