# Neon Sign Fabrication — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Let There Be Neon (NYC)** — https://www.lettherebeneonneon.com/ — Historic neon school offering hands-on courses. Excellent for practical bending technique (lessons 5-9, 17).

- **American Scientific Glassblowers Society (ASGS)** — https://www.glassblower.info/ — Professional organization with technical bulletins, safety standards, and glassworking resources. Best source for glass science fundamentals (lessons 1-4).

- **HyperPhysics (Georgia State University)** — https://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/ — Comprehensive physics education resource. Sections on atomic spectra, gas discharge, and glow discharge physics are excellent for lessons 10-13.

- **The Neon Museum (Las Vegas)** — https://www.neonmuseum.org/ — Educational programming, historical collection, restoration documentation. Great for design context and real-world examples (lessons 17-18, 20).

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos

- **YouTube: "scientific glassblowing"** — https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=scientific+glassblowing — Demonstrations of torch technique, tube bending, and annealing. Use for lessons 2, 5-6.

- **YouTube: "neon sign bending"** — https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=neon+sign+bending — Real-time neon fabrication from pattern to finished sign. Excellent for lesson 8 (complex bends) and lesson 17 (pattern translation).

- **YouTube: "glass bending torch technique"** — https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=glass+bending+torch+technique — Close-up technique demonstrations. Use for lesson 5.

- **YouTube: "neon sign making"** — https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=neon+sign+making — Full workflow documentation. Overview context for lesson 21.

### Interactive Tools

- **PhET: Neon Lights & Other Discharge Lamps** — https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/neon-lights-and-discharge-lamps — Interactive simulation of gas discharge with adjustable voltage and gas type. Perfect for lessons 10-14.

- **PhET: Discharge Lamps** — https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/discharge-lamps — Simplified discharge physics simulator. Good introduction for lesson 12.

- **HyperPhysics: Atomic Spectra** — https://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/quantum/atspec.html — Interactive energy level diagrams showing electron transitions. Use for lesson 10.

- **HyperPhysics: Glow Discharge** — https://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/glodis.html — Diagrams and explanations of discharge physics. Core reference for lessons 12-13.

- **HyperPhysics: Glow Discharge Zones** — https://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/glozone.html — Detailed breakdown of cathode fall and positive column. Reference for lesson 13.

### Books and Texts

- **"Scientific Glass Blowing" by Wheeler and Wheeler** — Classic text on glass properties and technique. Covers lessons 1-4 material in depth.

- **"Neon Techniques" (Handbook series)** — Trade publication series covering practical neon work. Hard to find but authoritative.

- **"Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion" by Francis Chen** — Graduate text; chapter 3 on gas discharge is accessible at intermediate level for motivated students (lessons 11-13 extension).

### Code and Simulations

- **Gas Discharge Simulators** — Several open-source physics simulators available on GitHub for modeling discharge physics. Search "gas discharge simulation" for options.

### People (practitioners and researchers to look up)

- **William Concannon** — Master neon bender, educator at Let There Be Neon, historian of the craft.

- **Meryl Pataky** — Neon artist and preservationist, co-author of neon technique guides.

- **James Clerk Maxwell** — Foundational work on gas kinetic theory relevant to collision processes (lesson 11 rabbit hole).

- **J.J. Thomson** — Discovered electron using cathode ray tubes (gas discharge apparatus). Historical connection for lesson 13.

- **Irving Langmuir** — Nobel laureate who studied gas discharge and coined the term "plasma." Connection to lessons 12-13.

### Unexpected Connections

- **Aurora borealis physics** — Same gas discharge mechanisms, just at atmospheric scale with solar wind as the voltage source. Spectacular natural example of lessons 10-13 concepts.

- **Fluorescent lighting** — Direct descendant of neon technology using mercury vapor and phosphors. Modern application of lesson 15 principles.

- **Plasma displays (obsolete technology)** — Used noble gas discharge for each pixel. Historical tech connection to lessons 11-12.

- **Spacecraft ion thrusters** — Noble gas ionization for propulsion. High-tech application of ionization physics from lesson 11.

- **Geiger counters** — Gas discharge device for radiation detection. Different regime (proportional vs. glow) but related physics (lesson 12).

## Safety Resources

- **NFPA 70: National Electrical Code (Article 600)** — Standards for electric signs and outline lighting. Critical for lesson 19-20.

- **High Voltage Safety Guides** — Multiple sources available; any university electrical safety manual covers the basics. Essential for lessons 19-20.

## Additional Learning Paths

- **Scientific glassblowing programs** — Cornell, MIT, Princeton, and other research universities offer formal training. More rigorous than neon-specific schools but excellent glass science foundation.

- **Community college glass programs** — Many offer cold glass and flameworking courses that cover overlapping technique.

- **Museum restoration programs** — The Neon Museum and similar institutions occasionally offer workshops on sign restoration, combining craft and preservation science.
