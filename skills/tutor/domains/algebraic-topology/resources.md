# Algebraic Topology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

**Allen Hatcher - Algebraic Topology** (Cornell University, Free Online)
https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf
The modern standard reference. Excellent balance of geometric intuition and rigorous proofs. Freely available PDF with outstanding diagrams. Covers fundamental group, covering spaces, homology, cohomology, and higher homotopy groups. Best for intermediate students who want complete treatments with motivation. ~550 pages, but modular — chapters can be read independently.

**Robert M. Switzer - Algebraic Topology: Homotopy and Homology** (Springer, 1975)
https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61923-6
More advanced perspective, systematically building to generalized homology theories. Assumes modest background and leads to spectral sequences and stable homotopy theory. Good for students wanting to go deeper after core material. More categorical than Hatcher.

**Tammo tom Dieck - Algebraic Topology** (EMS Press, 2008)
https://ems.press/books/etb/57
Two-part structure: Part I for two introductory courses (homotopy, homology), Part II for advanced applications (duality, characteristic classes, bordism, K-theory). Comprehensive and modern. Good alternative to Hatcher with different organizational approach.

**Alexander Kupers - Lectures on Algebraic Topology** (University of Toronto, 2023)
https://www.utsc.utoronto.ca/people/kupers/wp-content/uploads/sites/50/algtop-2020.pdf
Modern lecture notes from 2020 course. Clear exposition with good exercises. More concise than Hatcher (~180 pages). Covers fundamental group, covering spaces, homology, cohomology. Excellent as primary text for structured course at intermediate level.

**Clara Löh - Algebraic Topology: An Introductory Course** (University of Regensburg, 2018)
https://loeh.app.uni-regensburg.de/teaching/topologie1_ws1819/lecture_notes.pdf
Comprehensive lecture notes (~400 pages) from winter 2018/19 course. Detailed proofs with pedagogical emphasis. Good exercises throughout. Covers fundamental group through cohomology. More European style (definition-theorem-proof) than Hatcher's motivation-first approach.

**Tim Perutz - Algebraic Topology I** (UT Austin)
https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/perutz/PDF_files/AlgTop.pdf
Lecture notes covering fundamental group and homology. Concise but complete. Good supplement to main textbook for alternative perspective on core topics.

**Robert Ghrist - Homology: An Applied Perspective** (UPenn)
https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~ghrist/preprints/HAD.pdf
Applications-focused introduction connecting homology to sensor networks, data analysis, and engineering. Excellent for students wanting to see practical applications. Less comprehensive on theory, more emphasis on computation and TDA (topological data analysis).

### Reference Books

**Anant R. Shastri - Basic Algebraic Topology** (Routledge, 2013)
https://www.routledge.com/Basic-Algebraic-Topology/Shastri/p/book/9781466562431
Systematic introduction covering relative homotopy, fibrations, cofibrations, cell complexes, fundamental group, covering spaces, and homology. Good for students wanting categorical perspective from the start.

**James Munkres - Elements of Algebraic Topology** (Westview Press, 1984)
Classic textbook, more elementary than Hatcher. Good for beginners or as gentle introduction before tackling Hatcher. Strong on simplicial homology.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Video Lectures

**N.J. Wildberger - Algebraic Topology: A Beginner's Course** (UNSW, 2010)
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL41FDABC6AA085E78
Full course (47 videos) from University of New South Wales. Wildberger is calm, experienced lecturer with smooth pacing. Good for visual learners. Covers fundamental group, covering spaces, simplicial homology. More accessible than MIT OCW for intermediate students.

**Full course page**: https://freevideolectures.com/course/2721/algebraic-topology

**MIT OpenCourseWare - Algebraic Topology I** (Fall 2016)
https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-905-algebraic-topology-i-fall-2016/
Graduate-level course covering singular homology, CW complexes, homological algebra, cohomology, and Poincaré duality. Complete lecture videos, notes, and problem sets. More rigorous and faster-paced than Wildberger. Excellent for motivated intermediate students.

**Anthony Bosman - Algebraic Topology** (Andrews University)
Uses Hatcher's textbook with excellent video production. Great teaching quality. Specific links not available from search, but mentioned as highly recommended resource.

**Pierre Albin - Algebraic Topology** (University of Illinois)
Great lecturer with good video quality. Also uses Hatcher's textbook. Recommended for students who prefer lecture format over reading.

### Interactive Tools & Visualizations

**The Geometry Center - Outside In** (Sphere Eversion Video)
Classic visualization of sphere eversion (turning sphere inside-out). Demonstrates non-intuitive homotopies. Available on various platforms (YouTube, archive.org).

**GeoGebra - Topology Applets**
https://www.geogebra.org/
Interactive visualizations for fundamental group, covering spaces, surfaces. Search for "fundamental group" or "covering spaces" in GeoGebra materials. User-contributed content varies in quality.

**Desmos - Parametric Surface Visualizations**
https://www.desmos.com/calculator
Useful for visualizing surfaces (torus, Möbius strip, Klein bottle). Students can build intuition for quotient topology and identification spaces.

**Topology Applets - Various Sources**
Many university course pages include Java/JavaScript applets for visualizing homotopy, covering spaces, and homology. Quality varies. Check course pages from MIT, Cornell, Stanford.

### Computational Tools

**SageMath - Simplicial Complexes and Homology**
https://www.sagemath.org/
Free open-source mathematics software. Built-in support for simplicial complexes and homology computation. Excellent for verifying hand calculations and building computational intuition. Requires basic Python knowledge.

**CHomP (Computational Homology Project)**
http://chomp.rutgers.edu/
Specialized software for computing homology of cubical and simplicial complexes. More advanced, good for research-level computations.

**Ripser - Persistent Homology**
https://github.com/Ripser/ripser
Fast computation of persistent homology for topological data analysis. Connects classical algebraic topology to modern applications in data science.

### Applied & Computational Algebraic Topology

**Preparatory videos for Applied Algebraic Topology** (University of Iowa)
http://homepage.math.uiowa.edu/~idarcy/COURSES/TDA/TDAprelectures.html
Videos connecting classical algebraic topology to topological data analysis. Good for students interested in applications to machine learning and data science.

**Michael Robinson - Computational Algebraic Topology** (American University)
Course focused on computational aspects. Specific lecture videos not indexed from search, but course materials may be available from instructor's page.

**Class Central - Applied Algebraic Topology Courses**
https://www.classcentral.com/subject/applied-algebraic-topology
Aggregator of online courses. Lists 80+ courses on applied algebraic topology, including MOOC-style courses from various platforms. Quality varies.

## People to Follow

### Classic Figures (Historical Context)
- **Henri Poincaré** — founder of algebraic topology; introduced fundamental group, homology
- **Emmy Noether** — developed abstract algebraic approach to topology
- **Heinz Hopf** — Hopf fibration, Hopf invariant, fundamental contributions
- **Samuel Eilenberg** — homological algebra, category theory co-founder
- **Norman Steenrod** — cohomology operations, fiber bundles

### Modern Researchers (Living)
- **Allen Hatcher** (Cornell) — textbook author, fundamental group expert
- **Robert Ghrist** (UPenn) — applied algebraic topology, TDA pioneer
- **Gunnar Carlsson** (Stanford) — topological data analysis, persistent homology
- **Kathryn Hess** (EPFL) — homotopy theory, applications to neuroscience
- **Robert Bruner** (Wayne State) — homotopy groups of spheres computations

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### To Physics
- **Gauge Theory** — connections on fiber bundles, characteristic classes (Chern classes) classify gauge fields
- **String Theory** — cohomology of Calabi-Yau manifolds determines particle physics
- **Condensed Matter** — topological phases of matter (topological insulators), characterized by K-theory invariants

### To Data Science
- **Topological Data Analysis (TDA)** — persistent homology detects features in high-dimensional data
- **Shape Analysis** — Mapper algorithm (Gurjeet Singh, Gunnar Carlsson) for exploratory data visualization
- **Neuroscience** — blue brain project uses algebraic topology to analyze neural networks

### To Computer Science
- **Distributed Computing** — coverage problems in sensor networks (Ghrist's work)
- **Concurrency Theory** — directed algebraic topology models concurrent processes
- **Computer Graphics** — mesh processing, shape matching using topological signatures

### To Biology
- **Protein Folding** — knot theory and topology of protein structures
- **Phylogenetics** — tree space has non-trivial topology
- **DNA Topology** — knot theory applied to DNA supercoiling and recombination

### To Economics & Social Science
- **Game Theory** — fixed point theorems (Brouwer, Kakutani) prove existence of Nash equilibria
- **Social Network Analysis** — persistent homology detects communities and structure
- **Economics** — topological methods in general equilibrium theory
