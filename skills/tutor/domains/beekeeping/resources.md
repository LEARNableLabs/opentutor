# Beekeeping — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **The Hive and the Honey Bee** (Dadant & Sons, ed.) — Comprehensive reference, 740+ pages covering all aspects of apiculture. Industry standard text, updated periodically. Can be dense but authoritative. Intermediate students should use this as encyclopedia, not cover-to-cover read.

- **The Beekeeper's Handbook** by Diana Sammataro & Alphonse Avitabile — Practical management guide organized by seasonal tasks. Excellent for intermediate beekeepers building their annual management calendar. Includes troubleshooting charts and decision trees.

- **Biology of the Honey Bee** by Mark Winston — The best single-volume overview of honey bee biology. Accessible science writing. Essential for understanding the "why" behind management practices. Good bridge between academic research and practical beekeeping.

- **Honeybee Democracy** by Thomas Seeley — Focused on swarm intelligence and nest site selection. Beautifully written popular science. Excellent supplement for lessons on colony decision-making and swarm biology.

### University Extension Programs

- **University of Florida Honey Bee Research and Extension Lab**
  - https://entnemdept.ufl.edu/honey-bee/
  - Comprehensive curriculum, fact sheets, videos
  - Strong on Africanized bees and subtropical beekeeping
  - Dr. Jamie Ellis's extension materials are exceptionally clear

- **Penn State Extension Beekeeping**
  - https://extension.psu.edu/honeybee-biology-and-behavior
  - Excellent online courses and webinars
  - Strong on temperate climate management
  - Good disease and pest resources

- **Cornell University Pollinator Network**
  - https://pollinator.cals.cornell.edu/bees/beekeeping/
  - Master Beekeeping Program materials
  - Thomas Seeley's research group resources
  - New York climate focus but broadly applicable

- **UC Davis Honey and Pollination Center**
  - https://honey.ucdavis.edu/
  - Western US perspective
  - Strong on pollination biology and honey chemistry
  - Good almond pollination resources (even if not relevant to student)

### Online Courses

- **Bee Informed Partnership** — https://beeinformed.org/
  - Evidence-based management resources
  - Annual colony loss surveys (invaluable data on what works and what doesn't)
  - Online courses on IPM and monitoring
  - Free, science-backed, regularly updated

- **Master Beekeeping Programs** (various universities)
  - Structured progression from apprentice to master level
  - Cornell, University of Montana, and others offer these
  - Usually regional-specific, which is a feature not a bug

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### From Physics
- **Thermoregulation as thermodynamics** — Colony as heat engine, metabolic heat production, insulation
- **Waggle dance as vector encoding** — Bee as analog computer performing coordinate transformations

### From Computer Science
- **Swarm intelligence algorithms** — Particle swarm optimization, ant colony optimization, bee algorithm
- **Distributed systems** — Colony as distributed computing problem, consensus algorithms
- **Agent-based modeling** — Simulating colony behavior from individual bee rules

### From Economics
- **Optimal foraging theory** — Cost-benefit analysis in flower choice, marginal value theorem
- **Division of labor** — Task allocation, age polyethism as career progression
- **Colony as firm** — Resource allocation, labor markets within hive

### From Architecture
- **Comb construction** — Hexagonal tiling, minimal surface area structures, wax economy
- **Nest thermoregulation** — Passive solar design, insulation strategies, ventilation
- **Swarm site selection** — Site evaluation criteria, consensus decision-making processes

### From Medicine/Public Health
- **Social immunity** — Grooming behavior, hygienic behavior, propolis as antimicrobial
- **Disease transmission** — Mites as vectors, horizontal vs. vertical transmission
- **Herd immunity** — Not applicable to bees (can't vaccinate whole colony), but instructive contrast

### From Linguistics
- **Waggle dance as language** — Symbolic representation, abstract communication, information transfer
- **Does it meet language criteria?** — Displacement, productivity, arbitrariness (partially yes!)

### From Ecology
- **Pollination networks** — Mutualism, co-evolution, ecosystem services
- **Invasive species** — Africanized bees, varroa mites as introduced species
- **Metapopulation dynamics** — Swarming as dispersal, genetic flow between colonies

### From History
- **Agricultural Revolution** — Beekeeping as early animal husbandry (9,000 years ago)
- **Industrial Revolution** — Movable frame hive invention (Langstroth, 1852) as manufacturing innovation
- **Green Revolution** — Modern pesticides and pollinator decline
- **Anthropocene** — Colony Collapse Disorder as marker of human-altered ecosystems

## Tools & Equipment Resources

### Essential Tools
- **Refractometer** — For measuring honey moisture content (handheld, ~$30-80)
  - More reliable than "capped = ripe" rule
  
- **Alcohol wash jar or sugar roll tube** — For varroa monitoring
  - Homemade or commercial versions work
  - Essential for threshold-based IPM

- **Hive scale (optional but valuable)** — Monitor nectar flow and food stores remotely
  - Digital scales with remote monitoring available
  - Great data for learning colony dynamics

- **Inspection journal/app** — Paper or digital
  - Consistency matters more than medium
  - Include: date, weather, temperament, brood pattern, stores, observations, actions taken

### Where to Buy Equipment
- **Local bee supply** — Support local if available, immediate help with questions
- **Online suppliers** — Mann Lake, Dadant, Brushy Mountain Bee Farm, Betterbee
- **Build your own** — Many beekeepers make their own boxes, frames (woodworking hobby synergy)

### Safety Equipment
- **Bee suit/jacket** — Quality matters, ventilation important
- **Gloves** — Leather vs. nitrile (trade-off: protection vs. dexterity)
- **Smoker** — Stainless steel, proper fuel (burlap, pine needles, cardboard)
- **Hive tool** — Standard vs. J-hook (personal preference)

## Regional Variation Resources

Beekeeping is fundamentally local. Students should seek resources specific to their region:

- **Climate zone** — Northern vs. southern management differs dramatically
- **Flora** — Local nectar flows determine management timing
- **Pests** — Africanized bees (south), bears (rural), small hive beetles (south/humid)
- **Regulations** — State apiary inspection programs, registration requirements vary

**Action item for students**: Find your state's apiary inspector or extension apiculturist. They know local conditions better than any book.
