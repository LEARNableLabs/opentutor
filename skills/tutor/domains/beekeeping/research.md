# Beekeeping Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Colony Biology
- **Caste system** — queen, workers, drones; roles and lifecycle
- **Brood development** — egg, larva, pupa stages; timing and temperature requirements
- **Colony superorganism** — collective decision-making, thermoregulation, communication
- **Seasonal cycle** — buildup, nectar flow, dearth, overwintering

### Hive Management
- **Hive inspections** — what to look for, frequency, techniques
- **Swarm prevention and capture** — recognizing swarm signals, splits, swarm traps
- **Queen management** — finding queens, requeening, queen rearing basics
- **Equipment** — hive types (Langstroth, top-bar, Warre), frame manipulation, protective gear

### Health and Pests
- **Varroa mite management** — monitoring, treatment options (chemical vs. integrated)
- **Disease recognition** — American/European foulbrood, nosema, chalk brood
- **Pest management** — small hive beetle, wax moths, bears, skunks
- **Colony collapse disorder** — causes, prevention

### Production
- **Honey harvesting** — when to harvest, extraction methods, processing
- **Wax and propolis** — collection and uses
- **Pollination services** — managing bees for agriculture
- **Splits and expansion** — making increase, sustainable beekeeping

## Resource Quality Notes

**Intermediate level considerations:**
- Students at this level have basic hive management down (opening hives, recognizing brood patterns)
- Need deeper understanding of WHY (colony biology) not just WHAT (checklists)
- Ready for integrated pest management (not just "follow treatment protocol")
- Can handle more nuance: trade-offs between management strategies, regional variation
- Should start thinking about sustainable increase, not just maintaining existing colonies
- Need to develop diagnostic skills (is this normal? is this a problem?)

**Hands-on requirement:**
- Beekeeping cannot be learned purely from reading/videos
- Curriculum should emphasize what to observe, what to try, what to measure
- Each lesson should connect to actual hive inspections and seasonal tasks
- Students should be encouraged to keep inspection logs and track observations
