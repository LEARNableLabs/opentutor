# Research Summary — The Science of Coffee

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Coffee Biology & Cultivation
- **Coffea** genus botany (C. arabica vs. C. canephora/robusta)
- Growing conditions: altitude, climate, soil chemistry
- Cherry development and ripening biochemistry
- Terroir and flavor precursor formation
- Sustainable agriculture and climate impact

### 2. Processing Methods
- Wet (washed) vs. dry (natural) processing
- Honey/pulped natural processing
- Fermentation microbiology and flavor development
- Drying kinetics and moisture content targets
- Post-harvest defects and quality control

### 3. Roasting Chemistry
- Maillard reactions and caramelization
- First crack vs. second crack (physical and chemical changes)
- Roast profiles: light, medium, dark
- Volatile compound formation (aldehydes, ketones, furans, pyrazines)
- Heat transfer modes and roaster design

### 4. Extraction Science
- Solubility and extraction kinetics
- Grind size distribution and surface area
- Brew ratios, temperature, and contact time
- Extraction yield vs. strength (TDS)
- Brewing methods: espresso, pour-over, immersion, cold brew
- Water chemistry: hardness, alkalinity, pH

### 5. Sensory Science & Cupping
- Coffee flavor wheel (SCA)
- Olfaction and taste perception
- Acidity, sweetness, bitterness, body, aftertaste
- Cupping protocol and scoring
- Defects and off-flavors
- Sensory adaptation and palate training

## Key Sources

### Academic & Research Institutions
- **UC Davis Coffee Center** (https://coffee.ucdavis.edu/) — research on cultivation, processing, sensory science
- **World Coffee Research** (https://worldcoffeeresearch.org/) — breeding, agronomy, quality research
- **Specialty Coffee Association (SCA)** (https://sca.coffee/) — standards, education, flavor wheel, cupping protocols
- **Food Chemistry journals** — Maillard chemistry, volatile compounds, extraction kinetics

### Educational Resources
- **Barista Hustle** (https://www.baristahustle.com/) — technical articles on water chemistry, extraction, grind size
- **James Hoffmann (YouTube)** (https://www.youtube.com/@jameshoffmann) — expert explanations of roasting, brewing, sensory
- **Perfect Daily Grind** (https://perfectdailygrind.com/) — industry news and science articles
- **Coffee Chemistry textbooks** — "Coffee: Recent Developments" (Clarke & Vitzthum), "The Craft and Science of Coffee" (Folmer)

### Courses & Curricula
- **SCA Coffee Skills Program** — modular courses on brewing, roasting, green coffee, sensory
- **Barista Institute courses** — hands-on training with scientific foundations
- **MIT OpenCourseWare Chemistry** (https://ocw.mit.edu/) — general chemistry background for reaction mechanisms

## Target Audience Notes

At the **intermediate level**, students likely:
- Enjoy coffee and have tried different brewing methods
- Understand basic chemistry (acids/bases, organic molecules, phase changes)
- May have brewed at home with some attention to technique
- Want to understand WHY methods work, not just HOW

They need:
- Bridges between chemistry/biology and coffee flavor
- Practical experiments they can try at home
- Balance of theory and sensory exploration
- Real-world troubleshooting (why is my coffee bitter? sour? flat?)
