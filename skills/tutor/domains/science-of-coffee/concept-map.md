# The Science of Coffee — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Coffee species (arabica vs. robusta)** — genetic differences, caffeine content, flavor potential
2. **Terroir** — how altitude, soil, climate shape flavor precursors. Depends on: coffee species
3. **Cherry anatomy** — parchment, mucilage, seed structure
4. **Soil chemistry** — mineral uptake and flavor development. Depends on: terroir
5. **Climate adaptation** — temperature stress, disease, sustainable practices. Depends on: terroir
6. **Fermentation** — microbial activity during processing, acid formation. Depends on: cherry anatomy
7. **Processing methods (washed, natural, honey)** — mucilage removal strategies. Depends on: cherry anatomy, fermentation
8. **Drying kinetics** — moisture content targets, water activity. Depends on: processing methods
9. **Maillard reaction** — amino acids + reducing sugars → color, flavor, aroma. Depends on: (general chemistry prerequisite)
10. **First and second crack** — physical changes during roasting. Depends on: (general chemistry prerequisite)
11. **Roast degree** — light, medium, dark; acidity vs. body trade-offs. Depends on: Maillard reaction, first/second crack
12. **Volatile compounds** — aldehydes, ketones, phenols, furans. Depends on: Maillard reaction, roast degree
13. **Extraction kinetics** — what dissolves and when (acids → sugars → bitters). Depends on: (general chemistry prerequisite)
14. **Grind size and surface area** — particle distribution, extraction rate. Depends on: extraction kinetics
15. **Temperature and solubility** — how heat affects extraction efficiency. Depends on: extraction kinetics
16. **Extraction yield vs. strength (TDS)** — mass extracted vs. concentration. Depends on: extraction kinetics, grind size, temperature
17. **Water chemistry** — hardness, alkalinity, buffering capacity. Depends on: extraction yield, temperature
18. **Flavor wheel** — sensory vocabulary and categorization. Depends on: (sensory perception)
19. **Acidity perception** — pH vs. perceived brightness. Depends on: extraction kinetics, flavor wheel
20. **Temperature and taste** — how cooling changes perception. Depends on: volatile compounds, flavor wheel
21. **Cupping protocol** — standardized sensory evaluation. Depends on: flavor wheel, acidity perception

## Dependencies

### Cultivation → Processing
- **Processing methods** require understanding **cherry anatomy** because you need to know what mucilage is and where it sits to understand how different methods remove it.
- **Fermentation** builds on **cherry anatomy** because the mucilage is the substrate for microbial activity.

### Processing → Roasting
- **Maillard reaction** depends on having **reducing sugars** present, which are developed during **terroir/cultivation** and concentrated during **processing**.
- **Roast degree** choices are informed by **processing method** — natural-processed coffees often roast darker to balance fruitiness; washed coffees showcase acidity at lighter roasts.

### Roasting → Extraction
- **Extraction kinetics** are shaped by **roast degree** because darker roasts are more porous and extract faster.
- **Volatile compounds** from **roasting** are what we're trying to capture during **extraction**.

### Extraction → Sensory
- **Acidity perception** depends on understanding **extraction kinetics** — under-extraction yields sour acids, proper extraction yields bright acidity.
- **Temperature and taste** interactions explain why the same brew can taste different at different stages of cooling, connecting to **volatile compounds** and their release rates.
- **Cupping protocol** integrates all prior knowledge: you evaluate coffee from **fragrance** (dry grounds, roast volatiles) to **aroma** (wet grounds, extraction preview) to **taste** (full extraction, acidity/sweetness/bitterness balance).

## Critical Path

The minimum viable path through this curriculum (if a student needed to shortcut):

1. Coffee species → Cherry anatomy → Processing methods → Maillard reaction → Roast degree → Extraction kinetics → Grind size → Temperature → Extraction yield/TDS → Flavor wheel → Acidity perception

This path covers:
- What coffee is (species, anatomy)
- How it's processed (washed/natural basics)
- How it's roasted (Maillard, roast degree)
- How it's brewed (extraction basics, grind, temp, yield)
- How it's tasted (flavor wheel, acidity)

Everything else enriches this core understanding.

## Bottlenecks

### 1. Maillard Reaction (Lesson 11)
- **Why it's a bottleneck**: This is the first deep chemistry concept. Students need to connect abstract organic chemistry (amino acids, reducing sugars) to concrete sensory experience (brown color, nutty/caramel flavors).
- **Mitigation**: Use visual aids (color change photos), analogy to cooking (bread crust, seared steak), and emphasize that they don't need to memorize every compound — just understand the pattern (heat + sugar + protein → flavor).

### 2. Extraction Yield vs. Strength (Lesson 20)
- **Why it's a bottleneck**: These are commonly confused terms. Yield = how much of the coffee dissolved (%). Strength = how concentrated the brew is (TDS in mg/L or %).
- **Mitigation**: Use the "tea bag in a swimming pool" analogy — high yield (you extracted a lot from the tea bag) but low strength (diluted across tons of water). Provide worked examples with numbers.

### 3. Water Chemistry (Lesson 22)
- **Why it's a bottleneck**: This is the most chemically complex lesson (buffering, mineral interactions, ionic strength). It's also abstract — students can't see hardness or alkalinity.
- **Mitigation**: Keep it practical. Focus on "hard water makes coffee taste dull/chalky; soft water makes coffee taste bright/sharp." Provide a simple water recipe (Third Wave Water, Barista Hustle recipe) and explain why it works without getting lost in ion equations.

## Common Confusions

### Processing vs. Roasting
Students often conflate these stages. "Washed coffee is lighter" — but processing ≠ roast degree. Clarify: washed is a processing method (happens at origin, affects flavor clarity), light roast is a roast degree (happens at roastery, affects acidity/body).

### Acidity vs. Sourness
"Acidity" in coffee is positive (brightness, liveliness); "sourness" is negative (under-extraction, unripe fruit). Same chemical (acids), different perception. Connect to extraction: proper extraction = pleasant acidity; under-extraction = sour.

### Grind Size Direction
"Finer grind = stronger coffee" is half-true. Finer grind → more surface area → faster extraction → higher yield... but if you over-extract, it becomes bitter (not strong). Strength = concentration (brew ratio), not extraction quality.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic chemistry** — needed for: Maillard reaction, extraction kinetics, water chemistry
  - Acids, bases, pH
  - Organic functional groups (alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, esters)
  - Phase changes (solid, liquid, gas)
  - Solubility and concentration

- **Biology basics** — needed for: coffee species, cultivation, fermentation
  - Plant anatomy (not deep — just seeds, fruit structure)
  - Microbiology (fermentation)

- **Physics intuition** — needed for: heat transfer, extraction, grinding
  - Temperature and energy
  - Surface area
  - Diffusion (conceptual)

None of these need to be formal courses — a high school science background is sufficient. The curriculum will re-teach essentials in context.
