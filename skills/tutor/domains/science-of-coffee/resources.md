# The Science of Coffee — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Academic & Research

- **UC Davis Coffee Center** (https://coffee.ucdavis.edu/)
  - Covers: cultivation science, sensory research, sustainability, processing innovations
  - Why it's good: Peer-reviewed research made accessible; excellent for intermediate students who want academic rigor without dense jargon
  - Best for: Lessons 1-10 (cultivation, processing), lesson 26 (sensory)

- **World Coffee Research** (https://worldcoffeeresearch.org/)
  - Covers: coffee genetics, agronomy, climate adaptation, breeding programs, fermentation microbiology
  - Why it's good: Free reports and white papers; global perspective on coffee production
  - Best for: Lessons 1-5 (cultivation), lesson 6 (fermentation)

- **Specialty Coffee Association (SCA)** (https://sca.coffee/)
  - Covers: cupping protocols, flavor wheel, water quality standards, brewing standards, barista education
  - Why it's good: Industry-standard methods; the SCA sets the benchmarks for specialty coffee
  - Best for: Lessons 20 (extraction), 22 (water chemistry), 23-26 (sensory)

### Textbooks & Technical Guides

- **"The Craft and Science of Coffee"** edited by Britta Folmer (Academic Press)
  - Covers: Comprehensive — cultivation to consumption, chemistry, sensory, sustainability
  - Why it's good: Gold-standard reference; chapters written by leading researchers
  - Best for: All modules (reference for deep dives)

- **"Coffee: Recent Developments"** by Clarke & Vitzthum (Wiley-Blackwell)
  - Covers: Biochemistry, roasting chemistry, volatile compounds, health effects
  - Why it's good: Technical depth with clear explanations; intermediate-to-advanced
  - Best for: Lessons 11-15 (roasting), 17-20 (extraction)

- **SCA Water Quality Handbook** (https://sca.coffee/research/water-quality-handbook)
  - Covers: Water hardness, alkalinity, TDS, mineral composition, target ranges
  - Why it's good: Free, practical, backs up recommendations with chemistry
  - Best for: Lesson 22 (water chemistry)

### Online Courses

- **SCA Coffee Skills Program** (https://sca.coffee/education)
  - Modules: Introduction to Coffee, Brewing (Foundation, Intermediate, Professional), Roasting, Sensory Skills, Green Coffee
  - Why it's good: Modular, progressive, industry-recognized certifications
  - Best for: Students wanting structured, certification-track learning alongside this curriculum

### Unexpected Connections (cross-discipline links)

- **Wine chemistry** — tannins, acidity, terroir, fermentation microbiology. Many sensory concepts transfer.
- **Bread baking** — Maillard reactions in crust formation, fermentation in sourdough.
- **Tea science** — extraction kinetics, water chemistry, sensory vocabulary (umami, astringency).
- **Perfumery** — volatile compounds, aroma perception, terpenes (linalool in coffee and lavender).
- **Climate science** — coffee as a case study in crop vulnerability, adaptation strategies, sustainability.
- **Supply chain optimization** — traceability, direct trade, farmer income (economics + ethics).

These connections work as "rabbit holes" for students who want to explore beyond coffee itself.

## Tools & Equipment (for hands-on learning)

### Essential (what students should have or try to access)

- **Grinder** — burr grinder (hand or electric). Consistent grind is the #1 lever for better coffee.
- **Scale** — 0.1g precision. Brew by weight, not volume.
- **Thermometer** — for water temperature experiments.
- **Timer** — to track brew time.
- **Two brewing methods** — suggest pour-over (Hario V60, Chemex) and immersion (French press, AeroPress). Gives flexibility to experiment.

### Nice-to-have (for advanced experiments)

- **Refractometer** — measure TDS (total dissolved solids). VST or Atago. Not required but useful for lesson 20.
- **pH meter** — test acidity claims (lesson 24).
- **Cupping setup** — small bowls, cupping spoons. Can improvise with cereal bowls and soup spoons.
- **Water testing kit** — measure hardness/alkalinity of tap water (lesson 22).

### Where to buy coffee for experiments

- **Local specialty roasters** — freshest option; ask for roast date.
- **Online roasters** — recommend ones with detailed sourcing info (origin, processing, roast date):
  - **Onyx Coffee Lab** (https://onyxcoffeelab.com/)
  - **Heart Coffee Roasters** (https://www.heartroasters.com/)
  - **Tandem Coffee** (https://www.tandemcoffee.com/)
- **Direct from farms** (advanced)** — some farms sell direct; good for understanding supply chain.

**Tip**: For experiments comparing processing methods (lesson 8), buy a washed and natural version of the same origin (e.g., washed Ethiopian Yirgacheffe vs. natural Ethiopian Yirgacheffe). For roast level experiments (lesson 16), buy the same coffee at light, medium, and dark roasts (some roasters sell this as a "roast sampler").
