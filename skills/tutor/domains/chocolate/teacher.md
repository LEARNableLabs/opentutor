## Domain Hooks
1. **Chocolate's role in the Aztec economy** — cacao beans as currency, divine beverage, restricted consumption. Drop when discussing variety (Criollo was royalty's choice). Shows how flavor genetics had political weight.

2. **The mystery of Form VI** — discovered in 1966, decades after Forms I-V. Appears spontaneously in aged chocolate. Why did it hide? Kinetics. Transition from V→VI can take months. Connects to materials science concept: observation timescales matter.

3. **Swiss conching invention** — Rodolphe Lindt accidentally left a mixer running overnight in 1879, discovered smoother chocolate. Shows the interplay of accident and understanding. Modern conching is deliberate, but the discovery was serendipity.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Chocolate flavor is in the bean"**
   - **Why students think this**: We call them "cacao beans" and talk about variety differences
   - **Correction**: Raw beans are unpalatably bitter and astringent. Flavor is **created** through fermentation (precursors) and roasting (Maillard cascade). Genetics provides potential; processing realizes it.

2. **"Fermentation preserves the beans"**
   - **Why students think this**: Association with food preservation (pickles, yogurt)
   - **Correction**: Cacao fermentation **kills the seed**. It's controlled decomposition. Preservation is a side effect; the goal is enzymatic transformation.
