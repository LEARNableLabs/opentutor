# Immunology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Janeway's Immunobiology** (Murphy & Weaver, 10th edition) — the gold standard textbook. Comprehensive, well-illustrated, balances depth with accessibility. Free online edition available at NCBI: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK27092/
  - **Why it's good for intermediate students:** Conceptual frameworks before molecular details, excellent clinical correlations, beautiful diagrams
  - **Best for:** Core curriculum foundation, reference for all topics

- **Cellular and Molecular Immunology** (Abbas, Lichtman & Pillai, 10th edition) — mechanistically detailed, molecular focus
  - **Why it's good for intermediate students:** Deep dives on signaling pathways, receptor structure, molecular mechanisms
  - **Best for:** Students wanting more molecular detail than Janeway provides

- **How the Immune System Works** (Lauren Sompayrac, 6th edition) — conversational, conceptual, short
  - **Why it's good for intermediate students:** Demystifies complex topics with clear analogies and humor
  - **Best for:** Quick overviews, building intuition before tackling detailed mechanisms

### Online Courses

- **Johns Hopkins Immunology** (Coursera) — https://www.coursera.org/learn/hopkins-immunology
  - Structured video lectures by Andrea Cox
  - Intermediate level, clinically oriented
  - Includes quizzes and peer-reviewed assessments
  - **Best for:** Primary video lecture resource, systematic coverage

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 7.014: Introductory Biology** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-014-introductory-biology-spring-2005/
  - Includes immunology units with lecture notes
  - Undergraduate level
  - **Best for:** Problem sets, additional perspectives

### Scientific Organizations

- **British Society for Immunology** — https://www.immunology.org/
  - Educational resources, public engagement materials
  - Excellent infographics and explainer videos
  - **Best for:** Visual summaries, public-friendly explanations

- **American Association of Immunologists (AAI)** — https://www.aai.org/
  - Professional organization resources
  - Career information, research updates
  - **Best for:** Advanced topics, career exploration in immunology

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos

- **Armando Hasudungan** (YouTube) — https://www.youtube.com/@armandohasudungan
  - Hand-drawn whiteboard animations of immunology concepts
  - Clear, visual, intermediate level
  - Covers antibody structure, complement, hypersensitivity, autoimmunity
  - **Best for:** Visual learners, reinforcement of complex topics

- **Khan Academy Immunology** — https://www.khanacademy.org/science/biology/human-biology/immunology
  - Free video series covering immune system basics
  - Beginner to intermediate level
  - Includes practice questions
  - **Best for:** Accessible introductions, review

- **Osmosis** — https://www.osmosis.org/learn/Immunology
  - Medical school-level videos with animations
  - Clinical focus
  - **Best for:** Clinical correlations, pathology

- **Nature Video** — https://www.youtube.com/user/NatureVideoChannel
  - Research highlights, cutting-edge immunology
  - Includes Nobel Prize explainers (checkpoint inhibitors)

### Interactive Tools

- **HHMI BioInteractive** — https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/immunology
  - Click & Learn modules on immune system, vaccines, HIV
  - Interactive animations of antibody-antigen binding, T cell activation
  - Virtual labs and case studies
  - **Best for:** Hands-on exploration, visual learners, self-directed learning

- **RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB)** — https://www.rcsb.org/
  - 3D structures of antibodies, MHC molecules, TCRs, cytokines
  - Interactive molecular viewers
  - Examples: 1HZH (antibody), 1AKJ (MHC class I), 1FYT (TCR-pMHC complex)
  - **Best for:** Structural understanding, advanced students

- **ImmunoNodes** — http://immunobase.org/immunoNodes/
  - Interactive pathway diagrams
  - Cytokine networks, signaling cascades
  - **Best for:** Understanding interconnected pathways

- **Immune Epitope Database (IEDB)** — https://www.iedb.org/
  - Database of experimentally validated epitopes
  - Educational modules on epitope prediction
  - **Best for:** Research-oriented students, understanding antigen recognition

### Interactive Simulations

- **PhET Interactive Simulations** — https://phet.colorado.edu/
  - General biology simulations (gene expression, protein synthesis) applicable to immunology
  - **Best for:** Foundational molecular biology concepts

- **Virtual Immunology Lab** — Various universities host these; search for specific topics
  - ELISA simulations, antibody binding experiments
  - **Best for:** Understanding experimental techniques

## Code and Computational Resources

### Bioinformatics Tools

- **BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool)** — https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi
  - Compare antibody/TCR sequences
  - Identify conserved vs variable regions
  - **Best for:** Sequence analysis, understanding V(D)J diversity

- **IMGT (ImMunoGeneTics)** — http://www.imgt.org/
  - Database of immunoglobulin and TCR genes
  - Tools for analyzing receptor sequences
  - **Best for:** Advanced students interested in receptor genetics

### Data Visualization

- **ImmGen (Immunological Genome Project)** — https://www.immgen.org/
  - Gene expression data across immune cell types
  - Interactive browsers
  - **Best for:** Understanding cell-specific gene expression, research applications

- **scRNA-seq Data Browsers** — Various (Tabula Muris, Human Cell Atlas)
  - Single-cell RNA sequencing data showing immune cell diversity
  - **Best for:** Advanced students, understanding cellular heterogeneity

## Primary Literature (Key Papers)

### Foundational Discoveries

- **Burnet, F.M. (1957)** — "A modification of Jerne's theory of antibody production using the concept of clonal selection" — *Australian Journal of Science*
  - Clonal selection theory
  - **Best for:** Historical context, theoretical foundations

- **Tonegawa, S. (1983)** — "Somatic generation of antibody diversity" — *Nature*
  - Nobel Prize work on V(D)J recombination
  - **Best for:** Understanding how antibody diversity is generated

- **Zinkernagel, R.M. & Doherty, P.C. (1974)** — "Restriction of in vitro T cell-mediated cytotoxicity in lymphocytic choriomeningitis within a syngeneic or semiallogeneic system" — *Nature*
  - Discovery of MHC restriction
  - **Best for:** Understanding T cell recognition

### Modern Reviews

- **Nature Reviews Immunology** — https://www.nature.com/nri/
  - High-quality review articles
  - Recent advances, classic topics revisited
  - **Best for:** Deep dives after mastering basics

- **Annual Review of Immunology** — https://www.annualreviews.org/journal/immunol
  - Comprehensive yearly reviews
  - Expert perspectives on subfields
  - **Best for:** Advanced students, staying current

### Clinical Case Reports

- **New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM) Case Records** — https://www.nejm.org/
  - Clinical immunology cases
  - Diagnostic reasoning, pathophysiology
  - **Best for:** Applying immunology to clinical scenarios

## People to Know

### Historical Pioneers

- **Edward Jenner** (1749-1823) — developed the first vaccine (cowpox for smallpox)
- **Louis Pasteur** (1822-1895) — germ theory, vaccine development (rabies, anthrax)
- **Paul Ehrlich** (1854-1915) — side-chain theory (precursor to receptor theory), coined "magic bullet"
- **Élie Metchnikoff** (1845-1916) — discovered phagocytosis, Nobel Prize 1908
- **Frank Macfarlane Burnet** (1899-1985) — clonal selection theory, Nobel Prize 1960
- **Susumu Tonegawa** (b. 1939) — V(D)J recombination mechanism, Nobel Prize 1987
- **Peter Doherty & Rolf Zinkernagel** — MHC restriction, Nobel Prize 1996

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

### Immunology in Evolution

- **"Why zebras have stripes"** — hypothesis involves biting fly avoidance, connects to vector-borne diseases and immune challenges
- **Myxoma virus and rabbit evolution in Australia** — rapid coevolution of pathogen virulence and host immunity
- **Adaptive immunity only in vertebrates** — why? Relates to genome complexity, lymphocyte origins

### Immunology in Art and Culture

- **"The Speckled Monster" (Jennifer Lee Carrell)** — history of smallpox and variolation
- **"An Elegant Defense" (Matt Richtel)** — narrative immunology for general audiences
- **"The Emperor of All Maladies" (Siddhartha Mukherjee)** — cancer biology with immunotherapy chapters

### Immunology and Technology

- **CRISPR-based immune editing** — CAR-T cell engineering, HIV cure research
- **mRNA vaccine platform** — COVID-19 vaccines, future applications (cancer, malaria)
- **Organoid models** — thymic organoids for studying T cell development

### Cross-Disciplinary

- **Psychoneuroimmunology** — how stress affects immune function, mind-body connections
- **Ecological immunology** — how resource availability shapes immune investment in animals
- **Immunometabolism** — how cellular metabolism regulates immune responses (Warburg effect in T cells)
