# Medicinal Chemistry and Drug Design — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Drug Discovery Process** — from target identification to clinical trials
2. **Pharmacophores and Structure-Activity Relationships (SAR)** — understanding how molecular structure affects biological activity
3. **Drug-Target Interactions** — receptor binding, enzyme inhibition, molecular recognition
4. **Physicochemical Properties** — Lipinski's Rule of Five, ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion)
5. **Lead Optimization** — improving potency, selectivity, and drug-like properties
6. **Computer-Aided Drug Design (CADD)** — molecular docking, virtual screening, QSAR
7. **Prodrugs and Drug Metabolism** — metabolic pathways, cytochrome P450, bioactivation
8. **Bioisosteres and Scaffold Hopping** — structural modifications to improve properties
9. **Fragment-Based Drug Discovery** — building drugs from small molecular fragments
10. **Modern Approaches** — targeted covalent inhibitors, PROTACs, biologics vs small molecules

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Foye's Principles of Medicinal Chemistry** (8th ed., 2019) — comprehensive coverage, industry standard
- **The Organic Chemistry of Drug Design and Drug Action** (Silverman, 3rd ed., 2014) — mechanistic focus
- **Burger's Medicinal Chemistry and Drug Discovery** (8-volume series) — exhaustive reference
- **An Introduction to Medicinal Chemistry** (Patrick, 6th ed., 2017) — accessible intermediate-level text

### Online Courses
- **Drug Discovery** (UC San Diego, Coursera) — covers the full pipeline
- **MIT OCW: Drug Discovery and Development** — lecture notes and problem sets
- **Introduction to Drug Hunting** (Novartis, edX) — industry perspective

### Tools and Databases
- **PubChem** — free chemical database with bioactivity data
- **ChEMBL** — medicinal chemistry database from EMBL-EBI
- **DrugBank** — comprehensive drug and drug target database
- **SwissADME** — free web tool for ADME prediction
- **PyMOL** — molecular visualization
- **AutoDock Vina** — molecular docking
- **Schrödinger Suite** (academic license) — professional drug design tools

### Interactive Resources
- **Protein Data Bank (PDB)** — 3D structures of drug targets
- **ChimeraX** (UCSF) — advanced molecular visualization with tutorials
- **RDKit** — open-source cheminformatics toolkit (Python)
- **TeachOpenCADD** — Jupyter notebooks for computational drug design education

## Available Educational Materials

- **Video Lectures**: MIT OCW, Stanford's Drug Discovery seminar series
- **Case Studies**: Nature Reviews Drug Discovery case studies, FDA drug approval packages
- **Software Tutorials**: PyMOL tutorials, AutoDock Vina documentation, RDKit notebooks
- **Research Papers**: Journal of Medicinal Chemistry, ACS Medicinal Chemistry Letters
- **Industry Blogs**: Derek Lowe's "In the Pipeline" (Science Translational Medicine blog)

## Pedagogical Approach

This curriculum balances three modes:
1. **Conceptual** — pharmacophores, SAR, binding theory
2. **Practical** — using computational tools, reading drug literature
3. **Case-based** — real drug discovery examples (aspirin, Gleevec, COVID antivirals)

Intermediate level assumes organic chemistry background and basic biochemistry. Will build toward computational literacy without requiring programming experience.
