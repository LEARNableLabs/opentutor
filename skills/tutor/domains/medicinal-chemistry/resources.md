# Medicinal Chemistry and Drug Design — Resources

## Primary Textbooks

- **An Introduction to Medicinal Chemistry** (Patrick, 6th ed., 2017) — accessible, well-illustrated, perfect for intermediate students. Covers all major concepts with good clinical examples.
  
- **Foye's Principles of Medicinal Chemistry** (Lemke et al., 8th ed., 2019) — comprehensive industry standard. Detailed coverage of drug classes and mechanisms. Use as reference, not cover-to-cover.

- **The Organic Chemistry of Drug Design and Drug Action** (Silverman, 3rd ed., 2014) — mechanistic focus, excellent for understanding how drugs work at the molecular level. Strong on enzyme inhibition and metabolism.

- **Burger's Medicinal Chemistry and Drug Discovery** (Abraham, 8-volume series) — exhaustive reference for deep dives. Use specific chapters rather than reading sequentially.

## Recommended Learning Path

1. Start with **Patrick's textbook** (Chapters 1-10) for foundations
2. Use **SwissADME** and **PubChem** to explore real drug properties
3. Follow **TeachOpenCADD** Jupyter tutorials for hands-on computational practice
4. Read **Derek Lowe's blog** regularly for current events and industry perspective
5. Visualize protein-ligand complexes in **PyMOL** or **ChimeraX** (start with PDB 1IEP - imatinib)
6. Try **AutoDock Vina** tutorial for molecular docking
7. Read case studies in **Nature Reviews Drug Discovery**
8. Explore **ChEMBL** to analyze SAR data for approved drugs

## Quick Reference Cards

- **Lipinski's Rule of Five**: MW < 500, LogP < 5, H-bond donors ≤ 5, H-bond acceptors ≤ 10
- **Veber's Rules**: Rotatable bonds ≤ 10, TPSA ≤ 140 Å²
- **Rule of Three (fragments)**: MW < 300, LogP ≤ 3, H-bond donors ≤ 3, H-bond acceptors ≤ 3
- **Common bioisosteres**: -COOH ↔ tetrazole, -NH2 ↔ -OH, benzene ↔ pyridine, ester ↔ amide
- **CYP450 substrates (hot spots)**: aromatic rings, N-dealkylation, O-dealkylation, hydroxylation of aliphatic carbons
