# Development Economics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Poverty lines** — thresholds for defining who is poor (absolute, relative, multidimensional)
2. **Inequality measurement** — Gini coefficient, Lorenz curves, quantifying distribution
3. **Poverty traps** — mechanisms that lock individuals/regions in persistent poverty. Depends on: poverty lines
4. **Growth-poverty linkage** — how economic growth affects poverty rates. Depends on: poverty lines, inequality measurement
5. **Risk-sharing networks** — informal insurance through reciprocal relationships. Depends on: poverty traps
6. **Returns to schooling** — wage gains from additional education
7. **Credit constraints** — barriers to borrowing for productive investments. Depends on: poverty traps
8. **Opportunity costs** — trade-offs families face in education decisions. Depends on: returns to schooling, credit constraints
9. **Education quality** — distinction between access and learning outcomes. Depends on: returns to schooling
10. **Health externalities** — spillover effects of individual health investments. Depends on: risk-sharing networks
11. **Nutrition-productivity linkage** — how early-life health shapes adult outcomes. Depends on: poverty traps
12. **Cost-effectiveness** — comparing interventions by impact per dollar
13. **Microfinance** — small loans to entrepreneurs without collateral. Depends on: credit constraints
14. **Behavioral barriers to saving** — present bias, self-control, commitment. Depends on: poverty traps
15. **Informal credit markets** — moneylenders, ROSCAs, family loans. Depends on: credit constraints
16. **Adverse selection** — information asymmetries in credit markets. Depends on: informal credit markets
17. **Moral hazard** — hidden actions after contracts are signed. Depends on: informal credit markets
18. **Digital finance** — mobile money and financial inclusion. Depends on: microfinance
19. **Index insurance** — weather-based insurance for farmers. Depends on: moral hazard, adverse selection
20. **Basis risk** — mismatch between insurance payout and actual loss. Depends on: index insurance
21. **Technology adoption** — why farmers resist profitable innovations. Depends on: credit constraints, behavioral barriers to saving
22. **Social learning** — learning from peers' experiences. Depends on: technology adoption, risk-sharing networks
23. **Property rights** — secure land titles and their effects. Depends on: credit constraints
24. **Corruption** — rent-seeking and accountability failures
25. **Targeting** — identifying beneficiaries for social programs. Depends on: poverty lines
26. **Conditional cash transfers** — payments tied to behaviors (school, health). Depends on: targeting
27. **Transparency and monitoring** — reducing corruption through information. Depends on: corruption
28. **Randomized controlled trials** — experimental method for causal inference
29. **Internal validity** — whether results are causal within the study. Depends on: randomized controlled trials
30. **External validity** — whether results generalize beyond the study. Depends on: internal validity
31. **General equilibrium effects** — economy-wide impacts beyond direct effects. Depends on: external validity
32. **Scaling** — translating pilot results to national programs. Depends on: external validity, cost-effectiveness

## Dependencies

- **Poverty traps require poverty lines** because you can't identify trap mechanisms without defining poverty thresholds
- **Credit constraints explain multiple phenomena** — they underpin education underinvestment, technology non-adoption, and microfinance demand
- **Behavioral barriers build on poverty traps** — poverty shapes present bias and risk preferences
- **Informal credit markets arise from credit constraints** — when formal banks fail, informal lenders fill the gap
- **Adverse selection and moral hazard explain informal credit failures** — why interest rates are high and access is limited
- **Social learning requires understanding technology adoption** — peer effects matter most when initial adopters face uncertainty
- **External validity builds on internal validity** — you need causal results before asking if they generalize
- **Scaling requires cost-effectiveness and external validity** — successful pilots need both proven impact and context-appropriate design

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Microeconomics** — needed for: credit constraints, adverse selection, moral hazard, opportunity costs
- **Basic statistics** — needed for: inequality measurement, randomized controlled trials, cost-effectiveness analysis
- **Macroeconomics fundamentals** — needed for: growth-poverty linkage, general equilibrium effects, poverty traps

## Bottlenecks

Students often struggle with:

1. **Distinguishing correlation from causation** — understanding why simple comparisons mislead
2. **Behavioral economics integration** — reconciling rational choice with present bias, self-control issues
3. **External validity** — the tension between rigorous RCTs and generalizable policy lessons
4. **General equilibrium thinking** — moving beyond partial equilibrium to economy-wide effects

## Common Misconceptions

- "Microfinance eliminates poverty" — the evidence shows modest impacts on average, not transformative effects
- "Just give people money" — CCTs work, but targeting, conditionality, and political economy matter
- "RCTs solve everything" — they answer causal questions well but have limits (ethics, external validity, GE effects)
- "Technology adoption is about information" — often it's about risk, learning costs, complementary inputs, and credit
- "Corruption is a cultural problem" — it's about incentives, monitoring, and accountability structures
