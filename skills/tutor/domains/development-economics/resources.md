# Development Economics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Poor Economics" by Abhijit Banerjee and Esther Duflo** (2011) — Accessible, narrative-driven, evidence-based. Perfect for intermediate learners. Covers microfinance, health, education, entrepreneurship. https://www.pooreconomics.com/
- **"Development Economics" by Debraj Ray** (1998) — Comprehensive textbook with theory and evidence. More formal, good for reference. Covers growth, inequality, credit markets, institutions.
- **"Economic Development" by Michael Todaro and Stephen Smith** (12th ed, 2015) — Policy-oriented, covers macro and micro. Good for context on growth, trade, and development strategies.
- **"Randomized Evaluations in Development Economics" by Duflo, Glennerster, Kremer** (2007) — Methods chapter for understanding RCT design. https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Uses%20of%20Randomization%20in%20Development.pdf

### Academic Papers (landmark studies)

- **Miguel & Kremer (2004)**: Worms: Identifying Impacts on Education and Health in the Presence of Treatment Externalities. https://www.nber.org/papers/w8481
- **Banerjee et al (2015)**: The Miracle of Microfinance? Evidence from a Randomized Evaluation. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20130533
- **Dupas (2014)**: Short-Run Subsidies and Long-Run Adoption of New Health Products. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047272710000782
- **Conley & Udry (2010)**: Learning from Others: Technology Adoption and Social Networks. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.100.1.35
- **Ashraf, Karlan, Yin (2006)**: Tying Odysseus to the Mast: Evidence from a Commitment Savings Product in the Philippines. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.1.1.1
- **Suri & Jack (2016)**: The Long-Run Poverty and Gender Impacts of Mobile Money. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap9580

## Unexpected Connections (rabbit holes)

- **The Worm Wars** — Reanalysis debate over the Miguel-Kremer deworming study. Shows the scientific process, publication bias, and replication. https://www.3ieimpact.org/blogs/worm-wars-review-shows-deworming-programs-can-be-effective-some-settings
- **The Great Escape by Angus Deaton** — Historical perspective on how health and wealth diverged. Connects development to economic history.
- **Why Nations Fail (Acemoglu & Robinson)** — Institutional lens on development. Extractive vs. inclusive systems. Broader than RCTs.
- **Effective Altruism and GiveWell** — How cost-effectiveness analysis ($/DALY) guides philanthropy. Connects development research to donor decisions. https://www.givewell.org/
- **Scarcity by Mullainathan & Shafir** — How poverty shapes cognition and decision-making. Behavioral economics meets neuroscience.
- **The Tyranny of Metrics (Jerry Muller)** — Critique of over-reliance on quantification. Complements RCT enthusiasm with caution.
- **Development as Freedom (Amartya Sen)** — Capabilities approach, human development index. Philosophical foundations for development goals.
- **The Bottom Billion (Paul Collier)** — Focus on fragile states, conflict, natural resources. Complements micro-RCT approach with macro-political lens.
