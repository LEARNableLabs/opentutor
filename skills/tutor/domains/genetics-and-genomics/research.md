# Genetics and Genomics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Classical Genetics
- Mendelian inheritance patterns (dominant, recessive, codominance)
- Linkage and recombination
- Chromosomal theory of inheritance
- Non-Mendelian inheritance (incomplete dominance, epistasis, pleiotropy)
- Sex-linked traits and genetic mapping

### 2. Molecular Genetics
- DNA structure and replication
- Central dogma (DNA → RNA → protein)
- Gene expression and regulation
- Mutations and DNA repair mechanisms
- Epigenetics and chromatin modification

### 3. Genomics and Sequencing Technologies
- Genome organization (genes, introns, exons, regulatory elements)
- Sequencing technologies (Sanger, NGS, third-generation)
- Genome assembly and annotation
- Comparative genomics
- Functional genomics (transcriptomics, proteomics)

### 4. Population and Evolutionary Genetics
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium
- Genetic drift, selection, and gene flow
- Molecular evolution and phylogenetics
- Quantitative genetics
- Coalescent theory

### 5. Applied Genomics
- CRISPR and gene editing technologies
- Genetic engineering and synthetic biology
- Personalized medicine and pharmacogenomics
- Cancer genomics
- Agricultural genomics

## Learning Approach

For intermediate learners, the key is bridging classical genetics (which they may have seen at a basic level) with modern genomics. The curriculum should:

1. Start with foundational concepts but at a deeper level (mechanisms, not just patterns)
2. Emphasize the experimental methods and technologies that revealed genetic principles
3. Connect molecular mechanisms to observable phenotypes
4. Build computational literacy for genomics (how to use databases, interpret sequencing data)
5. Include real-world applications and ethical considerations
6. Balance breadth (survey of the field) with depth (key concepts mastered)

## Common Gaps

Students at this level often:
- Understand Mendelian genetics but struggle with molecular mechanisms
- Know DNA structure but not how it's organized into genomes
- Have heard of CRISPR but don't understand the underlying biology
- Can't connect population genetics math to real evolutionary processes
- Need more exposure to how genomics is actually done (computational skills)
