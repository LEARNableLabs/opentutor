# Fluid Dynamics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Fundamentals
- Continuum hypothesis and fluid properties (density, viscosity, pressure)
- Kinematics: Lagrangian vs Eulerian descriptions, streamlines, pathlines, streaklines
- Velocity and acceleration fields

### 2. Conservation Laws
- Conservation of mass (continuity equation)
- Conservation of momentum (Navier-Stokes equations)
- Conservation of energy (Bernoulli's equation and general energy equation)
- Vorticity and circulation

### 3. Inviscid Flow
- Euler equations
- Potential flow theory
- Irrotational flow and complex potential
- Elementary flows (sources, sinks, doublets, vortices)

### 4. Viscous Flow
- Laminar vs turbulent flow
- Reynolds number and flow regimes
- Boundary layer theory (Prandtl's equations)
- Drag and lift

### 5. Dimensional Analysis
- Buckingham Pi theorem
- Similarity and non-dimensional groups
- Model testing and scaling

### 6. Compressible Flow
- Speed of sound and Mach number
- Shock waves and expansion waves
- Isentropic flow relations

### 7. Turbulence (Introduction)
- Nature of turbulence
- Reynolds decomposition and turbulent stresses
- Energy cascade

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Kundu, Cohen & Dowling**: *Fluid Mechanics* (6th ed) — comprehensive, intermediate level, excellent for physical intuition
- **Panton, Ronald L.**: *Incompressible Flow* (4th ed) — strong mathematical treatment
- **White, Frank M.**: *Fluid Mechanics* (8th ed) — engineering-focused, practical
- **Anderson, John D.**: *Fundamentals of Aerodynamics* — excellent for compressible flow

- Oceanography and meteorology

## Recommended Learning Sequence

1. Start with kinematics and visualization (build intuition)
2. Develop conservation laws from first principles
3. Explore inviscid flows (simpler mathematics)
4. Introduce viscosity and boundary layers
5. Dimensional analysis throughout (connects theory to practice)
6. Advanced topics: turbulence, compressibility as capstones
