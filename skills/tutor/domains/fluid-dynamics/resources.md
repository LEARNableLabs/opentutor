# Fluid Dynamics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Kundu, Cohen & Dowling**: *Fluid Mechanics* (6th ed, 2015) — Excellent for intermediate level. Strong on physical intuition, broad coverage, good balance of theory and applications. Covers kinematics through turbulence. Available: academic libraries, [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Fluid-Mechanics-Pijush-K-Kundu/dp/0124059351)

- **Panton, Ronald L.**: *Incompressible Flow* (4th ed, 2013) — More mathematical than Kundu. Rigorous derivations, tensor notation introduced gently. Best for students comfortable with vector calculus. Emphasizes incompressible flows (good for most of this curriculum).

- **White, Frank M.**: *Fluid Mechanics* (8th ed, 2015) — Engineering-focused, practical examples, excellent problem sets. Widely used in undergraduate courses. Less theoretical depth but great for applications and intuition. [McGraw-Hill](https://www.mheducation.com/)

- **Anderson, John D.**: *Fundamentals of Aerodynamics* (6th ed, 2016) — Excellent for compressible flow and aerodynamics applications. Very accessible writing style, historical context, strong on boundary layers and airfoils.

### Online Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 2.25**: *Advanced Fluid Mechanics* (Fall 2013) — Graduate-level but accessible for strong intermediate students. Full lecture notes, problem sets, exams. [https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-25-advanced-fluid-mechanics-fall-2013/](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-25-advanced-fluid-mechanics-fall-2013/)

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 18.355**: *Fluid Mechanics* (Fall 2019) — More mathematical approach from math department perspective. [https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-355j-fluid-mechanics-fall-2019/](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-355j-fluid-mechanics-fall-2019/)

- **Stanford ME 335**: *Introduction to Turbulent Flows* — For the advanced turbulence sections. Some video lectures available through Stanford Engineering.

- **edX/Coursera**: Various offerings from TU Delft, MIT, and other institutions. Search "fluid mechanics" or "aerodynamics" for current courses.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos

- **Khan Academy Physics**: Fluids section — [https://www.khanacademy.org/science/physics/fluids](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/physics/fluids) — Accessible introductions to Bernoulli, pressure, buoyancy. Good for visual learners starting out.

- **minutephysics** (YouTube): [https://www.youtube.com/user/minutephysics](https://www.youtube.com/user/minutephysics) — Short, intuitive explanations. Search for videos on turbulence, Bernoulli, airfoil lift.

- **3Blue1Brown** (YouTube): [https://www.youtube.com/c/3blue1brown](https://www.youtube.com/c/3blue1brown) — Beautiful animations. "Divergence and curl" video excellent for vector calculus prerequisites. Some videos on differential equations relevant to Navier-Stokes.

- **Sixty Symbols** (YouTube): [https://www.youtube.com/user/sixtysymbols](https://www.youtube.com/user/sixtysymbols) — Physics videos from University of Nottingham. Several on turbulence and fluid dynamics.

- **National Committee for Fluid Mechanics Films (NCFMF)**: Classic 1960s educational films, now on YouTube. Dated production but timeless physics. "Eulerian and Lagrangian Description in Fluid Mechanics", "Pressure Fields and Fluid Acceleration", "Vorticity" are gems. [Search "NCFMF" on YouTube]

- **Van Dyke's Album of Fluid Motion**: Digitized flow visualization images and videos. Beautiful examples of boundary layers, vortices, turbulence. [Available through various academic libraries]

### Interactive Tools

- **NASA Glenn Research Center**: Educational applets and guides — [https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/) — Interactive demonstrations of Bernoulli, streamlines, airfoils, compressibility, Reynolds number. Excellent for building intuition.

- **FluidSim**: Educational fluid simulation software. Limited free versions available. Good for visualizing potential flow, streamlines, and elementary flows.

- **SketchyFluid**: Online potential flow simulator — search for "potential flow simulator" or "conformal mapping tool" for various online options.

- **OpenFOAM**: Open-source CFD software — [https://www.openfoam.com/](https://www.openfoam.com/) — For students interested in computational methods. Steep learning curve but powerful.

- **MATLAB/Python examples**: Many universities post flow visualization scripts. Search GitHub for "fluid dynamics visualization" or "Navier-Stokes solver."

- **WebGL Flow**: Browser-based fluid simulation demos. Search for "GPU fluid simulation" or "WebGL fluid" for real-time interactive examples.

### Code & Computation

- **Lorena Barba's CFD Python**: 12 steps to Navier-Stokes — [https://github.com/barbagroup/CFDPython](https://github.com/barbagroup/CFDPython) — Jupyter notebooks building up from simple diffusion to full Navier-Stokes. Python-based, excellent for learning both physics and numerics.

- **Philip Mocz's fluid dynamics notebooks**: [https://github.com/pmocz/fluidpy](https://github.com/pmocz/fluidpy) — Concise Python implementations of various fluid solvers.

- **Online Navier-Stokes solvers**: Search for "2D Navier-Stokes solver online" for interactive browser-based demonstrations.

### Communities & Forums

- **CFD Online**: [https://www.cfd-online.com/](https://www.cfd-online.com/) — Wiki, forums, job board. Excellent wiki entries on fundamentals (vorticity, boundary layers, turbulence models). Active community for CFD questions.

- **Physics Forums**: Fluid dynamics section — [https://www.physicsforums.com/forums/classical-physics.61/](https://www.physicsforums.com/forums/classical-physics.61/) — Q&A on conceptual and problem-solving questions.

- **r/FluidMechanics** (Reddit): Smaller community but good for quick questions and interesting flow visualizations.

## People (Researchers & Practitioners to Follow)

- **Ludwig Prandtl** (historical) — Father of boundary layer theory. Read about his revolutionary 1904 paper.

- **G.I. Taylor** (historical) — Fundamental contributions to turbulence, vorticity, and fluid instabilities.

- **Andrey Kolmogorov** (historical) — Turbulent energy cascade and statistical theory of turbulence.

- **L.F. Richardson** (historical) — Energy cascade concept, "big whorls have little whorls."

- **Lorena Barba** (contemporary) — Computational fluid dynamics, open education (CFD Python). [Twitter: @LorenaABarba]

- **Steven Strogatz** (contemporary) — Applied mathematics, chaos theory. Fluid dynamics connections to nonlinear dynamics. [Twitter: @stevenstrogatz]

- **Jane Wang** (Cornell) — Biological fluid dynamics, insect flight, vortex dynamics.

- **John Bush** (MIT) — Experimental fluid dynamics, hydrodynamics of walking on water, surface tension phenomena.

- **APS Division of Fluid Dynamics**: Annual meeting features Gallery of Fluid Motion — spectacular visualizations and cutting-edge research. [https://www.apsdfd.org/](https://www.apsdfd.org/)

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Disciplinary Links)

- **Weather and climate** — Atmospheric flows are fluids on a rotating sphere (geophysical fluid dynamics). Jet streams, hurricanes, Rossby waves all fluid phenomena. [NOAA resources: https://www.noaa.gov/]

- **Oceanography** — Ocean currents, tides, wave dynamics. Reynolds number ranges from very low (microorganisms) to very high (Gulf Stream). [Woods Hole: https://www.whoi.edu/]

- **Biomechanics** — Blood flow (pulsatile, non-Newtonian), swimming (low vs high Re, undulatory propulsion), flying (vortex lift, clap-and-fling). Fascinating applications of fluid principles.

- **Astrophysics** — Accretion disks, stellar winds, galactic dynamics. Fluid equations at cosmic scales (magnetohydrodynamics).

- **Sports** — Baseball curveballs (Magnus effect), golf ball dimples (turbulence, drag reduction), swimming technique (drag, vortex formation), sailing (Bernoulli, circulation).

- **Art and architecture** — Frank Gehry's fluid-inspired buildings, Calatrava's biomorphic structures. Flow visualization as art.

- **Music** — Acoustics in fluids (sound waves are pressure perturbations), organ pipes, wind instruments (Helmholtz resonance, vortex shedding).

- **Environmental engineering** — Pollutant dispersion (turbulent mixing), aerosol transport (COVID-19 spread), river dynamics and sediment transport.

- **Chemical engineering** — Mixing, reaction-diffusion systems, multiphase flows, microfluidics (lab-on-a-chip).
