# Representation Theory — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Representations of Finite Groups
- Group algebras and modules
- Maschke's theorem and complete reducibility
- Character theory and orthogonality relations
- Character tables and construction of representations
- Induced representations and Frobenius reciprocity

### 2. Lie Groups and Lie Algebras
- Matrix Lie groups (GL, SL, SO, SU)
- Exponential map and tangent spaces
- Lie algebra representations
- Universal enveloping algebra
- Relationship between Lie group and Lie algebra representations

### 3. Structure Theory
- Root systems and Dynkin diagrams
- Weight spaces and highest weight theory
- Cartan subalgebras
- Classification of simple Lie algebras
- Weyl character formula

### 4. Applications
- Quantum mechanics and symmetries
- Geometric representation theory
- Modular forms and number theory
- Quiver representations

## Key Sources

### Primary Textbooks
- **Fulton & Harris** — "Representation Theory: A First Course" (most commonly recommended for intermediate learners)
- **Serre** — "Linear Representations of Finite Groups" (classic text for finite groups)
- **Hall** — "Lie Groups, Lie Algebras and Representations" (emphasizes geometric approach)
- **Etingof et al.** — "Introduction to Representation Theory" (free MIT notes)

### Course Materials
- MIT OCW 18.712 (undergraduate level, comprehensive lecture notes)
- Stanford Math 122 (modules and group representation theory)
- Columbia graduate course (geometric aspects)

### Online Resources
- MIT free textbook: https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/representationtheorybook.pdf

- Peter Woit's course notes: https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/RepThy/

## Available Resources by Type

### Interactive Tools
- Atlas of Lie Groups: root system explorer, spherical unitary representation tools
- SageMath: computational representation theory
- GAP: computational discrete algebra for group representations
- String Applet and Quiver mutation tools

### Video Content
- MIT OpenCourseWare lectures
- "A Practical guide to representation theory" on YouTube

### Prerequisites Identified
- Strong linear algebra (vector spaces, eigenvalues, inner products)
- Abstract algebra (groups, rings, fields)
- Some familiarity with group theory (normal subgroups, quotients)
- For Lie theory: multivariable calculus and basic differential geometry helpful

## Pedagogical Notes from Research
- Most courses emphasize both algebraic and geometric perspectives
- Character theory is central to finite group representations
- Examples (symmetric groups, GL(n), SU(2)) should be worked in depth
- Computational tools help bridge abstract theory and concrete calculations
- Strong emphasis on applications motivates the abstract machinery
