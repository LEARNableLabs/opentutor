# Representation Theory — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Group representations** — Linear actions of groups on vector spaces via homomorphisms to GL(V)
2. **Permutation representations** — Concrete representations arising from group actions on sets
3. **Irreducible representations** — Representations with no proper invariant subspaces
4. **Subrepresentations and direct sums** — How representations decompose into simpler pieces
5. **Tensor products and dual representations** — Building new representations from old ones
6. **Characters** — Trace functions that encode representation data. Depends on: group representations
7. **Maschke's theorem** — Complete reducibility for finite groups over ℂ. Depends on: irreducible representations, direct sums
8. **Orthogonality relations** — Inner product structure on character space. Depends on: characters
9. **Character tables** — Complete classification of irreps via characters. Depends on: orthogonality relations, characters
10. **Induced representations** — Lifting representations from subgroups. Depends on: group representations, subrepresentations
11. **Frobenius reciprocity** — Adjointness of restriction and induction. Depends on: induced representations
12. **Young tableaux** — Combinatorial tools for symmetric group representations. Depends on: characters, irreducible representations
13. **Burnside's lemma** — Counting via group actions. Depends on: characters, permutation representations
14. **Matrix Lie groups** — Smooth groups of matrices (GL, SL, SO, SU). Depends on: group representations
15. **Lie algebras** — Tangent spaces to Lie groups with bracket structure. Depends on: matrix Lie groups
16. **Exponential map** — Bridge between Lie groups and Lie algebras. Depends on: Lie algebras, matrix Lie groups
17. **Lie bracket** — Non-commutative product measuring infinitesimal group commutators. Depends on: Lie algebras
18. **Lie algebra representations** — Homomorphisms preserving the bracket. Depends on: Lie algebras, group representations
19. **Universal enveloping algebra** — Associative algebra generated by a Lie algebra. Depends on: Lie algebra representations
20. **Weight spaces** — Eigenspaces for Cartan subalgebra action. Depends on: Lie algebra representations
21. **sl(2) representations** — Complete classification of sl(2,ℂ) representations. Depends on: weight spaces, Lie algebra representations
22. **Cartan subalgebras** — Maximal abelian subalgebras of diagonal elements. Depends on: Lie algebras, matrix Lie groups
23. **Root systems** — Eigenvalues of adjoint representation, encode Lie algebra structure. Depends on: Cartan subalgebras, weight spaces
24. **Simple roots** — Minimal generating set for root system. Depends on: root systems
25. **Dynkin diagrams** — Graphs encoding root system data. Depends on: simple roots, root systems
26. **Cartan matrix** — Numerical data determining Lie algebra. Depends on: simple roots
27. **Weight lattice** — Lattice of possible weights. Depends on: root systems, weight spaces
28. **Dominant weights** — Weights in Weyl chamber, parametrize irreps. Depends on: weight lattice, root systems
29. **Highest weight theory** — Classification via dominant highest weights. Depends on: dominant weights, weight spaces
30. **Verma modules** — Universal highest weight modules. Depends on: highest weight theory, universal enveloping algebra
31. **Weyl group** — Symmetry group of root system. Depends on: root systems
32. **Weyl character formula** — Explicit formula for characters of irreps. Depends on: Weyl group, highest weight theory
33. **Flag varieties** — Geometric spaces carrying representations. Depends on: matrix Lie groups, highest weight theory
34. **Borel-Weil-Bott theorem** — Geometric realization of representations. Depends on: flag varieties, highest weight theory

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies
- **Characters** require understanding group representations because they are trace functions on representation matrices
- **Irreducible representations** must be understood before studying decomposition because they are the atomic building blocks
- **Maschke's theorem** builds on irreducible representations and direct sums because it states every representation decomposes as a direct sum of irreducibles

### Finite Group Theory Dependencies
- **Character tables** require orthogonality relations because orthogonality is the computational tool for decomposing characters
- **Induced representations** build on general group representations and subrepresentations because they involve restriction and extension of representations
- **Frobenius reciprocity** requires induced representations because it relates induction and restriction functors
- **Young tableaux** depend on character theory because they provide explicit construction of symmetric group irreps

### Lie Theory Dependencies
- **Lie algebras** require matrix Lie groups because they are defined as tangent spaces to Lie groups
- **Exponential map** connects Lie algebras and matrix Lie groups because it integrates Lie algebra elements to group elements
- **Lie algebra representations** require both Lie algebras and group representations because they are the infinitesimal version of group representations
- **Weight spaces** require Lie algebra representations because they are eigenspace decompositions for abelian subalgebras

### Structure Theory Dependencies
- **Root systems** require Cartan subalgebras and weight spaces because roots are weights of the adjoint representation
- **Dynkin diagrams** encode root system data, specifically angles between simple roots
- **Highest weight theory** builds on dominant weights and weight spaces because it classifies irreps by their dominant highest weight
- **Weyl character formula** requires Weyl group and highest weight theory because it expresses characters in terms of highest weights and Weyl group action

### Advanced Dependencies
- **Verma modules** require highest weight theory and universal enveloping algebra because they are induced from one-dimensional representations
- **Geometric representation theory** (flag varieties, Borel-Weil-Bott) requires both Lie group structure and highest weight theory because it realizes representations geometrically

## Bottlenecks

### Character Theory (Lesson 5-9)
Students often struggle with the abstraction of characters. This is a critical bottleneck because:
- Characters are abstract (trace of a representation) but essential for all later work
- Orthogonality relations require comfort with inner products on function spaces
- Character tables demand both computational skill and conceptual understanding

### Lie Algebra vs. Lie Group (Lesson 13-16)
The relationship between Lie groups and Lie algebras is subtle:
- Exponential map is not always surjective
- Local vs. global structure confuses students
- Must understand both algebraic (Lie algebra) and geometric (Lie group) perspectives

### Root Systems (Lesson 21-22)
Root systems are the most abstract structural tool:
- Requires synthesizing linear algebra, symmetry, and algebraic structure
- Dynkin diagrams seem unmotivated without seeing the classification payoff
- Students often memorize diagrams without understanding what they encode

### Highest Weight Theory (Lesson 24-25)
The classification theorem via highest weights is the conceptual peak:
- Requires mastery of roots, weights, and Cartan subalgebras
- Verma modules are technically demanding
- Weyl character formula is computationally complex

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Linear algebra** — needed for: all concepts (representations are linear maps)
- **Abstract algebra** — needed for: groups, rings, fields, modules
- **Group theory** — needed for: normal subgroups, quotients, conjugacy classes, group actions
- **Multivariable calculus** — needed for: Lie groups (smooth manifolds), exponential map
- **Inner products** — needed for: character theory, orthogonality relations, Killing form

## Common Misconception Connections

- **Characters determine representations** — students think isomorphic characters imply isomorphic representations (true for finite groups over ℂ, but not in general)
- **All representations decompose** — Maschke's theorem is specific to finite groups (and compact groups); doesn't hold for infinite discrete groups or non-compact Lie groups in general
- **Lie algebra determines Lie group** — multiple Lie groups can have the same Lie algebra (e.g., SU(2) and SO(3) both have Lie algebra so(3))
- **Weights are unique** — weight spaces depend on choice of Cartan subalgebra (though highest weight is well-defined up to Weyl group)
