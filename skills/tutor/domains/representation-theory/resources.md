# Representation Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Fulton & Harris, "Representation Theory: A First Course"** (Springer) — The gold standard for intermediate learners. Covers finite groups, Lie algebras, and applications with geometric intuition. Excellent balance of theory and examples. [Available at Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-0979-9) | [Free PDF excerpt](https://mat.uab.cat/~pitsch/ReadingSeminar/Fulton-Harris.pdf)

- **Etingof et al., "Introduction to Representation Theory"** (AMS, 2016) — Based on MIT's 18.712 course. Free online. Comprehensive treatment from basic notions through quivers and finite-dimensional algebras. Ideal for self-study. [Free PDF](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/representationtheorybook.pdf) | [Lecture notes version](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/replect.pdf)

- **Serre, "Linear Representations of Finite Groups"** (Springer) — Classic text focusing on finite groups. Compact, elegant, proof-heavy. Best for students who enjoy algebraic rigor. Referenced by many courses as the canonical finite group text.

- **Hall, "Lie Groups, Lie Algebras and Representations: An Elementary Introduction"** (Springer) — Emphasizes geometric and analytic perspectives. Excellent for students with multivariable calculus background. Used in Columbia's graduate course.

- **Sepanski, "Compact Lie Groups"** (Springer, 2006) — More geometric approach, focuses on compact groups. Good complement to Hall for students interested in topology/geometry connections.

### Course Materials

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 18.712: Introduction to Representation Theory** — Comprehensive undergraduate course by Pavel Etingof. Includes lecture notes, problem sets, and full reading materials. [Course homepage](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-712-introduction-to-representation-theory-fall-2010/) | [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-712-introduction-to-representation-theory-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/) | [Lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-712-introduction-to-representation-theory-fall-2010/pages/lecture-notes/)

- **Stanford Math 122: Modules and Group Representation Theory** — Covers modules over PIDs, tensor products, group representations, character theory. [Course page](http://sporadic.stanford.edu/Math122/)

- **Columbia University Representation Theory (Peter Woit)** — Graduate-level course emphasizing geometric aspects. Excellent lecture notes and resource lists. [Course homepage](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/RepThy/)

- **Tim Seynnaeve's Lecture Notes** — Comprehensive lecture notes covering finite groups through Lie algebras. Clear exposition with good examples. [PDF](https://timseynnaeve.github.io/misc/Rep_Theory_Notes.pdf)

### Online Resource Collections

- **Mikhail Khovanov's Representation Theory Resources** — Curated list of textbooks, lecture notes, and online materials covering all aspects of representation theory. Maintained by Columbia. [Resource list](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~khovanov/resources/)

- **nLab: Representation Theory** — Category-theoretic perspective with extensive cross-references to modern approaches. Good for seeing connections to other areas. [nLab page](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/representation+theory)

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos

### Interactive Tools

- **Atlas of Lie Groups** — Computational tools for Lie group representations. Includes:
  - Spherical Unitary Explorer: interactive tool for spherical unitary representations of classical groups
  - Root Systems tool: visualization and computation with root systems
  - Character formulas and branching rules
  - [Main site](http://www.liegroups.org/)

- **SageMath** — Open-source mathematics software with extensive representation theory support:
  - Character table computation for finite groups
  - Root system calculations
  - Weight diagram visualization
  - Young tableaux and symmetric functions
  - [SageMath homepage](https://www.sagemath.org/)

- **GAP (Groups, Algorithms, Programming)** — System for computational discrete algebra:
  - Character tables for finite groups
  - Irreducible representation construction
  - Group theoretical calculations
  - [GAP homepage](https://www.gap-system.org/)

- **AMR Software Tools** — Collection of representation theory applets:
  - String Applet for representations of string algebras
  - Quiver mutation Java applet for cluster algebras
  - [AMR software page](https://amathr.org/software/)

### Code and Notebooks

- **Representation Theory in SageMath** — Official SageMath documentation includes tutorials and examples for representation theory computations. Jupyter notebooks available for interactive exploration.

- **GAP Tutorial: Representation Theory** — Built-in GAP tutorial covers character table computation, irrep construction, and applications to group theory.

## People to Follow

### Researchers with Excellent Expository Work

- **Pavel Etingof** (MIT) — Co-author of the free MIT textbook; expert on quantum groups and Cherednik algebras
- **Peter Woit** (Columbia) — Geometric representation theory; maintains excellent course webpage and blog
- **Mikhail Khovanov** (Columbia) — Maintains comprehensive resource list; expert on categorification
- **Brian Hall** (Notre Dame) — Author of accessible Lie groups textbook
- **William Fulton** (University of Michigan) — Co-author of the canonical intermediate textbook
- **Jean-Pierre Serre** — Fields medalist; classic text on finite group representations
- **Andrei Okounkov** (Columbia) — Fields medalist; connections to algebraic geometry and mathematical physics

### Historical Figures

- **Hermann Weyl** — Weyl character formula, representation theory of compact Lie groups
- **Issai Schur** — Schur's lemma, Schur polynomials, representation theory of GLn
- **Élie Cartan** — Classification of Lie algebras, Cartan subalgebras
- **Ferdinand Georg Frobenius** — Character theory, group representations, Frobenius reciprocity
- **Eugene Wigner** — Applications to quantum mechanics, Wigner-Eckart theorem

## Connections and Rabbit Holes

### Physics and Representation Theory

- **"Group Theory in Physics"** by Wu-Ki Tung — Connects representation theory to particle physics, Lie groups in gauge theory
- **Wigner's "Group Theory and its Application to the Quantum Mechanics of Atomic Spectra"** — Classic text on physics applications

### Geometric Representation Theory

- **Flag varieties and Borel-Weil-Bott theorem** — See Chriss & Ginzburg, "Representation Theory and Complex Geometry"
- **D-modules and perverse sheaves** — Hotta, Takeuchi & Tanisaki, "D-Modules, Perverse Sheaves, and Representation Theory"

### Combinatorial Representation Theory

- **Symmetric functions** — Stanley, "Enumerative Combinatorics, Volume 2" (Chapter 7)
- **Young tableaux** — Sagan, "The Symmetric Group: Representations, Combinatorial Algorithms, and Symmetric Functions"
- **Littlewood-Richardson rule** — Fulton's "Young Tableaux"

### Langlands Program

- **"An Introduction to the Langlands Program"** edited by Bernstein & Gelbart — Connects representation theory, automorphic forms, number theory
- **Frenkel, "Langlands Correspondence for Loop Groups"** — Geometric Langlands perspective

### Quantum Groups and Categorification

- **Kassel, "Quantum Groups"** — Deformations of universal enveloping algebras
- **Khovanov & Lauda** — Papers on categorified quantum groups and higher representation theory

### Modular Representation Theory

- **Alperin, "Local Representation Theory"** — Representations in characteristic p
- **Benson, "Representations and Cohomology"** — Cohomological methods in representation theory

## Advanced Topics for Further Study

- **Infinite-dimensional representations** — Verma modules, highest weight categories, BGG category O
- **Kac-Moody algebras** — Affine Lie algebras, Weyl-Kac character formula
- **Quiver representations** — Gabriel's theorem, Kac's theorem, preprojective algebras
- **Hecke algebras** — Kazhdan-Lusztig theory, canonical bases
- **Springer theory** — Springer resolution, Springer fibers, Weyl group representations
- **Tensor categories** — Tannakian reconstruction, fusion categories, modular tensor categories

## Unexpected Connections

- **Representation theory and chemistry** — Molecular symmetry groups, character theory in spectroscopy
- **Knot theory** — Jones polynomial via quantum groups, braid group representations
- **Combinatorics** — RSK correspondence, Schur positivity, plethysm
- **Number theory** — Automorphic representations, local-global principles, Galois representations
- **Topological quantum field theory** — Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants, Chern-Simons theory
- **Machine learning** — Equivariant neural networks, symmetry in deep learning
