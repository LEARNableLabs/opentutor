# Lockpicking — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Texts

- **MIT Guide to Lock Picking** (Ted the Tool, 1991) — The classic free educational resource. Covers fundamentals, exercises, tool fabrication, and legal considerations. Essential reading for understanding traditional pedagogy. Available at:
  - PDF: https://www.lysator.liu.se/mit-guide/MITLockGuide.pdf
  - HTML: https://www.lysator.liu.se/mit-guide/mit-guide.html
  - Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/MITLockGuide
  - Lockwiki: https://www.lockwiki.com/index.php/MIT_Guide_to_Lock_Picking

- **Practical Lock Picking** (Deviant Ollam, 2nd Edition) — Industry-standard instructional manual from basic to advanced techniques. Written by a renowned security educator. Best resource for intermediate learners transitioning to security-minded practice.
  - https://sciencedirect.com/book/9781597499897/practical-lock-picking

- **BosnianBill's LockLab Book Resources** — Curated list of essential lockpicking books with reviews and recommendations
  - https://locklab.com/resources/books-on-lock-picking/

### Online Course Platforms

- **Art of Lock Picking** — Comprehensive learning platform with structured courses, difficulty progression, and community support. Excellent for self-paced intermediate learners.
  - https://www.art-of-lockpicking.com/courses/

- **Atlas Obscura Lockpicking Course** — Live and on-demand video lessons with Schuyler Towne. Includes exercises, speed-picking techniques, and curated reading lists. Good production quality.
  - https://courses.atlasobscura.com/course/on-demand-lockpicking

- **Udemy: The Art of Lockpicking** — Complete guide course created by professional lockpickers. Includes lifetime access and video content.
  - https://www.udemy.com/course/the-ultimate-guide-to-lock-picking/

- **Class Central Lockpicking Directory** — Aggregates 60+ online lockpicking courses across platforms. Good for comparing course options.
  - https://www.classcentral.com/subject/lockpicking

- **Locksmith Training School** — Professional certification programs including lock picking modules. More professional/vocational than sport-focused.
  - https://locksmithingschool.com/lock-picking/
  - Resources page: https://locksmithingschool.com/resources/

## Supplementary (for engagement)

- **LockPickingLawyer** (YouTube) — Most popular lockpicking channel. Short, high-quality videos demonstrating techniques and exposing vulnerabilities in commercial locks. Excellent for seeing techniques in action and understanding security implications.

## Organizations & Communities

### TOOOL (The Open Organisation of Lockpickers)

- **TOOOL US** — 501(c)(3) nonprofit advancing public knowledge about locks through teaching, research, and competition. Hosts local chapters across North America.
  - https://toool.us/

- **TOOOL Netherlands** — Original TOOOL organization. Organizes Dutch Open Championships and LockCon conference. Pioneer of collaborative locksport.
  - https://toool.nl/Lockpicking

### Competitions & Events

- **LockCon** — Premier international gathering for locksport enthusiasts, organized by TOOOL since 2008. Features competitions, workshops, and community events.

- **Dutch Open Championships** — Annual competition since 2002 attracting international participants. Part of LockCon.

- **DEF CON Lockpick Village** — Annual Las Vegas hacker convention featuring lockpicking competitions, workshops, and vendor booths.

- **HOPE Conference** — Biennial North American hacker conference with lockpicking contests.

## Cross-Discipline Connections

### Security & Cryptography

- Physical security as analog to cryptographic security—understanding attack vectors, defense in depth, security through obscurity failures
- Kerckhoffs's principle applied to locks: security should rely on key secrecy, not mechanism secrecy

### Manufacturing & Engineering

- Tolerances and quality control in mass production
- Cost-security tradeoffs in consumer products
- Materials science: hardening, drill resistance, corrosion

### Game Theory & Economics

- Attacker-defender dynamics and arms races
- Security economics: cost to attack vs. cost to defend
- Market failures in consumer security products

### Psychology & Learning

- Tactile skill acquisition and deliberate practice
- Security theater and consumer trust
- Expertise development in tactile domains

### Legal & Ethical Studies

- Tool possession laws varying by jurisdiction
- Responsible disclosure in security research
- Professional licensing and locksmith industry regulation
- Intellectual property and lock patents

## Unexpected Connections

1. **Safecracking and Music** — Famous safecracker Peterman used audio feedback (stethoscope) to hear tumbler alignment. Connection between tactile/audio feedback and expertise development in music.

2. **Lock Patents as Research Tools** — USPTO and EPO patent databases contain detailed lock mechanism diagrams. Reverse-engineering from patents teaches both lock design and attack vectors.

3. **Origami and Lock Design** — Geometric folding principles appear in sidebar mechanisms and rotating disc designs. Spatial reasoning skills transfer between domains.

4. **Competitive Speed-Cubing and Speed-Picking** — Both require spatial reasoning, manual dexterity, and procedural memory. Practice methodologies transfer.

5. **Magic and Misdirection** — Locksport demonstrations at conventions borrow from magic performance—controlling audience attention, revealing mechanisms, creating wonder.

6. **Urban Exploration and Physical Security** — UrbEx community's lock knowledge overlaps with locksport. Shared interest in abandoned spaces and access techniques.

7. **Maker Culture and Tool Fabrication** — 3D printing enables custom tool creation. Overlap with broader maker/hacker communities creating custom solutions.

8. **Historical Cryptography** — Lock-and-key mechanisms parallel encryption-and-decryption. Both evolved through attacker-defender arms races across centuries.
