# Lockpicking — mechanics, security, and sport — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 8 lessons (31%)
- **Socratic questions** — 6 lessons (23%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (19%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 3 lessons (12%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 2 lessons (8%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (8%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Domain Hooks
1. **Lock manufacturing and economics** — Why do cheap locks dominate the market? What's the cost-security tradeoff? How do manufacturers balance pick resistance with key durability and manufacturing cost? *Drop during lesson 6 (manufacturing flaws) or lesson 22 (security ratings).*

2. **Lockpicking in popular media** — How do movies get it hilariously wrong? Why does Hollywood show instant picking with one tool? What real techniques actually look like vs. cinematic versions. *Drop during lesson 8 or 9 when students are struggling with SPP difficulty—validate that it's supposed to be hard.*

3. **Historical lock evolution** — Ancient Egyptian pin tumblers, medieval warded locks, Bramah challenge lock (unpicked for 67 years), race between lock designers and pickers. *Drop during lesson 17 (high-security locks) to contextualize modern arms race.*

4. **Covert entry and forensics** — How can locksmiths/forensics determine if a lock was picked vs. forced? What traces do techniques leave? 

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"I need to buy expensive tools to start"** — Students often think professional-grade picks are necessary. Reality: basic hooks and tension wrenches suffice; skill matters far more than tool quality. Corrective: Emphasize that BosnianBill and others often use homemade tools. Practice with basic gear first.

2. **"Lockpicking is about force/speed"** — Beginners over-tension and rake frantically. Reality: Light tension and patient feedback recognition beat aggressive approaches. Corrective: Demonstrate how heavy tension prevents pins from setting. Use the "barely enough to bind one pin" rule.

3. **"All locks work the same way"** — Students learn pin tumblers and assume all locks are variations. Reality: Wafer, disc detainer, lever, and combination locks require completely different mental models and techniques. Corrective: Early exposure to lock diversity prevents overgeneralization.
