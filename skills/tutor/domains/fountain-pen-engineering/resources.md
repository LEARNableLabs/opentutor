# Fountain Pen Engineering — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Reference Sites

- **Richard Binder's Pen Reference** (https://www.richardspens.com) — Comprehensive technical documentation on nib anatomy, repair techniques, feed design, and historical context. Written by a master nibmeister. Excellent diagrams and cross-sections. Ideal for intermediate learners who want engineering depth without academic jargon.

- **Peyton Street Pens Technical Library** (https://www.peytonstreetpens.com) — Detailed guides on nib grinding, tipping materials, flow problems, and custom modifications. Strong visual documentation of repair processes. Good for practical, hands-on learners.

- **Fountain Pen Network Forums** (https://www.fountainpennetwork.com) — Active community with deep technical discussions. Search archives for threads on specific engineering topics (ink chemistry, nib metallurgy, feed design). Variable quality but excellent for niche questions and real-world troubleshooting.

### Books

- **"Fountain Pens of the World" by Andreas Lambrou** — Comprehensive reference covering pen design evolution, manufacturing techniques, and engineering innovations across brands and eras. Strong on materials and mechanisms.

- **"The Fountain Pen: A Collector's Companion" by Alexander Crum Ewing** — Focused on design history and technical specifications. Good for understanding the evolution of nib geometry and filling systems.

- **"Fountain Pens: A Collector's Guide" by Stuart Schneider** — Accessible introduction with enough technical detail for intermediate study. Good starting point before diving into specialist texts.

### Academic and Technical Papers

- **"Capillary Filling of Fountain Pen Ink Channels"** — Research on fluid dynamics in microchannels applicable to feed design. Search on Google Scholar for recent publications.

- **"Materials Selection for Writing Instruments"** — Materials science perspective on alloy selection, wear resistance, and manufacturing constraints. Look for papers from engineering departments.

- **Patent databases (USPTO, EPO)** — Search for fountain pen patents from Pelikan, Pilot, Montblanc, Sailor for detailed engineering drawings and specifications. Excellent for understanding feed design and filling mechanisms.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos

- **Goulet Pens YouTube Channel** (https://www.youtube.com/user/TheGouletPenCompany) — "Nib Nook" series explains nib terminology and characteristics. "Ink Drop" videos review ink properties including shading, sheen, and flow. Beginner to intermediate level, very accessible.

- **Pen Habit YouTube Channel** (https://www.youtube.com/c/PenHabit) — "Fountain Pen 101" series covers basics with some engineering discussion. Good for visual learners.

- **Mark Bacas Nib Grinding Demonstrations** (search YouTube) — Watch a professional nibmeister grind custom nibs. Shows precision required and results achievable. Excellent for lessons 17 and 20.

- **John Mottishaw / NibsmiH Demonstrations** — Advanced nib customization techniques. Shows specialized grinds (needlepoint, waverly, architect).

- **Pilot Namiki Factory Tour** (search YouTube) — Behind-the-scenes look at industrial fountain pen manufacturing. Shows nib stamping, tipping welding, quality control. Rare glimpse into production engineering.

- **SBREBrown YouTube Channel** (https://www.youtube.com/c/sbrebrown) — Pen reviews with engineering focus. Discusses nib geometry, feed performance, ink compatibility in real pens.

### Interactive Tools

- **Goulet Pens Nib Nook** (https://www.gouletpens.com/pages/nib-nook) — Interactive visual guide to nib sizes, grinds, and characteristics. Good for understanding contact patch and line variation.

- **JetPens Fountain Pen Guide** (https://www.jetpens.com/blog/the-beginner-s-guide-to-fountain-pens/pt/782) — Interactive diagrams of pen anatomy and flow mechanisms. Excellent visual introduction.

- **Ink comparison tools** (search "fountain pen ink comparison database") — Several community-built databases allow side-by-side comparison of ink properties, shading, sheen, dry time.

- **Fluid dynamics simulators** (COMSOL, OpenFOAM) — Not pen-specific, but can model capillary flow in microchannels. Advanced learners can simulate feed designs.

- **Microscopy images** (search FPN forums, Reddit r/fountainpens) — Users share macro photography and microscopy of nib tips, tipping shapes, slit widths. Valuable for seeing real geometry variations.

### Code and Data

- **Ink property databases** (community-maintained spreadsheets) — Crowdsourced data on pH, dry time, water resistance, flow. Look for Google Sheets shared in r/fountainpens or FPN.

- **Python scripts for ink analysis** (GitHub) — Some hobbyists have written code for analyzing ink shading patterns from scanned writing samples. Search for "fountain pen ink analysis" on GitHub.

- **CAD models of nibs** (Thingiverse, GrabCAD) — Some users have created 3D models of nib geometry for educational purposes. Useful for visualizing tine curvature and tipping shape.

### Unexpected Connections

- **Medical capillary flow devices** — IV drips, microfluidic diagnostics use similar capillary principles. Cross-pollination with biomedical engineering.

- **3D printing and viscous material deposition** — Extrusion-based 3D printing faces similar flow control challenges (viscosity, surface tension, pressure). Fountain pen feed engineering informs additive manufacturing.

- **Automotive fuel injectors** — Precision fluid metering at different scales. Flow control and atomization principles overlap.

- **Inkjet printer engineering** — Similar ink formulation challenges (pH, viscosity, particle size) but different delivery mechanism. Pigment suspension research is shared.

- **Microfluidics and lab-on-a-chip** — Capillary-driven flow in channels is a hot research area. Fountain pen feeds are macro-scale microfluidic devices.

- **Watchmaking and precision manufacturing** — Similar tolerance requirements, hand-finishing techniques, and cottage industry structure. Nibmeisters and watchmakers share craftsmanship culture.

- **Calligraphy and typography** — Historical context for nib design. Understanding letterforms informs optimal nib geometry for different scripts.

- **Art conservation and archival science** — Iron gall ink longevity, document preservation, ink aging studies. Connects fountain pen chemistry to cultural heritage.

- **Sustainable materials engineering** — Some companies (Montblanc, Pelikan) experimenting with bio-based resins for barrels. Connects to polymer science and environmental engineering.

- **Biomimicry and capillary action in nature** — Trees use capillary action to transport water hundreds of feet. Gecko feet use surface tension for adhesion. Nature provides inspiration for flow optimization.

## Tools and Supplies for Hands-On Learning

If students want to experiment:

- **Loupe or magnifying glass** (10x-20x) — Inspect tipping geometry, slit width, tine alignment. ~$10-30.

- **Brass shims** (0.001"-0.004") — For measuring and adjusting slit width. Precision feeler gauges work. ~$10-20.

- **Micromesh polishing cloths** (12000 grit) — For smoothing nibs. ~$10-15 for a set.

- **pH test strips** — Measure ink acidity. Wide-range strips (1-14 pH) sufficient. ~$5-10.

- **Viscosity comparison** — Simple: time how long it takes different inks to flow down a tilted plate. No equipment needed.

- **Macro photography setup** — Smartphone + clip-on macro lens can reveal tipping geometry. ~$15-30 for decent lens.

- **Starter pens for experimentation** — Pilot Metropolitan, LAMY Safari, Platinum Prefounte are inexpensive and well-engineered. ~$15-30 each.

- **Ink samples** — Goulet Pens and other vendors sell 2ml samples for testing without buying full bottles. ~$1.50-2.50 per sample.

## Equipment for Advanced Study

For students who want to go deeper:

- **Digital microscope** (USB, 50x-1000x) — Detailed tipping inspection. ~$30-100.

- **Precision scale (0.001g)** — For ink formulation experiments. ~$20-40.

- **Graduated cylinders and beakers** — Mixing inks from components. ~$20-30 for a set.

- **Syringe filters** (0.45 micron) — Remove particles from inks. ~$10 for a pack.

- **pH meter (digital)** — More accurate than strips. ~$15-30 for basic model.

- **Rheometer** (for viscosity) — Professional equipment is expensive ($5k+), but simple viscosity comparisons can be done with stopwatch and tilted surface.

- **Spectrophotometer** (for color analysis) — Professional equipment expensive, but colorimeter apps exist for smartphones.

## Where to Find More

- **Patent databases** — USPTO (patents.google.com) has detailed engineering drawings for feed designs, filling mechanisms, nib geometries. Free.

- **Google Scholar** — Search "fountain pen" + "capillary" / "ink chemistry" / "nib" for academic papers. Many are behind paywalls but abstracts are free.

- **WorldCat** — Find specialist books on fountain pens in nearby libraries. Many out-of-print references are only available used or in libraries.

- **Archive.org** — Some vintage pen catalogs and manuals are digitized. Historical context for design evolution.

- **Trade magazines** (Pen World, Write Here archives)** — Industry publications with technical articles. Some archives available online.
