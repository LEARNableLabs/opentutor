# Psychogeography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books
- **Merlin Coverley, *Psychogeography* (2006)** — Best single introduction. Clear, concise, historically grounded. Covers origins, key figures, and contemporary practice. Perfect for intermediate students.
- **Guy Debord, *The Society of the Spectacle* (1967)** — Foundational text. Dense but essential. Read sections 1-8, 165-174 at minimum. Available online: https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/debord/society.htm
- **Michel de Certeau, *The Practice of Everyday Life* (1980)** — Chapter "Walking in the City" is canonical. More accessible than Debord, phenomenologically rich.
- **Rebecca Solnit, *Wanderlust: A History of Walking* (2000)** — Broader than psychogeography but excellent on walking as cultural practice, politics of urban movement.
- **Iain Sinclair, *London Orbital* (2002)** — Contemporary psychogeographic writing. Walk around M25 motorway; sharp, essayistic, place-specific.
- **Will Self, *Psychogeography* (2007)** — Collected essays. Accessible, witty, demonstrates contemporary practice without academic jargon.

### Academic Papers and Essays
- **Guy Debord, "Theory of the Dérive" (1956)** — Essential primary source. Short, clear. Available: https://www.cddc.vt.edu/sionline/si/theory.html
- **"Definitions" from *Internationale Situationniste* #1 (1958)** — Key terms defined by the SI themselves. Available: https://www.cddc.vt.edu/sionline/si/definitions.html
- **Situationist International Online** (https://www.cddc.vt.edu/sionline/) — Full digital archive of SI journals, texts, and documents
- **Tate Modern: Situationist International** (https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/s/situationist-international) — Art historical context, visual resources

## Interactive Tools and Platforms

### Mapping Tools
- **QGIS** (https://qgis.org/) — Free, open-source GIS software for creating psychogeographic maps with emotional/affective data layers
- **OpenStreetMap** (https://www.openstreetmap.org/) — Collaborative mapping platform; use as base for psychogeographic overlays
- **Google My Maps** (https://www.google.com/maps/d/) — Simple tool for creating custom maps with routes, markers, and notes
- **GPS Drawing** (https://www.gpsdrawing.com/) — Track walks and create drawings with GPS traces
- **Emotional Data Lab** (https://emotionaldatalab.com/) — Tools and methods for collecting and visualizing emotional data

### Documentation Tools
- **Strava / Komoot** — GPS tracking apps (repurposed for derives)
- **Field Notes** — Physical notebooks for analog documentation
- **Voice Memos / Sound Recording Apps** — Capture ambient sound and verbal observations during walks
- **Instagram / VSCO** — Visual documentation (despite their complicity in spectacle!)

## Unexpected Connections

### Indigenous Place-Based Knowledge
- Connection: Indigenous peoples' deep emotional and spiritual relationships to land prefigure and complicate psychogeography
- Resources: Robin Wall Kimmerer, *Braiding Sweetgrass*; Indigenous geography scholarship
- Critical question: How can psychogeography avoid colonial extraction of place-based knowledge?

### Flânerie and Gender
- Connection: The 19th-century flâneur (Baudelaire) is psychogeography's precursor, but a privileged male figure
- Resources: Lauren Elkin, *Flâneuse* (2016); feminist critiques of walking and urban access
- Critical question: Who has freedom to wander cities? How do gender, race, and class shape psychogeographic possibility?

### Ecology and Climate Change
- Connection: Climate change transforms cities' emotional geography (heat, flooding, migration)
- Resources: Emerging ecocritical psychogeography; climate urbanism scholarship
- Wild card: Psychogeography of disappearing cities (Venice, Jakarta, Miami)

### Skateboarding and Parkour
- Connection: Alternative ways of reading and using urban space; táctica and détournement in action
- Resources: Iain Borden, *Skateboarding, Space and the City* (2001)
- Engagement: Students often connect immediately to this — more visceral than Debord

### Mental Health and Neurodiversity
- Connection: How different neurotypes experience urban space; sensory processing and emotional geography
- Resources: Emerging research on autism and urban design; mental health geographies
- Teaching angle: Psychogeography as tool for understanding diverse urban experiences

### Sound and Acoustic Ecology
- Connection: Soundscapes as emotional geography; noise pollution and sonic ambiance
- Resources: R. Murray Schafer, *The Soundscape* (1977); acoustic ecology movement
- Practice: Sound dérives — walking with attention to audio environment

### Food and Urban Gardening
- Connection: Edible cities, guerrilla gardening, food justice as psychogeographic practice
- Resources: Fallen Fruit project; food desert mapping
- Engagement: Combines psychogeography with tangible activism and placemaking
