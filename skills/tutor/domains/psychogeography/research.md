# Psychogeography — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Historical Origins
- **Situationist International (1957-1972)** — Guy Debord, Constant Nieuwenhuys, Asger Jorn
- **Letterist International** — precursor movement (1952-1957)
- **Psychogeography as resistance** — critique of capitalist urbanism and spectacle
- **Links to Surrealism and Dadaism** — earlier movements exploring chance and urban experience

### 2. Core Concepts
- **Dérive (drift)** — unplanned journey through urban landscape, guided by emotional terrain
- **Détournement** — subversive repurposing of urban elements and maps
- **Psychogeographic mapping** — representing emotional and psychological effects of place
- **The city as site of transformation** — urbanism as political and affective practice

### 3. Theoretical Frameworks
- **Spectacle and everyday life** — Debord's critique of commodity capitalism
- **Unitary urbanism** — integration of art and daily life in urban space
- **Ambient atmosphere** — how environments shape mood and behavior
- **Edgeland and liminal spaces** — boundaries, thresholds, forgotten zones

### 4. Methods and Practices
- **Walking as research method** — embodied urban exploration
- **Mapping techniques** — hand-drawn maps, overlays, collage, digital tools
- **Documentation** — photographs, audio recordings, field notes, video
- **Collaborative derives** — group exploration and shared observation

### 5. Contemporary Applications
- **Urban design and planning** — incorporating emotional geography into development
- **Art and performance** — contemporary psychogeographic art practices
- **Digital psychogeography** — GPS tracking, location-based media, app-based derives
- **Political activism** — Occupy, right to the city movements, spatial justice

## Teaching Considerations

This topic combines **history, theory, and practice** in equal measure. Students need exposure to:
1. Historical context (1950s-1970s radical politics)
2. Philosophical frameworks (Marxism, phenomenology, critical theory)
3. Hands-on methods (walking, mapping, documenting)

The intermediate level should emphasize **practical application** while building theoretical literacy. Students should complete at least 2-3 actual derives and create their own psychogeographic maps.

**Key challenge**: balancing abstract theory with embodied practice. Too much Debord becomes academic; too much walking becomes tourism. The sweet spot is theory-informed practice.
