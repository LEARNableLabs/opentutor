# Color Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Books

- **Interaction of Color by Josef Albers** (1963) — The canonical text on how color perception changes with context. Includes practical exercises with color papers. Essential for lessons 14-15 on simultaneous contrast and color interaction. Perfect for intermediate students who want to see counterintuitive phenomena firsthand.

- **The Elements of Color by Johannes Itten** — Condensed examination of color theory with colored illustrations and practical exercises. Covers color wheel organization, harmony schemes, and subjective/objective color properties. Good complement to lessons 12-18 on relationships and harmony.

- **Color Theory by Patti Mollica** — Practical guide including color psychology, pigment characteristics, color schemes, and mixing. More application-focused than theoretical. Useful for lessons 19-23 (psychology) and 24-27 (application).

- **Cognition through Color by Jules Davidoff** — Modern neuropsychology perspective on color as a perceptual building block. Research-oriented but accessible. Excellent for lessons 2-5 on perception and cognition.

### University Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Art of Color (ES-298)** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/es-298-art-of-color-spring-2005/pages/syllabus/
  Studio seminar covering visual design principles, color theory, and the science of color (physics, neuroscience, chemistry, mathematics). Includes projects on color balance, expression, warm-cold colors. Excellent structural model for this curriculum.

- **San Jose State University: ART 14 — Color** (syllabus) — https://www.sjsu.edu/art/docs/greensheets/syllabi_2214_fall2021/ART_014_01_Guyer_Leonie_Fall%202021.pdf
  Introduction to fundamental principles, methodologies, and function of color from physical, artistic, psychological, semiotic, and cultural perspectives. Good reference for interdisciplinary approach.

- **Northwest College: Foundation Color Theory** (syllabus) — https://area10.nwc.edu/syllabi/pdf/all/543244.pdf
  Lecture/lab course with quizzes on color properties, color structures, and color psychology. Shows assessment strategies for color theory.

### Research and Academic Resources

- **New York Public Library: Color Theory Research Guide — Perception & Psychology** — https://libguides.nypl.org/colortheory/perception
  Curated academic resources on color perception and psychology. Good for diving deeper into lessons 1-5 and 19-23.

- **Horizon Lab: Tutorials on Color Science** — https://horizon-lab.org/colorvis/
  Flexible visualization tools connected to the open textbook "Foundations of Visual Computing". Interactive explorations of color science concepts. Excellent for lessons 9-11 on color systems.

- **Behavioral Design Academy: Color Psychology for UX, Design and Marketing** — https://www.behavioraldesign.academy/wp-content/uploads/color_psychology_book_alterspark_25_42347951.pdf
  Free book on color in UX, design, and marketing contexts. Practical applications for lessons 19-23 and 24-26.

## Interactive Tools and Simulators

### Core Learning Tools

- **PhET Color Vision** — https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/color-vision
  **Essential for lessons 1-2, 4, 7-8**. Make a rainbow by mixing RGB light, change wavelengths of monochromatic beams, filter white light, view as solid beams or individual photons. Vital for understanding additive color and trichromatic vision. Includes color vision deficiency simulation.

- **Munsell Color Theory Simulation Tool** — https://munsell.com/color-blog/color-theory-simulation-tool/
  **Essential for lesson 9**. Interactive tool presenting Munsell color charts in randomized order. Students find the correct order of color chips and naturally learn value and chroma. Hands-on way to internalize the Munsell system.

- **Swatchery: Learn Color Theory Free** — https://swatchery.com/color-theory
  **Essential for lessons 10, 12-13, 16, 20, 24, 27**. Interactive tutorial covering color harmonies, color wheel, HSB and RGB models, contrast, and temperature through hands-on exercises. Designed for designers; perfect for intermediate practical application.

- **SimBucket Color Simulations** — https://www.simbucket.com/teaching-color-theory-with-simbucket-simulations/
  **Essential for lessons 7-8**. Five simulations: "Painting with CMY," "RGB Color Addition," "RGB Lighting," "Stage Lighting." Demonstrates color reflection, absorption, and perception. Great for understanding additive vs. subtractive color.

- **Simulations4All: Color Vision & RGB Mixing Simulator** — https://simulations4all.com/simulations/color-vision-rgb-mixing
  **Supplementary for lessons 7, 26**. Useful for optics, perception psychology, graphic design. Demonstrates additive vs. subtractive mixing. Includes color contrast ratio checking for accessibility.

### Advanced Tools

- **Color Oracle** (download) — Free colorblindness simulator for Windows, Mac, Linux. Shows what your design looks like with deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia. Use in lesson 4 and 26.

- **Coolors.co** — https://coolors.co/
  Fast color palette generator. Good for lesson 24 palette building exercises.

- **Adobe Color** — https://color.adobe.com/
  Color wheel tool for creating harmonies, exploring trends, testing accessibility. Practical application tool for lessons 16, 24, 26.

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Physics
- **Rayleigh scattering** — Why the sky is blue and sunsets are red (lesson 1)
- **Black body radiation** — Why hot objects glow red, then white, then blue (lesson 5, color temperature)
- **Thin-film interference** — Structural color in soap bubbles, oil slicks, butterfly wings (rabbit hole for lesson 1)

### Biology and Evolution
- **Flower evolution and bee vision** — Bees see UV; flowers evolved UV patterns invisible to humans
- **Fruit ripening signals** — Red/yellow fruits signal ripeness to primate eyes (trichromatic vision as evolutionary advantage)
- **Camouflage and warning coloration** — Evolutionary color strategies in animals (connects to lesson 19)

### Linguistics
- **Color term evolution** — Languages develop color words in predictable order: black/white → red → green/yellow → blue → brown → others (Brent Berlin and Paul Kay)
- **The Sapir-Whorf hypothesis** — Does language shape color perception? (lesson 21 rabbit hole)
- **Russian blues** — Russian speakers distinguish light blue (goluboy) and dark blue (siniy) faster than English speakers

### Neuroscience
- **Color and memory** — Color enhances memory encoding and recall
- **Synesthesia** — Grapheme-color synesthesia (seeing letters/numbers in color)
- **Mood and color** — Seasonal affective disorder and light color temperature

### Technology
- **Bayer filter pattern** — How camera sensors capture color with R, G, G, B grid
- **OLED vs. LCD color reproduction** — Different display technologies, different gamuts
- **HDR and wide color gamut** — Modern displays exceeding sRGB; perceptual implications

### Art History
- **Invention of synthetic pigments** — How chemistry expanded artists' palettes (Prussian blue, cadmium yellow, synthetic ultramarine)
- **Impressionism and color theory** — How Chevreul's simultaneous contrast influenced Seurat and pointillism
- **Rothko and color field painting** — Using color interaction to create emotional depth

### Cultural Anthropology
- **Color in ritual and ceremony** — Cross-cultural use of color in religious contexts
- **Gendered color associations** — Pink/blue associations are recent (early 1900s) and culturally specific
- **Color in food culture** — Why "blue" food is rare and often unappetizing (no naturally blue foods)
