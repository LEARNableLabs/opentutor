# Anthropology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Kinship Systems and Terminology
- Descent systems (unilineal, bilateral, cognatic)
- Kinship terminology systems (Hawaiian, Eskimo, Iroquois, Crow, Omaha, Sudanese)
- Cross-cultural variation in family organization
- Alliance theory vs. descent theory

### 2. Marriage Patterns and Exchange
- Exchange systems (direct, generalized, restricted)
- Bride wealth, dowry, and reciprocity
- Incest taboos and exogamy rules
- Political alliances through marriage

### 3. Ritual Theory and Practice
- Rites of passage (Van Gennep's model)
- Liminality and communitas (Victor Turner)
- Ritual efficacy and social cohesion
- Symbolism and meaning-making

### 4. Cultural Evolution
- Dual inheritance theory (genes + culture)
- Cumulative cultural evolution
- Social learning mechanisms
- WEIRD societies and cross-cultural psychology
- Niche construction and coevolution

### 5. Symbolic and Interpretive Approaches
- Thick description (Geertz)
- Symbols, meaning, and interpretation
- Structuralism (Lévi-Strauss)
- Critique of interpretive methods

### 6. Evolutionary Anthropology
- Human behavioral ecology
- Costly signaling and prestige
- Cooperation and altruism
- Religion as adaptation

## Key Sources

### Classic Texts
- Lévi-Strauss, Claude. *The Elementary Structures of Kinship* (1949) — foundational alliance theory
- Turner, Victor. *The Ritual Process* (1969) — ritual, liminality, communitas
- Geertz, Clifford. *The Interpretation of Cultures* (1973) — symbolic anthropology
- Fox, Robin. *Kinship and Marriage* (1967) — kinship systems overview
- Van Gennep, Arnold. *The Rites of Passage* (1909) — ritual phases

### Contemporary Works
- Henrich, Joseph. *The WEIRDest People in the World* (2020) — cultural evolution and psychology
- Boyd, Robert & Richerson, Peter. *Not By Genes Alone* (2005) — dual inheritance theory
- Bloch, Maurice. *Ritual, History and Power* (1989) — political dimensions of ritual
- Sperber, Dan. *Explaining Culture* (1996) — epidemiology of representations
- Whitehouse, Harvey. *Modes of Religiosity* (2004) — ritual frequency and memory

## Research Notes

This curriculum focuses on three interconnected domains:
1. **Kinship** as the fundamental organizing principle of human society
2. **Ritual** as the mechanism for creating meaning and social bonds
3. **Cultural evolution** as the framework for understanding variation and change

The intermediate level assumes:
- Basic understanding of anthropological concepts (culture, ethnography, comparison)
- Some exposure to evolutionary thinking (natural selection, adaptation)
- Willingness to engage with theoretical debates
- Interest in both qualitative and quantitative approaches

The curriculum balances:
- Classic ethnographic cases (Nuer, Trobriand, !Kung)
- Theoretical frameworks (structuralism, functionalism, evolutionism)
- Contemporary debates (cultural vs. genetic evolution, WEIRD bias)
- Methodological approaches (fieldwork, comparison, modeling)
