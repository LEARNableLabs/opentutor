# Anthropology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Cultural variation** — societies organize kinship, ritual, and meaning in fundamentally different ways
2. **Ethnographic method** — participant observation and comparison as ways of knowing culture
3. **Cultural relativism** — understanding practices within their own context, not universal standards
4. **Social kinship** — kinship as social construction, not biological fact. Depends on: cultural variation
5. **Descent** — tracing relatedness through specific genealogical lines (patrilineal, matrilineal, bilateral). Depends on: social kinship
6. **Affinity** — relationships created through marriage, not birth. Depends on: social kinship
7. **Lineages and segmentary systems** — nested groups based on common descent. Depends on: descent
8. **Kinship terminology** — language systems that classify relatives. Depends on: descent, affinity
9. **Cross vs parallel cousins** — terminological distinctions that reflect marriage rules. Depends on: kinship terminology
10. **Alliance theory** — marriage as exchange between groups (Lévi-Strauss). Depends on: affinity, kinship terminology
11. **Ritual definition** — formalized, repetitive, symbolic actions with social functions
12. **Rites of passage** — rituals marking transitions between social statuses. Depends on: ritual definition
13. **Liminality** — the ambiguous middle phase of ritual transitions (Van Gennep, Turner). Depends on: rites of passage
14. **Communitas** — intense social bonding in liminal states. Depends on: liminality
15. **Costly signaling** — costly ritual actions as honest signals of commitment. Depends on: ritual definition
16. **Symbolic anthropology** — culture as system of meanings (Geertz). Depends on: cultural relativism
17. **Thick description** — interpreting symbolic meaning in context. Depends on: symbolic anthropology, ethnographic method
18. **Dual inheritance theory** — humans inherit both genes and culture. Depends on: cultural variation
19. **Cultural transmission** — how culture passes between individuals. Depends on: dual inheritance theory
20. **Social learning strategies** — biases in who and what to copy (prestige, conformity, success). Depends on: cultural transmission
21. **Cumulative cultural evolution** — culture builds on previous innovations (ratchet effect). Depends on: cultural transmission
22. **Gene-culture coevolution** — genes and culture evolve together. Depends on: dual inheritance theory
23. **WEIRD psychology** — Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic societies are psychological outliers. Depends on: cultural variation, gene-culture coevolution
24. **Kinship intensity** — strong kinship ties vs weak ties; shapes psychology and institutions. Depends on: descent, alliance theory, WEIRD psychology

## Dependencies

### Kinship Dependencies
- **Descent** requires understanding that kinship is socially constructed, because biological relatedness is reinterpreted through cultural rules
- **Lineages** build on descent concepts because they organize people into political groups based on shared ancestry
- **Kinship terminology** depends on both descent and affinity because terminology systems reflect rules about who can marry whom
- **Alliance theory** requires understanding affinity and terminology because it explains marriage as creating political alliances between groups

### Ritual Dependencies
- **Liminality** requires understanding rites of passage because it's the middle phase (separation → **liminality** → incorporation)
- **Communitas** builds on liminality because the ambiguous status of liminal individuals creates intense social bonds
- **Costly signaling** connects to ritual definition because expensive/painful rituals signal commitment more honestly than cheap ones
- **Thick description** depends on ethnographic method and symbolic anthropology because interpreting meaning requires immersion and context

### Cultural Evolution Dependencies
- **Cultural transmission** requires dual inheritance theory because transmission is the mechanism of cultural evolution
- **Social learning strategies** build on cultural transmission because they explain *what* and *who* people choose to learn from
- **Cumulative cultural evolution** depends on transmission because innovations must be reliably passed on to accumulate
- **Gene-culture coevolution** requires dual inheritance theory because genes and culture affect each other's evolution
- **WEIRD psychology** depends on gene-culture coevolution and cultural variation because it explains how kinship institutions shaped psychology

### Cross-Domain Integration
- **Alliance theory** connects kinship to ritual because marriage exchanges often involve ceremonial prestations
- **Costly signaling** connects ritual to cultural evolution because ritual costliness can evolve as an honest signal
- **Kinship intensity** integrates all three domains because tight kin networks affect ritual practice and shape cultural transmission patterns
- **WEIRD psychology** integrates all three because the Church's marriage and family policies (kinship) broke down intensive kin networks, creating new forms of social organization and ritual practice

## Bottlenecks (concepts that block progress if not mastered)

1. **Social kinship** — if students don't grasp that kinship is cultural, not biological, they'll misinterpret everything about descent, terminology, and alliance
2. **Liminality** — central to understanding ritual structure; Turner's insights unlock much of ritual theory
3. **Cultural transmission** — the foundational mechanism of cultural evolution; without it, concepts like cumulative evolution don't make sense
4. **Dual inheritance theory** — the paradigm shift from "culture vs. biology" to "culture AND biology evolve together"

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Kinship is about biology"** — Students assume kinship terms map to genetic relatedness. Reality: kinship is a cultural system that may or may not track biology.
2. **"Rituals are irrational"** — Students see ritual as meaningless tradition. Reality: rituals have multiple functions (meaning, cohesion, signaling, memory).
3. **"Cultural evolution = progress"** — Students think evolution means improvement. Reality: cultural evolution is just change; not teleological.
4. **"Alliance theory vs descent theory is either/or"** — Students think one must be right. Reality: both capture different aspects of kinship systems.
5. **"Symbols have fixed meanings"** — Students treat symbols like dictionaries. Reality: meaning is context-dependent and negotiated (Geertz's point).
6. **"Cultural evolution is Lamarckian"** — Students confuse cultural and genetic inheritance. Reality: they're different but interacting systems.
7. **"WEIRD people are normal, others are exotic"** — Students universalize their own psychology. Reality: WEIRD societies are cross-cultural outliers.

## Prerequisite Topics (from outside this curriculum)

- **Basic evolutionary concepts** (natural selection, adaptation, fitness) — needed for: dual inheritance theory, gene-culture coevolution, costly signaling
- **Cross-cultural awareness** — needed for: cultural relativism, ethnographic method
- **Basic social science concepts** (norms, institutions, groups) — needed for: understanding kinship as social structure, ritual functions

## Learning Pathways

### Fast Track (assumes strong prerequisites)
Start at lesson 3 (What is kinship?) if student has solid ethnographic and evolutionary background.

### Deep Dive Extensions
- **After kinship module** → read Lévi-Strauss's *Elementary Structures* or Schneider's *Critique of Kinship*
- **After ritual module** → read Turner's *Ritual Process* or Rappaport's *Ritual and Religion in the Making of Humanity*
- **After cultural evolution module** → read Henrich's *Secret of Our Success* or Boyd & Richerson's *Culture and the Evolutionary Process*

### Parallel Learning
- **Ethnographic case studies** can be read alongside the curriculum: Nuer (Evans-Pritchard), Trobriand (Malinowski), !Kung (Lee), Balinese (Geertz)
- **Contemporary debates** can be explored in parallel: kinship and reproductive technology, ritual and nationalism, WEIRD replication crisis
