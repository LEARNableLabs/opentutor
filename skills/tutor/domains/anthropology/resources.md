# Anthropology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Ember, Carol R., Melvin Ember, and Peter N. Peregrine. *Anthropology* (15th ed., 2018)** — Comprehensive four-field intro. Strong on cross-cultural comparison and ethnographic examples. Good for intermediate students who want breadth.
  - Publisher: Pearson

- **Stone, Linda, Paul F. Lurquin, and Luigi Luca Cavalli-Sforza. *Genes, Culture, and Human Evolution* (2007)** — Integrates biological and cultural evolution. Excellent for the cultural evolution module.
  - Blackwell Publishing

- **Parkin, Robert and Linda Stone (eds.). *Kinship and Family: An Anthropological Reader* (2004)** — Classic and contemporary readings on kinship theory. Includes Lévi-Strauss, Schneider, and feminist critiques.
  - Blackwell Publishing

- **Whitehouse, Harvey and James Laidlaw (eds.). *Ritual and Memory* (2004)** — Integrates cognitive and social approaches to ritual. Bridges Turner's symbolic approach and evolutionary perspectives.
  - AltaMira Press

### Classic Ethnographies

- **Evans-Pritchard, E.E. *The Nuer* (1940)** — Foundational study of segmentary lineage system. Case study for lessons 4, 7.
  - Oxford University Press
  - Available: https://archive.org/details/nuer00evanrich

- **Malinowski, Bronislaw. *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (1922)** — Classic Trobriand ethnography. Kula ring, matrilineal kinship, exchange.
  - Routledge
  - Available: https://archive.org/details/argonautsofthewe033976mbp

- **Turner, Victor. *The Ritual Process: Structure and Anti-Structure* (1969)** — Essential for lessons 11-12 on liminality and communitas.
  - Aldine Transaction

- **Geertz, Clifford. *The Interpretation of Cultures* (1973)** — Includes "Deep Play: Notes on the Balinese Cockfight." Essential for lesson 15.
  - Basic Books

### Theoretical Works

- **Lévi-Strauss, Claude. *The Elementary Structures of Kinship* (1949/1969)** — Alliance theory foundation. Challenging but essential for lesson 8.
  - Beacon Press

- **Fox, Robin. *Kinship and Marriage: An Anthropological Perspective* (1967)** — Accessible overview of kinship systems. Good intermediate-level synthesis.
  - Cambridge University Press

- **Boyd, Robert and Peter J. Richerson. *Not By Genes Alone: How Culture Transformed Human Evolution* (2005)** — Dual inheritance theory for general audience. Essential for lessons 16-20.
  - University of Chicago Press

- **Henrich, Joseph. *The WEIRDest People in the World: How the West Became Psychologically Peculiar and Particularly Prosperous* (2020)** — Integrates kinship, institutions, and cultural evolution. Key for lesson 19.
  - Farrar, Straus and Giroux

- **Henrich, Joseph. *The Secret of Our Success: How Culture Is Driving Human Evolution, Domesticating Our Species, and Making Us Smarter* (2016)** — Cumulative cultural evolution and social learning. Excellent for lessons 17-18.
  - Princeton University Press

## Interactive Tools and Databases

### Ethnographic Databases

- **eHRAF World Cultures** (Human Relations Area Files)
  - https://hraf.yale.edu/
  - Cross-cultural database with full ethnographies. Requires institutional access, but some free samples.

- **D-PLACE** (Database of Places, Language, Culture, and Environment)
  - https://d-place.org/
  - Open access cross-cultural database. Maps cultural traits geographically.

- **Ethnographic Atlas** (online version)
  - https://escholarship.org/uc/item/62c5c02n
  - Murdock's cross-cultural database. 1,267 societies coded for kinship, marriage, subsistence.

## Study Tips for This Curriculum

1. **Read ethnographies alongside theory** — Don't just learn "matrilineal descent"; read about the Trobriand Islanders. Concepts stick when anchored to people and places.

2. **Diagram kinship systems** — Draw them out. Kinship is spatial; diagrams make abstract rules concrete.

3. **Watch rituals** — YouTube has footage of rites of passage, weddings, funerals from around the world. Watch with Turner's framework in mind.

4. **Apply to your own culture** — After each lesson, ask: "How does my culture do this?" Anthropology's power is defamiliarization.

5. **Engage with debates** — Don't memorize "the answer." Anthropology is full of productive disagreements. Pick a side and argue for it.

6. **Use cross-cultural databases** — D-PLACE and Ethnographic Atlas let you test hypotheses. "Do matrilineal societies have different marriage patterns?" Check the data.

7. **Follow contemporary research** — Read SAPIENS articles, listen to podcasts. Anthropology is alive and relevant to current issues.
