# Decision Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"An Introduction to Decision Theory" by Martin Peterson** (Cambridge University Press, 2017)  
  Comprehensive intermediate-level treatment. Clear exposition of both normative and descriptive theories. Covers EU, paradoxes, and alternatives.  
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/introduction-to-decision-theory/

- **"Rational Choice in an Uncertain World" by Reid Hastie & Robyn Dawes** (Sage, 2010)  
  Psychology perspective on decision making. Strong on behavioral anomalies and cognitive biases. Accessible for intermediate students.  
  https://us.sagepub.com/en-us/nam/rational-choice-in-an-uncertain-world/book230558

- **"Decision Theory: Principles and Approaches" by Giovanni Parmigiani & Lurdes Inoue** (Wiley, 2009)  
  More technical but still intermediate-friendly. Good for students comfortable with calculus and probability.  
  https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Decision+Theory%3A+Principles+and+Approaches-p-9780470746684

- **"Thinking, Fast and Slow" by Daniel Kahneman** (2011)  
  Popular science treatment of prospect theory and behavioral economics. Excellent for motivation and real-world examples.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking,_Fast_and_Slow

### Open Courseware

- **MIT 14.123 Microeconomic Theory III** (Spring 2015)  
  Graduate-level course but several lectures are accessible to strong intermediate students. Covers expected utility, risk aversion, and uncertainty.  
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-123-microeconomic-theory-iii-spring-2015/

- **MIT 15.067 Competitive Decision-Making and Negotiation** (Spring 2011)  
  Undergraduate course with decision trees, game theory, and applied decision analysis.  
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-067-competitive-decision-making-and-negotiation-spring-2011/

- **Stanford Decision Analysis course materials**  
  (Various instructors) — applied focus with case studies from medicine, business, engineering.

### Online Articles and Encyclopedias

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Decision Theory**  
  Authoritative, comprehensive, technical but readable. The go-to reference.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/decision-theory/

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Normative Theories of Rational Choice**  
  Focus on the normative/prescriptive side: what rational agents should do.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rationality-normative-utility/

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — St. Petersburg Paradox**  
  Deep dive on one of the classic paradoxes. Includes history and modern resolutions.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paradox-stpetersburg/

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Ellsberg Paradox**  
  Detailed treatment of ambiguity aversion and its implications.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paradox-ellsberg/

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Preferences**  
  Foundational entry on preference relations, orderings, and rationality axioms.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/preferences/

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Bayes' Theorem**  
  Covers probabilistic updating, subjective probability, and Bayesian epistemology.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bayes-theorem/

### Code and Computational Tools

- **Python Libraries**:
  - `scipy.stats` — probability distributions and statistical functions
  - `numpy` — numerical computation for expected values, utilities
  - `matplotlib` / `seaborn` — plotting utility functions and decision trees
  - `pgmpy` — Bayesian networks and decision graphs (advanced)

- **R Packages**:
  - `DecisionAnalysis` — tools for decision trees, influence diagrams
  - `ggplot2` — visualization
  - `bayesplot` — Bayesian analysis visualization

- **Jupyter Notebooks**:
  - Many university courses share notebooks on GitHub for EU calculations, prospect theory simulations, Bayesian updating.

- **Decision Tree Software**:
  - **TreeAge** (commercial, but free student versions) — industry standard for medical decision analysis
  - **DPL** (Decision Programming Language) — for complex decision models
  - **SilverDecisions** (open source) — web-based decision tree tool  
    https://silverdecisions.pl/

### Academic Papers (Accessible)

- **Kahneman & Tversky (1979), "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk"**  
  The foundational paper. Readable and hugely influential.  
  (Available via Google Scholar or university libraries)

- **Ellsberg (1961), "Risk, Ambiguity, and the Savage Axioms"**  
  Introduces the Ellsberg paradox. Classic paper, still very readable.

- **Savage (1954), "The Foundations of Statistics"**  
  Dense, but excerpts are assigned in many courses. The origin of subjective expected utility.

- **Allais (1953), "Le Comportement de l'Homme Rationnel devant le Risque"**  
  Original Allais paradox paper. French original, but English translations available.

- **Arrow (1951), "Alternative Approaches to the Theory of Choice in Risk-Taking Situations"**  
  Early work on risk aversion and utility. Introduces Arrow-Pratt measures.

### Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

- **Voting Theory and Social Choice**  
  Arrow's Impossibility Theorem connects individual preferences to collective decisions.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arrows-theorem/

- **Game Theory**  
  Decision theory with strategic interaction. Nash equilibrium as EU maximization in games.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/game-theory/

- **AI Alignment**  
  How do we specify utility functions for AI systems? Value alignment research.  
  https://www.alignmentforum.org/

- **Ergodicity Economics**  
  Critique of expected utility based on time vs. ensemble averages. Ole Peters' work.  
  https://ergodicityeconomics.com/

- **Newcomb's Paradox**  
  A decision problem where causal and evidential reasoning diverge. Deeply weird.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/decision-causal/

- **Quantum Decision Theory**  
  Attempts to model decision making using quantum probability. Speculative but fascinating.

- **Medical Decision Making**  
  Journal and field applying decision theory to healthcare. Diagnostic reasoning, treatment choice.  
  https://journals.sagepub.com/home/mdm

- **Effective Altruism**  
  Movement using expected value calculations to maximize social impact.  
  https://www.effectivealtruism.org/

To help students find the right resource at the right time:

- **Lessons 1-5 (Foundations)**: Stanford Encyclopedia (Preferences), Khan Academy (intro material)

- **Lessons 17-22 (Uncertainty)**: Stanford Encyclopedia (Decision Theory, Ellsberg), Savage excerpts, Bayesian updating notebooks

## Recommended Learning Paths

### For visual learners:
- 3Blue1Brown videos → Desmos utility functions → GeoGebra decision trees → TreeAge software

### For formal/mathematical learners:
- Peterson textbook → Stanford Encyclopedia → MIT OCW → academic papers → Python/R implementations

### For applied/practical learners:
- Kahneman book → LessWrong → 80,000 Hours → medical decision-making cases → real-world problem sets

### For philosophical learners:
- Stanford Encyclopedia tour → Savage excerpts → normative/descriptive debates → Newcomb's paradox → AI alignment
