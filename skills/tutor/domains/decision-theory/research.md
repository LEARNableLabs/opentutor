# Decision Theory Research — Intermediate Level

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Foundations of Rational Choice
- Preference orderings and axioms of rationality
- Transitivity, completeness, and independence
- Revealed preference theory
- Arrow's Impossibility Theorem (brief intro)

### 2. Expected Utility Theory
- Von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms
- Utility functions and representation theorems
- Risk attitudes (risk aversion, neutrality, seeking)
- Allais paradox and violations of independence

### 3. Decision Making Under Risk
- Lotteries and probability distributions
- Expected value vs. expected utility
- Certainty equivalents and risk premiums
- St. Petersburg paradox
- Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky)

### 4. Decision Making Under Uncertainty
- Distinction between risk and uncertainty
- Savage's subjective expected utility
- Ellsberg paradox and ambiguity aversion
- Maximin, maximax, minimax regret
- Bayesian decision theory

### 5. Applications & Behavioral Anomalies
- Multi-attribute utility theory
- Sequential decision making
- Information value
- Behavioral decision theory and common biases
- Descriptive vs. normative theories

## Key Sources

### Academic Textbooks
- **"An Introduction to Decision Theory" by Martin Peterson** (Cambridge, 2017) — comprehensive intermediate treatment
- **"Rational Choice in an Uncertain World" by Reid Hastie & Robyn Dawes** (Sage, 2010) — psychology perspective
- **"Decision Theory: Principles and Approaches" by Parmigiani & Inoue** (Wiley, 2009) — technical but accessible

## Prerequisites Identified
- Probability theory (conditional probability, Bayes' rule)
- Basic calculus (derivatives for marginal utility)
- Set theory and logic (for axioms)
- Some exposure to economics or game theory (helpful but not essential)

## Exit Capabilities (Intermediate Level)
- Construct and solve decision problems using expected utility
- Recognize when EU maximization applies vs. fails
- Apply Bayesian updating to sequential decisions
- Identify common cognitive biases in decision making
- Distinguish normative, descriptive, and prescriptive approaches
- Use decision trees and influence diagrams
- Calculate certainty equivalents and risk premiums
- Understand foundational paradoxes and their implications

## Pedagogical Notes from Research
- Heavy emphasis on paradoxes and counterexamples in the literature — great for engagement
- Real-world applications are abundant (medical, finance, policy)
- Natural progression: simple gambles → expected utility → violations → extensions
- Strong visual/graphical tradition (indifference curves, decision trees)
- Active debate between normative ideals and descriptive reality — good for critical thinking
