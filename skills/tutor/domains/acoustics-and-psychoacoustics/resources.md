# Acoustics and Psychoacoustics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **David M. Howard & Jamie A.S. Angus, "Acoustics and Psychoacoustics" (5th Edition, Routledge, 2017)** — The definitive textbook bridging physics and perception. Covers wave mechanics, room acoustics, auditory physiology, and psychoacoustic phenomena with clear explanations. Companion website includes audio examples, interactive calculators, and self-test questions. Ideal for intermediate learners with interest in music and audio technology.  
  https://www.routledge.com/Acoustics-and-Psychoacoustics/Howard-Angus/p/book/9781138859876

- **Jennifer J. Lentz, "Psychoacoustics: Auditory Perception of Listeners with Normal Hearing and Hearing Loss" (2nd Edition, Plural Publishing)** — Comprehensive psychoacoustics text with accessible writing and strong clinical orientation. Includes audio demonstrations, lab exercises, and learning objectives for each chapter. Emphasizes both normal hearing and hearing loss, making it inclusive and clinically relevant.  
  https://www.pluralpublishing.com/publications/psychoacoustics-auditory-perception-of-listeners-with-normal-hearing-and-hearing-loss

- **Brian C.J. Moore, "An Introduction to the Psychology of Hearing" (6th Edition, Brill, 2012)** — Advanced textbook covering the latest research in psychoacoustics. More research-focused than Howard & Angus. Good for students who want to go deeper after completing the curriculum.

### University Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Acoustics of Speech and Hearing (6.551J)** — Graduate-level course with lecture notes, assignments, and reading lists. Reviews physical processes in speech production, propagation, and reception. Excellent for understanding auditory anatomy and speech-specific psychoacoustics.  
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-551j-acoustics-of-speech-and-hearing-fall-2004/

- **Berklee Online: Psychoacoustics for Music Production** — Examines human auditory processing from the eardrum to the auditory cortex, with applications to music production and mixing. Perfect for students interested in audio engineering applications.  
  https://online.berklee.edu/courses/psychoacoustics-for-music-production

- **California State University, Sacramento: Acoustics and Psychoacoustics (CSAD 612)** — Syllabus available covering basic acoustic properties and psychophysical aspects of frequency, pitch, intensity, loudness, and temporal processes.  
  https://www.csus.edu/college/health-human-services/communication-sciences-disorders/_internal/_documents/audiology/aud-syllabus-csad612-acoustics-and-psychoacoustics.pdf

## Applied Topics

### Audio Engineering & Music Production

- **Bobby Owsinski's "Mixing Engineer's Handbook"** — Practical audio mixing with psychoacoustic principles. Explains why certain EQ, compression, and spatial techniques work based on perception.
- **Bob Katz, "Mastering Audio: The Art and the Science"** — Covers loudness perception, dynamic range, and psychoacoustically-informed mastering.
- **Perceptual audio coding**: Papers on MP3, AAC, Ogg Vorbis — how masking is exploited for compression.

### Speech and Language

- **Peter Ladefoged, "A Course in Phonetics"** — Acoustic phonetics, how speech sounds are produced and perceived.
- **Formant synthesis and vocoding**: Understanding how timbre and pitch are encoded separately in speech.

### Hearing Loss and Audiology

- **Audiograms and pure-tone audiometry**: How hearing sensitivity is measured across frequencies.
- **Cochlear implants**: How electrical stimulation bypasses damaged hair cells; implications for frequency and pitch coding.
- **Hearing aids**: Compression, feedback cancellation, noise reduction — all psychoacoustically motivated.

### Neuroscience and Computation

- **Malcolm Slaney & Richard Lyon, "Lyon's Cochlear Model"** — Computational model of basilar membrane mechanics and auditory processing.
- **Dan Ellis, "PLP and RASTA features"** — Perceptual linear prediction for speech recognition, inspired by critical bands and loudness.
- **Computational Auditory Scene Analysis (CASA)**: Algorithms to separate sound sources, inspired by human auditory scene analysis.

## Unexpected Connections

- **Architecture and room acoustics**: Concert hall design uses psychoacoustic principles for optimal reverberation and clarity.
- **Virtual reality and spatial audio**: HRTF-based 3D audio rendering for immersive VR/AR experiences.
- **Wildlife bioacoustics**: How other species (bats, dolphins, elephants) use sound; their auditory systems differ from humans.
- **Soundscape ecology**: Using psychoacoustic metrics to assess environmental sound quality and biodiversity.
- **Neurodiversity**: Autism spectrum and hyperacusis (sound sensitivity), misophonia (negative reactions to specific sounds), auditory processing disorder.
- **Music therapy**: Using psychoacoustic principles (beat frequencies, consonance/dissonance) for therapeutic purposes.
- **Sound design for film/games**: Creating emotional impact through spectral content, spatial cues, and masking.
