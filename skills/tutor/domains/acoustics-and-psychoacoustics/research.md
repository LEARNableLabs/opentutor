# Acoustics and Psychoacoustics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Physical Acoustics
- Wave mechanics and sound propagation
- Simple harmonic motion, complex waveforms, Fourier analysis
- Reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference
- Room acoustics and reverberation

### 2. Auditory Physiology
- Anatomy of the ear (outer, middle, inner)
- Cochlear mechanics and frequency analysis
- Neural encoding and auditory pathways
- Hair cell function and tonotopic organization

### 3. Psychoacoustic Phenomena
- Pitch perception and frequency discrimination
- Loudness and intensity coding
- Temporal resolution and gap detection
- Masking (simultaneous and temporal)
- Sound localization and binaural hearing
- Timbre and spectral envelope

### 4. Applied Psychoacoustics
- Audio engineering and music production
- Speech perception
- Hearing loss and audiometry
- Sound quality metrics and perceptual audio coding

## Key Sources

### Academic Courses
- **MIT OCW - Acoustics of Speech and Hearing (6.551J)**: Graduate-level course covering physical processes in speech production, propagation, and reception
- **Berklee Online - Psychoacoustics for Music Production**: Examines auditory processing from eardrum to auditory cortex, with applications to mixing and production
- **California State Sacramento - Acoustics and Psychoacoustics (CSAD 612)**: Covers basic properties of acoustics and psychophysical aspects of frequency, pitch, intensity, loudness, and temporal processes
- **University of Salford - Psychoacoustics and Musical Acoustics**: Covers auditory system physiology and physics, loudness, pitch, and frequency bands

### Textbooks and Resources
- **Howard & Angus, "Acoustics and Psychoacoustics" (5th ed., Routledge)**: Comprehensive text with accompanying audio examples, questions, and interactive web resources. Ideal for intermediate learners interested in music and audio.
- **Lentz, "Psychoacoustics: Auditory Perception of Listeners with Normal Hearing and Hearing Loss" (Plural Publishing)**: Includes audio demonstrations and lab exercises, with emphasis on inclusivity and clinical applications.
- **MUTOR (Music Technology Online Repository)**: Free online resource presenting findings from psychoacoustics studies with interactive elements.

### Interactive Tools and Demonstrations
- Audio files demonstrating masking, pitch perception, localization cues
- Online frequency generators and spectrum analyzers
- Companion websites for major textbooks with calculation tools

## Topic Scope for Intermediate Level
At intermediate level, the curriculum assumes:
- Basic physics (waves, frequency, amplitude)
- Some mathematics (trigonometry, basic calculus helpful but not required)
- Interest in sound, music, or audio technology

Focus will be on:
- Understanding both physical and perceptual aspects of sound
- Connecting measurements to experiences
- Building intuition through demonstrations and real-world examples
- Balancing theory with practical applications
