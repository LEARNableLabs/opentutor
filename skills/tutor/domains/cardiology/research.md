# Cardiology — The Heart as an Engineering System
## Research Summary

### Major Subtopics

1. **Cardiac Mechanics** — The heart as a biomechanical pump: pressure-volume relationships, Frank-Starling mechanism, contractility, compliance, wall stress
2. **Electrical System** — Action potentials, conduction pathways, ECG interpretation, pacemaker cells, refractory periods
3. **Hemodynamics** — Fluid dynamics of blood flow: Poiseuille's law, resistance networks, compliance, wave propagation, impedance matching
4. **Control Systems** — Autonomic regulation, baroreceptor reflexes, chemoreceptors, hormonal control (renin-angiotensin), feedback loops
5. **Energetics & Metabolism** — Oxygen consumption, efficiency, metabolic substrates, ATP production, ischemia
6. **Material Properties** — Tissue mechanics, stress-strain relationships, viscoelasticity, remodeling, valve biomechanics
7. **Clinical Engineering** — Heart failure as system failure, assist devices (LVAD, pacemakers), prosthetic valves, diagnostic tools

### Key Sources

- Guyton & Hall, *Textbook of Medical Physiology* (14th ed.) — Chapters 9-13 cover cardiac mechanics, hemodynamics, and regulation
- Mohrman & Heller, *Cardiovascular Physiology* (9th ed.) — Concise, engineering-oriented approach

### Pedagogical Approach

For **intermediate level**, emphasize:
- Quantitative reasoning (calculate resistance, compliance, cardiac output)
- Systems thinking (feedback loops, homeostasis, failure modes)
- Engineering analogies (pumps, circuits, control systems, pipes)
- Visual interpretation (PV loops, Wiggers diagrams, ECGs)
- Clinical relevance (what breaks and why)

Assume prerequisites: basic biology, calculus, introductory physics (fluids, electricity).
