## Domain Hooks
- **Spider silk in space** — NASA experiments on web-building in zero gravity reveal how much spiders rely on proprioception vs gravity cues. Drop this in during web architecture lessons.

- **Peacock spiders** — Australian Maratus species with iridescent displays and elaborate courtship dances. Wonderful entry to sexual selection and structural coloration. Use during behavior/evolution modules.

- **Diving bell spiders** — *Argyroneta aquatica* lives underwater in air bubble webs. Fascinating extreme adaptation. Good for discussing physiological constraints and gas exchange.

- **Spider goats** — Transgenic goats producing spider silk proteins in milk (Nexia Biotechnologies project). Excellent for discussing synthetic biology and biomimetics. Use during applied module.

- **Ancient spider fossils** — 300+ million year old spiders in amber and rock. Some already had spinnerets. Good for evolutionary timescales and "how old is silk?" questions.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Spiders are insects"** — The most fundamental error. Students often default to "bug = insect" categorization. Emphasize the Chelicerata clade distinction early and reinforce throughout. Point out: 8 legs vs 6, no antennae, fused body segments, different developmental pattern. Make this stick by highlighting functional consequences of body plan differences.

2. **"Spider webs are all the same"** — Students often conflate orb webs with "the spider web." In reality, web architecture varies enormously (orb, sheet, funnel, tangle, cobweb, none), and many spiders don't build prey-capture webs at all. Emphasize diversity of hunting strategies and match web type to spider family and ecology.

3. **"Silk is just sticky string"** — Oversimplification that misses the material science. Students need to understand that silk is a family of materials with different properties (dragline vs capture spiral vs egg sac silk), each optimized for specific functions.
