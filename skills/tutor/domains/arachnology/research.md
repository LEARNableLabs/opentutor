# Arachnology Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Spider Anatomy and Physiology
- External anatomy (cephalothorax, abdomen, appendages)
- Internal systems (circulatory, respiratory, digestive, nervous)
- Sensory biology (mechanoreception, chemoreception, vision)
- Hydraulic locomotion and muscle systems
- Molting and development

### 2. Spider Behavior and Ecology
- Prey capture strategies (web-building, ambush, active hunting)
- Mating behaviors and sexual selection
- Parental care and social spiders
- Predator-prey interactions
- Habitat preferences and distribution

### 3. Silk Biology and Biomechanics
- Silk gland types and specialization
- Molecular structure of silk proteins (spidroins)
- Material properties (tensile strength, elasticity, toughness)
- Web architecture and engineering
- Non-web silk uses (draglines, egg sacs, sperm webs)

### 4. Spider Diversity and Evolution
- Major spider families and their characteristics
- Phylogenetic relationships within Araneae
- Venom evolution and function
- Adaptations to extreme environments
- Convergent evolution examples

### 5. Applied Arachnology
- Biomimetic applications of spider silk
- Spiders in agriculture and pest control
- Medical applications of venom compounds
- Conservation of rare spider species

### Silk Science Resources
- Materials science journals covering spider silk biomimetics

## Available Educational Materials

The intermediate level should bridge observational natural history with mechanistic explanations. Strong visual resources available for anatomy and web structure. Molecular biology of silk provides connection to materials science and protein biochemistry. Behavioral ecology offers opportunities for critical thinking about evolutionary trade-offs.

## Concept Progression Notes

Start with observable structures → internal systems → behavior and function → molecular mechanisms → evolutionary patterns. Silk science can be woven throughout as a unifying thread connecting anatomy, behavior, and molecular biology.
