# Arachnology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Arachnid characteristics** — defining features that distinguish spiders from insects and other arthropods
2. **Body plan** — cephalothorax and abdomen organization, basic segmentation
3. **Chelicerata** — evolutionary position within arthropods
4. **Hydraulic locomotion** — hemolymph-driven movement system. Depends on: body plan
5. **Hemolymph pressure** — fluid mechanics of spider movement. Depends on: body plan
6. **Joint mechanics** — how spiders achieve motion without extensors. Depends on: hydraulic locomotion
7. **Ecdysis** — molting process and constraints. Depends on: body plan
8. **Exoskeleton growth** — developmental implications of external skeleton. Depends on: ecdysis
9. **Morphological diversity** — variation in spider forms. Depends on: body plan, ecological niches
10. **Functional anatomy** — how structure relates to function. Depends on: morphological diversity
11. **Mechanoreception** — vibration and touch sensing systems
12. **Trichobothria** — specialized sensory hairs. Depends on: mechanoreception
13. **Visual acuity** — range of eye capabilities across spider families. Depends on: ecological niches
14. **Principal eyes** — high-resolution camera eyes of jumping spiders. Depends on: visual acuity
15. **Sensory integration** — how multiple senses combine. Depends on: mechanoreception, visual acuity
16. **Signal transmission** — how web vibrations convey information. Depends on: mechanoreception
17. **Sensory filtering** — discriminating prey from noise. Depends on: signal transmission
18. **Social behavior** — rare cooperative systems in spiders. Depends on: sensory integration
19. **Kin selection** — evolutionary basis of cooperation. Depends on: social behavior
20. **Silk gland types** — seven specialized gland systems
21. **Spinneret anatomy** — silk delivery structures. Depends on: silk gland types
22. **Gland specialization** — each gland produces different silk. Depends on: silk gland types
23. **Material properties** — strength, elasticity, toughness of silk
24. **Tensile strength** — resistance to breaking under tension. Depends on: material properties
25. **Protein structure** — molecular organization underlying properties. Depends on: material properties
26. **Spidroin proteins** — family of silk proteins. Depends on: protein structure
27. **Amino acid composition** — building blocks of spidroins. Depends on: spidroin proteins
28. **Molecular organization** — how amino acids arrange into functional proteins. Depends on: amino acid composition
29. **Spinning process** — transformation from liquid to solid. Depends on: spidroin proteins
30. **pH gradient** — chemical environment change during spinning. Depends on: spinning process
31. **Shear forces** — mechanical forces during fiber formation. Depends on: spinning process
32. **Phase transition** — liquid-to-solid transformation. Depends on: pH gradient, shear forces
33. **Web architecture** — structural design of orb webs. Depends on: silk gland types, material properties
34. **Engineering trade-offs** — balancing competing design requirements. Depends on: web architecture
35. **Dragline silk** — safety line and structural silk. Depends on: silk gland types
36. **Ballooning** — aerial dispersal using silk. Depends on: dragline silk
37. **Venom evolution** — origins and diversification of toxins
38. **Neurotoxins** — nerve-targeting compounds. Depends on: venom evolution
39. **Prey immobilization** — functional role of venom. Depends on: neurotoxins
40. **Sexual selection** — mate choice and competition. Depends on: social behavior
41. **Sexual cannibalism** — female consumption of males. Depends on: sexual selection
42. **Reproductive strategies** — evolutionary trade-offs in mating. Depends on: sexual selection, sexual cannibalism
43. **Adaptive radiation** — diversification into many niches. Depends on: morphological diversity
44. **Habitat diversity** — range of environments occupied. Depends on: adaptive radiation
45. **Foraging ecology** — prey capture strategies and energetics. Depends on: habitat diversity
46. **Convergent evolution** — independent evolution of similar solutions. Depends on: adaptive radiation
47. **Biomimetics** — engineering inspired by spider silk. Depends on: material properties, spidroin proteins
48. **Recombinant proteins** — synthetic production of spidroins. Depends on: spidroin proteins
49. **Biological control** — spiders as pest predators. Depends on: foraging ecology
50. **Ecosystem services** — broader ecological benefits. Depends on: biological control
51. **Pharmacology** — drug applications of venom. Depends on: venom evolution, neurotoxins
52. **Drug development** — creating medicines from venom compounds. Depends on: pharmacology

## Dependencies

### Foundational Cluster (Concepts 1-10)
Basic anatomy and body plan form the foundation. Everything builds from understanding the unique arachnid body organization and how it constrains and enables other features.

**Critical bottleneck**: Hydraulic locomotion (concepts 4-6) is non-intuitive and requires solid grasp of body plan. Students often default to insect/vertebrate muscle models.

### Sensory Cluster (Concepts 11-19)
Sensory systems depend on understanding body plan but are largely independent of silk science. Can be taught in parallel or before silk.

**Critical bottleneck**: Students often overestimate the role of vision in spiders. Mechanoreception must be established as the primary sense for most species.

### Silk Science Cluster (Concepts 20-36)
This is the most complex and hierarchical cluster. Must progress: anatomy → properties → molecular → process → function.

**Critical bottleneck**: The molecular-to-macroscale transition (concepts 26-32) is challenging. Students need to connect protein structure to material properties through the spinning process.

### Evolution and Ecology Cluster (Concepts 37-46)
Builds on all previous clusters. Requires understanding morphology, sensory systems, and silk to interpret evolutionary patterns.

**Critical bottleneck**: Sexual selection and cannibalism (concepts 40-42) often distract from deeper evolutionary principles. Keep focus on trade-offs and constraints.

### Applied Cluster (Concepts 47-52)
Synthesizes earlier material. Students should see connections across all previous clusters.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic biology** — needed for understanding anatomy, physiology, evolutionary concepts
- **Chemistry fundamentals** — needed for protein structure, pH gradients, molecular organization
- **Evolution and natural selection** — needed for adaptive radiation, sexual selection, convergent evolution
- **Basic physics** (helpful but not required) — enhances understanding of material properties, hydraulic systems, vibration transmission

## Common Misconception Points

- **Concept 4-6**: Students expect muscles like vertebrates; hydraulic system seems "inefficient"
- **Concept 13-14**: Overestimating role of vision across all spider families
- **Concept 23-25**: Conflating strength with toughness; missing the unique combination in silk
- **Concept 29-32**: Treating spinning as simple extrusion rather than complex phase transition
- **Concept 41-42**: Viewing sexual cannibalism as "mistake" rather than potential evolutionary strategy
- **Concept 47-48**: Underestimating difficulty of synthetic silk production

## Learning Sequences

### Linear Path (as presented in curriculum)
Anatomy → Senses → Silk → Ecology → Applications

This path builds foundational knowledge before tackling complex systems.

### Alternative Path (for chemistry-focused students)
Silk molecular structure early → Then anatomy and behavior → Applications

This leverages existing chemistry knowledge to build engagement.

### Spiral Path (for struggling students)
Alternate between concrete (anatomy, behavior) and abstract (molecular, evolutionary). Return to silk science multiple times at increasing depth.
