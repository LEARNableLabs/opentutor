# Arachnology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks
- **Foelix, Rainer F. "Biology of Spiders" (3rd edition, 2011)** — The standard comprehensive reference. Covers anatomy, physiology, behavior, ecology, and silk biology. Excellent diagrams. Advanced undergraduate to graduate level, but chapters are accessible for motivated intermediate students. Focus on chapters 2 (anatomy), 4 (sensory organs), 5 (silk), and 8 (ecology).

- **Ruppert, Fox, & Barnes "Invertebrate Zoology" (7th edition)** — Chapter on Chelicerata provides excellent evolutionary and comparative context. Good for understanding where spiders fit in arthropod phylogeny.

### Open Educational Resources
- **UC Berkeley Museum of Paleontology — Spider resources** — https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/arthropoda/arachnida/spiders.html — Excellent introduction to spider diversity, anatomy, and evolution. Clear diagrams and accessible text.

- **Australian Museum — Spider section** — https://australian.museum/learn/animals/spiders/ — Outstanding resource for spider diversity, identification, and biology. Many species profiles with photos.

- **Burke Museum (University of Washington) — Spider Myths** — https://www.burkemuseum.org/collections-and-research/biology/arachnology-and-entomology/spider-myths — Excellent resource for addressing misconceptions. Rod Crawford's myth-busting is evidence-based and engaging.

### Museums and Collections
- **American Museum of Natural History — Spiders** — https://www.amnh.org/explore/ology/zoology/spiders — Interactive educational resources, videos, and activities. Good for visual learners.

- **Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History** — https://naturalhistory.si.edu/ — Extensive spider collections and educational materials.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos and Documentaries

- **Deep Look (PBS Digital Studios/KQED)** — https://www.youtube.com/c/KQEDDeepLook — Outstanding short-form nature documentaries. Search for spider-specific episodes:
  - "How Spiders Avoid Getting Stuck in Their Own Webs"
  - "Jumping Spiders Can Think Ahead, Plan Detours"
  - "The Amazing (and Deadly) Science of Jumping Spider Vision"

- **BBC Earth — Spider videos** — https://www.youtube.com/user/BBCEarth — High-quality footage of spider behavior, hunting, and mating.

- **Smarter Every Day — "The Secret Mechanism Inside a Mechanical Spider"** — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9EDXjFFp1qE — While focused on mechanical spider, excellent explanation of hydraulic systems that parallels spider locomotion.

- **National Geographic — Jumping Spiders** — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/invertebrates/facts/jumping-spiders — Species profiles and behavior videos.

### Interactive Tools and Databases

- **iNaturalist** — https://www.inaturalist.org/ — Community science platform for species identification. Excellent for learning to identify spider families and practicing observational skills. Search for "Araneae" to explore spider observations.

- **Protein Data Bank (PDB) — Spidroin structures** — https://www.rcsb.org/ — Search for spider silk proteins (e.g., PDB ID: 3LR2, 2KHM) to visualize molecular structures. Requires some comfort with molecular visualization but very powerful for understanding structure-function relationships.

- **World Spider Catalog** — https://wsc.nmbe.ch/ — Comprehensive taxonomic database. Advanced resource but useful for looking up spider families and distributions.

### Research Articles (accessible)

- **Vollrath, F. & Knight, D.P. (2001) "Liquid crystalline spinning of spider silk" Nature** — https://www.nature.com/articles/35066000 — Classic paper on silk spinning mechanism. Figure 2 is particularly valuable.

- **Greco, G. et al. (2020) "Properties of biomimetic artificial spider silk fibers tuned by PostSpin Bath Incubation" Molecules** — Open access article on synthetic silk production.

- **Harland, D. & Jackson, R. (2004) "Eight-legged cats and how they see" Journal of Experimental Biology** — Accessible review of jumping spider vision. Open access.

### Code and Data

- **Spider Silk Sequence Database** — Limited public databases, but NCBI GenBank has many spidroin sequences. Search "spidroin" or "spider silk protein" — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/

- **R package 'spider'** — While focused on DNA barcoding, contains some spider-relevant datasets and could be explored by students with R experience.

## Unexpected Connections (for rabbit holes)

### Biomimetics and Engineering
- **Spiber Inc.** — Japanese company producing synthetic spider silk proteins (Brewed Protein™). Commercial applications in textiles. https://www.spiber.inc/en/

- **Bolt Threads** — Another synthetic spider silk company. Created Microsilk™ for fashion industry. Interesting case study in applied biology.

- **NASA spider web studies** — Web-building in zero gravity and under influence of drugs. Classic research with surprising insights into sensory integration.

### Art and Culture
- **Textile art from spider silk** — Golden silk from *Nephila* spiders has been woven into fabric (Cape exhibited at V&A Museum). Extremely rare, demonstrates cultural value of silk.

- **Louise Bourgeois "Maman"** — Giant spider sculptures, exploring themes of motherhood. Connects to spider maternal care behaviors.

### Neuroscience and Medicine
- **Ziconotide (Prialt)** — Pain medication derived from cone snail venom, similar approach being explored with spider venom peptides.

- **Spider venom peptides as insecticides** — Biopesticide development using spider neurotoxins specific to insects.

### Ecology and Conservation
- **Spiders as biological indicators** — Used to assess ecosystem health and habitat quality. Ground spider communities particularly sensitive to disturbance.

- **Cave spiders** — Highly endemic, often endangered. Interesting for discussions of conservation priorities.

## Tools for Hands-On Learning

### Observation and Identification
- **Hand lens or macro lens** — For observing spider anatomy in detail
- **iNaturalist app** — For identifying spiders encountered in the field
- **Guide to spiders of North America** (or relevant region) — Field guide for identification practice

### Experimental (if accessible)
- **Web measurement tools** — Calipers, rulers for measuring web dimensions and geometry
- **UV light** — Many scorpions (arachnids) fluoresce under UV; some spiders also show interesting patterns
- **High-speed camera** (smartphone slow-mo works)** — For observing jumping spider leaps, prey capture

### Visualization
- **Molecular visualization software** — PyMOL, Jmol, or ChimeraX for viewing protein structures from PDB
- **Image analysis software** — ImageJ/Fiji for measuring web architecture from photos

## Additional Online Resources

- **American Arachnological Society** — https://www.americanarachnology.org/ — Professional society with educational resources and journal access

- **British Arachnological Society** — https://www.britishspiders.org.uk/ — Excellent resources for UK/European spiders, but general biology resources applicable globally

- **Annual Review of Entomology** — https://www.annualreviews.org/journal/ento — Publishes periodic review articles on spider biology. University library access often needed.

- **Arachnology** (journal published by British Arachnological Society) — Primary research journal

- **Journal of Arachnology** — American Arachnological Society journal, open access archive

## Recommended Learning Sequence Through Resources

1. **Start with visual engagement**: AMNH Ology, Australian Museum, Deep Look videos
2. **Build anatomical foundation**: UC Berkeley UCMP resources, Foelix chapters 2-3
3. **Understand silk science**: Deep Look silk videos → Vollrath & Knight paper → PDB exploration
4. **Explore behavior**: Jumping spider videos → Jackson's work on Portia → sexual selection papers
5. **Apply knowledge**: iNaturalist practice → synthetic silk companies → biomimetics articles
6. **Deepen evolutionary understanding**: Gillespie's adaptive radiation work → phylogenetic resources

## Resource Quality Notes

**High quality, intermediate-appropriate**: UC Berkeley UCMP, Australian Museum, Deep Look, most Nature/Science papers (focus on figures and results sections)

**Requires guidance**: Foelix textbook (detailed but can be dense), primary literature (methods sections often too technical), PDB structures (need orientation to molecular visualization)

**Avoid**: Sensationalist "deadly spider" content that focuses on fear rather than biology, oversimplified children's books that lack mechanistic explanations, outdated taxonomy (pre-2000 phylogenies)
