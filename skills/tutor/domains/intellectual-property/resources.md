# Intellectual Property — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Open Textbooks & Casebooks
- **"Intellectual Property: Law & the Information Society—Cases and Materials"** by James Boyle and Jennifer Jenkins — Open casebook covering trademark, copyright, patent, and trade secret with foundational cases, secondary readings, and problem sets. Excellent for intermediate students seeking case-based learning. [Open Textbook Library](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/intellectual-property-law-the-information-society-cases-and-materials)

- **"Intellectual Property in the New Technological Age"** by Peter S. Menell (UC Berkeley), Mark A. Lemley (Stanford), and Robert P. Merges (UC Berkeley) — Leading casebook for survey courses, updated regularly to address technological changes

### Commercial Textbooks (Comprehensive)
- **"Intellectual Property: The Law of Trademarks, Copyrights, Patents, and Trade Secrets" (6th Edition)** by Deborah E. Bouchoux — Comprehensive overview with practical tools, examples, and activities. Good for students who want structured explanations alongside cases. [Cengage](https://www.cengage.com/c/intellectual-property-the-law-of-trademarks-copyrights-patents-and-trade-secrets-6e-bouchoux/9780357767474/)

- **"Intellectual Property, Patents, Trademarks, and Copyright in a Nutshell" (7th Edition)** by Arthur R. Miller and Michael H. Davis — Concise authoritative overview, excellent as supplementary reference

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Economics & Game Theory
- **Patent thickets and hold-up problems** — How overlapping patents create barriers to innovation
- **Tragedy of the anticommons** — When too many IP rights fragment innovation
- **Network effects in copyright** — Why some works become culturally dominant
- **Optimal patent length models** — Nordhaus and subsequent economic models of IP duration

### Computer Science & AI
- **Reverse engineering and software trade secrets** — What can't be protected if code is decompilable?
- **AI authorship and inventorship** — Who owns AI-generated works? The DABUS patent cases
- **Training data copyright** — Fair use in machine learning context (ongoing litigation)
- **Adversarial examples** — ML attacks that generate infringing works below detection threshold

### Biology & Medicine
- **Gene patents** — Myriad Genetics case (genes unpatentable but cDNA patentable)
- **Research exemption** — Does patent law unduly restrict scientific research?
- **Drug patents and access to medicine** — TRIPS flexibilities, compulsory licensing
- **Biotechnology licensing** — Bayh-Dole Act and university tech transfer

### Art & Cultural Studies
- **Appropriation art** — When is copying creative work fair use? (Richard Prince, Shepard Fairey)
- **Sampling in music** — Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. ("Thou shalt not steal")
- **Fan fiction and transformative works** — Copyright vs. participatory culture
- **Indigenous knowledge and biopiracy** — Should traditional knowledge get IP protection?

### Philosophy & Ethics
- **Lockean labor theory** — Do creators have natural rights to their creations?
- **Utilitarian vs. deontological IP justifications** — Incentives vs. moral rights
- **Information wants to be free** — Cyberlibertarian critiques of IP
- **Access to knowledge (A2K) movement** — Global South perspectives on IP imperialism

### History
- **Statute of Anne (1710)** — First modern copyright law
- **Venetian Patent Statute (1474)** — Medieval origins of patent law
- **The Copyright Term Extension Act debates** — Eldred v. Ashcroft and the public domain
- **Patent medicine shows and trademark law** — Consumer protection origins

### International Relations
- **TRIPS Agreement** — Trade-Related Aspects of IP Rights minimum standards
- **WIPO** — World Intellectual Property Organization treaties and harmonization
- **Compulsory licensing in pandemics** — COVID-19 vaccine patent waiver debates
- **China's IP enforcement evolution** — From copying to innovation economy

### Psychology & Behavioral Economics
- **Endowment effect in IP** — Why creators overvalue their works
- **Attribution and plagiarism psychology** — Why giving credit matters beyond law
- **Innovation and intrinsic motivation** — Do IP rights crowd out creativity? (Lepper studies)

### Environmental Law
- **Green patents and climate tech** — Should environmental innovations have shorter terms or compulsory licensing?
- **Geographic indications** — Champagne, Parmigiano-Reggiano, and terroir
- **Seed patents and food security** — Monsanto and farmer saved seed disputes

## Keeping Current

IP law evolves rapidly with technology and new cases. Students should:

- Follow **SCOTUS IP dockets** — Major cases reshape doctrine regularly
- Monitor **USPTO rule changes** — Patent examination guidelines frequently updated
- Read **IP law blogs** — IP Watchdog, Patently-O, The 1709 Blog (copyright focus)
- Watch **Congressional IP hearings** — DMCA reform, AI copyright, patent troll legislation
- Track **international developments** — EU Copyright Directive, Brexit IP transitions, WIPO treaties

This field rewards staying curious about "what happens when new technology meets old law?"
