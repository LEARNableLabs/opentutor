# Intellectual Property — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Incentive Theory** — IP grants limited monopolies to incentivize creation and disclosure
2. **Public Domain** — Ideas, expired rights, and non-protectable material accessible to all
3. **Disclosure vs. Secrecy Tradeoff** — Core strategic choice between patent/copyright (public) and trade secrets (private)
4. **Constitutional Basis** — Progress Clause limits IP to "limited Times" for utilitarian purpose
5. **Idea-Expression Dichotomy** — Only expressions are protectable, not underlying ideas

6. **Trade Secret Definition** — Information with independent economic value, not generally known, subject to reasonable measures. Depends on: 5
7. **Secrecy Requirement** — Information must not be readily ascertainable. Depends on: 6
8. **Reasonable Measures** — Efforts to maintain secrecy (NDAs, access controls, security). Depends on: 6, 7
9. **Misappropriation** — Improper acquisition, disclosure, or use of trade secrets. Depends on: 6, 7, 8
10. **Inevitable Disclosure** — Doctrine preventing employees from working where secrets might be used. Depends on: 9

11. **Patentable Subject Matter** — What can be patented (processes, machines, manufactures, compositions). Depends on: 4
12. **Novelty** — Invention must be new, not anticipated by prior art. Depends on: 11
13. **Non-Obviousness** — Invention must not be obvious to person skilled in the art (PHOSITA). Depends on: 11, 12
14. **Utility** — Invention must have specific, substantial, credible use. Depends on: 11
15. **Patent Claims** — Precise legal definition of invention's scope. Depends on: 11, 12, 13
16. **Claim Construction** — Interpretation of claim language determines patent scope. Depends on: 15
17. **Patent Prosecution** — Examination process where applicant and examiner negotiate claims. Depends on: 15
18. **Literal Infringement** — Accused product contains every claim element. Depends on: 16
19. **Doctrine of Equivalents** — Infringement where differences are insubstantial. Depends on: 18
20. **Invalidity Defense** — Patent can be challenged as failing patentability requirements. Depends on: 12, 13, 14
21. **Software Patent Eligibility** — Abstract ideas unpatentable unless technological improvement. Depends on: 11

22. **Copyrightable Subject Matter** — Original works of authorship fixed in tangible medium. Depends on: 5
23. **Originality Requirement** — Minimal creativity plus independent creation (Feist). Depends on: 22
24. **Fixation** — Work must be recorded in stable medium. Depends on: 22
25. **Fair Use** — Four-factor test allowing unauthorized use in certain contexts. Depends on: 22, 23
26. **Transformative Use** — New purpose/character weighs toward fair use. Depends on: 25
27. **DMCA Safe Harbor** — ISPs protected if they follow takedown procedures. Depends on: 22
28. **DMCA Anti-Circumvention** — Prohibition on defeating technological protection measures. Depends on: 27
29. **Work for Hire** — Employer/commissioner owns copyright in certain circumstances. Depends on: 22
30. **Derivative Works** — Adaptations that transform underlying work. Depends on: 23, 29
31. **Compilation Protection** — Thin copyright in selection/arrangement. Depends on: 23

32. **IP Portfolio Strategy** — Combining multiple forms of protection for layered defense. Depends on: 3, 6, 11, 22
33. **Licensing** — Granting permission to use IP while retaining ownership. Depends on: 32

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies
- **Trade secret, patent, and copyright law** all depend on understanding the **constitutional basis** (Concept 4) and **idea-expression dichotomy** (Concept 5)
- **Disclosure vs. Secrecy tradeoff** (Concept 3) is the fundamental strategic decision that drives all other choices

### Trade Secret Dependencies
- **Misappropriation** (Concept 9) requires understanding all three elements: **trade secret definition** (6), **secrecy requirement** (7), and **reasonable measures** (8)
- **Inevitable disclosure** (Concept 10) is an enforcement doctrine that only makes sense after understanding **misappropriation** (9)

### Patent Dependencies
- **Novelty** (Concept 12) and **non-obviousness** (Concept 13) both build on **patentable subject matter** (11), and non-obviousness also requires novelty as a foundation
- **Patent claims** (Concept 15) integrate all patentability requirements (11-14) into a legal instrument
- **Claim construction** (Concept 16) is necessary to understand both **literal infringement** (18) and defenses
- **Invalidity defenses** (Concept 20) loop back to the patentability requirements (12-14), creating a feedback cycle

### Copyright Dependencies
- **Originality** (Concept 23) and **fixation** (Concept 24) are parallel requirements, both building on **copyrightable subject matter** (22)
- **Transformative use** (Concept 26) is the key modern development in **fair use** (25) doctrine
- **Derivative works** (Concept 30) and **compilations** (Concept 31) are both special cases of **originality** (23)
- **Work for hire** (Concept 29) determines initial ownership, affecting all downstream rights

### Integration Dependencies
- **IP portfolio strategy** (Concept 32) requires understanding the **disclosure vs. secrecy tradeoff** (3) plus the basics of trade secrets (6), patents (11), and copyright (22)

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts unlock large portions of the curriculum when mastered:

1. **Idea-Expression Dichotomy** (Concept 5) — Understanding this distinction is crucial for both patent (abstract ideas exception) and copyright (only expressions protected)
2. **Disclosure vs. Secrecy Tradeoff** (Concept 3) — This strategic framework governs the entire field and must be internalized early
3. **Patent Claims** (Concept 15) — All patent infringement and validity analysis hinges on claim interpretation
4. **Fair Use** (Concept 25) — Most copyright disputes involve fair use; it's the safety valve for the entire copyright system
5. **Originality** (Concept 23) — Determines scope of copyright protection for all types of works

## Mind-Blowing Moments

- **Coca-Cola's $3B recipe** — Realizing that secrecy can be more valuable than a 20-year patent monopoly
- **The "Person Skilled in the Art"** — The legal fiction that obviousness is judged from the perspective of a hypothetical average practitioner
- **Transformative Use Evolution** — How a 1994 rap parody case (Campbell) reshaped all of fair use doctrine
- **Software Patent Paradox** — Same code can be copyrightable but not patentable, or vice versa
- **The Public Domain Bargain** — IP is fundamentally a deal: society grants temporary monopoly in exchange for eventual public access
- **Work for Hire Trap** — Freelancers often unknowingly surrender all copyright unless they negotiate carefully
- **DMCA Notice-and-Takedown** — How private parties became copyright enforcers without judicial oversight

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"IP protects ideas"** — No, only specific expressions, inventions, or secret information; abstract ideas cannot be owned
2. **"Poor man's copyright"** — Mailing yourself a copy does nothing; copyright exists upon fixation
3. **"If it's on the internet, it's public domain"** — Public accessibility ≠ public domain
4. **"Trade secrets last forever"** — Only while they remain secret and maintain their value
5. **"Fair use means I can use 10%"** — No bright-line percentage rule exists; it's a four-factor balancing test
6. **"Patents are always better than trade secrets"** — Depends on reverse-engineerability, enforcement costs, and strategic goals
7. **"You need ™ or © symbols to have rights"** — Notice helps but is not required for protection in most cases
8. **"Obviousness means obvious to anyone"** — No, it's judged from perspective of PHOSITA, not layperson or genius
9. **"Small changes avoid copyright infringement"** — Substantial similarity test looks at overall expression, not line-by-line comparison
10. **"First to invent wins"** — US switched to first-to-file system in 2013 (America Invents Act)

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic Legal Reasoning** — Needed for case analysis, statutory interpretation, and policy arguments (affects all concepts)
- **Contract Law** — Needed for licensing (Concept 33), NDAs (Concept 8), work-for-hire agreements (Concept 29)
- **Property Law** — Needed for understanding ownership, assignment, and transferability (affects Concepts 6, 15, 22)
- **Market Competition** — Needed for understanding economic value (Concepts 1, 6, 32)
- **Constitutional Law** — Needed for understanding First Amendment limits and Progress Clause (Concept 4)
- **Civil Procedure** — Needed for understanding enforcement, litigation strategy, and remedies (Concepts 9, 18, 20, 27)

## Teaching Sequence Rationale

The curriculum follows this progression:

1. **Policy Foundation** (Lessons 1-4) — Why IP exists and how to choose between types
2. **Trade Secrets** (Lessons 5-9) — Start with simplest form (no registration, common law roots)
3. **Patents** (Lessons 10-18) — Most complex requirements and highest technical difficulty
4. **Copyright** (Lessons 19-26) — More familiar subject matter but subtle doctrines
5. **Integration** (Lessons 27-28) — Portfolio strategy and future challenges

This order moves from **conceptual framework → simplest doctrine → most complex doctrine → moderate complexity → strategic synthesis**.
