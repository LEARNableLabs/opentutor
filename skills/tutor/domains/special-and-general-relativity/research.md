# Research Summary — Special and General Relativity

## Major Subtopics

### Special Relativity
- Postulates of special relativity (constancy of light speed, equivalence of inertial frames)
- Lorentz transformations and their geometric interpretation
- Kinematic effects: time dilation, length contraction, relativity of simultaneity
- Minkowski spacetime and spacetime diagrams
- 4-vectors and tensor notation
- Relativistic dynamics: energy, momentum, mass-energy equivalence
- Paradoxes and their resolution (twin paradox, ladder paradox)

### General Relativity
- Equivalence principle (weak and strong forms)
- Curved spacetime and Riemannian geometry
- Geodesics and the geodesic equation
- Riemann curvature tensor and its contractions
- Einstein field equations and stress-energy tensor
- Schwarzschild solution and black holes
- Gravitational waves and their detection
- Cosmological applications (FRW metrics, expanding universe)
- Experimental tests (perihelion precession, gravitational lensing, GPS corrections)

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **"Spacetime and Geometry" by Sean Carroll** — Modern approach, excellent for intermediate students, strong on conceptual foundations
- **"A First Course in General Relativity" by Bernard Schutz** — Standard undergraduate text, very pedagogical
- **"Special Relativity" by A.P. French** — Classic introduction to SR with emphasis on physical intuition
- **"Gravity: An Introduction to Einstein's General Relativity" by James Hartle** — Physics-first approach, minimal prerequisites
- **"Gravitation" by Misner, Thorne, Wheeler** — Comprehensive reference (advanced)

### Online Courses
- MIT OCW 8.033 (Relativity) — Special relativity with worked problems
- MIT OCW 8.962 (General Relativity) — Full GR course with lecture notes
- Stanford Leonard Susskind lectures — "The Theoretical Minimum" series on special and general relativity
- Sean Carroll's GR notes (available online) — Widely used, clear exposition

### Video Resources

- Veritasium special relativity videos — Experimental perspectives

### Interactive Tools

## Notes for Curriculum Design

- Start with special relativity to build geometric intuition before introducing curvature
- Emphasize visual/geometric reasoning — spacetime diagrams are essential
- Use thought experiments extensively (Einstein's elevator, light clocks, etc.)
- For intermediate level: include tensor notation but focus on physical interpretation
- Connect to experimental evidence throughout (muon decay, GPS, LIGO)
- Address common paradoxes explicitly — they're pedagogically valuable
- General relativity section can be conceptual for most students, with optional mathematical deep-dives
