# Meditation Neuroscience — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Attention and Cognitive Control
- Focused attention (FA) and open monitoring (OM) practices
- Attentional networks: alerting, orienting, executive control
- Cognitive enhancement and sustained attention
- Working memory improvements

### 2. Brain Structure and Neuroplasticity
- Gray matter changes in meditators vs. controls
- Cortical thickness in prefrontal and insular cortices
- Hippocampal volume and memory systems
- Dose-response relationships (hours of practice)

### 3. Stress and Emotion Regulation
- HPA axis and cortisol response
- Amygdala reactivity and habituation
- Prefrontal-amygdala connectivity
- Vagal tone and parasympathetic activation

### 4. Default Mode Network
- Mind-wandering and self-referential processing
- Posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) deactivation
- Meditation and the "self"
- Meta-awareness and network transitions

### 5. Social and Affective Neuroscience
- Compassion meditation (loving-kindness, metta)
- Empathy networks and mirror neurons
- Prosocial behavior changes
- Insula and emotional awareness

### 6. Clinical Applications
- Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)
- Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT)
- Depression, anxiety, PTSD applications
- Pain modulation and chronic pain

### 7. Methodological Considerations
- fMRI, EEG, structural imaging techniques
- Control groups and expectation effects
- Defining "meditation" across traditions
- Individual differences and trait vs. state effects

## Major Research Findings

1. **8 weeks of MBSR** shows measurable changes in gray matter density (hippocampus, PCC, TPJ, cerebellum)
2. **Long-term meditators** show enhanced gamma synchrony during compassion meditation
3. **Attention training** reduces attentional blink and improves sustained attention
4. **Default mode network** shows reduced activity in experienced meditators even at rest
5. **Amygdala reactivity** decreases after mindfulness training, correlating with stress reduction
6. **Dose-response**: structural changes correlate with lifetime practice hours
7. **Pain processing**: meditation decouples sensory and affective pain components

## Curriculum Design Notes

This is an intermediate-level curriculum assuming:
- Basic neuroscience literacy (brain regions, neurotransmitters, imaging methods)
- Familiarity with psychological concepts (attention, emotion, memory)
- Interest in evidence-based contemplative science

The curriculum balances:
- Mechanistic neuroscience (what changes and how)
- Phenomenology (what practitioners experience)
- Clinical applications (what works for whom)
- Critical thinking (limitations, hype vs. evidence)
