# Oncology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Cell cycle dysregulation** — loss of normal growth control in cancer cells
2. **Oncogenes** — mutated genes that drive uncontrolled cell division (e.g., RAS, MYC)
3. **Tumor suppressor genes** — genes that normally prevent cancer; their loss enables cancer (e.g., TP53, BRCA)
4. **Two-hit hypothesis** — both copies of a tumor suppressor must be lost for cancer to develop
5. **Multistep carcinogenesis** — cancer requires multiple sequential mutations over time. Depends on: 2, 3
6. **Driver vs passenger mutations** — distinguishing causative mutations from incidental ones. Depends on: 5
7. **Apoptosis evasion** — cancer cells avoid programmed cell death. Depends on: 2, 3
8. **Hallmarks of cancer** — unifying framework describing capabilities all cancers acquire. Depends on: 1-7
9. **Angiogenesis** — tumor-induced blood vessel formation to supply nutrients. Depends on: 8
10. **Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)** — process enabling cancer cells to migrate. Depends on: 8
11. **Metastatic cascade** — multi-step process of cancer spread to distant organs. Depends on: 10
12. **Tumor microenvironment** — supporting cells and signals around tumor. Depends on: 9
13. **Immune evasion** — how tumors escape immune surveillance. Depends on: 12
14. **Chemotherapy mechanism** — cytotoxic drugs that preferentially kill dividing cells. Depends on: 1
15. **Therapeutic index** — ratio of efficacy to toxicity for cancer drugs. Depends on: 14
16. **Radiation therapy** — using ionizing radiation to damage cancer DNA. Depends on: 1
17. **Targeted therapy** — drugs designed against specific cancer mutations. Depends on: 2, 3, 6
18. **Checkpoint inhibitors** — immunotherapy that unleashes T cells against cancer. Depends on: 13
19. **CAR-T therapy** — engineered immune cells targeting cancer. Depends on: 13
20. **Tumor heterogeneity** — genetic diversity within a single tumor. Depends on: 5, 6
21. **Clonal evolution** — Darwinian selection of cancer cells during treatment. Depends on: 20
22. **Drug resistance mechanisms** — intrinsic or acquired treatment resistance. Depends on: 21
23. **Minimal residual disease** — trace cancer remaining after treatment. Depends on: 22
24. **Liquid biopsies** — blood tests to detect circulating tumor DNA. Depends on: 23
25. **Precision oncology** — matching treatments to specific tumor mutations. Depends on: 6, 17
26. **Clinical trial phases** — structured testing of new cancer therapies. Depends on: 15

## Critical Dependencies

### Early Foundation (Lessons 1-6)
- **Cell cycle dysregulation → all downstream concepts** — students must understand normal cell cycle control before any cancer biology makes sense
- **Oncogenes + Tumor suppressors → Multistep carcinogenesis** — the "two types of genes" model is prerequisite for understanding why multiple hits are needed
- **Two-hit hypothesis → Tumor suppressors** — explains familial cancer predisposition and why both alleles matter

### Tumor Biology (Lessons 7-11)
- **Hallmarks framework → everything after lesson 7** — this unifying framework contextualizes all subsequent mechanisms
- **Angiogenesis + Immune evasion → Treatment strategies** — understanding how tumors survive sets up why we target these processes

### Treatment Understanding (Lessons 12-18)
- **Cell cycle knowledge → Chemotherapy AND Radiation** — both exploit rapid division; requires lesson 1
- **Specific mutations → Targeted therapy** — can't understand precision medicine without knowing driver mutations
- **Immune evasion → Immunotherapy** — checkpoint inhibitors only make sense after understanding PD-L1 evasion

### Resistance and Clinical Practice (Lessons 19-26)
- **Tumor heterogeneity → Drug resistance** — CRITICAL bottleneck. Students often don't grasp that tumors are populations under selection
- **Clonal evolution → Adaptive therapy** — requires understanding cancer as evolving ecosystem
- **All treatment types → Clinical decision-making** — integrating knowledge to evaluate real cases

## Key Bottlenecks

1. **Multistep carcinogenesis** (Lesson 5) — students often think "one mutation = cancer" from pop-culture. Breaking this misconception is critical before moving forward.

2. **Hallmarks framework** (Lesson 7) — this is a major inflection point. Students who grasp this framework can contextualize everything afterward; those who don't will see disconnected facts.

3. **Tumor heterogeneity** (Lesson 20) — paradigm shift from "cancer is one thing" to "cancer is an evolving population." Essential for understanding resistance.

4. **Therapeutic index** (Lesson 12) — understanding why we can't just "kill all cancer cells" is crucial for appreciating treatment limitations and side effects.

## Common Misconceptions (by concept)

### Cancer Initiation
- **Misconception**: "Cancer is caused by one genetic mutation"  
  **Reality**: Most cancers require 4-7 driver mutations accumulated over years

- **Misconception**: "All mutations cause cancer"  
  **Reality**: Most mutations are neutral (passengers); only drivers matter

- **Misconception**: "Cancer is contagious" or "Cancer is one disease"  
  **Reality**: Cancer is 200+ distinct diseases with different genetics

### Tumor Progression
- **Misconception**: "Tumors are homogeneous masses of identical cells"  
  **Reality**: Tumors are heterogeneous ecosystems with diverse subclones

- **Misconception**: "Metastasis is random"  
  **Reality**: Metastasis is a selective process; most disseminated cells fail

- **Misconception**: "Big tumors are more dangerous"  
  **Reality**: Small metastases often determine survival, not primary tumor size

### Treatment
- **Misconception**: "Chemotherapy just poisons everything"  
  **Reality**: Chemo exploits faster division in cancer cells; there IS selectivity (though imperfect)

- **Misconception**: "Targeted therapy cures cancer"  
  **Reality**: Resistance develops; targeted drugs extend survival but rarely cure

- **Misconception**: "Immunotherapy works by boosting the immune system"  
  **Reality**: It removes brakes (checkpoint blockade) or redirects attack (CAR-T), not general boosting

- **Misconception**: "Radiation kills cells instantly"  
  **Reality**: Radiation damages DNA; death occurs when cells try to divide

### Resistance
- **Misconception**: "Resistance means the drug stops working"  
  **Reality**: Resistant subclones were present or emerged; it's selection not drug failure

- **Misconception**: "Higher drug doses overcome resistance"  
  **Reality**: Often makes things worse by killing sensitive cells faster, accelerating resistant clone expansion

## Prerequisite Topics

### Required
- **Cell biology** — mitosis, cell cycle phases (G1, S, G2, M), checkpoints
- **Molecular biology** — DNA structure, transcription, translation, proteins
- **Genetics** — genes, alleles, mutations, inheritance patterns
- **Basic biochemistry** — signal transduction, protein function

### Helpful but not essential
- **Immunology** — T cells, antibodies (will be taught in context for lessons 10, 16, 18)
- **Pharmacology** — drug mechanisms (will be introduced as needed)
- **Statistics** — clinical trial interpretation (simplified for lesson 25)

## Learning Pathways

### Fast track (strong biology background)
- Students with recent molecular biology can move through lessons 1-6 in 3-4 days
- Can combine lessons 12-14 (treatment overview) if familiar with medical concepts
- Focus extra time on lessons 19-22 (resistance), the intellectually deepest section

### Standard track (intermediate level)
- Follow curriculum order
- Expect lessons 7 (hallmarks) and 20 (heterogeneity) to require extra time
- Use review lessons (6, 13, 20) to consolidate before moving forward

### Slower track (weaker prerequisite background)
- May need supplementary review of cell cycle before lesson 1
- Split lesson 3 (multistep carcinogenesis) into two sessions
- Add extra practice with cancer genomics databases (lesson 5) before advancing

## Connections to Other Domains

- **Evolutionary biology** — clonal evolution, selection pressure, adaptive dynamics
- **Ecology** — tumor microenvironment as ecosystem, competitive release
- **Pharmacology** — drug mechanisms, resistance, combination therapy
- **Epidemiology** — cancer incidence, risk factors, screening
- **Bioethics** — clinical trials, right-to-try, resource allocation
- **Data science** — cancer genomics, biomarker discovery, predictive models
