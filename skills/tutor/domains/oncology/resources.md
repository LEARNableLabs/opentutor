# Oncology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **The Biology of Cancer** by Robert A. Weinberg (2013, 2nd ed.)  
  https://wwnorton.com/books/9780815342205  
  *The gold standard for cancer biology. Comprehensive, mechanistic, excellent figures. Intermediate-to-advanced level. Use chapters 2-6 for lessons 1-6, chapter 11 for metastasis, chapter 16 for treatment.*

- **The Emperor of All Maladies** by Siddhartha Mukherjee (2010)  
  https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/siddhartha-mukherjee  
  *Not a textbook but a brilliant cancer history. Humanizes the science. Assign excerpts for context on chemotherapy development (lesson 12) and clinical trials (lesson 25).*

- **DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology** (11th ed., 2018)  
  https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/ovid/devita-hellman-and-rosenbergs-cancer-principles--practice-of-oncology-1525  
  *Clinical reference. Dense. Use for treatment protocols (lesson 18) and clinical trial design (lesson 25).*

### Online Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 7.28: Molecular Biology: Cellular Signaling and Cancer**  
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-28-molecular-biology-cellular-signaling-and-cancer-spring-2020/  
  *Excellent for oncogene/tumor suppressor mechanisms (lessons 1-4). Lecture notes and problem sets available.*

- **Johns Hopkins University: Introduction to the Biology of Cancer (Coursera)**  
  https://www.coursera.org/learn/cancer  
  *Free audit option. Covers hallmarks framework and treatment basics. Good supplementary videos for lessons 7-11.*

- **Stanford Cancer Biology Course (online lectures)**  
  https://med.stanford.edu/cancer.html  
  *Advanced but excellent seminars on current research. Use selectively for immunotherapy (lesson 16) and precision medicine (lesson 24).*

### Key Research Papers (open access)

- **Hanahan & Weinberg (2011): "Hallmarks of Cancer: The Next Generation"**  
  https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0092-8674(11)00127-9  
  *Essential reading for lesson 7. Defines the framework used throughout the curriculum.*

- **Vogelstein et al. (2013): "Cancer Genome Landscapes"**  
  https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1235122  
  *Great overview of driver mutations across cancer types. Use for lesson 5-6.*

- **Gerlinger et al. (2012): "Intratumor Heterogeneity and Branched Evolution Revealed by Multiregion Sequencing"**  
  https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa1113205  
  *Classic paper demonstrating tumor heterogeneity. Use for lesson 20.*

- **Ribas & Wolchok (2018): "Cancer Immunotherapy Using Checkpoint Blockade"**  
  https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aar4060  
  *Comprehensive immunotherapy review. Use for lesson 16.*

## Supplementary Resources

### Educational Websites

- **National Cancer Institute (NCI) — cancer.gov**  
  https://www.cancer.gov/  
  *Best free resource. Patient and professional sections. Excellent for background on all topics. PDQ (Physician Data Query) treatment summaries are gold standard.*

- **Nature Education: Cancer Biology**  
  https://www.nature.com/scitable/topic/cancer-7/  
  *Peer-reviewed educational modules on specific topics. Great visual explanations.*

- **CancerQuest (Emory University)**  
  https://www.cancerquest.org/  
  *Interactive modules on cancer biology and treatment. Excellent animations for angiogenesis (lesson 8) and metastasis (lesson 9).*

- **Cancer.Net (ASCO patient education site)**  
  https://www.cancer.net/  
  *Patient-facing but high-quality. Use for translating concepts to plain language (teach-back lessons 11, 26).*

- **American Cancer Society**  
  https://www.cancer.org/  
  *Broad coverage of cancer types and treatment. Good for treatment overviews (lessons 12-16).*

### Video Lectures and Channels

- **Khan Academy: Cancer**  
  https://www.khanacademy.org/science/biology/cellular-molecular-biology/stem-cells-and-cancer  
  *Excellent animated videos on cell cycle, oncogenes, tumor suppressors (lessons 1-4). Beginner-friendly.*

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Eric Lander's Introduction to Biology (lectures on cancer)**  
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-016-introductory-biology-fall-2018/  
  *Search for cancer-specific lectures. Lander is an exceptional teacher.*

- **HHMI BioInteractive: Cancer**  
  https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/cancer  
  *Short films and animations. "Cancer: The Enemy Within" is excellent for hallmarks (lesson 7).*

- **Nature Video: The Immune System vs Cancer**  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6UYfL4WV-o  
  *Great visual explanation of immune checkpoint blockade (lesson 16).*

- **Kurzgesagt: What is Cancer?**  
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z2k2fSU_Xtg  
  *Accessible overview for lesson 1. Beautiful animation.*

### Interactive Tools and Databases

- **COSMIC — Catalogue of Somatic Mutations in Cancer**  
  https://cancer.sanger.ac.uk/cosmic  
  *Essential for lesson 5. Students should explore mutation landscapes for specific cancer types.*

- **cBioPortal for Cancer Genomics**  
  https://www.cbioportal.org/  
  *Visualize cancer genomic data. Use for lessons 5, 6, 24 (precision medicine).*

- **The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)**  
  https://www.cancer.gov/tcga  
  *Massive genomic dataset. More advanced but powerful for showing real data (lesson 24).*

- **OncoKB — Oncology Knowledge Base (Memorial Sloan Kettering)**  
  https://www.oncokb.org/  
  *Precision oncology database linking mutations to therapies. Use for lesson 24.*

- **NCCN Guidelines for Patients**  
  https://www.nccn.org/patients  
  *Free evidence-based treatment algorithms. Use for lesson 17-18 to analyze real treatment decisions.*

- **GeneCards — Human Gene Database**  
  https://www.genecards.org/  
  *Look up any oncogene or tumor suppressor. Good reference for lessons 2-4.*

### Code and Computational Resources

- **Bioconductor (R packages for cancer genomics)**  
  https://www.bioconductor.org/  
  *For students interested in computational approaches. Packages like TCGAbiolinks, maftools for analyzing mutation data.*

- **Google Colab Notebooks on Cancer Data Analysis**  
  https://colab.research.google.com/  
  *Search for TCGA analysis notebooks. Hands-on practice with real data (lesson 5, 24).*

- **UCSC Xena Browser**  
  https://xenabrowser.net/  
  *Visualize TCGA data. More user-friendly than raw TCGA portal (lesson 24).*

### Clinical Trial Information

- **ClinicalTrials.gov**  
  https://clinicaltrials.gov/  
  *Search for cancer trials. Use in lesson 25 to explore phase I-III trials in real time.*

- **FDA Oncology Approvals**  
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/hematologyoncology-cancer-approvals-safety-notifications  
  *Track recent drug approvals. Great for lesson 25 (clinical trials → approval pipeline).*

## People to Know

### Historical Figures
- **Sidney Farber** — pioneer of chemotherapy (methotrexate for childhood leukemia). Relevant for lesson 12.
- **Janet Rowley** — discovered chromosomal translocations in cancer (Philadelphia chromosome in CML). Relevant for lesson 3.
- **Judah Folkman** — discovered angiogenesis and anti-angiogenic therapy. Relevant for lesson 8.
- **James Allison & Tasuku Honjo** — Nobel Prize for checkpoint inhibitor discovery. Relevant for lesson 16.

### Contemporary Researchers
- **Douglas Hanahan & Robert Weinberg** — authors of "Hallmarks of Cancer" framework. Follow their work.
- **Bert Vogelstein (Johns Hopkins)** — cancer genetics, TP53 research, liquid biopsies. Relevant for lessons 2, 21.
- **Charles Swanton (Francis Crick Institute)** — tumor heterogeneity and evolution. Relevant for lesson 20.
- **Carl June (UPenn)** — CAR-T therapy pioneer. Relevant for lesson 16.
- **Robert Gatenby (Moffitt Cancer Center)** — adaptive therapy and evolutionary oncology. Relevant for lesson 22.
- **Jennifer Doudna & Emmanuelle Charpentier** — CRISPR (future cancer therapy applications). Relevant for future directions.

### Patient Advocates and Communicators
- **Siddhartha Mukherjee** — oncologist, author of "The Emperor of All Maladies." Humanizes cancer science.
- **Emily Whitehead** — first pediatric CAR-T patient. Powerful story for lesson 16.

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

### Cancer and Evolution
- **"Why don't whales get cancer?" — Peto's Paradox**  
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4858318/  
  Elephants have 20 copies of TP53. Connects to lesson 2.

- **Transmissible Cancers in Nature**  
  Tasmanian devil facial tumor disease: https://www.cancerquest.org/patients/cancer-biology/cancer-other-species  
  Contagious cancer cells (not virus). Wild card for lesson 7.

### Cancer and Metabolism
- **The Warburg Effect**  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/nrc2981  
  Cancer cells use glycolysis even with oxygen. Connects to lesson 8-9. Emerging therapeutic target.

### Cancer in Space
- **NASA: Cancer Risk from Cosmic Radiation**  
  https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/elements/space-radiation  
  DNA damage rates in astronauts. Connects to lesson 14 (radiation) or lesson 3 (mutations).

### Cancer and Aging
- **Senolytics and Cancer Prevention**  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41568-019-0213-8  
  Removing senescent cells may prevent cancer. Connects to lesson 5 (why cancer rates rise with age).

### Cancer Drug Repurposing
- **Metformin (diabetes drug) and cancer**  
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5934556/  
  Epidemiological evidence that metformin reduces cancer risk. Connects to lesson 15 (targeted therapy).

### Cancer and Artificial Intelligence
- **Deep Learning for Pathology and Diagnosis**  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-021-01343-4  
  AI outperforming pathologists in some contexts. Connects to lesson 23-24 (diagnostics, precision medicine).

## Final Notes

This resource list is curated for **intermediate-level** students. Prioritize:
1. **NCI (cancer.gov)** — most reliable, comprehensive, free
2. **Khan Academy** — best introductory videos
3. **COSMIC and cBioPortal** — hands-on data exploration
4. **Hanahan & Weinberg paper** — conceptual framework
5. **NCCN guidelines** — clinical decision-making

For each lesson, select 1-2 resources that best match the learning objectives. Don't overwhelm with the full list — curate based on student needs and interests.
