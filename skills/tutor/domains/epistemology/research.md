# Epistemology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Sources of Knowledge
- Perception and empiricism
- Reason and rationalism
- Testimony and social epistemology
- Memory and introspection
- A priori vs. a posteriori knowledge

### 2. The Nature of Knowledge
- Justified true belief (JTB) analysis
- Gettier problems and post-Gettier analyses
- Internalism vs. externalism
- Reliabilism and virtue epistemology
- Foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism

### 3. Skepticism
- Ancient skepticism (Pyrrhonism, Academic skepticism)
- Cartesian skepticism and the demon problem
- External world skepticism
- Skepticism about other minds
- Responses to skepticism (foundationalism, contextualism, etc.)

### 4. Justification and Evidence
- Epistemic justification vs. warrant
- Evidentialism
- Defeaters and undercutting
- Epistemic probability
- Inference to the best explanation

### 5. Truth and Theories of Truth
- Correspondence theory
- Coherence theory
- Pragmatist theory
- Deflationary theories
- Semantic theories (Tarski)

### 6. Contemporary Issues
- Feminist epistemology and standpoint theory
- Social epistemology
- Formal epistemology
- Disagreement and peer disagreement
- Epistemic injustice
- The ethics of belief

## Curriculum Design Notes

For **intermediate** level:
- Assumes some exposure to philosophical thinking and argumentation
- Can handle technical terminology with clear definitions
- Should engage with primary sources (short excerpts)
- Focus on classic problems and contemporary responses
- Build from intuitive cases to formal theories
- Emphasize skill development: argument analysis, counterexample generation, theory evaluation

The curriculum should move from:
1. What is knowledge? (intuitive cases, JTB, Gettier)
2. Sources of knowledge (perception, reason, testimony)
3. Structure of justification (foundationalism, coherentism, etc.)
4. Skeptical challenges and responses
5. Contemporary extensions (social epistemology, formal approaches, applied issues)

Target ~24-26 lessons with strategic review days after major conceptual shifts.
