## Domain Hooks
- **The Münchhausen trilemma** — the three-way choice between infinite regress, circular reasoning, and arbitrary stopping points. Drop this in Lesson 12 when introducing foundationalism to motivate the whole module.

- **Zhuangzi's butterfly dream** — "Am I a man dreaming I'm a butterfly, or a butterfly dreaming I'm a man?" A non-Western skeptical puzzle that's more playful than Cartesian skepticism. Use in Lesson 18 to show skepticism isn't uniquely Western.

- **The Lottery Paradox** — you're justified in believing "my ticket won't win" (it almost certainly won't), and similarly for every ticket, but you know one ticket will win. Can you know a ticket won't win? Connects to statistical evidence and stakes. Drop in Lesson 15 (internalism/externalism) or Lesson 23 (reliabilism).

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Knowledge is just belief that happens to be true"** — Students often miss the justification requirement. Emphasize that lucky guesses aren't knowledge. Use clear cases: a stopped clock showing the right time, a wild conspiracy theory that turns out to be true.

2. **"Truth and justification are the same thing"** — Students conflate having good reasons with being right. Stress that justified beliefs can be false (you have good evidence but are mistaken) and true beliefs can be unjustified (lucky guesses). Truth is about the world; justification is about reasons.

3. **"A priori knowledge means innate knowledge or knowledge before birth"** — Clarify that "independent of experience" means you don't need to go check the world, not that you're born knowing it. Use math examples: you learn arithmetic through experience, but "2 + 2 = 4" is justified a priori.
