# Viking Navigation — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Viking Age chronology** — temporal and geographic scope (793-1066 CE, Scandinavia to North Atlantic)
2. **Ship typology** — longships vs. knarrs, clinker construction, sail technology
3. **Atlantic routes** — Iceland, Greenland, Vinland settlement patterns
4. **Saga literature** — oral tradition, textual transmission, reliability as historical sources
5. **Archaeological evidence** — ship burials, harbor sites, navigation artifacts
6. **Hull speed** — relationship between ship length, speed, and travel time estimation
7. **Coastal piloting** — landmark navigation, soundings, safe harbor identification
8. **Dead reckoning** — speed × time = distance; compass course memory without instruments
9. **Open ocean challenges** — loss of visual reference, error accumulation, psychological factors
10. **Solar altitude** — measuring sun height to determine latitude. Depends on: 9
11. **Sun compass/bearing dial** — Uunartoq disc, directional sun shadow devices. Depends on: 10
12. **Arctic phenomena** — midnight sun, continuous daylight, shadow-based navigation. Depends on: 10
13. **Stellar navigation** — using Polaris and constellations for direction and latitude. Depends on: 10
14. **Environmental cues** — ocean currents, wave patterns, wildlife, ice blink as navigation aids. Depends on: 9
15. **Saga sunstone references** — Hrafns saga and other textual mentions of navigational crystals. Depends on: 4
16. **Light polarization** — Rayleigh scattering creates polarization patterns in sky. Depends on: 10
17. **Birefringence** — double refraction in calcite/Iceland spar crystals. Depends on: 16
18. **Polarization-based solar detection** — finding sun position through clouds using crystal optics. Depends on: 16, 17
19. **Alderney wreck evidence** — 2013 calcite discovery on 16th-century shipwreck route. Depends on: 15, 18
20. **Kennings and directional language** — poetic encoding of navigation knowledge in sagas. Depends on: 4, 15
21. **Cognitive geography** — mental maps, route memory, oral transmission of sailing directions. Depends on: 7, 8
22. **Experimental archaeology** — modern reconstruction voyages testing historical methods. Depends on: 6, 10, 18
23. **Embodied expertise** — integration of multiple sensory and cognitive navigation systems. Depends on: ALL

## Dependencies

### Critical Chains

**Dead Reckoning → Celestial → Integration**
- Dead reckoning (8) must come before celestial navigation (10) because you need to understand basic position tracking before adding astronomical complexity
- Solar navigation (10, 11, 12) forms the foundation for understanding both stellar methods (13) and the sunstone problem (16-19)
- All navigation methods converge at synthesis (23)

**Saga Criticism → Sunstone Evaluation**
- Understanding saga reliability (4) is essential before evaluating sunstone textual evidence (15)
- This critical lens then informs how we assess the Alderney wreck findings (19) and poetic navigation clues (20)

**Optics Foundation → Sunstone Physics**
- Basic solar observation (10) must precede polarization concepts (16)
- Polarization understanding (16) is required for birefringence (17)
- Both (16) and (17) together enable understanding polarization-based solar detection (18)
- This chain represents the steepest conceptual climb in the curriculum

### Bottleneck Concepts

1. **Dead reckoning (8)** — unlocks all subsequent navigation methods; students must grasp this fundamental technique
2. **Solar altitude (10)** — gateway to both stellar navigation and sunstone physics
3. **Light polarization (16)** — bottleneck for the entire sunstone module; physics background varies widely among students
4. **Saga reliability (4)** — critical thinking foundation that affects evaluation of all textual evidence

### Mind-Blowing Moments

- **Lesson 11** — realizing you can determine latitude from shadow length alone
- **Lesson 18** — discovering the sky has an invisible polarization pattern like a compass
- **Lesson 19** — the "aha" moment when calcite birefringence suddenly makes the sun visible through clouds
- **Lesson 23** — synthesis moment recognizing how Vikings integrated dozens of subtle cues into unconscious expertise

## Common Misconceptions

### About ships
- **Misconception**: All Viking ships were longships designed for raids
  - **Reality**: Most Atlantic voyages used knarrs (cargo ships) optimized for stability and cargo capacity

### About compasses
- **Misconception**: Vikings must have had some kind of magnetic compass
  - **Reality**: Magnetic compasses weren't used in European navigation until the 12th century, after the Viking Age

### About sunstones
- **Misconception**: Sunstones were common navigation tools with clear saga evidence
  - **Reality**: Only one saga mentions a sunstone, and no confirmed Viking-era sunstone has been found in a navigation context

### About accuracy
- **Misconception**: Dead reckoning is wildly inaccurate over ocean distances
  - **Reality**: Skilled navigators could maintain ~5% accuracy over multi-day voyages using speed estimation and course memory

### About sagas
- **Misconception**: Sagas are either entirely accurate history or entirely fiction
  - **Reality**: Sagas blend historical events with literary elaboration; truth value varies by passage and must be evaluated critically

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic astronomy** (sun and star movement across sky) — needed for lessons 11-15
- **Geographic coordinate concepts** (latitude/longitude framework) — needed for lesson 11
- **Light and optics basics** (reflection, refraction) — needed for lessons 16-19
- **Critical reading of historical texts** — needed for lessons 4, 15, 20, 22
- **Basic physics intuition** (vectors, forces, waves) — helpful for lessons 7, 14, 16-19

## Extensions and Rabbit Holes

- **Polynesian navigation** — fascinating parallel development of non-instrument ocean navigation
- **Medieval Islamic navigation** — contemporary astronomical techniques and instruments
- **Modern inertial navigation** — how submarines and aircraft navigate without external references
- **Animal navigation** — how birds use magnetic fields and polarized light (connects to lesson 16)
- **Historical metrology** — Viking distance units and time measurement
- **Cognitive archaeology** — reconstructing ancient mindsets and knowledge systems
- **Maritime law and customs** — how navigation expertise conferred social status
- **Climate during Viking Age** — Medieval Warm Period impacts on North Atlantic navigation
