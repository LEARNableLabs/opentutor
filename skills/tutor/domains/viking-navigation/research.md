# Viking Navigation — Research Summary

## Overview

Viking navigation represents a sophisticated intersection of practical seafaring, astronomical observation, material technology, and oral tradition. This topic combines maritime archaeology, historical textual analysis (sagas), experimental archaeology, and physical optics.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Maritime Context
- Viking Age geography and trade routes (ca. 793-1066 CE)
- Ship technology and design (longships vs. knarrs)
- Settlement patterns (Iceland, Greenland, Vinland)
- Archaeological evidence from ship burials and harbor sites

### 2. Navigation Techniques
- **Coastal piloting** — using landmarks, soundings, and wildlife patterns
- **Dead reckoning** — speed estimation, time measurement, course memory
- **Celestial observation** — sun, stars, and horizon phenomena
- **Environmental cues** — ocean currents, wave patterns, ice blink, bird behavior

### 3. The Sunstone Question
- Saga references (Hrafns saga Sveinbjarnarsonar, others)
- Candidate minerals: Iceland spar (calcite), cordierite, tourmaline
- Polarization physics and atmospheric optics
- Experimental archaeology findings (2011-2018 studies)
- The Alderney crystal discovery

### 4. Textual Evidence
- Old Norse sagas as navigation records
- Place-name evidence for routes
- Landnámabók and settlement records
- Limitations and biases in saga interpretation

### 5. Material Culture
- Navigation tools: sunboard/bearing dial, lead lines, wind vanes
- Uunartoq disc (possible bearing dial, 1948 discovery)
- Absence of magnetic compasses (pre-12th century)
- Ship instrumentation debate

## Pedagogical Considerations

This topic works well at intermediate level because:
- **Accessible entry** — Vikings, ships, and sagas are culturally familiar
- **Multidisciplinary richness** — combines history, physics, archaeology, literature
- **Active research** — ongoing debates about sunstones create intellectual excitement
- **Hands-on possibilities** — students can experiment with polarization, sun angles, navigation math
- **Mystery element** — "How did they cross the Atlantic without compasses?" hooks curiosity

Challenges:
- Separating saga romanticism from archaeological evidence
- Understanding polarization optics without advanced physics background
- Dealing with uncertainty (many navigation methods are inferred, not documented)

## Curriculum Structure Rationale

**25 lessons** organized into 5 modules:
1. **Viking World & Sources** (5 lessons) — context and evidence types
2. **Ships & Seafaring** (5 lessons) — technology and routes
3. **Navigation Fundamentals** (6 lessons) — dead reckoning, celestial, environmental
4. **The Sunstone Mystery** (5 lessons) — polarization, crystals, experiments
5. **Sagas & Integration** (4 lessons) — textual evidence and synthesis

Difficulty progression: gentle start (1-2), build through celestial and optical physics (3-4), peak at polarization physics (5), settle at synthesis (2-3).

Review lessons after lessons 6, 13, and 20.
