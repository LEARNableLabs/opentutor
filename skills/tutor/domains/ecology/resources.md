# Ecology and Population Dynamics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Ecology: Concepts and Applications" by Manuel Molles & Anna Sher** (9th edition, 2023)
  - Comprehensive intermediate text with strong applied focus
  - Excellent case studies from diverse ecosystems
  - Good balance of theory and real-world examples
  - Student-friendly without sacrificing depth
  - https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/ecology-concepts-applications-molles-sher/M9781264122615.html

- **"A Primer of Ecology" by Nicholas J. Gotelli** (4th edition, 2008)
  - Mathematical approach to population and community ecology
  - Clear derivations of key models (logistic, Lotka-Volterra, metapopulation)
  - Problem sets with ecological thinking emphasis
  - Best for building quantitative intuition
  - https://www.sinauer.com/a-primer-of-ecology-fourth-edition.html

- **"Ecology: From Individuals to Ecosystems" by Begon, Townsend & Harper** (4th edition, 2006)
  - Classic comprehensive text covering all scales
  - Strong on evolutionary perspectives in ecology
  - Detailed treatment of life history theory
  - May be dense for some intermediate students but excellent reference
  - https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Ecology%3A+From+Individuals+to+Ecosystems%2C+4th+Edition-p-9781405111171

- **"Introduction to Population Ecology" by Larry L. Rockwood** (2nd edition, 2015)
  - Focused treatment of population dynamics
  - Excellent on density dependence, life tables, and demographic models
  - Case studies from wildlife management and conservation
  - https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Introduction+to+Population+Ecology%2C+2nd+Edition-p-9781118947586

- **Khan Academy: Biology > Ecology and the Environment**
  - Accessible video modules on core concepts
  - Good for foundational understanding before diving deeper
  - Covers population ecology, community ecology, ecosystems
  - https://www.khanacademy.org/science/ap-biology/ecology-ap

- **Nature Education Knowledge: Ecology**
  - Peer-reviewed, citable educational modules
  - Written by active researchers
  - Covers current topics and classic concepts
  - Free access, excellent for building on textbook knowledge
  - https://www.nature.com/scitable/knowledge/library/ecology-13228167/

- **SERC (Science Education Resource Center): Teaching Quantitative Skills in the Geosciences**
  - Datasets and activities for hands-on ecological analysis
  - Population growth, biodiversity, nutrient cycling modules
  - Designed for active learning
  - https://serc.carleton.edu/quantskills/index.html

### Interactive Tools and Simulations

- **NetLogo Models Library: Biology**
  - Agent-based models for population dynamics, predator-prey, competition
  - Free, downloadable, runs on any computer
  - Wolf Sheep Predation, Rabbits Grass Weeds, Virus, Cooperation models
  - Students can modify parameters and explore emergent phenomena
  - https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/index.cgi?path=Sample%20Models/Biology

- **EcoLab**
  - Population dynamics simulator for growth models and species interactions
  - Web-based, user-friendly interface
  - Good for exploring parameter sensitivity
  - http://www.ecolab.com.au/

- **PopTools** (Excel add-in)
  - Population modeling tools for matrix models, stochastic growth, PVA
  - Free download, works in Microsoft Excel
  - Useful for conservation-focused students
  - https://www.poptools.org/

- **Virtual Biology Lab: Population Biology**
  - Simulations for population dynamics and evolution
  - Includes data collection and analysis tools
  - https://www.mhhe.com/biosci/genbio/virtual_labs/BL_16/BL_16.html

- **Graphs of Logistic Population Growth** (Duke Math)
  - Interactive phase diagrams for logistic growth
  - Explore continuous vs discrete time models
  - Shows transition to chaos in discrete logistic map
  - https://services.math.duke.edu/education/ccp/materials/diffeq/logistic/logi1.html

## Unexpected Connections (for "wait, what?" moments)

### Ecology ↔ Economics
- **Tragedy of the commons** — Hardin's classic essay applies population growth logic to resource management
- **Discounting and sustainability** — economic time preferences mirror ecological r vs K strategies
- Read: Costanza et al. "The value of the world's ecosystem services" (Nature 1997)

### Ecology ↔ Epidemiology
- **SIR models** for disease spread are mathematically identical to predator-prey models (infected = predators, susceptible = prey)
- **Herd immunity** is a form of density-dependent disease control
- COVID-19 provides real-time examples of logistic growth, flattening curves, R0 (basic reproductive number)
- Read: Anderson & May "Infectious Diseases of Humans" (classic text)

### Ecology ↔ Urban Planning
- **Urban heat islands** affect species distributions and phenology
- **Habitat corridors** in cities (greenways, wildlife crossings) apply metapopulation theory
- **Green infrastructure** mimics ecosystem services (stormwater management, air quality)
- Look up: "Urban ecology" as a growing subdiscipline, Cary Institute's Baltimore Ecosystem Study

### Ecology ↔ Network Science
- **Food webs** are networks — can analyze with graph theory (centrality, modularity, degree distribution)
- **Scale-free networks** and small-world properties appear in ecological systems
- **Robustness** to species loss relates to network topology
- Read: Dunne et al. "Network structure and biodiversity loss in food webs" (Ecology Letters 2002)

### Ecology ↔ Philosophy
- **What is a species?** — ecological niche concept vs phylogenetic concept, implications for conservation
- **Intrinsic vs instrumental value** — should we protect species for their own sake or for human benefit?
- **The wild** vs **the built** — what counts as "natural"? Urban ecosystems, novel ecosystems, rewilding debates
- Read: Emma Marris "Rambunctious Garden: Saving Nature in a Post-Wild World"

### Ecology ↔ Art and Literature
- **Aldo Leopold's "A Sand County Almanac"** — foundational conservation writing, land ethic
- **Rachel Carson's "Silent Spring"** — launched modern environmental movement, documented DDT impacts
- **Annie Dillard's "Pilgrim at Tinker Creek"** — poetic naturalism, deep observation
- **Indigenous storytelling** — many cultures encode ecological knowledge in oral traditions (salmon life cycles, fire ecology, seasonal harvests)

### Ecology ↔ Data Science / Machine Learning
- **Species distribution modeling** uses machine learning (MaxEnt, random forests) to predict habitat suitability
- **Remote sensing** classification algorithms identify land cover, track deforestation
- **Acoustic monitoring** with neural networks identifies species from sound recordings
- **Computer vision** automates camera trap image analysis for wildlife surveys
- Explore: "Deep learning in ecology" literature, eBird data science challenges
