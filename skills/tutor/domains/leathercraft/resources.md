# Leathercraft — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books
- **The Art of Hand Sewing Leather** by Al Stohlman — Comprehensive guide to stitching techniques, essential for pattern construction understanding. Classic reference, suitable for intermediate students building on basic sewing knowledge.
- **Carving Leather** by Al Stohlman — Definitive text on tooling techniques. Covers swivel knife use, beveling, shading, backgrounding with clear photo sequences. Perfect for lessons 1-11.
- **Figure Carving Finesse** by Al Stohlman — Advanced carving techniques including organic forms, shading, and complex compositions. Use for lessons 7-9 when students are ready for artistic tooling.
- **The Leatherworking Handbook** by Valerie Michael — Broad coverage of techniques including dyeing, finishing, and pattern design. Good all-around reference for intermediate students.
- **How to Dye Leather** by Chris Gronski (Weaver Leather) — Focused guide on dye types, application methods, and troubleshooting. Essential for lessons 13-17.

### Courses & Structured Programs
- **Leathercraft Masterclass** (https://leathercraftmasterclass.com/) — Phil Hobson's modular courses cover tooling, construction, and finishing at intermediate-advanced level. Well-structured video lessons with project-based learning.
- **Tandy Leather Library** (https://tandyleather.com/pages/leather-library) — Free downloadable PDFs and video tutorials covering basic through intermediate techniques. Excellent supplement for all lessons.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### YouTube Channels (video demonstrations)
- **Corter Leather** (https://www.youtube.com/c/CorterLeather) — Detailed intermediate-advanced technique videos, especially strong on construction, edge finishing, and dyeing. Calm, methodical presentation style.
- **Ian Atkinson / LeatherHub** (https://www.youtube.com/c/LeatherHub) — Comprehensive beginner-to-intermediate series covering tools, techniques, and projects. Excellent tool reviews and technique comparisons.
- **Chuck Dorsett** (https://www.youtube.com/c/ChuckDorsett) — Focus on carving and tooling, especially Sheridan-style floral work. Great for lessons 7-11 on complex carving.
- **Nigel Armitage** — Artistic tooling and design, European perspective. Good for design inspiration and alternative approaches.

### Interactive Tools
- **Adobe Illustrator / Inkscape** — Vector design software for pattern drafting. Illustrator is industry standard (paid), Inkscape is free and open-source. Essential for lessons 18-22.
- **Fusion 360** — 3D CAD software with free hobbyist license. Useful for designing complex 3D-formed leather pieces and visualizing pattern assembly.
- **Pattern calculators** — Various online tools for box construction, bag dimensions, belt length calculators. Search "leather pattern calculator" for specific project types.

### Communities & Forums
- **r/Leathercraft** (https://www.reddit.com/r/Leathercraft/) — Active Reddit community with daily posts, critique threads, and beginner questions. Excellent for troubleshooting and motivation.
- **Leatherworker.net** (https://leatherworker.net/) — Traditional forum with deep technical discussions, pattern sharing, and tool reviews. More experienced user base than Reddit.
- **Discord servers** — Several leathercraft Discord communities exist with real-time chat and critique. Search for current active servers.

## Unexpected Connections (rabbit holes)

### Cross-discipline Links
- **Calligraphy and lettering** — Swivel knife technique has similarities to broad-edge pen control. Students with calligraphy background often excel at clean line work.
- **Printmaking (relief printing)** — Backgrounded leather carving creates raised designs similar to woodblock prints. Understanding figure-ground relationships translates between disciplines.
- **Color theory (painting)** — Dye gradient and antiquing techniques apply color theory principles. Resources from watercolor painting (wet-on-wet, gradation) transfer well.
- **Mechanical engineering (CAD)** — Pattern design for complex 3D forms uses similar spatial reasoning as product design and mechanical drafting.
- **Bookbinding** — Many bookbinding techniques (edge finishing, decorative tooling, leather covering) overlap with leathercraft. Medieval manuscript covers are masterworks of tooled leather.

### Historical Contexts
- **Medieval guild systems** — Leatherworkers (cordwainers, saddlers, glovers) were separate guilds with distinct techniques and trade secrets.
- **Art Nouveau movement** — Late 1800s / early 1900s saw revival of decorative leather tooling in Arts & Crafts movement. Study tooled book covers from this period.
- **Western American saddle-making** — Sheridan-style carving emerged from practical saddle decoration in Wyoming ranching culture. Rich historical connection.
- **Japanese leather history** — Japan had limited leather tradition due to Buddhist influence, but modern Japanese leathercraft represents highest level of precision and minimalism.

### Material Science
- **Tanning chemistry** — Vegetable tanning vs. chrome tanning involves fascinating organic chemistry. Understanding fiber structure explains tooling behavior.
- **Dye chemistry** — Aniline vs. pigment dyes, mordants, pH effects. Deep dive into colorant science.
- **Biodegradability and sustainability** — Vegetable-tanned leather is biodegradable; modern alternatives (Piñatex, mushroom leather, lab-grown) raise interesting questions about future of the craft.

## Advanced Resources (for students wanting to go deeper)

### Specialized Techniques
- **Leather molding and 3D forming** — Wet molding for masks, armor, sculptural forms
- **Inlay and overlay** — Combining multiple leather layers for decorative effects
- **Airbrushing** — Photorealistic dyeing and gradient control
- **Figure carving** — Animals, portraits, narrative scenes
- **Saddle and tack making** — Functional equestrian equipment (advanced construction)

### Academic & Historical
- **Saddlery and Harness Making** by Edited by Paul N. Hasluck — Historical techniques from early 1900s
- **The Encyclopedia of Rawhide and Leather Braiding** by Bruce Grant — Comprehensive coverage of braiding (specialty area)
- **Museum collections** — Victoria & Albert Museum (London), Metropolitan Museum of Art (New York) have significant historical tooled leather collections. Study for design inspiration.

## Current Trends (as of 2026)

- **Minimalist / Japanese-inspired design** — Movement away from heavily tooled western styles toward clean lines, precise construction, subtle details
- **Sustainability focus** — Interest in vegetable-tanned leather for biodegradability, ethical sourcing, natural dyes
- **Digital pattern design** — Increased use of CAD/vector tools, pattern sharing communities, laser cutting for precision
- **Hybrid techniques** — Combining traditional hand-tooling with modern tools (laser engraving, CNC cutting, 3D-printed hardware)
- **Community learning** — Maker spaces, local guilds, online communities replacing traditional apprenticeships
