# Caving and Vertical Rope Techniques — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Static rope properties** — kernmantle construction, stretch behavior, strength ratings
2. **Natural and artificial anchors** — load-bearing capacity, redundancy, equalization
3. **Personal rigging system** — harness, cow's tails, rack configuration, safety checks
4. **NSS/NFPA standards** — rope specifications, equipment ratings, inspection protocols
5. **Accident analysis and risk assessment** — failure modes, human factors, decision-making
6. **Frog system mechanics** — croll, hand ascender, foot loops, body positioning. Depends on: 1, 3
7. **Ropewalker system** — upper ascender, ergonomics, efficiency trade-offs. Depends on: 6
8. **Ascender cam mechanics** — toothed vs smooth, rope sheath damage, device selection. Depends on: 1, 6
9. **Ascending efficiency** — rest positions, pacing, breathing, mental strategies. Depends on: 6, 7
10. **Mitchell and backup systems** — alternative ascending methods, tight spaces. Depends on: 6, 8
11. **Rappel rack mechanics** — bar configuration, friction control, heat dissipation. Depends on: 3
12. **Rope heating and speed management** — thermal limits, glove failure, locked-off descent. Depends on: 11
13. **Descender comparison** — rack, bobbin, Petzl Stop, auto-locking vs manual. Depends on: 11
14. **Edge protection and abrasion** — rope protectors, deviation rigging, edge geometry. Depends on: 2, 12
15. **Edge transition technique** — cow's tail management, body positioning, commitment. Depends on: 3, 11, 14
16. **On-rope multi-tasking** — survey work, equipment management, field efficiency. Depends on: 12, 15
17. **Changeover technique** — descent-to-ascent, ascent-to-descent, efficiency, safety sequence. Depends on: 6, 11
18. **Rebelay mechanics** — rope-to-rope transfer, anchor loading, fall potential. Depends on: 2, 17
19. **Deviation vs rebelay strategy** — rigging choices, rope management, clean descents. Depends on: 14, 18
20. **Passing knots and obstacles** — prusik loops, ascender repositioning, problem-solving. Depends on: 6, 8
21. **Prusik escape** — self-rescue from jammed ascender, backup prusiks, emergency procedures. Depends on: 8, 20
22. **Haul systems** — mechanical advantage, pulley placement, solo rescue limits. Depends on: 2, 21
23. **Emergency decision-making** — contingency planning, rope sacrifice, risk calculation. Depends on: 5, 21, 22

## Dependencies

### Foundation Layer
- **Rope properties (1)** must be understood before selecting ascending systems (6) or descending devices (11-13)
- **Anchors (2)** are prerequisite to edge protection (14), rebelays (18), and haul systems (22)
- **Personal rigging (3)** is required for both ascending (6) and descending (11) safely
- **Standards (4)** inform all equipment choices and rigging decisions
- **Accident analysis (5)** builds risk awareness needed for emergency decisions (23)

### Ascending Chain
- **Frog system (6)** is the foundation for understanding ropewalker (7), cam mechanics (8), and efficiency (9)
- **Cam mechanics (8)** explains why certain ascenders damage rope and informs passing knots (20)
- **Ascending efficiency (9)** depends on mastering basic frog/ropewalker technique first
- **Alternative systems (10)** build on understanding the standard approaches

### Descending Chain
- **Rack mechanics (11)** must be mastered before understanding speed/heat issues (12)
- **Speed management (12)** is critical for edge work (15) and safe descents
- **Edge protection (14)** requires understanding rope behavior under load (1, 12)
- **Edge transition (15)** combines cow's tail technique (3), rack control (11), and edge awareness (14)

### Integration Layer
- **Changeovers (17)** integrate ascending (6-10) and descending (11-16) skills
- **Rebelay technique (18)** builds on changeover mastery and anchor understanding (2, 17)
- **Deviation strategy (19)** requires understanding both edge work (14) and rebelays (18)
- **Passing obstacles (20)** combines ascender mechanics (8) with problem-solving

### Advanced/Rescue Layer
- **Prusik escape (21)** requires deep understanding of cam mechanics (8) and obstacle navigation (20)
- **Haul systems (22)** depend on anchor knowledge (2) and self-rescue skills (21)
- **Emergency decisions (23)** integrate risk assessment (5), self-rescue (21), and rescue systems (22)

## Bottlenecks

### Critical Prerequisite Concepts
1. **Rope behavior (1)** — if students don't understand static rope properties, they'll make dangerous equipment choices throughout
2. **Anchors (2)** — weak anchor understanding cascades into problems with edge protection, rebelays, and rescue systems
3. **Frog system (6)** — serves as reference point for all ascending variations; must be solid before moving on
4. **Changeovers (17)** — integration point for ascending + descending; common struggle area that blocks advanced work

### Common Sticking Points
- **Cam mechanics (8)** — students often don't grasp why toothed cams damage rope, leading to bad habits
- **Edge transitions (15)** — fear and technique deficits combine; requires mental and physical practice
- **Rebelays (18)** — spatial reasoning challenge; students get confused about which rope to be on when
- **Prusik escape (21)** — emergency technique that's hard to practice safely; often undertrained

## Prerequisite Topics (from outside this curriculum)

- **Basic caving safety** — needed for: all lessons (cave environment awareness)
- **Knot tying** — needed for: anchors (2), backup systems (10, 21), haul systems (22)
- **Basic physics/statics** — needed for: anchor loading (2), cam mechanics (8), haul systems (22)
- **Risk assessment fundamentals** — needed for: accident analysis (5), emergency decisions (23)

## Conceptual Misconceptions (anticipated)

1. **"Dynamic rope is always better because it's safer"** — misunderstands why static rope is essential for ascending
2. **"More friction = safer descent"** — ignores rope heating and control issues
3. **"Ascending is just like climbing a ladder"** — misses the importance of rhythm, breathing, and efficiency
4. **"The rope is the safety system"** — confuses the rope (load path) with actual redundancy in rigging
5. **"You can always climb back up if something goes wrong"** — underestimates fatigue and psychological factors
6. **"European techniques are better/worse than American"** — fails to understand context-dependent tool choice
7. **"Practice makes perfect"** — ignores the importance of practicing *correct* technique vs. reinforcing bad habits
