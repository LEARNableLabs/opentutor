# Caving and Vertical Rope Techniques — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **On Rope: North American Vertical Rope Techniques** (NSS) — The definitive SRT manual. Multiple editions available through NSS bookstore. Covers everything from basic knots to complex rigging. At intermediate level, focus on chapters 5-9 (ascending/descending systems and changeovers). Available at https://caves.org/pub/bnl/bnl.htm

- **Vertical Caving** (NSS Vertical Section publications) — Technical articles, rigging guides, and technique refinements published by the NSS Vertical Section. More advanced and specialized than On Rope. https://caves.org/section/vertical/

- **Alpine Caving Techniques** (Georges Marbach and Bernard Tourte) — European perspective with more alpine-influenced rigging. Good for comparing American vs European approaches. Shows alternative techniques like the Mitchell system and alpine-style rebelays.

- **NSS Safety and Techniques Committee** — Accident reports, safety bulletins, and best practices. Essential reading for understanding what goes wrong and why. https://caves.org/committee/salonsafety/

- **NFPA Standards** — National Fire Protection Association standards for rope (NFPA 1983) and equipment. Technical but important for understanding equipment ratings. https://www.nfpa.org/

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos
- **Petzl Professional YouTube Channel** — High-quality rope access and rescue technique videos. Many techniques apply directly to caving. Search for "Petzl ascending" or "Petzl changeover". https://www.youtube.com/user/PetzlProfessional

- **Bob & Bob Productions** — Classic instructional videos from the 1980s-90s. Dated production but solid technique. "On Rope" video series complements the book.

- **Friars Hole Caving** — Modern caving videos showing vertical systems in real cave contexts. Good for seeing how rigging looks on actual trips. Search YouTube for "Friars Hole vertical".

- **NCRC Training Videos** — National Cave Rescue Commission publishes training materials including vertical rescue scenarios. https://ncrc.us/

### Interactive Tools
- **Rigging Calculators** — Online tools for calculating rope stretch, anchor loads, and mechanical advantage. Search for "rope rigging calculator" or "anchor load calculator".

- **Knot Tying Apps** — AnimatedKnots.com has good caving knot animations (figure-8, bowline, alpine butterfly, prusik).

- **Virtual Rigging Simulators** — Some caving clubs have developed rigging simulation software, though none are widely distributed. Check regional grotto websites.

### Code/Simulations
- **RopeCalc** — Spreadsheet tools for rope stretch and load calculations. Available through some caving forums.

- **Friction Model Simulations** — Physics-based models of descender friction. Academic papers occasionally publish code (search for "rappel friction simulation" on arXiv).

## Tools and Equipment Manufacturers

- **Petzl** — French manufacturer, dominates the market. Full line of ascenders, descenders, harnesses, and rigging gear. Excellent technical documentation. https://www.petzl.com/US/en/Sport

- **CMC Rescue** — American manufacturer specializing in rescue equipment. Makes the classic CMC rappel rack. https://www.cmcrescue.com/

## Organizations and Communities

- **National Speleological Society (NSS)** — Primary caving organization in North America. Publishes standards, runs conventions, maintains grotto network. https://caves.org/

- **NSS Vertical Section** — Specialized section focused on vertical techniques, training, and equipment. Publishes "Nylon Highway" newsletter. https://caves.org/section/vertical/

- **NCRC (National Cave Rescue Commission)** — Training organization for cave rescue. Offers levels 1-4 certification including extensive vertical rescue training. https://ncrc.us/

- **CavingUK** — British caving forums with active technical discussions. Good for European perspective. https://www.cavinguk.co.uk/

- **CaveChat** — Active online caving community with vertical techniques subforum. https://cavechat.org/

## People (influential in vertical caving)

- **Bill Cuddington** — Pioneer of American SRT techniques in the 1960s-70s. Co-author of early editions of "On Rope".

- **Tom Rea** — Lead author of modern "On Rope" editions. NSS Vertical Section veteran.

- **John Cordova** — Cave rescue expert, NCRC instructor, developed many modern rescue techniques.

- **Bruce Smith** — Equipment designer, Petzl consultant, wrote extensively on vertical techniques.

- **Chris Howes** — British caving photographer and author, documented European vertical techniques.

## Cross-Discipline Connections

- **Rope Access Industry (SPRAT/IRATA)** — Industrial rope access workers use nearly identical techniques. SPRAT (Society of Professional Rope Access Technicians) and IRATA (Industrial Rope Access Trade Association) publish excellent training manuals. Many cavers work in rope access. https://sprat.org/ and https://irata.org/

- **Canyoneering** — Descending techniques overlap heavily. Canyoneering resources (books by Tom Jones, ACA training materials) offer complementary perspectives. https://canyoneering.net/

- **Arboriculture** — Tree climbing uses SRT extensively. ISA (International Society of Arboriculture) training materials cover ascending systems in detail. https://www.isa-arbor.com/

- **Rock Climbing Rescue** — Books like "Self-Rescue" by David Fasulo cover prusik escapes, hauls, and other techniques applicable to caving. https://www.mountaineersbooks.org/

- **Military Rope Work** — Special operations units train extensively in rope techniques. FM 3-97.61 (Military Mountaineering) is publicly available and covers many relevant skills.

## Unexpected Connections (wild cards)

- **Biomechanics of Cycling** — Research on pedaling efficiency, hip angle, and power transfer applies directly to ascending system optimization. Search for "cycling biomechanics hip angle" for insights into foot loop positioning.

- **Sailing Rigging** — Traditional sailing rigging theory (block and tackle, mechanical advantage, line management) informs caving hauling systems. "The Complete Rigger's Apprentice" by Brion Toss is excellent.

- **Theatrical Rigging** — Stage riggers solve similar problems (safely suspending humans, changeovers, fall protection). OSHA theater rigging guidelines have useful insights.

- **Astronaut Training** — NASA EVA (spacewalk) procedures include extensive rope/tether work. The psychological aspects of working in exposed, high-consequence environments are similar. Search for "NASA EVA training" for videos.

- **Historical Rope Techniques** — Medieval siege warfare, maritime rope work, and mountaineering history show the evolution of rope techniques. "The Ashley Book of Knots" (1944) is a classic reference.

- **Ergonomics Research** — Industrial ergonomics studies on repetitive motion, load distribution, and fatigue management apply to long ascents. Search for "industrial ergonomics load carrying" for research papers.

## Regional Grotto Resources

Many local caving clubs (grottos) offer vertical training courses and mentorship. Find your local grotto at https://caves.org/committee/i-o/grottos/grottos.shtml. Notable grottos with strong vertical programs:

- **Vertical Section Grottos** — NSS Vertical Section maintains a list of grottos with active vertical training
- **Pittsburgh Grotto** — Regular vertical training in TAG (Tennessee-Alabama-Georgia) region
- **Texas Speleological Survey** — Active vertical caving in deep Texas pits
- **Mid-Atlantic grottos** — Strong vertical tradition in West Virginia caves

## Courses and Certifications

- **NCRC Levels 1-4** — Progressive cave rescue training with extensive vertical components. Level 3+ includes technical rescue rigging. https://ncrc.us/

- **NSS Convention Vertical Workshops** — Annual workshops at NSS Convention, hands-on instruction from experts

- **Regional Vertical Training** — Many grottos offer weekend vertical training courses (frog systems, changeovers, rescue basics)

- **SPRAT/IRATA Certification** — While focused on industrial rope access, Level 1-3 certifications provide excellent foundational skills applicable to caving

- **Outward Bound / NOLS** — Outdoor leadership schools occasionally include caving/vertical modules in wilderness courses

## Academic Papers and Technical Literature

- **American Alpine Journal** — Occasionally publishes rope technique articles applicable to caving
- **International Journal of Speleology** — Academic caving journal, sometimes covers equipment and techniques
- **SPRAT Journal** — Rope access industry journal, highly technical rigging articles
- **Nylon Highway** — NSS Vertical Section newsletter, practical technique articles
