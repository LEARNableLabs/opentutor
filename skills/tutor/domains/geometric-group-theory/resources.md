# Geometric Group Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **A Course on Geometric Group Theory** by Brian H. Bowditch
  - Self-contained introduction, perfect for intermediate level
  - Focus on hyperbolic groups and foundations
  - Freely available PDF: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/280-2009/bhb-ggtcourse.pdf
  - **Use for:** Lessons 2-5, 10-11, 14, 16-17

- **Geometric Group Theory** by Cornelia Druțu and Michael Kapovich
  - Comprehensive graduate-level reference
  - Encyclopedic coverage, excellent for deep dives
  - Freely available PDF: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/ggt.pdf
  - **Use for:** Lessons 3, 7, 11, 16-17, 19-21, 27-28

- **Topics in Geometric Group Theory** by Pierre de la Harpe
  - University of Chicago Press, 2000
  - Thematic approach, good for examples and applications
  - **Use for:** Module 6 (examples)

- **Metric Spaces of Non-positive Curvature** by Martin Bridson and André Haefliger
  - Springer, 1999
  - Definitive reference for CAT(0) geometry
  - **Use for:** Lessons 19-21

- **Office Hours with a Geometric Group Theorist** edited by Matt Clay and Dan Margalit
  - Princeton University Press, 2017
  - Accessible essays on key topics and open problems
  - **Use for:** Motivation and rabbit holes

- **Group Explorer**
  - Free software for drawing Cayley diagrams
  - Visualize group structure, test properties
  - http://groupexplorer.sourceforge.net
  - **Use for:** Lessons 4-5, 8-9 (hands-on exploration of Cayley graphs)

- **GAP (Groups, Algorithms, Programming)**
  - Open-source computational group theory software

  - https://www.gap-system.org

- **SageMath**

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Computer Science
- **Word problem and computational complexity** — connections to formal languages, Turing machines
- **Automatic groups and regular languages** — finite automata recognize group elements
- **Rewriting systems** — relate to GGT via Dehn's algorithm
- **Cryptography** — braid groups, hyperbolic groups used in cryptographic protocols

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Dynamics
- **Boundaries and ergodic theory** — measure theory on Gromov boundaries
- **Random walks on groups** — probability theory meets geometry
- **Group actions on manifolds** — Zimmer program, rigidity

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Number Theory
- **Hyperbolic 3-manifold volumes** — relate to Bloch group, L-functions
- **Lattices in Lie groups** — arithmetic groups have geometric properties
- **Expander graphs** — connect to Ramanujan graphs, spectral gaps

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Logic
- **Model theory of free groups** — Sela's work on Tarski's problems
- **First-order theory** — which groups have decidable first-order theory?

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Quantum Computing
- **Topological quantum field theory** — mapping class groups act on Hilbert spaces
- **Quantum error correction** — uses group theory and topology

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Biology
- **Protein folding** — symmetry groups of molecular structures
- **Phylogenetic trees** — tree structures model evolutionary relationships

### Geometric Group Theory ↔ Art and Design
- **Escher's tessellations** — hyperbolic plane tilings, triangle groups
- **Architectural symmetry** — wallpaper groups, crystallographic groups

## Recommended Reading Path

### For Intermediate Students (this curriculum level)

**Start here:**
1. Bowditch, *A Course on Geometric Group Theory* (Chapters 1-5)
2. McCammond's online resources for supplementary materials
3. Use Group Explorer to visualize examples as you read

**Then explore:**
4. Druțu-Kapovich, *Geometric Group Theory* (selected sections on topics of interest)
5. Bridson-Haefliger, *Metric Spaces of Non-positive Curvature* (Chapters I-II for CAT(0))

**For curiosity and inspiration:**
6. Clay-Margalit, *Office Hours with a Geometric Group Theorist*
7. Expository papers by Bridson, Farb, Bestvina on specific topics

### Beyond This Curriculum (Next Steps)

- **Measured group theory** — L² cohomology, amenability, property (T)
- **Geometric finiteness** — JSJ decomposition, Cannon-Thurston maps
- **Rigidity theory** — Mostow rigidity, superrigidity, quasi-isometric rigidity
- **Boundaries in detail** — topology, dynamics, measure theory on ∂G
- **Mapping class groups** — GGT applied to surface automorphisms
- **Out(Fₙ)** — automorphisms of free groups, analog of mapping class groups
- **Random groups** — probabilistic methods in GGT

## How to Use These Resources

1. **Primary reading:** Start with Bowditch for core lessons, supplement with Druțu-Kapovich for depth
2. **Computational practice:** Use Group Explorer and GAP for every module — don't just read, compute
3. **Visual learning:** GeoGebra for hyperbolic geometry, YouTube for motivation and intuition
4. **Curiosity-driven:** When a lesson sparks interest, follow the topic-specific recommendations
5. **Community:** Join MathOverflow, read blogs (Terence Tao, Gowers, etc.) for modern perspectives
6. **Papers:** When ready, read survey papers by contemporary leaders — they're more accessible than original research

## Staying Current

- **arXiv.org** — preprint server, search "geometric group theory" or "GR" (Group Theory) subject
- **MathSciNet reviews** — summaries of recent papers
- **AMS Notices** — expository articles, "What Is..." series
- **Blogs:** Low Dimensional Topology blog, Terence Tao's blog (occasionally covers GGT)
- **Conferences:** GAGTA, Groups Geometry and Dynamics, MSRI/ICERM workshops post videos online

## Final Note

Geometric group theory is a young field (1980s onward) with active research and open problems. Don't just consume resources passively — compute examples, draw pictures, ask "what if?" questions, and explore computational tools. The best learning happens when you try to verify claims yourself before reading proofs.
