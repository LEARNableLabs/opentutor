# Geometric Group Theory — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Group Presentations and Word Problem**
   - Free groups, generators and relations
   - Dehn's fundamental problems (word, conjugacy, isomorphism)
   - Decidability and algorithms

2. **Group Actions on Geometric Spaces**
   - Group actions on trees (Bass-Serre theory)
   - Actions on metric spaces
   - Proper and cocompact actions
   - Fundamental domains

3. **Cayley Graphs and Geometric Invariants**
   - Cayley graphs as geometric objects
   - Quasi-isometry and geometric properties
   - Growth functions and growth rates
   - Ends of groups

4. **Hyperbolic Groups**
   - Gromov hyperbolicity
   - Thin triangles and δ-hyperbolic spaces
   - Boundary at infinity
   - Hyperbolic groups vs. hyperbolic spaces

5. **Non-positive Curvature**
   - CAT(0) spaces and cube complexes
   - Metric spaces of non-positive curvature
   - Flat torus theorem
   - Geometric structures on groups

6. **Examples and Applications**
   - Baumslag-Solitar groups
   - Lamplighter groups
   - Thompson groups
   - Surface groups and Fuchsian groups
   - Triangle groups and reflection groups
   - Coxeter groups

7. **Advanced Topics**
   - Automatic groups
   - Geometric finiteness
   - Rigidity theorems
   - Connections to topology and low-dimensional geometry

## Key Sources

### Primary Textbooks

- **A Course on Geometric Group Theory** by Brian H. Bowditch
  - Self-contained introduction, excellent for establishing foundations
  - Focus on hyperbolic groups
  - Available: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/280-2009/bhb-ggtcourse.pdf

- **Geometric Group Theory** by Cornelia Druțu and Michael Kapovich
  - Comprehensive graduate-level reference
  - Encyclopedic coverage of the field
  - Available: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/ggt.pdf

- **Topics in Geometric Group Theory** by Pierre de la Harpe
  - University of Chicago Press, 2000

- **Metric Spaces of Non-positive Curvature** by Martin Bridson and André Haefliger

## Available Educational Materials

### Prerequisites Typically Assumed
- Basic group theory (subgroups, quotients, homomorphisms)
- Fundamental group and covering spaces from algebraic topology
- Basic metric space topology
- Some exposure to graph theory

### Pedagogical Approaches in Literature

Most intermediate courses follow this progression:
1. Start with concrete examples (free groups, surface groups)
2. Build geometric intuition through Cayley graphs
3. Introduce quasi-isometry as the key equivalence relation
4. Study specific classes (trees, hyperbolic groups, CAT(0))
5. Apply to classical problems (word problem, rigidity)

Visual and computational tools (Group Explorer, GAP) are emphasized for building intuition before formal arguments.
