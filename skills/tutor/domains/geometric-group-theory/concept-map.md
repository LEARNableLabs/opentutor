# Geometric Group Theory — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Group presentations** — expressing groups via generators and relations
2. **Free groups** — groups with no relations, building blocks for all presentations. Depends on: group presentations
3. **Word problem** — determining if two words represent the same element. Depends on: group presentations
4. **Cayley graphs** — visualizing groups as metric spaces with edges for generators. Depends on: group presentations
5. **Quasi-isometry** — equivalence relation identifying groups with the same large-scale geometry. Depends on: Cayley graphs
6. **Growth functions** — measuring how fast balls grow in Cayley graphs. Depends on: Cayley graphs, quasi-isometry
7. **Ends of groups** — counting "directions to infinity" in Cayley graphs. Depends on: Cayley graphs
8. **Group actions on trees** — understanding group structure through tree actions. Depends on: Cayley graphs, free groups
9. **Bass-Serre theory** — decomposing groups via graphs of groups. Depends on: group actions on trees
10. **Baumslag-Solitar groups** — concrete examples of HNN extensions. Depends on: Bass-Serre theory
11. **δ-hyperbolic spaces** — metric spaces where geodesic triangles are thin. Depends on: Cayley graphs
12. **Hyperbolic plane** — model space for negative curvature. Depends on: δ-hyperbolic spaces
13. **Hyperbolic groups** — groups with hyperbolic Cayley graphs. Depends on: δ-hyperbolic spaces, quasi-isometry
14. **Dehn's algorithm** — fast solution to word problem in hyperbolic groups. Depends on: hyperbolic groups, word problem
15. **Gromov boundary** — compactification at infinity for hyperbolic spaces. Depends on: hyperbolic groups
16. **CAT(0) spaces** — metric spaces with non-positive curvature via comparison geometry. Depends on: hyperbolic plane
17. **CAT(0) cube complexes** — combinatorial CAT(0) spaces built from cubes. Depends on: CAT(0) spaces
18. **Flat torus theorem** — structure of abelian subgroups in CAT(0) groups. Depends on: CAT(0) spaces
19. **Surface groups** — fundamental groups of surfaces, classical hyperbolic examples. Depends on: hyperbolic groups
20. **Fuchsian groups** — discrete subgroups of isometries of hyperbolic plane. Depends on: surface groups, hyperbolic plane
21. **Triangle groups** — groups generated by reflections in triangle sides. Depends on: Fuchsian groups
22. **Coxeter groups** — abstract reflection groups. Depends on: triangle groups
23. **Thompson groups** — groups of piecewise-linear homeomorphisms. Depends on: group presentations
24. **Lamplighter groups** — wreath product examples with interesting geometry. Depends on: Cayley graphs, growth functions
25. **Automatic groups** — groups with regular language word recognition. Depends on: word problem, Cayley graphs
26. **3-manifold groups** — fundamental groups with geometric structures. Depends on: hyperbolic groups, CAT(0) spaces

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies

- **Cayley graphs** require understanding **group presentations** because they encode generators as edges and relations as cycles
- **Quasi-isometry** builds on **Cayley graphs** because we need a metric structure to define coarse equivalence
- **Word problem** depends on **group presentations** because we need to understand how relations allow rewriting

### Geometric Action Dependencies

- **Group actions on trees** extend **Cayley graphs** to general tree structures beyond free groups
- **Bass-Serre theory** requires **group actions on trees** to decompose groups via quotient graphs
- **Baumslag-Solitar groups** illustrate **Bass-Serre theory** as concrete HNN extension examples

### Hyperbolic Dependencies

- **Hyperbolic groups** depend on both **δ-hyperbolic spaces** (the metric condition) and **quasi-isometry** (invariance under change of generators)
- **Gromov boundary** requires **hyperbolic groups** because non-hyperbolic spaces may not have well-defined boundaries
- **Dehn's algorithm** uses **hyperbolic groups** structure to solve the **word problem** efficiently via thin triangles

### CAT(0) Dependencies

- **CAT(0) spaces** generalize **hyperbolic spaces** to non-positive (rather than negative) curvature
- **CAT(0) cube complexes** provide combinatorial models of **CAT(0) spaces** using hyperplane structures
- **Flat torus theorem** uses **CAT(0) spaces** to constrain abelian subgroups via splitting geometric factors

### Example Dependencies

- **Surface groups** are the primary natural examples of **hyperbolic groups** (for genus ≥ 2)
- **Fuchsian groups** combine **surface groups** with **hyperbolic plane** via discrete isometry actions
- **Triangle groups** are special **Fuchsian groups** generated by reflections
- **Coxeter groups** abstract **triangle groups** to higher dimensions and general reflection patterns
- **Automatic groups** depend on **Cayley graphs** and the **word problem** but provide algorithmic efficiency
- **3-manifold groups** synthesize **hyperbolic groups** and **CAT(0) spaces** via Thurston's geometrization

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Quasi-isometry (Lesson 7)
This is the **central concept** of geometric group theory. Students must internalize that geometric properties are those preserved by quasi-isometry. Without this, the entire field appears arbitrary. Everything from growth functions to hyperbolicity depends on understanding that "same large-scale geometry" is the right equivalence relation.

### δ-hyperbolic spaces (Lesson 14)
The thin triangle condition is both geometric and algebraic. Students struggle to see why this definition captures "negative curvature" intuitively. Spend extra time on examples (trees are 0-hyperbolic, hyperbolic plane is δ-hyperbolic) before introducing hyperbolic groups.

### Bass-Serre theory (Lesson 11)
This is technically demanding and requires comfort with both group actions and graph theory. It's a significant difficulty spike. Students need to see why decomposing groups via tree actions is powerful before diving into technical details.

### Gromov boundary (Lesson 17)
Requires topological sophistication. Students need to understand both the metric structure (Gromov product) and the topology (compactification). This is where geometric group theory touches analysis and topology deeply.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Cayley graphs depend on the generating set"** — TRUE, but quasi-isometry class does NOT depend on the choice of finite generating set. The geometry is invariant even though the graph changes.

2. **"Hyperbolic groups act on hyperbolic spaces"** — BACKWARDS. Hyperbolic groups are groups whose Cayley graph is a hyperbolic metric space. They don't need to act on anything external.

3. **"CAT(0) is stronger than hyperbolicity"** — Neither implies the other! Hyperbolic = negative curvature bounded away from 0. CAT(0) = non-positive curvature. The Euclidean plane is CAT(0) but not hyperbolic.

4. **"Free groups are trivial examples"** — Free groups are FUNDAMENTAL. They act on trees, they're hyperbolic, they solve the word problem trivially, and every group is a quotient of a free group.

5. **"The word problem is about algorithms"** — It's deeper: whether an algorithm EXISTS (decidability). Some groups have solvable word problem, some don't. This is a geometric property!

6. **"Geometric properties are about visualization"** — Geometry here means metric properties preserved by quasi-isometry. It's rigorous, not just pictures (though pictures help immensely).

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Group theory basics** — needed for group presentations, actions, quotients (all concepts)
- **Fundamental group** — needed for surface groups, Fuchsian groups, 3-manifold groups
- **Covering spaces** — needed for understanding group actions geometrically
- **Metric space topology** — needed for Cayley graphs, quasi-isometry, hyperbolicity, CAT(0)
- **Graph theory basics** — needed for Cayley graphs, trees, Bass-Serre theory

## Learning Clusters

Concepts naturally cluster into these sub-units:

**Cluster 1: Presentations and Graphs** (Lessons 1-6)
- Group presentations, free groups, word problem, Cayley graphs
- Foundation for everything else

**Cluster 2: Coarse Geometry** (Lessons 7-9)
- Quasi-isometry, growth functions, ends
- The "right" equivalence relation for geometric group theory

**Cluster 3: Trees** (Lessons 10-13)
- Tree actions, Bass-Serre theory, Baumslag-Solitar groups
- Decomposition and structural results

**Cluster 4: Hyperbolicity** (Lessons 14-18)
- δ-hyperbolic spaces, hyperbolic plane, hyperbolic groups, Dehn's algorithm, boundary
- The star example class

**Cluster 5: Non-positive Curvature** (Lessons 19-21)
- CAT(0) spaces, cube complexes, flat torus theorem
- Weaker than hyperbolicity, still very geometric

**Cluster 6: Examples** (Lessons 22-28)
- Surface groups, triangle groups, Coxeter groups, Thompson groups, lamplighter groups, automatic groups, 3-manifolds
- Concrete instantiations and connections

## Key Conceptual Transitions

- **Lesson 7 (quasi-isometry)** — shift from "groups have many Cayley graphs" to "groups have one coarse geometry"
- **Lesson 11 (Bass-Serre theory)** — shift from "groups act" to "actions reveal internal structure"
- **Lesson 14 (hyperbolicity)** — shift from "metric spaces have curvature" to "thin triangles = negative curvature"
- **Lesson 16 (hyperbolic groups)** — shift from "geometric definition" to "algebraic consequences" (Dehn's algorithm, finite presentation)
- **Lesson 19 (CAT(0))** — shift from "negative curvature" to "non-positive curvature" and understanding the difference
