# Translation Studies — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Essential Textbooks

- **Jeremy Munday, Sara Ramos Pinto, Jacob Blakesley — "Introducing Translation Studies: Theories and Applications" (4th ed., Routledge)**  
  The definitive introductory textbook used on courses worldwide. Covers theoretical paradigms, key concepts, and case studies across genres. Includes extensive online resources: quizzes, flashcards, video introductions, curated journal articles, weblinks, PowerPoint slides.  
  https://www.amazon.com/Introducing-Translation-Studies-Theories-Applications/dp/1032516569  
  https://routledgetextbooks.com/textbooks/9780367370510/

- **Brian James Baer & Christopher D. Mellinger — "Translating Texts: An Introductory Coursebook on Translation and Text Formation"**  
  Practical coursebook focusing on translation process and text types. Strong on applied methods.  
  Available on Routledge Translation Studies Portal.

- **Lawrence Venuti — "The Translator's Invisibility: A History of Translation" (2nd ed.)**  
  Seminal work on translator visibility, domestication vs. foreignization, and the politics of translation. Essential for understanding ethics and ideology discussions.

- **Gideon Toury — "Descriptive Translation Studies and Beyond" (revised ed.)**  
  Foundational text for DTS, norms, and the target-oriented approach. Dense but essential for theoretical grounding.

- **Mona Baker — "In Other Words: A Coursebook on Translation" (3rd ed.)**  
  Practical guide to translation at word, phrase, grammar, and text levels. Excellent for applied linguistic analysis.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

- **Translation and cognitive science** — How do bilinguals process translation? Eye-tracking and keystroke logging studies reveal cognitive load and decision-making processes.

- **Translation and game localization** — Video game translation combines technical, creative, and cultural challenges. Growing industry with unique constraints (text expansion, UI limits, cultural taboos, humor localization).

- **Translation and law** — Legal translation (contracts, patents, court proceedings) has life-or-death consequences. Fascinating case studies of mistranslations causing legal disputes.

- **Translation and music** — Translating song lyrics, opera libretti, musical theater. Constraints of melody, rhythm, rhyme, and singability.

- **Translation and comics/manga** — Visual-verbal interplay, onomatopoeia, text in speech bubbles, right-to-left vs. left-to-right reading direction.

- **Translation and accessibility** — Subtitles for the deaf/hard-of-hearing (SDH), audio description for the blind, easy-to-read translations. How accessibility needs reshape translation priorities.

- **Translation and global activism** — How translation enables or hinders social movements. Translation of protest slogans, activist texts, international solidarity campaigns.

- **Translation and machine learning** — How neural MT works under the hood (attention mechanisms, transformers, BLEU scores). The data hunger of MT systems and ethical implications of training data.

## Quality Standards and Frameworks

- **ISO 17100:2015** — International standard for translation services (requirements for core processes, resources, and quality management)

- **EN 15038** — European quality standard for translation services (superseded by ISO 17100 but still referenced)

- **MQM (Multidimensional Quality Metrics)** — Error typology framework for translation quality assessment

- **DQF (Dynamic Quality Framework)** — Industry-led quality benchmarking initiative

- **TAUS Quality Dashboard** — Collaborative platform for translation quality metrics
