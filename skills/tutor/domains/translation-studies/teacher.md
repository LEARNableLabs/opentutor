## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Translation is a mechanical transfer of meaning"**  
   Why students get this wrong: Monolingual students or those with only basic language learning experience often view translation as decoding one language and encoding another, missing the cultural, pragmatic, and rhetorical dimensions.  
   How to correct it: Introduce untranslatability examples early (lesson 4). Use famous translation debates (e.g., how to translate "you" in French, gendered language, cultural-specific humor) to show that meaning is negotiated, not transferred.

2. **"There is one correct translation"**  
   Why students get this wrong: Prescriptive language education and the desire for clear answers make students seek "the right translation."  
   How to correct it: Lesson 2 directly addresses this by showing how norms and contexts shape choices. Use comparative analysis (lesson 11) where students see multiple respected translations of the same text making different valid choices.
