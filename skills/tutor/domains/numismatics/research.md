# Numismatics Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Fundamentals of Numismatic Analysis
- Coin terminology and anatomy (obverse, reverse, legend, field, exergue)
- Basic classification systems
- Handling and documentation practices
- Digital imaging and photography

### 2. Dating and Attribution
- Stylistic analysis and typology
- Die studies and die linkage
- Overstrikes and countermarks
- Hoard evidence and find contexts
- Mint marks and magistrate names

### 3. Iconography and Political Messaging
- Portrait types and propaganda
- Religious and mythological symbols
- Commemorative issues
- Imperial succession and legitimacy
- Local vs. imperial iconography

### 4. Metallurgy and Production
- Minting technologies (struck, cast, pressed)
- Metal composition and debasement
- Die production and metallurgy
- Ancient vs. modern forgery detection
- X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analysis

### 5. Coins as Historical Evidence
- Economic history (trade routes, inflation, monetary policy)
- Political history (succession, civil wars, usurpation)
- Social history (language distribution, cultural exchange)
- Archaeological context
- Chronological frameworks

### 6. Regional Traditions
- Greek coinages (city-states, kingdoms, federations)
- Roman Republican and Imperial
- Persian and Near Eastern
- Celtic and Northern European
- Medieval and Islamic numismatics

## Pedagogical Approach for Intermediate Level

At intermediate level, students should:
- Move beyond simple identification to analytical interpretation
- Understand methods for dating, attribution, and authentication
- Apply numismatic evidence to historical questions
- Critically evaluate sources and context
- Integrate multiple lines of evidence (iconography, metallurgy, find context)
- Develop independent research skills with databases and collections

The curriculum emphasizes **coins as historical evidence** rather than collecting or connoisseurship, focusing on how numismatists extract historical information from monetary artifacts.
