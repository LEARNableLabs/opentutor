# Numismatics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Coin anatomy and terminology** — obverse, reverse, legend, field, exergue, die, denomination
2. **Monetary vs non-monetary objects** — what qualifies as currency vs medals, tokens, weights
3. **Documentation standards** — how to describe, photograph, and catalog coins professionally
4. **Digital numismatic databases** — OCRE, CoinArchives, museum collections, search strategies
5. **Ancient monetary systems** — denominations, purchasing power, circulation contexts
6. **Stylistic analysis** — artistic periods, die cutting styles, regional variations. Depends on: 1, 3
7. **Epigraphic evidence** — mint marks, magistrate names, legends, dating inscriptions. Depends on: 1
8. **Die studies** — identifying die linkages, production sequences, workshop patterns. Depends on: 1, 6
9. **Overstrikes and countermarks** — evidence of reuse, chronological implications. Depends on: 1, 7
10. **Political propaganda** — portrait types, legitimacy claims, messaging on coinage. Depends on: 1, 2
11. **Religious iconography** — gods, myths, symbols, divine associations. Depends on: 1, 10
12. **Commemorative issues** — historical events, victories, building programs on coins. Depends on: 10, 11
13. **Succession iconography** — civil wars, usurpation, competing claimants. Depends on: 10, 12
14. **Minting technology** — striking, casting, die production methods. Depends on: 1
15. **Metal composition and debasement** — fineness, alloy, fiscal policy, inflation. Depends on: 14
16. **XRF and metallurgical analysis** — non-destructive testing, composition analysis. Depends on: 15
17. **Forgery detection** — authentication techniques, ancient vs modern fakes. Depends on: 6, 8, 14, 16
18. **Hoard evidence** — find contexts, distribution patterns, burial dates. Depends on: 4, 5, 7
19. **Trade networks** — coin distribution, economic zones, GIS mapping. Depends on: 18
20. **Economic history from coins** — inflation, monetary crises, policy changes. Depends on: 5, 15, 18
21. **Cultural exchange** — iconographic borrowing, language spread, cross-cultural influence. Depends on: 11, 19
22. **Historical methodology** — integrating numismatic evidence with archaeology, texts, material culture. Depends on: all previous

## Dependencies

### Foundational Skills (Lessons 1-5)
- **Documentation** requires understanding **coin anatomy** because you must use precise terminology
- **Database searching** requires knowing **terminology** and **monetary systems** to construct effective queries
- **Monetary systems** builds on understanding **coin anatomy** to interpret denominations and relationships

### Attribution and Dating (Lessons 6-10)
- **Die studies** require understanding **stylistic analysis** because die linkage depends on recognizing artistic hands and workshop patterns
- **Overstrikes** depend on **epigraphic evidence** because the relationship between undertype and overtype inscriptions establishes chronology
- **Stylistic analysis** is the foundation for all dating methods, providing relative chronologies
- **Epigraphic evidence** (mint marks, names) gives absolute dates that anchor stylistic sequences

### Iconography (Lessons 11-15)
- **Religious iconography** builds on **political propaganda** because divine associations reinforce ruler legitimacy
- **Succession iconography** requires understanding both **political propaganda** and **commemorative issues** to decode civil war messaging
- **Commemorative issues** combine **political** and **religious iconography** to celebrate specific events

### Production and Authentication (Lessons 16-19)
- **Metal composition** depends on understanding **minting technology** because production methods constrain alloy possibilities
- **XRF analysis** requires knowledge of **metal composition** to interpret spectroscopic results
- **Forgery detection** synthesizes **stylistic analysis**, **die studies**, **minting technology**, and **metallurgy** to spot anachronisms

### Historical Evidence (Lessons 20-25)
- **Trade networks** build on **hoard evidence** because distribution patterns emerge from aggregate find data
- **Economic history** integrates **monetary systems**, **metal composition**, and **hoard evidence** to track fiscal policy
- **Cultural exchange** uses **religious iconography** and **trade networks** to trace cross-cultural influences
- **Historical methodology** synthesizes ALL techniques to build historical arguments

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Bottleneck 1: Coin Anatomy and Terminology (Lesson 1-2)
Without mastering basic terminology, students cannot:
- Describe coins accurately
- Search databases effectively
- Read scholarly literature
- Communicate with other numismatists

**Resolution strategy**: Heavy practice with visual identification, database searching, and description writing in early lessons.

### Bottleneck 2: Stylistic Analysis (Lesson 7)
Stylistic dating is subjective and requires visual training. Students often struggle to see subtle die-cutting differences.

**Resolution strategy**: Comparative exercises with dated sequences, side-by-side die comparisons, and focus on diagnostic features before holistic style.

### Bottleneck 3: Integrating Multiple Evidence Types (Lessons 20-25)
Students tend to focus on one line of evidence (e.g., only iconography) and neglect others. Historical arguments require synthesis.

**Resolution strategy**: Case studies that explicitly require combining style, metallurgy, iconography, and find context. Teach-back exercises that force articulation of multi-faceted arguments.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Coins always tell the truth about history"** — Students must learn that coins are propaganda sources with biases and gaps. Address in Lesson 11 (political messaging) and revisit in Lesson 24 (source criticism).

2. **"Dating is precise"** — Many students expect calendar-year precision when most coins have 5-20 year date ranges. Address in Lesson 7 (stylistic analysis) and Lesson 9 (die studies).

3. **"All ancient coins are valuable/rare"** — Common types were mass-produced. Address in Lesson 5 (monetary systems) and Lesson 21 (hoards).

4. **"Iconography is universal"** — Symbols had different meanings in different cultures and periods. Address in Lesson 12 (religious iconography) and Lesson 23 (cultural exchange).

5. **"Debasement always equals crisis"** — Metal composition changes had complex causes (silver sources, policy, technology). Address in Lesson 17 (debasement) and Lesson 22 (economic history).

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic world history** — needed for chronology, political context (all lessons)
- **Ancient civilizations** — needed for cultural context, understanding polities (Lessons 5, 10-15, 21-25)
- **Archaeological methods** — needed for understanding find context, stratigraphy, hoards (Lessons 4, 18-21)
- **Basic metallurgy concepts** — helpful for understanding alloys, testing methods (Lessons 15-19)

## Extension Pathways

After completing this curriculum, students can branch into:

1. **Regional specializations** — Greek, Roman, Islamic, Medieval, Byzantine numismatics
2. **Methodological depth** — advanced die studies, statistical hoard analysis, metallurgical chemistry
3. **Historical applications** — economic history, political history, art history using numismatic evidence
4. **Museum/collection work** — cataloging, conservation, exhibition design
5. **Digital humanities** — database design, computational numismatics, network analysis of coin circulation
