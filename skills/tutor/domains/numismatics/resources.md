# Numismatics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Foundational Reading

- **Howgego, C. *Ancient History from Coins* (Routledge, 1995)** — The standard intermediate text. Clearly written, focuses on coins as historical evidence rather than collecting. Perfect match for this curriculum's approach. Covers Greek and Roman periods.

- **Burnett, A. *Coinage in the Roman World* (Seaby, 1987)** — Comprehensive overview of Roman monetary systems, iconography, and production. Good for Modules 4-5.

- **Grierson, P. *Numismatics* (Oxford, 1975)** — Classic introduction to numismatic methodology. Dense but authoritative. Good reference for die studies and attribution methods.

- **Metcalf, W. (ed.) *The Oxford Handbook of Greek and Roman Coinage* (Oxford, 2012)** — Advanced reference with chapters by leading scholars. Use for deep dives on specific topics.

### Online Courses and Structured Learning

- **Yale Online Numismatics Resources** (https://guides.library.yale.edu/numismatics) — Curated research guides, bibliography, methodology resources.

- **American Numismatic Society Education Programs** (https://numismatics.org/learn/) — Introductory tutorials, terminology guides, and educational materials.

- **British Museum Coin Collection** (https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/subjects/coins-and-medals) — Educational materials, curator essays, and thematic collections.

## Digital Collections and Databases

### Essential Research Tools

- **Online Coins of the Roman Empire (OCRE)** (http://numismatics.org/ocre/) — Searchable database of Roman Imperial coinage with high-quality images, die studies, and metallurgical data. Essential for Modules 2-5. Free and comprehensive.

- **American Numismatic Society Digital Library** (https://numismatics.org/digitallibrary/) — Full-text access to classic numismatic references, journals, and monographs. Historical scholarship and methodology.

- **CoinArchives** (https://www.coinarchives.com/) — Aggregated auction catalogs with prices, images, and descriptions. Good for seeing condition variation and market values. Subscription for full access, but basic search is free.

- **British Museum Collection Online** (https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection) — Over 1 million objects including extensive coin holdings. High-resolution images, provenance data, scholarly descriptions.

- **Portable Antiquities Scheme (PAS)** (https://finds.org.uk/) — Database of archaeological finds in England and Wales, including thousands of coins with find-spot data. Essential for Lesson 21 (hoards and distribution).

### Specialized Databases

- **Coinage of the Roman Republic Online (CRRO)** (http://numismatics.org/crro/) — Companion to OCRE for Republican period. Die studies and iconographic analysis.

- **Dumbarton Oaks Byzantine Coin Database** (https://www.doaks.org/resources/online-exhibits/byzantine-coins) — Byzantine numismatics with excellent iconographic detail.

- **Sylloge Nummorum Graecorum** (various institutions) — Standardized publications of Greek coin collections. Check ANS, British Museum, and other institutional SNGs online.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

- **Physics and Numismatics**: Particle accelerators for non-destructive coin analysis; X-ray tomography revealing internal structure of plated forgeries. Check CERN and Fraunhofer Institute research.

- **Network Science**: Graph theory applied to coin circulation patterns; die-linkage networks as social networks. Connects to computer science and complexity theory.

- **Forensics**: Same techniques used for art forgery detection apply to coins — provenance analysis, materials science, historical context. FBI Art Crime Team methods.

- **Economics**: Modern monetary policy debates echo ancient debasement controversies. Compare Roman 3rd-century crisis to modern inflation debates or cryptocurrency "debasement" through forks.

- **Climate Science**: Tree-ring data and ice cores reveal silver mining peaks and pollution, correlating with coin production. Climate archaeology meets numismatics.

- **Machine Learning**: Coin attribution via computer vision; automated die matching; forgery detection through ML classification. Connect to AI/CV courses.

- **Anthropology**: Gift exchange, prestige economies, and non-monetary coin uses (burial goods, votives, medallions). Economic anthropology perspective.

- **Political Science**: Propaganda theory applied to ancient coins; modern "signaling" in political communication has ancient parallels.

### Weird and Wonderful Examples

- **Pot-bellied Pigs Coinage** (Chinese spade money) — Non-circular "coins" shaped like tools. Challenges definition of coin.

- **Rai Stones of Yap** — 12-foot limestone "coins" that never moved. Ownership changed via social consensus. Blockchain before blockchain.

- **Siege Money** — Desperate coinages during military sieges using church bells, roof lead, or playing cards. Example: Leiden 1574, Mafeking 1900.

- **Coin Hoards as Time Capsules** — Pompeii and Herculaneum coins frozen in AD 79; shipwreck hoards preserving exact trade cargo.

- **Ancient Vending Machines** — Hero of Alexandria's holy water dispenser (1st c. AD) used 5-drachma coins. Connects to engineering history.

- **Gresham's Law in Action** — "Bad money drives out good." Track silver flight during debasement periods; silver hoarding in Lesson 17.

- **Coins in Space** — Apollo 11 carried a silver Eisenhower dollar prototype. Modern continuation of coin-as-symbol tradition.

## Tools for Numismatic Research

### Photography and Documentation
- **Macro photography guides** — Coin photography requires specific lighting (raking light for relief). Check ANS documentation standards.
- **RTI (Reflectance Transformation Imaging)** — Captures surface detail better than photos. Used for die studies.
- **Image stacking software** — Helicon Focus or Zerene Stacker for extended depth of field

### Analysis Software
- **ImageJ / Fiji** — Free image analysis software for measuring, comparing coins
- **Die Study Tools** — Specialized software for die linkage analysis
- **QGIS** — GIS software for mapping finds (free, open-source)
- **R or Python** — Statistical analysis of hoard data, metallurgy

### Reference Materials
- **Abbreviation guides** — Numismatic literature uses extensive abbreviations (RIC, BMC, BMCRE, etc.)
- **Online catalogs** — CoinProject, WildWinds, acsearch.info for quick reference images
- **Metallurgical databases** — Published XRF datasets for comparison

## Ethical Considerations

### Looting, Collecting, and the Antiquities Market

- **UNESCO 1970 Convention** — Post-1970 unprovenanced coins are ethically problematic
- **ACCG vs archaeological community** — Ongoing debates about collecting ancient coins
- **Portable Antiquities Scheme model** — How legal frameworks can balance collecting and scholarship
- **Readings**: 
  - Brodie, N. *The Destruction and Plunder of Archaeological Heritage* (2002)
  - Renfrew, C. *Loot, Legitimacy and Ownership* (2000)

**Teaching approach**: Present multiple perspectives. Many students enter numismatics through collecting; don't alienate, but raise awareness of ethical issues. Emphasize that **museum and database study is now as valuable as physical access**.

## Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

**Starting a coin ID?**
1. Metal? (gold/silver/bronze)
2. Size and weight estimate
3. Obverse portrait/image
4. Reverse image
5. Legend reading (partially is OK)
6. Search OCRE, British Museum, or CoinArchives with those terms

**Can't read the legend?**
- Try reading backwards (some legends run counterclockwise)
- Check for abbreviations (IMP = Imperator, AVG = Augustus, etc.)
- Look for regnal numbers (TRIB POT XV = 15th tribunician power)

**Dating a coin?**
- Portrait style (early, mature, late in reign)
- Titles in legend (Caesar vs Augustus vs Imperator order matters)
- Reverse types often commemorative (can date to specific events)
- Mint marks for provincial issues

**Stuck on iconography?**
- Check Theoi.com for mythological identifications
- ANS collections often have iconographic descriptions
- Compare to other coins with same reverse type

**Need metallurgical data?**
- Roman Economy Project has published XRF datasets
- ANS collection records sometimes include analysis
- Journal articles on specific hoards often publish data
