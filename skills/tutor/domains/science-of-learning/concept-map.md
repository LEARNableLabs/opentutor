# Science of Learning — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Memory systems** — working memory, long-term memory, semantic vs episodic
2. **Encoding vs retrieval** — getting information in vs getting it out
3. **Forgetting curve** — Ebbinghaus's law of forgetting over time
4. **Metacognition** — thinking about thinking; awareness of cognitive processes
5. **Cognitive load** — limited capacity of working memory
6. **Chunking** — grouping information to overcome working memory limits
7. **Retrieval practice** — testing yourself strengthens memory more than rereading. Depends on: encoding, retrieval
8. **Testing effect** — phenomenon where retrieval strengthens memory traces. Depends on: retrieval practice
9. **Active recall** — deliberately retrieving information from memory. Depends on: retrieval practice
10. **Spaced repetition** — distributing practice over time. Depends on: forgetting curve
11. **Distributed practice** — spacing out study sessions rather than massing. Depends on: spaced repetition
12. **Interleaving** — mixing practice on different topics. Depends on: retrieval practice, discrimination
13. **Blocking** — practicing one topic at a time (less effective than interleaving)
14. **Elaboration** — connecting new information to existing knowledge. Depends on: encoding, long-term memory
15. **Self-explanation** — explaining material to yourself in your own words. Depends on: elaboration
16. **Dual coding** — combining visual and verbal information. Depends on: encoding, working memory
17. **Concrete examples** — grounding abstract concepts in specific instances. Depends on: elaboration
18. **Desirable difficulties** — challenges that slow learning but boost retention. Depends on: retrieval practice, generation effect
19. **Generation effect** — self-generated information is remembered better. Depends on: active recall, elaboration
20. **Judgments of learning (JOL)** — predicting future recall. Depends on: metacognition
21. **Illusions of competence** — mistaking familiarity for mastery. Depends on: metacognition, fluency
22. **Fluency** — ease of processing that can mislead about learning
23. **Recognition vs recall** — identifying vs generating; recall is harder but more diagnostic. Depends on: retrieval
24. **Dunning-Kruger effect** — low performers overestimate competence. Depends on: metacognition
25. **Calibration** — accuracy of confidence judgments. Depends on: judgments of learning, feedback
26. **Self-regulated learning (SRL)** — managing your own learning process. Depends on: metacognition
27. **Goal setting** — defining achievable learning targets. Depends on: self-regulated learning
28. **SMART goals** — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound. Depends on: goal setting
29. **Study planning** — allocating time and strategies. Depends on: self-regulated learning, time management
30. **Strategy selection** — choosing appropriate learning techniques. Depends on: metacognition, self-regulated learning
31. **Self-monitoring** — tracking learning progress. Depends on: metacognition, self-regulated learning
32. **Formative assessment** — ongoing checks for understanding. Depends on: self-monitoring, retrieval practice
33. **Help-seeking** — knowing when and how to get assistance. Depends on: self-monitoring, metacognition
34. **Transfer** — applying learning to new contexts. Depends on: elaboration, mental models
35. **Near vs far transfer** — transfer to similar vs dissimilar contexts. Depends on: transfer
36. **Mental models** — structured knowledge representations. Depends on: elaboration, chunking
37. **Expertise development** — progression from novice to expert. Depends on: deliberate practice, mental models
38. **Deliberate practice** — focused, effortful practice with feedback. Depends on: self-regulated learning, retrieval practice

## Dependencies

### Foundational Layer
- **Metacognition** is the foundation for self-regulated learning, monitoring, and strategy selection
- **Memory systems** understanding is prerequisite for all learning strategies
- **Encoding vs retrieval** distinction explains why certain strategies work

### Strategy Layer
- **Retrieval practice** requires understanding that retrieval strengthens memory more than re-encoding
- **Spaced repetition** builds on the forgetting curve — we space reviews to catch memories before they fade
- **Interleaving** leverages retrieval practice plus discrimination between similar concepts
- **Elaboration** requires connecting to long-term memory structures
- **Dual coding** works because of how working memory processes verbal and visual channels

### Monitoring Layer
- **Judgments of learning** depend on metacognitive awareness
- **Illusions of competence** arise when fluency is mistaken for deep learning
- **Calibration** improves through feedback and self-testing (retrieval practice)
- **Dunning-Kruger effect** is a failure of metacognitive monitoring at low skill levels

### Self-Regulation Layer
- **Strategy selection** requires knowing the strategies (retrieval, spacing, etc.) AND metacognitive monitoring
- **Study planning** integrates goal-setting, time management, and strategy knowledge
- **Self-monitoring** uses formative assessment (often retrieval-based)
- **Help-seeking** depends on accurate self-monitoring and calibration

### Advanced Layer
- **Transfer** requires building robust mental models through elaboration and varied practice
- **Expertise** develops through deliberate practice (which includes spaced, retrieval-based, varied practice)

## Key Bottlenecks

### 1. Metacognitive Awareness
**Why it's a bottleneck:** Without metacognition, students can't monitor learning, detect illusions of competence, or regulate strategies. It underpins everything in modules 3-5.

**Prerequisites:** Basic self-awareness, willingness to reflect

**Taught in:** Lessons 2, 4, 13

### 2. Retrieval Practice
**Why it's a bottleneck:** Most powerful learning strategy, but counterintuitive. Students resist testing themselves because it feels harder than passive review.

**Prerequisites:** Understanding memory systems, encoding vs retrieval

**Taught in:** Lessons 5, 7, 8

**Blocks:** Spaced repetition, interleaving, calibration, formative assessment

### 3. Illusions of Competence
**Why it's a bottleneck:** Students who can't detect fluency-based illusions will keep using ineffective strategies (rereading, highlighting) and think they're working.

**Prerequisites:** Metacognition, understanding of recognition vs recall

**Taught in:** Lessons 14, 15, 17

**Blocks:** Calibration, accurate self-monitoring, effective strategy selection

### 4. Self-Regulated Learning Framework
**Why it's a bottleneck:** Knowing strategies isn't enough — students need to plan, monitor, and adapt.

**Prerequisites:** Metacognition, strategy knowledge, goal-setting

**Taught in:** Lessons 18-23

**Blocks:** Sustained learning, transfer, expertise development

## Common Misconception Clusters

### Cluster 1: Passive Review is Effective
- Misconceptions: rereading = learning, highlighting = encoding, familiarity = mastery
- Corrected by: retrieval practice lessons, illusions of competence

### Cluster 2: Intelligence is Fixed
- Misconceptions: "I'm just bad at this," talent > effort, learning is innate
- Corrected by: desirable difficulties, expertise development, deliberate practice

### Cluster 3: Comfort = Learning
- Misconceptions: easy = effective, struggle = failure, fast = good
- Corrected by: desirable difficulties, generation effect, productive struggle

### Cluster 4: More Time = More Learning
- Misconceptions: hours studied = learning, massed practice is efficient
- Corrected by: spaced repetition, strategy quality > quantity, interleaving

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic study experience** — needed for: recognizing current habits, experimenting with new strategies
- **Note-taking familiarity** — needed for: elaboration, self-explanation, dual coding strategies
- **Willingness to experiment** — needed for: trying counterintuitive strategies like retrieval practice and interleaving
- **No advanced prerequisites** — this is foundational material about learning itself

## Concept Progression Notes

### Early (Lessons 1-10)
Focus on WHAT and WHY — introduce memory systems, then evidence-based strategies. Build intuition through concrete examples. Students should be trying strategies immediately.

### Middle (Lessons 11-17)
Focus on MONITORING — now that students know strategies, teach them to detect when they're fooling themselves. Illusions of competence are the critical insight.

### Late (Lessons 18-27)
Focus on REGULATION — integrate everything into self-directed learning systems. Transfer from "learning these strategies" to "becoming a learning scientist."

### Difficulty Peaks
- Lesson 13 (judgments of learning) — meta-level thinking is hard
- Lesson 19 (study planning) — synthesis and application
- Lessons 25-26 (transfer and expertise) — abstract, long-term thinking
