# Science of Learning — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books

- **"Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning"** by Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III, and Mark A. McDaniel (2014)
  - **What it covers:** Retrieval practice, spaced repetition, interleaving, desirable difficulties, illusions of competence
  - **Why it's good for intermediate learners:** Highly readable, balances research and storytelling, immediately practical. Perfect level for this curriculum.
  - **URL:** https://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674729018

- **"Understanding How We Learn: A Visual Guide"** by Yana Weinstein and Megan Sumeracki (2018)
  - **What it covers:** Six evidence-based learning strategies (retrieval, spacing, interleaving, elaboration, concrete examples, dual coding)
  - **Why it's good for intermediate learners:** Written by The Learning Scientists, visual format, concise explanations, research-backed
  - **URL:** https://www.routledge.com/Understanding-How-We-Learn-A-Visual-Guide/Weinstein-Sumeracki/p/book/9781138561724

- **"How Learning Works: Seven Research-Based Principles for Smart Teaching"** by Susan A. Ambrose et al. (2010)
  - **What it covers:** Prior knowledge, organization, motivation, practice, feedback, metacognition, goal-directed practice
  - **Why it's good for intermediate learners:** Research-based, excellent for understanding *why* strategies work, teaching-focused but applicable to self-learning
  - **URL:** https://www.wiley.com/en-us/How+Learning+Works%3A+Seven+Research+Based+Principles+for+Smart+Teaching-p-9780470484104

- **"A Mind for Numbers: How to Excel at Math and Science"** by Barbara Oakley (2014)
  - **What it covers:** Focused and diffuse thinking, chunking, procrastination, memory techniques, testing strategies
  - **Why it's good for intermediate learners:** Written by the "Learning How to Learn" instructor, STEM-focused but broadly applicable
  - **URL:** https://barbaraoakley.com/books/a-mind-for-numbers/

### Research Papers (Open Access)

- **"Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology"** by John Dunlosky et al. (2013)
  - **What it covers:** Rankings of 10 learning techniques based on research evidence (retrieval practice and distributed practice are top-rated)
  - **URL:** https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/courses/dunloskyimprovinglearning.pdf

- **"Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention"** by Henry L. Roediger III and Jeffrey D. Karpicke (2006)
  - **What it covers:** Seminal research on the testing effect — retrieval practice beats restudying
  - **URL:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1876761/

- **"Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning"** by Elizabeth L. Bjork and Robert A. Bjork (2011)
  - **What it covers:** Desirable difficulties framework — challenges that hurt short-term performance but boost long-term retention
  - **URL:** https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

- **"Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review"** by Nicholas C. Soderstrom and Robert A. Bjork (2015)
  - **What it covers:** Distinction between performance (test tomorrow) and learning (retention long-term)
  - **URL:** https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

### Courses

- **"Learning How to Learn: Powerful Mental Tools to Help You Master Tough Subjects"** — Barbara Oakley and Terrence Sejnowski (Coursera)
  - **What it covers:** Focused/diffuse modes, chunking, procrastination, memory techniques, learning strategies

- **"The Science of Learning"** — Various institutions (edX, Coursera, MIT OpenCourseWare)

### Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

- **Memory Sports and Memory Palaces**
  - World Memory Championships, method of loci techniques
  - **URL:** https://www.worldmemorychampionships.com/
  - **Connection:** Ancient mnemonic techniques that leverage spatial memory, dual coding, and elaboration

- **Spaced Repetition in Language Learning (Duolingo, Memrise)**
  - Mass-market apps using learning science
  - **URL:** https://www.duolingo.com/, https://www.memrise.com/
  - **Connection:** Real-world application of spacing and retrieval at scale

- **Learning Science in Medical Education**
  - High-stakes domain that heavily uses retrieval practice, spaced repetition
  - **URL (example):** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6296555/
  - **Connection:** Shows how professionals use these strategies in expert domains

- **AI Tutoring and Personalized Learning**
  - Khan Academy, Duolingo, adaptive learning platforms
  - **Connection:** Bloom's 2-sigma problem — can technology replicate one-on-one tutoring gains?

- **Expertise Research (Chess, Music, Sports)**
  - Anders Ericsson's deliberate practice research
  - **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-expertise-and-expert-performance/
  - **Connection:** How experts build mental representations through practice — applies to *learning* expertise too

- **The Replication Crisis and Growth Mindset**
  - Recent replication studies on Carol Dweck's mindset research
  - **URL (example):** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y
  - **Connection:** Teaches critical thinking about learning science research itself

## Advanced Deep Dives (for rabbit holes)

- **"The Cambridge Handbook of Cognition and Education"** edited by John Dunlosky and Katherine A. Rawson (2019)
  - Comprehensive academic overview, excellent for advanced learners
  - **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-cognition-and-education/

- **"Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise"** by Anders Ericsson and Robert Pool (2016)
  - Deliberate practice, expertise acquisition, critique of "10,000 hour rule"
  - **URL:** https://www.harpercollins.com/products/peak-anders-ericssonrobert-pool

- **"Ultralearning: Master Hard Skills, Outsmart the Competition, and Accelerate Your Career"** by Scott Young (2019)
  - Self-directed intensive learning, meta-learning strategies
  - **URL:** https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/ultralearning/

- **Cognitive Load Theory Literature**
  - John Sweller's work on working memory limits and instructional design
  - **URL (intro):** https://www.instructionaldesign.org/theories/cognitive-load/

- **Transfer of Learning Research**
  - When and why learning transfers to new contexts
  - **URL (example paper):** Search "Barnett & Ceci (2002) When and where do we apply what we learn?"
