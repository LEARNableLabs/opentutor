# Behavioral Ecology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Davies, Krebs & West: "An Introduction to Behavioural Ecology" (4th edition, 2012)** — The standard textbook. Comprehensive, well-illustrated, accessible. Covers all core topics with excellent examples. Good for intermediate students; balances theory and empiricism. Available at university libraries and as e-book. [Wiley-Blackwell catalog page](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/An+Introduction+to+Behavioural+Ecology%2C+4th+Edition-p-9781405114165)

- **Alcock & Rubenstein: "Animal Behavior" (11th edition, 2019)** — More natural history-focused than Davies et al., with vivid case studies and beautiful photography. Emphasizes evolutionary perspective. Good complement to theory-heavy treatment. [Sinauer/Oxford catalog](https://global.oup.com/ushe/product/animal-behavior-9780878939664)

- **Dugatkin: "Principles of Animal Behavior" (4th edition, 2020)** — Integrates neural, hormonal, and developmental mechanisms with evolutionary analysis. Good for students interested in proximate-ultimate integration. [University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/P/bo45478344.html)

- **Westneat & Fox (eds): "Evolutionary Behavioral Ecology" (2010)** — Advanced textbook with chapters by leading researchers. Each chapter covers one topic in depth. Good reference for diving deeper on specific areas. [Oxford Scholarship Online](https://academic.oup.com/book/9355)

- **MIT OCW: 9.20 Animal Behaviour (Spring 2014)** — Full course with lecture notes, readings, and assignments by Prof. Thomas W. Polger. Covers Tinbergen's questions, foraging, mating, social behavior. [OCW course page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-20-animal-behavior-spring-2014/)

- **Stanford: Evolution and Ecology of Animal Behavior (BIO 117)** — Syllabus and reading list often available via department. Covers game theory, sexual selection, cooperation. Look for materials from Prof. Joan Roughgarden or similar faculty.

## Supplementary Resources

### Videos and Documentaries

- **BBC Natural History Unit documentaries** — "Life" series, "Planet Earth", "The Hunt", "Dynasties" — stunning footage with behavioral ecology narration. Available on BBC iPlayer, Netflix, or DVD. Use clips to illustrate concepts (e.g., "The Hunt" for predator-prey dynamics, "Dynasties" for social behavior).

- **Cornell Lab of Ornithology: Bird Academy courses** — "The Basics of Bird Behavior" and other courses with video, quizzes, and expert instruction. [Bird Academy](https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/)

- **Howard Hughes Medical Institute BioInteractive** — Short films on stickleback evolution, rock pocket mouse adaptation, Grants' finch research. Excellent for showing evolution in action. [BioInteractive video library](https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/video)

- **TEDx Talks on behavioral ecology** — Search for talks by researchers like Marlene Zuk (sexual selection), Jennifer Verdolin (prairie dogs), or Meg Crofoot (collective behavior). Variable quality but accessible entry points.

- **YouTube channels:**
  - **Animal Behavior - Michael D. Breed** — Course lectures from University of Colorado
  - **Ben Garrod** — British evolutionary biologist with engaging short videos on sexual selection, cooperation
  - **PBS Eons** — Evolutionary history including behavior (e.g., "How Did Flowers Evolve?", social insect evolution)

### Interactive Tools and Simulations

- **EvoBeaker (SimBio)** — Virtual labs for sexual selection (stickleback fish), foraging, life history evolution. Requires subscription but widely used in university courses. [SimBio EvoBeaker](https://simbio.com/evobeaker)

- **NetLogo Models Library** — Free agent-based models of foraging, flocking, cooperation, evolutionary games. Download NetLogo and explore Ecology and Evolution models. [NetLogo Models Library](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/)

- **Evolutionary Game Theory applet** — Interactive hawk-dove game, prisoner's dilemma, and other game theory models. Adjust payoffs and see ESS. [Available from various university websites, e.g., U Washington]

- **Optimal Foraging Simulation (Shodor Foundation)** — Simple interactive for exploring diet choice and patch use. [Shodor Interactivate](http://www.shodor.org/interactivate/)

- **Bird ID and behavior apps** — Merlin (Cornell), eBird — for observing real animal behavior. Encourage students to watch local birds and connect to theory.

### Code and Data

- **R packages for behavioral ecology:**
  - `adehabitatHR` — home range and habitat selection analysis
  - `move` — animal movement data analysis
  - `asnipe` — animal social network analysis
  - `lme4` — mixed models for repeated measures behavioral data

- **Movebank** — Global database of animal movement data (GPS tracking). Explore real datasets of migration, foraging, territoriality. [movebank.org](https://www.movebank.org/)

- **Animal Behaviour journal data archive** — Many papers deposit data in Dryad or Figshare. Search for datasets on foraging, mate choice, social networks to do secondary analyses.

- **GitHub repos:**
  - Search "optimal foraging" or "evolutionary game theory" for educational simulations in Python or R
  - Many professors post course materials on GitHub (check behavioral ecology syllabi for links)

### Primary Literature (Key Papers)

Students at intermediate level can start reading classic papers and recent reviews:

- **Tinbergen, N. (1963). "On aims and methods of ethology."** *Zeitschrift für Tierpsychologie* 20:410-433. — The foundational paper on four questions. [Available via JSTOR or university library]

- **Maynard Smith, J. & Price, G.R. (1973). "The logic of animal conflict."** *Nature* 246:15-18. — Introduces ESS and hawk-dove game. Highly readable. [Nature archive](https://www.nature.com/articles/246015a0)

- **Hamilton, W.D. (1964). "The genetical evolution of social behaviour I & II."** *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 7:1-52. — The original kin selection papers. Challenging but essential. [ScienceDirect]

- **Trivers, R.L. (1972). "Parental investment and sexual selection."** In *Sexual Selection and the Descent of Man* (B. Campbell, ed.). — Clear exposition of parental investment theory. [Available in most university libraries]

- **Charnov, E.L. (1976). "Optimal foraging, the marginal value theorem."** *Theoretical Population Biology* 9:129-136. — The classic marginal value theorem paper. Mathematical but conceptually clear. [Elsevier]

- **Zahavi, A. (1975). "Mate selection—a selection for a handicap."** *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 53:205-214. — The handicap principle. Controversial initially but now widely accepted. [ScienceDirect]

- **Grafen, A. (1990). "Biological signals as handicaps."** *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 144:517-546. — Mathematical proof that handicaps work. Advanced but influential. [ScienceDirect]

- **Bateson, M. & Kacelnik, A. (1996). "Preferences for fixed and variable food sources: variability in amount and delay."** *Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior* 63:313-329. — Empirical test of risk sensitivity. [Wiley Online Library]

### Journals (Open Access Options)

- *Animal Behaviour* — flagship journal, mix of empirical and theoretical
- *Behavioral Ecology* — ISBE journal, strong on theory
- *Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology* — broad scope, publishes a lot
- *Ethology* — European tradition, mix of classical ethology and modern BE
- *Evolution* — publishes behavioral work with evolutionary focus
- *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* — high-impact, often open access behavioral papers
- *PLOS ONE*, *PeerJ*, *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* — fully open access options for recent work

Look for special issues and reviews in these journals for synthesis papers.

## Unexpected Connections (for engagement)

- **Behavioral economics and animal decision-making** — Concepts like loss aversion, reference dependence, and framing effects appear in animal choice behavior. Kahneman & Tversky's prospect theory has animal parallels.

- **Robotics and swarm intelligence** — Engineers use ant algorithms and bee decision-making to design robot swarms. Behavioral ecology informs artificial intelligence.

- **Human behavior** — Evolutionary psychology, cultural evolution, and behavioral ecology share tools and concepts. Parent-offspring conflict applies to weaning, sibling rivalry, adolescence.

- **Plant behavior** — Yes, plants make decisions (where to grow roots, when to flower, how to allocate resources). Optimal foraging logic applies to root placement, MVT applies to pollen foraging by plants (via pollinators).

- **Microbes** — Even bacteria show social behavior: quorum sensing, biofilm formation, division of labor. Game theory applies to bacterial warfare and cooperation.

- **Sports analytics** — Game theory models from behavioral ecology (e.g., penalty kicks as a mixed-strategy ESS) apply to sports strategy.

## Curated Topic Pathways

If students want to specialize or go deeper on specific areas:

- **Foraging and decision-making** → Read Stephens & Krebs (1986) *Foraging Theory*; explore neuroscience of decision-making; connect to optimal control theory in engineering

- **Sexual selection** → Read Andersson (1994) *Sexual Selection*; explore sensory ecology and mate choice; connect to evolutionary genetics and speciation

- **Cooperation and social evolution** → Read Dugatkin (1997) *Cooperation Among Animals*; explore social networks and multilevel selection; connect to human cooperation and institutions

- **Communication** → Read Bradbury & Vehrencamp (2011) *Principles of Animal Communication*; explore sensory ecology and neuroethology; connect to linguistics and semiotics

- **Applied behavioral ecology** → Read Berger-Tal & Saltz (2016) *Conservation Behavior*; explore wildlife management and human-wildlife conflict; connect to environmental policy
