# Behavioral Ecology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Tinbergen's four questions** — framework for analyzing behavior at four levels: mechanism, ontogeny, adaptive value, phylogeny
2. **Proximate vs ultimate causation** — how behavior works vs why it evolved
3. **Levels of analysis** — mechanistic, developmental, functional, evolutionary perspectives on the same behavior
4. **Decision rules** — simple behavioral algorithms that produce adaptive outcomes. Depends on: Tinbergen's four questions
5. **Heuristics** — fast, frugal decision mechanisms that work well in natural environments. Depends on: decision rules
6. **Optimization models** — mathematical frameworks for predicting adaptive behavior. Depends on: proximate vs ultimate causation
7. **Fitness currency** — the metric (energy, offspring, survival) used to evaluate behavioral outcomes. Depends on: optimization models
8. **Tradeoffs** — constraints that prevent simultaneous maximization of all fitness components. Depends on: fitness currency
9. **Optimal diet model** — when to accept or reject prey items based on profitability. Depends on: optimization models, tradeoffs
10. **Prey profitability** — energy gain per handling time. Depends on: optimal diet model
11. **Marginal value theorem** — predicts optimal time to leave a depleting patch. Depends on: optimization models, diminishing returns
12. **Patch residence time** — how long to stay in a resource patch. Depends on: marginal value theorem
13. **Risk sensitivity** — preference for variance in reward depending on energetic state. Depends on: optimal diet model, tradeoffs
14. **Energy budget rule** — risk-averse when well-fed, risk-prone when starving. Depends on: risk sensitivity
15. **Sexual selection** — evolution driven by competition for mates rather than survival. Depends on: optimization models
16. **Anisogamy** — asymmetry in gamete size that drives sex differences in behavior. Depends on: sexual selection
17. **Parental investment** — resources devoted to offspring at cost to future reproduction. Depends on: anisogamy, tradeoffs
18. **Costly signals** — signals maintained as honest because they impose a fitness cost. Depends on: sexual selection
19. **Handicap principle** — only high-quality individuals can afford to signal. Depends on: costly signals
20. **Honest signaling** — signals reliably indicate quality or intention. Depends on: handicap principle
21. **Cryptic female choice** — post-copulatory mate choice mechanisms. Depends on: sexual selection, parental investment
22. **Sperm competition** — competition between ejaculates of different males. Depends on: cryptic female choice
23. **Parent-offspring conflict** — genetic asymmetries create divergent optima for parents and offspring. Depends on: parental investment, inclusive fitness
24. **Clutch size** — optimal number of offspring per reproductive bout. Depends on: parental investment, tradeoffs
25. **Life history strategies** — integrated suites of traits affecting reproduction and survival. Depends on: clutch size, tradeoffs
26. **r vs K selection** — selection for fast reproduction in unstable environments vs competitive ability in stable ones. Depends on: life history strategies
27. **Altruism** — behavior that benefits others at a cost to actor's direct fitness. Depends on: fitness currency
28. **Kin selection** — selection favoring behaviors that help genetic relatives. Depends on: altruism
29. **Hamilton's rule** — altruism evolves when rb > c (relatedness × benefit > cost). Depends on: kin selection
30. **Inclusive fitness** — direct fitness plus effects on relatives weighted by relatedness. Depends on: Hamilton's rule
31. **Reciprocal altruism** — cooperation among unrelated individuals maintained by reciprocation. Depends on: altruism
32. **Tit-for-tat** — strategy of cooperating then copying partner's last move. Depends on: reciprocal altruism
33. **Cheater detection** — mechanisms to identify and punish non-reciprocators. Depends on: reciprocal altruism
34. **Group living costs** — increased competition, disease, conspicuousness. Depends on: tradeoffs
35. **Dilution effect** — individual predation risk decreases with group size. Depends on: group living costs
36. **Collective vigilance** — group members share predator detection duty. Depends on: group living costs
37. **Game theory** — mathematical analysis of strategic interactions. Depends on: optimization models
38. **Hawk-dove game** — model of contest behavior with two pure strategies. Depends on: game theory
39. **Evolutionary stable strategy (ESS)** — strategy that resists invasion by alternative strategies. Depends on: hawk-dove game
40. **Frequency dependence** — fitness of a strategy depends on its frequency in the population. Depends on: ESS
41. **Contest behavior** — competitive interactions over resources. Depends on: game theory, tradeoffs
42. **Assessment** — evaluating opponent's fighting ability or resource value. Depends on: contest behavior
43. **Signal honesty** — reliability of signals in communication. Depends on: honest signaling
44. **Index signals** — signals physically constrained to be honest (e.g., body size and call pitch). Depends on: signal honesty
45. **Conventional signals** — arbitrary signals maintained by learning or coevolution. Depends on: signal honesty
46. **Alarm calls** — vocalizations warning of predators. Depends on: signal honesty, kin selection
47. **Pursuit-deterrence signals** — signals to predators that pursuit would be futile. Depends on: honest signaling
48. **Aposematism** — conspicuous warning signals in defended prey. Depends on: honest signaling
49. **Sensory exploitation** — signals evolve to tap into pre-existing receiver biases. Depends on: signal evolution
50. **Receiver bias** — sensory or cognitive preferences in signal receivers. Depends on: sensory exploitation
51. **Phenotypic plasticity** — ability to express different phenotypes in different environments. Depends on: decision rules, tradeoffs
52. **Reaction norms** — function mapping environment to phenotype. Depends on: phenotypic plasticity
53. **Behavioral syndromes** — correlated behaviors across contexts. Depends on: phenotypic plasticity
54. **Applied behavioral ecology** — using behavioral principles for conservation and management. Depends on: all core concepts

## Dependencies

- **Decision rules** require understanding **Tinbergen's four questions** because they represent proximate mechanisms (how) that produce ultimate outcomes (why)
- **Optimization models** build on **proximate vs ultimate causation** because they predict ultimate function while acknowledging proximate constraints
- **Marginal value theorem** extends **optimization models** to patch-leaving decisions by finding the optimal balance between staying (diminishing returns) and leaving (travel costs)
- **Risk sensitivity** integrates **optimal diet model** and **tradeoffs** because variance preference depends on whether expected payoff meets energetic needs
- **Parental investment** builds on **anisogamy** and **tradeoffs** because sex differences in gamete size create different optimal reproductive strategies
- **Costly signals** combine **sexual selection** and **tradeoffs** because signal costs ensure only high-quality individuals can afford to signal
- **Parent-offspring conflict** requires **parental investment** and **inclusive fitness** because genetic asymmetries (0.5 relatedness to self, 0.25 to siblings) create divergent optima
- **Hamilton's rule** formalizes **kin selection** by specifying when helping relatives increases inclusive fitness
- **Reciprocal altruism** differs from **kin selection** by explaining cooperation among unrelated individuals through repeated interactions
- **ESS** builds on **game theory** by identifying strategies that are stable against invasion by mutants
- **Contest behavior** integrates **game theory** and **tradeoffs** because fighting involves costs (injury, energy) and benefits (resources, mates)
- **Pursuit-deterrence signals** combine **honest signaling** and predator-prey dynamics because signaling to predators seems paradoxical but makes sense if it ends unprofitable chases
- **Sensory exploitation** explains signal evolution through **receiver bias** rather than signal costs, showing an alternative route to signal-receiver coevolution
- **Phenotypic plasticity** integrates **decision rules** and **tradeoffs** because organisms face costs and constraints in adjusting to environments

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are prerequisites for many downstream ideas:

1. **Optimization models** — foundation for understanding adaptive behavior quantitatively
2. **Tradeoffs** — essential for understanding why animals don't maximize everything simultaneously
3. **Fitness currency** — necessary for evaluating behavioral outcomes
4. **Sexual selection** — gateway to understanding mating systems, signaling, and life history
5. **Inclusive fitness** — key to understanding cooperation, altruism, and family conflict
6. **Game theory** — essential for analyzing social interactions and strategic behavior
7. **Honest signaling** — central to understanding communication across contexts

## Common Misconception Nodes

These concepts are frequently misunderstood and need special attention:

- **Optimization ≠ perfection** — optimal behavior balances multiple constraints and tradeoffs
- **Proximate ≠ ultimate** — mechanisms don't explain function, function doesn't explain mechanisms
- **Hamilton's rule** — students often confuse relatedness (genetic) with kinship (social)
- **ESS ≠ best strategy** — it's stable, not necessarily optimal
- **Altruism** — behavior is altruistic based on fitness effects, not intention
- **Honest signals** — honesty doesn't require consciousness or morality, just evolutionary stability
- **Group selection** — most apparent group benefits actually arise from individual or kin selection

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Natural selection** — needed for understanding adaptive value and evolutionary logic
- **Basic genetics** — needed for inclusive fitness, parent-offspring conflict, sexual selection
- **Introductory ecology** — needed for understanding environmental context of decisions
- **Basic probability** — needed for optimization models, risk sensitivity, game theory
- **Graph reading** — needed for interpreting tradeoffs, reaction norms, fitness landscapes
