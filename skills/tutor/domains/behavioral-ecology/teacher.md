## Domain Hooks
- **Marginal value theorem → economics** — The patch-leaving rule is identical to economic models of when to sell an asset or leave a job. Drop this when discussing foraging to show how behavioral ecology connects to human decision-making.

- **Handicap principle → art and culture** — Costly signaling theory has been applied to human conspicuous consumption, art, religion, and even humor. Introduce when discussing sexual selection signals to spark discussion about human behavior.

- **Hawk-dove game → nuclear deterrence** — Game theory in behavioral ecology parallels Cold War strategic thinking. The ESS mixed strategy is analogous to probabilistic retaliation. Use this when covering game theory to show cross-disciplinary connections.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **Optimization means perfection** — Students expect animals to behave optimally in all contexts, forgetting constraints, evolutionary lag, and tradeoffs. *Correction:* Emphasize that "optimal" means "best given constraints," not "perfect." Use examples of animals making "mistakes" in novel environments (e.g., moths flying into lights, birds attacking reflections) to show that adaptations are environment-specific.

2. **Proximate = ultimate** — Students conflate "how" and "why" questions, or assume mechanistic explanations answer evolutionary questions. *Correction:* Use Tinbergen's four questions repeatedly. For any behavior, ask "How does it work? How does it develop? What is its function? What is its evolutionary history?" Show that complete understanding requires all four levels.
