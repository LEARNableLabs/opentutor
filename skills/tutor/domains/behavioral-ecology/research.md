# Research Summary — Behavioral Ecology: Animal Decision-Making

## Major Subtopics

1. **Optimal Foraging Theory** — how animals maximize energy intake while minimizing costs (search time, handling time, predation risk)
2. **Game Theory and ESS** — evolutionary stable strategies, hawk-dove games, frequency-dependent selection
3. **Mate Choice and Sexual Selection** — direct/indirect benefits, good genes, runaway selection, cryptic choice
4. **Parental Investment** — life history tradeoffs, clutch size, parent-offspring conflict
5. **Social Behavior** — cooperation, altruism, kin selection, reciprocity, group living costs/benefits
6. **Communication and Signaling** — honest signals, costly signaling theory, sensory exploitation, ritualization
7. **Predator-Prey Dynamics** — vigilance, alarm calls, dilution effect, confusion effect, inspection behavior
8. **Habitat Selection** — ideal free distribution, patch choice, natal philopatry vs dispersal
9. **Phenotypic Plasticity** — behavioral flexibility, reaction norms, developmental vs activational plasticity

## Key Theoretical Frameworks

- **Tinbergen's Four Questions** — proximate mechanisms, ontogeny, adaptive value, phylogeny
- **Marginal Value Theorem** — when to leave a patch
- **Hamilton's Rule** — rb > c for altruism evolution
- **Sexual Selection Theory** — intrasexual competition vs intersexual choice
- **Life History Theory** — r vs K selection, pace-of-life syndromes

## Key Sources

- **Textbooks**: Davies, Krebs & West "An Introduction to Behavioural Ecology" (4th ed); Alcock "Animal Behavior" (11th ed)
- **Empirical systems**: great tits (foraging), sticklebacks (sexual selection), dung flies (mating), meerkats (cooperation), corvids (cognition)
- **Classic papers**: Maynard Smith & Price (1973) ESS; Trivers (1972) parental investment; Zahavi (1975) handicap principle
- **Modern approaches**: neurogenomics, collective behavior, movement ecology, evolutionary robotics

## Key Experiments & Case Studies

- Krebs et al. (1977) — great tit foraging in aviaries
- Bateson & Healy (2005) — bumblebee risk sensitivity
- Guppies in Trinidad — predation-driven evolution (Endler, Reznick)
- Blue-footed boobies — sibling negotiation (Drummond)
- Wild chimpanzees — cooperation and politics (Goodall, de Waal)
- Barn swallow tail length — sexual selection (Møller)
- Tunicate tadpoles — developmental decision-making (Marshall & Keough)
