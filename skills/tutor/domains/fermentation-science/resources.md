# Fermentation Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Principles of Fermentation Technology"** (Stanbury & Whitaker) — Comprehensive reference covering fermentation media, sterilization, fermenter design, effluent treatment, and economics. Standard university textbook for fermentation science programs. Available through university libraries.
  - https://bookstore.uoguelph.ca/p-34819-principles-of-fermentation-technology.aspx
  - https://biokamikazi.files.wordpress.com/2013/09/principles_of_fermentation_technology-stanburry_whittaker.pdf

### University Courses & Syllabi

- **Harvard University — Food Fermentation: The Science of Cooking with Microbes** — Explores history, biochemistry, and flavor science of fermented foods and beverages. Excellent for intermediate learners bridging science and application.
  - https://pll.harvard.edu/course/food-fermentation-science-cooking-microbes

- **Oregon State University — Fermentation Science Option** — BS program covering wine, beer, spirits, cheese, yogurt, soy sauce, pickles, breads, and fermented vegetables. Syllabus and course descriptions provide excellent curriculum structure.
  - https://catalog.oregonstate.edu/college-departments/agricultural-sciences/food-science-technology/food-science-sustainable-technologies-bs-hbs/fermentation-science-option/

- **Purdue University — Fermentation Sciences Major** — 120-credit bachelor program with detailed requirements. Good for understanding prerequisite progression.
  - https://catalog.purdue.edu/preview_program.php?catoid=15&poid=24908
  - https://ag.purdue.edu/department/foodsci/fermentation-sciences.html

- **University of British Columbia — FNH 405** — Food fermentation course with library resources. Syllabus includes recommended readings.
  - https://courses.landfood.ubc.ca/syllabus/term/2022W1_V/course/FNH_V%20405%20101%202022W1/source/FNH_405_syllabus.pdf

- **UC Davis — Fermentation Lecture Series** — Video lectures covering fermentation, energy processes, ethanol, lactic acid, glucose, alcohol, bacteria, oxygen, CO2, beer, cheese, and yeast.
  - https://video.ucdavis.edu/media/Fermentation+lecture/0_8hoa0yvc/151672082

### Academic Reference Materials

- **Biology LibreTexts — Fermented Foods** — Comprehensive coverage of fermentation in food processing, conversion of carbohydrates to alcohols and organic acids, LAB products (yogurt, cheese, sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles).
  - https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Microbiology/Microbiology_(Boundless)/17:_Industrial_Microbiology/17.05:_Food_Preservation/17.5A:_Fermented_Foods

- **Agriculture.Institute — Microbiology and Biochemistry of Fermentation Overview** — Concise overview of fermentation microbiology and biochemistry fundamentals.

### Interactive Tools & Simulators

- **Fermentation Kinetics & Microbial Growth Curve Simulator** — Outstanding free interactive tool covering:
  - 4-phase microbial growth (lag, exponential, stationary, death)
  - 8 fermentation types: bread, beer, wine, yogurt, sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha, vinegar
  - Monod kinetics, Luedeking-Piret product formation
  - Arrhenius temperature effects, pH monitoring, CO2 production
  - Hands-on exploration of fermentation variables
  - https://simulations4all.com/simulations/fermentation-kinetics-growth-curve-simulator

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Fermentation Beyond Food

- **Biofuel production** — Ethanol fermentation for renewable energy (same biochemistry, different feedstock and scale)
- **Pharmaceutical manufacturing** — Antibiotics, insulin, and biologics produced via fermentation (penicillin from *Penicillium*, recombinant proteins from engineered yeast)
- **Wastewater treatment** — Anaerobic digestion uses fermentation to break down organic waste and produce biogas

### Cultural & Historical

- **Naval history** — Sauerkraut and scurvy prevention on long voyages (Captain Cook)
- **Agricultural revolution** — Fermentation as food preservation enabling sedentary civilization
- **Coffee and chocolate** — Flavor development through fermentation of coffee cherries and cacao pods
- **Japanese cuisine** — Miso, soy sauce, natto, sake all depend on *Aspergillus* koji fermentation

### Climate & Sustainability

- **Food waste reduction** — Fermentation as preservation reduces spoilage
- **Low-energy food processing** — Fermentation requires no heat, minimal energy input
- **Traditional ecological knowledge** — Indigenous fermentation practices as climate-adapted food systems
