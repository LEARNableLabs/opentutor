# History of Color — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books

- **Victoria Finlay** — *Color: A Natural History of the Palette* (2002)
  - Journalistic investigation of pigment origins — Finlay traveled to Afghanistan for lapis, China for vermilion, etc.
  - Great for narrative and human stories behind materials.
  - Intermediate-friendly: accessible prose, minimal chemistry depth.

- **Philip Ball** — *Bright Earth: Art and the Invention of Color* (2001)
  - Chemistry and art history integrated — traces pigment development from caves to modernism.
  - More technical than Finlay; good for chemical context.
  - Best chapters: Egyptian blue, synthetic dyes, 20th-century pigments.

- **Michel Pastoureau** — *Blue: The History of a Color* (2001), and series on other colors
  - Cultural and symbolic history — how blue became associated with Virgin Mary, royalty, then jeans.
  - Also: *Black* (2008), *Green* (2014), *Red* (2017), *Yellow* (2019).
  - Light on chemistry, heavy on semiotics and social history.

- **Kassia St. Clair** — *The Secret Lives of Color* (2016)
  - 75 short essays on specific colors and pigments — great for bite-sized stories.
  - Accessible, narrative-driven, good for engagement hooks.
  - Example: "Mummy Brown," "Baker-Miller Pink," "Scheele's Green."

- **Amy Butler Greenfield** — *A Perfect Red: Empire, Espionage, and the Quest for the Color of Desire* (2005)
  - Deep-dive on cochineal — Spanish monopoly, piracy, industrial espionage.
  - Shows how one color connects colonialism, trade, and chemistry.

### Academic Resources

- **ColourLex** — https://www.colourlex.com/
  - Art material encyclopedia — detailed pigment profiles, chemical compositions, historical use.
  - Searchable database: "vermilion" returns chemical formula, historical sources, degradation patterns.
  - Intermediate-advanced; some entries assume chemistry background.

- **Getty Conservation Institute** — https://www.getty.edu/conservation/
  - Technical research on pigment analysis, conservation techniques.
  - Publications: https://www.getty.edu/conservation/publications_resources/
  - Best for: Egyptian blue research, degradation studies, spectroscopy.

- **Victoria & Albert Museum — Materials & Techniques** — https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/conservation-techniques
  - Textile and dye focus — indigo, madder, cochineal, synthetic dyes.
  - Good images of historical fabrics with pigment annotations.

- **Metropolitan Museum of Art — Heilbrunn Timeline** — https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/
  - Art history context with material details.
  - Search "pigments," "dyes," "textiles" for relevant essays.
  - Excellent for connecting artworks to chemical innovations.

### Historical Texts (optional deep dives)

- **Cennino Cennini** — *Il Libro dell'Arte* (The Craftsman's Handbook, c. 1400)
  - Renaissance artist's manual — recipes for pigments, binders, techniques.
  - Available in translation: https://archive.org/details/craftsmanshandbo00cenn

- **Robert Dossie** — *The Handmaid to the Arts* (1758)
  - 18th-century pigment recipes and techniques.
  - Shows pre-synthetic chemistry methods.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **The Dress (2015)** — viral photo demonstrating color perception variability.
  - Tie-in to lesson 27 (color vision): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_dress

- **Anish Kapoor and Vantablack** — artist monopolizing "the blackest black," sparking controversy.
  - Connects to historical pigment monopolies (Tyrian purple, ultramarine).
  - https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/anish-kapoor-vantablack-180969442/

- **Stuart Semple's "Pinkest Pink"** — counter-reaction to Kapoor, open-source pigments.
  - Democratization of color in the 21st century.
  - https://www.cultureremix.com/stuart-semple-pinkest-pink/

- **International Klein Blue (IKB)** — Yves Klein patenting a specific ultramarine shade in 1960.
  - Can you own a color? Legal and artistic debates.

- **Qualia and Philosophical Zombies** — philosophy of consciousness using "seeing red" as test case.
  - Tie-in to lesson 27 (perception): does everyone's "red" look the same?
  - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia/

- **Quantum Dots in Displays** — modern TVs use semiconductor nanocrystals to produce pure colors.
  - Chemistry → art → technology loop continues.
  - https://www.nature.com/articles/nphoton.2013.238

- **Biomimicry: Structural Color** — butterfly wings, peacock feathers create color through nanostructures, not pigments.
  - Challenges pigment/dye binary from lesson 1.
  - Applications in textiles, anti-counterfeiting.

- **The Color of Money** — why is US currency green? (chromium oxide, anti-counterfeiting).
  - Connects pigment choice to trust, authority, security.
