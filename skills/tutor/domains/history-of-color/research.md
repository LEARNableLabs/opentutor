# Research — History of Color

## Overview

The history of color spans chemistry, art, culture, trade, and technology. This curriculum explores how humans discovered, synthesized, and assigned meaning to pigments and dyes across civilizations.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Material Science of Color
- **Pigments vs. Dyes** — particle suspension vs. molecular bonding
- **Mineral pigments** — ochres, lapis lazuli, cinnabar, verdigris
- **Organic dyes** — indigo, madder, cochineal, Tyrian purple
- **Synthetic colors** — mauveine (1856), alizarin, modern chemistry

### 2. Ancient Color Technologies
- **Prehistoric ochres** — cave paintings, burial practices
- **Egyptian blue** — first synthetic pigment (ca. 2500 BCE)
- **Phoenician purple** — mollusks, royal monopolies, trade
- **Roman pigments** — vermilion, lead white, green earth

### 3. Medieval and Renaissance Innovation
- **Ultramarine** — Afghan lapis, cost more than gold
- **Vermilion** — mercury sulfide, alchemy to chemistry
- **Lead-tin yellow** — lost and rediscovered
- **Oil painting** — new binders unlock color possibilities

### 4. Color Trade and Empire
- **Spice routes** — saffron, turmeric, indigo
- **Colonial extraction** — cochineal from Americas, indigo plantations
- **Industrial chemistry** — Perkin's mauve, the coal tar revolution
- **Standardization** — Pantone, RAL, color systems

### 5. Cultural Meanings
- **Color symbolism** — varies by culture (white = mourning in China, purity in West)
- **Sacred colors** — saffron in Buddhism, purple in Christianity
- **Gendered color** — pink/blue reversal in 20th century
- **Political colors** — red flags, yellow stars, symbolic resistance

### 6. Modern Color Science
- **Color theory** — Newton, Goethe, Munsell, opponent process
- **Perception** — rods, cones, tetrachromacy, color blindness
- **Digital color** — RGB, CMYK, hex codes, color spaces
- **Conservation** — fading, degradation, restoration

## Key Sources

### Books
- **Victoria Finlay** — *Color: A Natural History of the Palette* (2002) — journalistic deep-dives into pigment origins
- **Philip Ball** — *Bright Earth: Art and the Invention of Color* (2001) — chemistry and art history
- **Michel Pastoureau** — *Blue: The History of a Color* (2001) and other monographs — cultural symbolism
- **Kassia St. Clair** — *The Secret Lives of Color* (2016) — accessible, story-driven

### Academic Resources
- **Getty Conservation Institute** — pigment databases, technical analysis
- **ColourLex** — art material encyclopedia
- **Victoria & Albert Museum** — textile and dye collections
- **Smithsonian** — Color in a New Light exhibition materials

### Video/Interactive
- **Tate** — pigment videos and conservation case studies
- **Yale Courses (YouTube)** — Roman art, Renaissance painting technique
- **Science History Institute** — chemical innovations in color

### Courses
- No single comprehensive MOOC found; topic typically split across:
  - Art history courses (pigment use)
  - Chemistry courses (synthesis)
  - Anthropology courses (cultural meaning)

## Student Level Match

**Intermediate** — assumes:
- General chemistry knowledge (elements, compounds, reactions)
- Basic art history familiarity
- Interest in interdisciplinary connections

Does NOT require:
- Advanced organic chemistry
- Art conservation training
- Deep anthropology background

## Gaps and Opportunities

- **Cross-cultural comparison** — most resources are Eurocentric; extra effort needed for Asian, African, Indigenous color traditions
- **Hands-on learning** — natural dye experiments, pigment grinding (can be suggested as optional activities)
- **Contemporary issues** — sustainability of synthetic dyes, digital color accessibility
