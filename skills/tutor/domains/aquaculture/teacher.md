# Aquaculture and fisheries science — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 7 lessons (28%)
- **Socratic questions** — 5 lessons (20%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (20%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (16%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (8%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 2 lessons (8%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 44% accessible (1-2), 36% standard (3), 20% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 10: "Case study: How did New England groundfish collapse?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 15: "Can we recycle the water and grow fish indoors?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "What happens when the ocean warms and acidifies?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 22: "How can fisheries adapt to climate change?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 24: "Can integrated multi-trophic aquaculture solve the feed problem?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers aquaculture and fisheries science, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: fisheries value, protein supply, ecosystem services, trophic levels, energy transfer, food web structure, population dynamics, recruitment, mortality, growth, stock assessment, catch data, survey methods, abundance indices, overfishing (and 68 more).
