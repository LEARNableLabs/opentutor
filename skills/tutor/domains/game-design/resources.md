# Game Design — Resources

## Primary Sources (For Lesson Content)

### Foundational Paper
- **MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research** (Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek, 2004)
  - URL: https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf
  - The original paper defining the MDA framework
  - Essential reading — short, dense, foundational
  - Best for: intermediate students who can handle academic writing
  - Use throughout curriculum as primary reference

### Textbooks & Books
- **The Cores of Game Design: Mechanics, Economics, Narrative, and Aesthetics** by Yvens R. Serpa (Routledge)
  - URL: https://www.routledge.com/The-Cores-of-Game-Design-Mechanics-Economics-Narrative-and-Aesthetics/Serpa/p/book/9781032397542
  - Expands MDA to include economic systems and narrative
  - Practical, prototype-focused approach
  - Best for: students who want to go deeper into resource economies and player-driven economies

- **Creating Games** (Online Textbook)
  - URL: https://pressbooks.usnh.edu/creatinggames/chapter/mechanics-dynamics-and-aesthetics/
  - Free online resource with dedicated MDA chapter
  - Accessible writing, good for beginners transitioning to intermediate
  - Includes examples and exercises

### University Courses (MIT OpenCourseWare)
- **Game Design (CMS-608)** — Fall 2010
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/cms-608-game-design-fall-2010/
  - Non-digital games focus (board, card, sports, tabletop RPGs)
  - Audio lectures available: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/cms-608-game-design-fall-2010/video_galleries/audio-lectures/
  - Best for: understanding mechanics without programming
  - Use for: Lessons 4-10 (Mechanics module)

- **Creating Video Games (CMS-611J)** — Fall 2014
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/cms-611j-creating-video-games-fall-2014/
  - Digital game design and production
  - Session 14 on Aesthetics: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/cms-611j-creating-video-games-fall-2014/resources/lecture-14-aesthetics/
  - Team-based project work examples
  - Best for: Lessons 18-24 (Aesthetics module) and Lesson 27 (playtesting)

- **Learn to Build Your Own Videogame with Unity and Kinect**
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-3-003-learn-to-build-your-own-videogame-with-the-unity-game-engine-and-microsoft-kinect-january-iap-2017/
  - Hands-on development workshop
  - Best for: students who want to implement prototypes digitally

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Psychology
- **Flow Theory** (Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi)
  - Book: Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience
  - Connection: explains "submission" aesthetic and optimal difficulty curves
  - Use in: Lessons 16, 19 (difficulty, satisfaction)

- **Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan)
  - Connection: autonomy, competence, relatedness map to player motivation
  - Explains why players engage with games
  - Use in: Lesson 18 (aesthetics), Lesson 23 (fellowship)

- **Skinner Box & Operant Conditioning**
  - Connection: variable reward schedules create compulsion
  - Ethical considerations in game design
  - Use in: Lesson 19 (Candy Crush, submission)

### Economics
- **Game Theory** (Nash, Von Neumann)
  - Connection: equilibria, zero-sum games, cooperative games
  - Differs from MDA but informs competitive balance
  - Use in: Lessons 9, 15 (randomness, meaningful choices)

- **Behavioral Economics** (Kahneman, Tversky)
  - Connection: loss aversion, framing effects, cognitive biases
  - Explains player psychology in resource decisions
  - Use in: Lesson 8 (resource economies)

### Literature & Narrative
- **Procedural Rhetoric** (Ian Bogost)
  - Connection: games as argumentation through mechanics
  - Bridges mechanics and meaning
  - Use in: Lesson 26 (ludonarrative dissonance)

- **Hero's Journey** (Joseph Campbell)
  - Connection: narrative structure in games
  - Maps to pacing and difficulty curves
  - Use in: Lesson 22 (narrative aesthetic)

### Mathematics & Computer Science
- **Complexity Theory**
  - Connection: emergence from simple rules (cellular automata, Conway's Game of Life)
  - Use in: Lesson 11 (emergence)

- **Network Theory**
  - Connection: social dynamics in multiplayer games
  - Use in: Lesson 23 (fellowship)

### Art & Design
- **Universal Design & Accessibility**
  - Connection: mechanics that include/exclude players
  - Ethical and practical considerations
  - Use throughout: how do mechanics affect who can play?

- **Gestalt Principles**
  - Connection: visual feedback, "juice," clarity of game state
  - Use in: Lesson 19 (sensation)

### Sociology
- **Community Formation & Norms**
  - Connection: how multiplayer games create cultures
  - Use in: Lesson 23 (fellowship)

- **Stigma & Identity**
  - Connection: representation in games, player identity expression
  - Use in: Lesson 24 (expression aesthetic)

## Specific Game Examples (For Case Studies)

### Classic Examples (Widely Accessible)
- **Tetris** — emergence from simple mechanics
- **Chess** — action space, meaningful choices, depth
- **Tic-Tac-Toe** — solved game, lack of depth
- **Monopoly** — positive feedback loops, runaway dynamics
- **Rock-Paper-Scissors** — balanced intransitivity

### Digital Examples (Well-Known)
- **Dark Souls** — difficulty curves, challenge aesthetic, mastery
- **Journey** — emotional design, fellowship in silence, fantasy/narrative
- **Minecraft** — expression, discovery, sandbox design
- **Candy Crush** — sensation, submission, juice
- **Portal** — challenge, discovery, elegant mechanics

### Deep Cuts (For Rabbit Holes)
- **Dwarf Fortress** — emergence, simulation depth, discovery
- **Braid** — mechanics as metaphor, time manipulation
- **Papers, Please** — ludonarrative consonance, mechanics as narrative
- **This War of Mine** — emotional design, challenge as survival
- **Undertale** — subverting mechanics, player agency, narrative integration
- **EVE Online** — player-driven economy, emergent politics
- **Slay the Spire** — deckbuilding roguelike, combinatorial depth
- **Dead by Daylight** — asymmetric multiplayer, 1v4 balance
- **Katamari Damacy** — positive feedback loop as core mechanic

### Board Game Examples
- **Go** — emergence from minimal rules
- **Pandemic** — cooperative dynamics
- **Magic: The Gathering** — metagame evolution, combinatorial depth
- **Codenames** — fellowship, discovery
- **Azul** — resource conversion, meaningful choices

## Where to Go Next (After This Curriculum)

1. **Production & Implementation** — learn Unity, Unreal, or Godot; study game development
2. **Specialized Design** — deep dive into specific genres (4X, fighting games, narrative, puzzle)
3. **Systems Design** — progression systems, economies, balancing tools
4. **UX & Accessibility** — how players understand and interact with systems
5. **Narrative Design** — branching narratives, environmental storytelling, ludonarrative integration
6. **Game AI** — behavior trees, pathfinding, procedural generation
7. **Player Psychology** — motivation theory, retention, ethical design
8. **Level Design** — spatial design, pacing, environmental storytelling
9. **Business & Production** — monetization, live ops, production pipelines
10. **Critical Game Studies** — games as culture, representation, politics
