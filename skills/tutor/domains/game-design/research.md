# Game Design Research Summary

## Topic Overview

Game design using the MDA (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics) framework — a formal approach to analyzing and designing games by breaking them into three interconnected components:
- **Mechanics**: Rules, actions, algorithms, core systems
- **Dynamics**: Runtime behavior emerging from mechanics and player interaction
- **Aesthetics**: Emotional responses evoked in players

## Major Subtopics

1. **MDA Framework Foundations** — understanding the designer-vs-player perspective, the MDA lens
2. **Mechanics Design** — rule systems, action spaces, resources, constraints
3. **Dynamics & Emergence** — feedback loops, player agency, emergent behavior, balancing
4. **Aesthetic Taxonomies** — the 8 types of fun (sensation, fantasy, narrative, challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression, submission)
5. **Integrative Analysis** — using MDA to critique existing games and iterate on designs
6. **Practical Application** — prototyping, playtesting, iteration

## Key Sources Found

### Foundational Paper
- **MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research** (Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek, 2004)
  - URL: https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf
  - The original paper defining the framework
  - Used in game design courses worldwide

### Textbooks & Academic Resources
- **The Cores of Game Design: Mechanics, Economics, Narrative, and Aesthetics** (Yvens R. Serpa, Routledge)
  - URL: https://www.routledge.com/The-Cores-of-Game-Design-Mechanics-Economics-Narrative-and-Aesthetics/Serpa/p/book/9781032397542
  - Expands MDA to include economic systems
  - Practical, prototype-focused approach

- **Creating Games** (Online Textbook)
  - URL: https://pressbooks.usnh.edu/creatinggames/chapter/mechanics-dynamics-and-aesthetics/
  - Dedicated MDA chapter with examples
  - Free online resource

## Notes for Curriculum Design
- Strong academic foundation with the 2004 paper
- Multiple university courses available for reference
- Good balance of theory (MDA paper) and practice (MIT courses, Unity)
- Covers both digital and non-digital games
- Framework is widely adopted and well-documented
- Intermediate level should focus on applying MDA to analyze and critique games, not just understanding the framework
