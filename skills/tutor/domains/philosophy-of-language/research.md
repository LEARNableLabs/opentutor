# Philosophy of Language — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Foundations of Meaning and Reference
- Distinction between sense and reference (Frege)
- The problem of empty names and identity statements
- Compositionality and semantic structure
- Use vs. mention distinction

### 2. Description Theory of Reference
- Russell's theory of descriptions
- Definite descriptions and logical form
- Strawson's critique and presupposition theory
- Scope ambiguities in descriptions

### 3. Causal Theory of Reference
- Kripke's causal-historical theory
- The naming ceremony and causal chains
- Rigid designation vs. non-rigid descriptors
- Modal arguments against descriptivism

### 4. Natural Kind Terms
- Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment
- Division of linguistic labor
- Reference fixing vs. meaning determination
- Natural kinds and scientific essentialism

### 5. Advanced Topics
- Indexicals and demonstratives (Kaplan)
- Two-dimensional semantics
- Direct reference theory
- Semantic externalism and internalism debates

## Key Sources

### Primary Texts
- Frege, G. "On Sense and Reference" (1892) - foundational paper
- Russell, B. "On Denoting" (1905) - theory of descriptions
- Kripke, S. *Naming and Necessity* (1980) - causal theory
- Putnam, H. "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975) - semantic externalism

## Target Audience Considerations

**Intermediate Level**
- Assumes: basic logic, familiarity with philosophical argumentation
- Target depth: engage with primary texts, understand key arguments, compare competing theories
- Avoid: heavy technical semantics, formal logic beyond propositional/predicate
- Emphasize: intuition pumps, thought experiments, real-world applications
