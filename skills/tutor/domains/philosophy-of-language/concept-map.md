# Philosophy of Language — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Sense vs. Reference** — Frege's distinction: sense is the cognitive mode of presentation, reference is the object picked out
2. **Empty Names** — referring expressions that lack referents (e.g., 'Sherlock Holmes', 'the present King of France')
3. **Compositionality** — the principle that the meaning of a complex expression is determined by the meanings of its parts and how they're combined
4. **Use-Mention Distinction** — using a word to refer to something vs. mentioning the word itself
5. **Singular Terms** — expressions that purport to pick out a single individual (names, descriptions, pronouns)
6. **Theory of Descriptions** — Russell's analysis of definite descriptions as quantified expressions, not referring terms. Depends on: singular terms, compositionality
7. **Logical Form** — the underlying logical structure of a sentence that determines its semantic properties. Depends on: theory of descriptions
8. **Presupposition** — what a sentence takes for granted rather than asserts. Depends on: theory of descriptions, assertion
9. **Scope Ambiguity** — ambiguities arising from different possible scopes of quantifiers and descriptions. Depends on: logical form, quantification
10. **Descriptivism** — the theory that proper names are synonymous with descriptions or clusters of descriptions. Depends on: theory of descriptions
11. **Causal-Historical Theory** — Kripke's view that reference is established through initial naming and maintained through causal chains. Depends on: descriptivism (as contrast)
12. **Naming Ceremony** — the initial baptism that fixes the reference of a name. Depends on: causal-historical theory
13. **Reference Borrowing** — acquiring the use of a name from others in the linguistic community. Depends on: causal chains
14. **Rigid Designation** — a term that refers to the same object in all possible worlds where that object exists. Depends on: causal-historical theory, modal semantics
15. **Modal Arguments** — arguments using possible worlds to show that names and descriptions differ in modal profile. Depends on: rigid designation, counterfactuals
16. **Essential vs. Accidental Properties** — properties an object must have vs. properties it happens to have. Depends on: modal arguments
17. **Natural Kinds** — categories that carve nature at its joints (water, gold, tiger). Depends on: rigid designation
18. **Semantic Externalism** — the thesis that meanings are partly determined by factors external to the speaker's mind. Depends on: causal-historical theory, natural kinds
19. **Twin Earth** — Putnam's thought experiment showing that molecular structure, not internal state, determines meaning. Depends on: semantic externalism, natural kinds
20. **Division of Linguistic Labor** — the social distribution of semantic expertise within a linguistic community. Depends on: semantic externalism, reference borrowing
21. **Reference Fixing vs. Meaning** — the distinction between what determines reference and what constitutes meaning. Depends on: causal-historical theory, semantic externalism
22. **Internalism vs. Externalism** — debate over whether meanings are in the head or partly constituted by external factors. Depends on: semantic externalism
23. **Narrow vs. Wide Content** — content that supervenes on internal states vs. content that depends on environment. Depends on: internalism vs. externalism
24. **Indexicals** — context-sensitive expressions like 'I', 'here', 'now'. Depends on: reference, context
25. **Demonstratives** — expressions like 'that', 'this' that require perceptual salience or pointing. Depends on: indexicals, direct reference
26. **Character vs. Content** — Kaplan's distinction between the linguistic meaning (character) and what's said in context (content). Depends on: indexicals
27. **Direct Reference** — the view that certain terms contribute their referents directly to propositions, without descriptive mediation. Depends on: rigid designation, demonstratives
28. **Necessary A Posteriori** — statements that are necessarily true but knowable only empirically. Depends on: rigid designation, essential properties, natural kinds
29. **Two-Dimensional Semantics** — framework separating epistemic and metaphysical possibility. Depends on: necessary a posteriori, possible worlds

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies
- **Compositionality** requires understanding **sense vs. reference** because we need to know what semantic values compose
- **Theory of descriptions** builds on **compositionality** and **singular terms** because it reanalyzes how descriptions contribute to sentence meaning
- **Logical form** depends on **theory of descriptions** for analyzing the structure beneath surface grammar

### Description Theory Dependencies
- **Presupposition** requires **theory of descriptions** to understand the Russell-Strawson debate
- **Scope ambiguity** requires **logical form** and **quantification** to see how different parsings yield different meanings
- **Descriptivism** extends **theory of descriptions** from descriptions to proper names

### Causal Theory Dependencies
- **Causal-historical theory** is motivated by problems with **descriptivism**, so understanding the latter is essential
- **Rigid designation** depends on **causal-historical theory** and **modal semantics** (possible worlds framework)
- **Modal arguments** require **rigid designation** to show the semantic difference between names and descriptions
- **Essential vs. accidental properties** emerges from **modal arguments** about what's true in all vs. some possible worlds

### Natural Kinds Dependencies
- **Natural kinds** extends **rigid designation** from proper names to kind terms
- **Semantic externalism** is motivated by the behavior of **natural kind terms** and **causal-historical theory**
- **Twin Earth** depends on understanding **semantic externalism** and **natural kinds**
- **Division of linguistic labor** requires **semantic externalism** and **reference borrowing**
- **Reference fixing vs. meaning** distinguishes what determines reference (causal chain) from what meaning consists in (varies between theories)

### Advanced Topics Dependencies
- **Indexicals** require understanding basic **reference** and **context-sensitivity**
- **Demonstratives** build on **indexicals** and connect to **direct reference**
- **Character vs. content** requires **indexicals** to motivate the distinction
- **Direct reference** connects back to **rigid designation** and forward to **demonstratives**
- **Necessary a posteriori** requires **rigid designation**, **essential properties**, and **natural kinds**
- **Two-dimensional semantics** is a sophisticated framework addressing puzzles from **necessary a posteriori** statements

## Critical Bottlenecks

1. **Sense vs. Reference** — if students don't grasp this distinction, they'll struggle with everything that follows
2. **Theory of Descriptions** — Russell's analysis is the foundation for understanding both descriptivism and the causal theory's rejection of it
3. **Rigid Designation** — central to modal arguments, natural kinds, and the necessary a posteriori; students must internalize what it means for a term to refer to the same thing across possible worlds
4. **Semantic Externalism** — the shift from internalist to externalist semantics is conceptually difficult but essential for natural kinds and advanced topics

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Propositional Logic** — needed for understanding logical form, quantification, scope ambiguities
- **Modal Logic** — needed for possible worlds semantics, rigid designation, modal arguments
- **Philosophical Argumentation** — needed for evaluating thought experiments, identifying presuppositions, constructing counterexamples
- **Basic Metaphysics** — needed for essential vs. accidental properties, possible worlds, natural kinds

## Common Conceptual Traps

1. Conflating **sense and reference** after initially distinguishing them — students often backslide
2. Thinking **rigid designation** means "always refers" rather than "refers to the same thing in all worlds where it refers"
3. Misunderstanding **Twin Earth** as about different meanings rather than showing that meaning depends on external facts
4. Confusing **reference fixing** (initial baptism) with **meaning** (what a term contributes to truth conditions)
5. Over-generalizing **causal theory** to all referring expressions when it primarily targets proper names and natural kind terms
