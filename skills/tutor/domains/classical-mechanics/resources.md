# Classical Mechanics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

**For Intermediate Learners:**

- **John R. Taylor** — *Classical Mechanics* (University Science Books, 2005)
  - Best undergraduate text: clear explanations, excellent problems, builds intuition before formalism
  - Covers Lagrangian thoroughly, Hamiltonian moderately, perfect for intermediate level
  - Chapter 6 (Calculus of Variations), Chapter 7 (Lagrange's Equations), Chapter 13 (Hamiltonian Mechanics)
  
- **Jerry B. Marion & Stephen T. Thornton** — *Classical Dynamics of Particles and Systems* (5th ed.)
  - Standard undergraduate text, thorough coverage, many worked examples
  - Good balance of theory and application
  
**Graduate Texts (for deeper dives):**

- **Herbert Goldstein, Charles Poole, John Safko** — *Classical Mechanics* (3rd ed., Addison Wesley)
  - The canonical graduate text, comprehensive and rigorous
  - Chapter 2 (Variational Principles), Chapter 8 (Canonical Transformations), Chapter 9 (Hamilton-Jacobi)
  - Dense but authoritative
  
- **L.D. Landau & E.M. Lifshitz** — *Mechanics* (Course of Theoretical Physics, Vol. 1)
  - Terse, elegant, powerful — not for beginners but illuminating for those ready
  - Starts with principle of least action (unusual but beautiful approach)
  
- **V.I. Arnold** — *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics* (2nd ed.)
  - Geometric/modern approach emphasizing symplectic geometry
  - For mathematically sophisticated students
  
**Alternative Approaches:**

- **Louis N. Hand & Janet D. Finch** — *Analytical Mechanics*
  - Modern treatment with computational examples and Mathematica notebooks
  - Good bridge to modern physics

### Unexplored Connections & Rabbit Holes

**Cross-Disciplinary Links:**

1. **Control Theory & Robotics**
   - Lagrangian mechanics is foundational for robot dynamics
   - Lagrange multipliers for constrained optimization
   - Modern robotic control uses Hamiltonian formulations
   
2. **Economics & Optimization**
   - Calculus of variations → optimal control theory → dynamic programming
   - Hamiltonian methods in economic growth models
   
3. **Thermodynamics**
   - Legendre transformations connect thermodynamic potentials
   - U(S,V) ↔ F(T,V) ↔ G(T,P) ↔ H(S,P)
   - Same mathematical structure as Lagrangian ↔ Hamiltonian
   
4. **General Relativity**
   - Einstein-Hilbert action → Einstein field equations
   - Hamiltonian formulation of GR (ADM formalism)
   
5. **Quantum Field Theory**
   - Lagrangian densities for fields
   - Path integral formulation (Feynman) uses classical action
   
6. **Geometric Algebra**
   - Symplectic geometry, Poisson manifolds
   - Modern reformulation using differential forms
   
7. **Machine Learning**
   - Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) for sampling
   - Neural ODEs and Hamiltonian neural networks
   - Physics-informed machine learning

## Further Reading (Beyond the Curriculum)

**If you want to go deeper:**

- **Hamilton-Jacobi Theory** — see Goldstein Ch. 10, Landau Ch. 7
- **Symplectic Geometry** — Arnold's *Mathematical Methods*, Abraham & Marsden's *Foundations of Mechanics*
- **Chaos and Nonlinear Dynamics** — Strogatz's *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*
- **Integrable Systems** — Babelon, Bernard, Talon's *Introduction to Classical Integrable Systems*
- **Geometric Mechanics** — Marsden & Ratiu's *Introduction to Mechanics and Symmetry*

**If you want breadth:**

- **Rigid Body Dynamics** — see Goldstein Ch. 4-5
- **Celestial Mechanics** — see Taylor Ch. 8, or Murray & Dermott's *Solar System Dynamics*
- **Continuum Mechanics** — transition from particles to fields
- **Statistical Mechanics** — natural next step (phase space reappears!)
