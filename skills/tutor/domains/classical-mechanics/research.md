# Classical Mechanics Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Calculus of Variations
- Euler-Lagrange equations
- Functional derivatives
- Constraints and Lagrange multipliers
- Brachistochrone and other classic problems

### 2. Lagrangian Formulation
- Principle of least action
- Generalized coordinates and constraints
- Lagrangian for various systems
- Symmetries and Noether's theorem
- Small oscillations and normal modes

### 3. Hamiltonian Formulation
- Legendre transformation
- Hamilton's equations
- Phase space and canonical coordinates
- Poisson brackets
- Canonical transformations

### 4. Advanced Topics
- Hamilton-Jacobi theory
- Action-angle variables
- Generating functions
- Symplectic geometry (conceptual introduction)
- Connections to quantum mechanics

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Goldstein, Poole, Safko** — "Classical Mechanics" (3rd ed.) — canonical graduate text, comprehensive coverage
- **Taylor** — "Classical Mechanics" — more accessible, excellent for intermediate learners
- **Landau & Lifshitz** — "Mechanics" (Vol. 1) — terse but powerful, mathematical rigor
- **Hand & Finch** — "Analytical Mechanics" — modern approach with computational examples

### University Courses
- **MIT OpenCourseWare 8.223** (Classical Mechanics II) — Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalism
- **MIT OpenCourseWare 8.09** (Classical Mechanics III) — advanced topics
- **Stanford Physics 110** — intermediate classical mechanics
- **Perimeter Institute** — various lectures on analytical mechanics

### Video Lectures
- **Leonard Susskind** — Stanford's "Classical Mechanics" lecture series
- **MIT OCW** — Prof. Deepto Chakrabarty's lectures
- **For the Allure of Physics** — Walter Lewin (foundational context)

## Pedagogical Notes from Research

- **Building intuition first** — students often jump to formalism without understanding why these formulations are powerful
- **Computational examples** — modern pedagogy emphasizes numerical solutions alongside analytical
- **Visual representation** — phase space portraits and configuration space are crucial
- **Motivation through problems** — problems that are hard in Newtonian mechanics but easy in Lagrangian/Hamiltonian (e.g., coupled oscillators, rotating frames)
