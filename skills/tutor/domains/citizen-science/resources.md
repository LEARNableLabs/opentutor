# Citizen Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks & Foundational Books

- **Citizen Science: Public Participation in Environmental Research** (Dickinson & Bonney, 2012)
  - Comprehensive overview of contributory and collaborative models
  - Strong focus on ecological and biodiversity monitoring
  - Best for: understanding historical development and natural science applications
  - https://www.cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801449116/

- **The Rightful Place of Science: Citizen Science** (Cavalier & Kennedy, 2016)
  - Explores policy implications and science-society relationships
  - Shorter, more accessible than Dickinson & Bonney
  - Best for: ethical and political dimensions
  - https://cspo.org/research/rightful-place/

- **Citizen Science: Innovation in Open Science, Society and Policy** (Hecker et al., 2018)
  - European perspective, includes ECSA ten principles
  - Strong on data quality, standards, and policy impacts
  - Best for: international perspectives and policy applications
  - https://www.uclpress.co.uk/products/107613

- **Participatory Action Research** (Kindon, Pain & Kesby, 2007)
  - Broader than citizen science, covers participatory research methods
  - Critical geographic and social science perspectives
  - Best for: power dynamics, co-creation, community-based research
  - https://uk.sagepub.com/en-gb/eur/participatory-action-research-approaches-and-methods/book227584

- **Data Feminism** (D'Ignazio & Klein, 2020)
  - Power and ethics in data work, applicable to citizen science data
  - Best for: critical analysis of who benefits from data collection
  - https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu/

### Academic Papers (Key Readings)

- Bonney et al. (2009) "Citizen Science: A Developing Tool for Expanding Science Knowledge and Scientific Literacy" *BioScience*
  - Foundational framework for learning outcomes
  - https://academic.oup.com/bioscience/article/59/11/977/256499

- Wiggins & Crowston (2011) "From Conservation to Crowdsourcing: A Typology of Citizen Science" *HICSS*
  - Typology of project types and organizational models
  - https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5718663

- Shirk et al. (2012) "Public Participation in Scientific Research: a Framework for Deliberate Design" *CAISE*
  - THE framework for participation levels (contributory/collaborative/co-created)
  - https://www.informalscience.org/public-participation-scientific-research-framework-deliberate-design

- Haklay (2013) "Citizen Science and Volunteered Geographic Information: Overview and Typology of Participation" *Crowdsourcing Geographic Knowledge*
  - Geographic information focus, strong on technology platforms
  - https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-007-4587-2_7

- Kosmala et al. (2016) "Assessing data quality in citizen science" *Frontiers in Ecology*
  - Statistical approaches to validation and quality control
  - https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/15-1634

### Interactive Tools & Platforms

- **Zooniverse** — largest platform for online crowdsourcing projects
  - Astronomy, ecology, humanities, climate, medicine
  - Project builder for creating your own projects
  - https://www.zooniverse.org/

- **iNaturalist** — biodiversity observation platform
  - Mobile app, AI-assisted species ID, community verification
  - Excellent example of human-AI collaboration
  - https://www.inaturalist.org/

- **eBird** — bird observation database (Cornell Lab)
  - Gold standard for long-term ecological monitoring
  - Massive scale, flexible participation
  - https://ebird.org/

- **SciStarter** — project database and participant matching
  - Search projects by topic, location, time commitment
  - Tracks contributions across projects
  - https://scistarter.org/

- **CitSci.org** — project management platform
  - Tools for designing protocols, collecting data, analyzing
  - https://www.citsci.org/

- **GLOBE Observer** — NASA's environmental observation app
  - Cloud cover, land cover, mosquito habitats, tree height
  - https://observer.globe.gov/

- **CoCoRaHS** — Community Collaborative Rain, Hail and Snow Network
  - Precipitation monitoring with simple rain gauges
  - Example of low-tech, high-impact citizen science
  - https://www.cocorahs.org/

## Case Study Collections

### Exemplar Projects (Deep Dives)

- **Galaxy Zoo** — astronomy classification, 100M+ classifications
  - Crowdsourcing at massive scale, consensus methods
  - Led to new morphological discoveries, volunteer-authored papers
  - https://www.galaxyzoo.org/

- **eBird** — bird observations, billions of records, 25+ years
  - Long-term ecological data, statistical modeling, policy impacts
  - Example of sustained engagement and scientific productivity
  - https://ebird.org/science

- **Foldit** — protein folding game, gamification done right
  - Players solved unsolved protein structures, published in *Nature*
  - Demonstrates value of human spatial reasoning over algorithms (pre-AlphaFold)
  - https://fold.it/

- **iNaturalist** — biodiversity observations, 100M+ observations
  - Community verification, AI assistance, research-grade data
  - Example of human-AI collaboration
  - https://www.inaturalist.org/

- **Old Weather** — historical ship logs, climate reconstruction
  - Transcription crowdsourcing, historical data recovery
  - https://www.oldweather.org/

- **Snapshot Serengeti** — camera trap images, wildlife monitoring
  - Massive image dataset, consensus classification, ecological insights
  - https://www.snapshotserengeti.org/

- **Safecast** — radiation monitoring post-Fukushima
  - Community-led, DIY sensors, open data, crisis response
  - Example of grassroots mobilization and data activism
  - https://safecast.org/

- **Public Lab** — environmental justice monitoring
  - Low-cost tools (balloon mapping, spectrometry), community ownership
  - Activist roots, DIY ethos, open hardware
  - https://publiclab.org/

### Failure Case Studies (Learn from Mistakes)

- Projects with high dropout rates due to lack of feedback
- Data unusable due to poor protocol design
- Community backlash when researchers extracted data without reciprocity
- Digital divide excluding target populations
- AI systems replacing volunteers, killing motivation

(These are discussed in literature and workshops but less publicly documented — use academic papers and conference proceedings)
