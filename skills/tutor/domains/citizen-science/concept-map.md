# Citizen Science — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Participatory research** — research involving non-professional scientists in data collection, analysis, or interpretation
2. **Amateur science history** — historical traditions of naturalists and hobbyist scientists. Foundation for modern citizen science
3. **Public engagement** — strategies for involving communities in scientific work
4. **Crowdsourcing** — distributing tasks across many volunteers. Depends on: participatory research
5. **Distributed cognition** — cognitive tasks spread across people and tools. Depends on: crowdsourcing
6. **Online platforms** — web/mobile tools enabling large-scale participation. Depends on: crowdsourcing
7. **Motivation** — intrinsic and extrinsic drivers for participation. Depends on: public engagement
8. **Learning outcomes** — knowledge and skills gained by participants. Depends on: motivation, public engagement
9. **Social capital** — community networks and relationships formed. Depends on: public engagement
10. **Shirk framework** — levels of participation (contributory, collaborative, co-created). Depends on: participatory research
11. **Contributory projects** — volunteers contribute data, professionals analyze. Depends on: Shirk framework
12. **Collaborative projects** — volunteers help refine design and analyze. Depends on: Shirk framework
13. **Co-created research** — communities lead questions and methods. Depends on: Shirk framework
14. **Scalability** — ability to handle large volunteer numbers. Depends on: online platforms, task design
15. **Task design** — structuring activities for volunteer completion. Depends on: crowdsourcing
16. **Accessibility** — removing barriers to participation. Depends on: task design, public engagement
17. **Participatory design** — co-designing with stakeholders. Depends on: co-created research
18. **Stakeholder engagement** — involving affected communities. Depends on: participatory design
19. **Power sharing** — distributing decision-making authority. Depends on: stakeholder engagement, co-created research
20. **Standardization** — ensuring consistent methods across volunteers. Depends on: task design
21. **Protocols** — detailed procedures for data collection. Depends on: standardization
22. **Training materials** — resources for volunteer skill-building. Depends on: protocols
23. **Recruitment strategies** — methods for attracting volunteers. Depends on: motivation, public engagement
24. **Retention** — keeping volunteers engaged over time. Depends on: motivation, community building
25. **Community building** — fostering volunteer networks. Depends on: social capital, retention
26. **Error detection** — identifying mistakes in volunteer data. Depends on: standardization, protocols
27. **Consensus models** — using agreement among volunteers for validation. Depends on: error detection
28. **Expert validation** — professional review of volunteer data. Depends on: error detection
29. **Statistical filtering** — using statistics to identify outliers. Depends on: error detection
30. **Training design** — creating effective learning experiences. Depends on: training materials, learning outcomes
31. **Calibration** — aligning volunteer judgments with standards. Depends on: training design
32. **Feedback loops** — providing volunteers with results of their work. Depends on: motivation, retention
33. **Gamification** — applying game elements to science tasks. Depends on: motivation, task design
34. **Science literacy** — understanding of scientific concepts and processes. Depends on: learning outcomes
35. **Evaluation methods** — assessing project impacts. Depends on: learning outcomes
36. **Inter-rater reliability** — agreement among observers. Depends on: calibration, consensus models
37. **Uncertainty quantification** — measuring data quality statistically. Depends on: statistical filtering, inter-rater reliability
38. **Data ownership** — rights over collected data. Depends on: power sharing, stakeholder engagement
39. **Open data** — publicly accessible datasets. Depends on: data ownership
40. **Policy impact** — influence on regulations and decisions. Depends on: evaluation methods
41. **Extractive research** — taking from communities without benefit. Depends on: power sharing
42. **Reciprocity** — mutual benefit in partnerships. Depends on: extractive research, stakeholder engagement
43. **Decolonization** — challenging colonial research practices. Depends on: power sharing, reciprocity
44. **Digital divide** — unequal access to technology. Depends on: accessibility, online platforms
45. **Inclusive design** — creating for diverse participants. Depends on: accessibility, digital divide
46. **Project builders** — tools for creating citizen science projects. Depends on: online platforms
47. **Human-AI collaboration** — combining human and machine intelligence. Depends on: distributed cognition
48. **Automated validation** — machine checking of data quality. Depends on: error detection, human-AI collaboration
49. **Sensor networks** — distributed environmental monitoring. Depends on: online platforms, standardization
50. **Low-cost sensors** — affordable monitoring devices. Depends on: sensor networks, accessibility

## Dependencies

### Foundational Layer
- **Public engagement** is the bedrock — without it, you cannot recruit or retain volunteers
- **Participatory research** defines the paradigm shift from traditional research hierarchies
- **Amateur science history** provides context and legitimacy for volunteer contributions

### Task Design Layer
- **Task design** must balance scientific rigor with **accessibility** — too complex and volunteers drop out, too simple and data lacks value
- **Scalability** depends on **online platforms** and careful **task design** that can be repeated by thousands
- **Standardization** through **protocols** is critical for data quality, but must not create barriers to **accessibility**

### Engagement Layer
- **Motivation** (intrinsic learning, contribution) drives initial **recruitment** and long-term **retention**
- **Community building** creates **social capital** that sustains projects beyond individual motivation
- **Feedback loops** reinforce **motivation** and **learning outcomes** simultaneously

### Quality Layer
- **Training design** and **calibration** are prerequisites for reliable data
- **Error detection** strategies (consensus, expert review, statistical) work in combination — no single method suffices
- **Inter-rater reliability** measures whether **calibration** and **training** have succeeded

### Power & Ethics Layer
- **Power sharing** is foundational to genuine **co-created research**
- **Extractive research** is the anti-pattern that **reciprocity** and **decolonization** counter
- **Data ownership** and **open data** policies reflect underlying power dynamics

### Technology Layer
- **Online platforms** enable **scalability** but can exacerbate the **digital divide**
- **Human-AI collaboration** extends capacity but must not replace meaningful volunteer contributions
- **Sensor networks** democratize environmental monitoring when using **low-cost sensors**

## Bottlenecks

1. **Data quality vs. accessibility** — Making tasks simple enough for broad participation while maintaining scientific rigor is the central design challenge

2. **Power sharing** — Truly co-created research requires ceding control, which many professional researchers resist. This bottleneck limits projects from reaching the highest levels of participation.

3. **Retention** — Easy to recruit, hard to retain. Projects that don't invest in **community building** and **feedback loops** see 90%+ attrition.

4. **Statistical validation** — Understanding **inter-rater reliability** and **uncertainty quantification** requires statistical sophistication many project leads lack.

5. **Digital divide** — Excellent **online platforms** don't help communities without internet access or devices. **Inclusive design** requires offline alternatives.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"More volunteers = better data"** — Without proper **training**, **protocols**, and **validation**, more volunteers can mean more noise. Quality requires investment in **calibration**.

2. **"Gamification always increases engagement"** — **Gamification** works for some but can trivialize the science for others. Intrinsic **motivation** (learning, contribution) often outlasts extrinsic rewards.

3. **"Citizen science is only for simple tasks"** — While many projects use **contributory** models with simple tasks, **co-created research** tackles complex questions with deep volunteer involvement.

4. **"AI will replace volunteer classifiers"** — **Automated validation** works well on large labeled datasets, but human judgment remains superior for edge cases, novel phenomena, and contextual interpretation.

5. **"Open data solves equity issues"** — **Open data** policies don't automatically ensure equitable access or benefit. **Data ownership** and **reciprocity** must be explicitly designed.

6. **"Participation automatically improves science literacy"** — **Learning outcomes** require intentional design. Data collection alone doesn't guarantee understanding of scientific concepts or processes.

## Critical Paths

### For launching a successful project:
Amateur science history → Participatory research → Shirk framework → Task design → Protocols → Training design → Recruitment → Retention

### For ensuring data quality:
Protocols → Standardization → Training design → Calibration → Error detection → Inter-rater reliability → Uncertainty quantification

### For equitable partnerships:
Public engagement → Stakeholder engagement → Participatory design → Power sharing → Reciprocity → Data ownership

### For sustained engagement:
Motivation → Recruitment → Community building → Feedback loops → Learning outcomes → Retention
