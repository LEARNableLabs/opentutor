# Climate Science — Modeling and Feedbacks: Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Energy Balance Models** — Simple analytical models that can be solved by hand to understand basic climate dynamics
2. **Atmospheric Physics** — Radiation, greenhouse effect, atmospheric circulation patterns
3. **Ocean Circulation** — Thermohaline circulation, heat transport, ocean-atmosphere coupling
4. **Climate Feedbacks** — Ice-albedo, water vapor, cloud feedbacks, carbon cycle interactions
5. **General Circulation Models (GCMs)** — Computational models used by IPCC and climate researchers
6. **Model Evaluation & Uncertainty** — Parameterization, sensitivity analysis, model intercomparison

## Key Educational Resources Found

### Textbooks

- **Climate System Dynamics and Modelling** by Hugues Goosse (Cambridge University Press) — Graduate/advanced undergraduate text covering all climate system components (atmosphere, ocean, land, ice) with emphasis on feedbacks and interactions
- **Global Warming: Understanding the Forecast** by David Archer (Wiley, 2011) — Accessible, mostly qualitative introduction
- **Climate System Modeling** by K.E. Trenberth (Cambridge University Press, 2009)

### University Courses

- **MIT OCW 12.340 Global Warming Science** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/12-340-global-warming-science-spring-2012/) — Uses hierarchy of models from simple analytical to 1D numerical
- **University of Michigan CLIMATE 589: The Art of Climate Modeling** — Hands-on with CESM and MPAS models
- **University of Chicago Coursera: Global Warming I** (https://www.coursera.org/learn/global-warming) — Includes exercises building simple climate models (analytical and numerical)
- **Andreas Schmittner's Climate Modeling Course Notes** (https://andreasschmittner.github.io/Teaching/ATS421-521/2015/CourseNotes.pdf)

### Video & Interactive Resources

- **MIT Open Learning Library 12.340x** (https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+12.340x+1T2020/jump_to/block-v1:MITx+12.340x+1T2020+type@sequential+block@GCMs) — Module on GCMs

### Key Topics Emphasized Across Sources

- **Feedback mechanisms** are central to intermediate climate science — positive feedbacks (ice-albedo, water vapor, permafrost methane) vs negative feedbacks (blackbody radiation, lapse rate)
- **Model hierarchy** — progression from zero-dimensional energy balance to GCMs
- **Hands-on modeling** — building simple models in Python/Julia is pedagogically effective
- **Ocean-atmosphere coupling** — critical interaction often underemphasized in intro courses
- **Uncertainty quantification** — essential for understanding climate projections

## Available Interactive Tools

- Climate model simulators (mentioned in MIT climate seminar resources)
- Python/Julia modeling exercises (Class Central aggregations)
- MARGO framework for scenario exploration

## Research Gaps Identified

While there are excellent resources for the extremes (popular science and advanced graduate work), intermediate-level resources that bridge analytical understanding with computational practice are limited. The sweet spot is: enough physics to understand the mechanisms, enough computation to build working models, not so much detail that students drown in parameterization schemes.
