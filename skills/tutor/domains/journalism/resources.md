# Journalism and Investigative Reporting — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Handbooks
- **"The Investigative Reporter's Handbook" (IRE)** — comprehensive guide covering every phase of investigative work from story selection through publication. Industry standard for intermediate-to-advanced reporters.
- **"Story-Based Inquiry: A Manual for Investigative Journalists" by Mark Lee Hunter** — systematic methodology for planning and executing investigations. Available free from UNESCO and GIJN.
- **"The Art of Access: Strategies for Acquiring Public Records" by David Cuillier & Charles N. Davis** — deep dive into FOIA, state open records laws, and advocacy for transparency.
- **"Verification Handbook" (European Journalism Centre)** — free online guide to verifying user-generated content, digital evidence, and crisis reporting. Essential for digital verification.

### University Courses and Syllabi
- **Columbia Journalism School** (https://journalism.columbia.edu/) — Graduate program with strong investigative focus; public syllabi available from various faculty.
- **UC Berkeley Graduate School of Journalism** (https://journalism.berkeley.edu/) — Investigative Reporting Program with case studies and methodology.
- **Northwestern Medill School** (https://www.medill.northwestern.edu/) — Investigative specialization with emphasis on accountability journalism.
- **Arizona State Cronkite School** (https://cronkite.asu.edu/) — Strong investigative program with Carnegie-Knight News21 investigative projects.

### Professional Organizations
- **Investigative Reporters and Editors (IRE)** (https://www.ire.org/) — Essential membership organization. Resource center with 25,000+ tip sheets, annual conference, regional training, database library.
- **Global Investigative Journalism Network (GIJN)** (https://gijn.org/) — International network with multilingual guides, tool directories, and case studies from 80+ countries.
- **ProPublica** (https://www.propublica.org/) — Leading nonprofit newsroom. "Reporting Recipes" series breaks down investigation methods by topic (https://www.propublica.org/series/reporting-recipes).
- **Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project (OCCRP)** (https://www.occrp.org/) — Cross-border investigations, tools for financial investigations, offshore leak databases.

### Interactive Tools and Platforms

#### Document Management
- **DocumentCloud** (https://www.documentcloud.org/) — Free platform for uploading, annotating, and publishing source documents. Supports OCR, search, and embedding.
- **Overview Docs** (https://www.overviewdocs.com/) — Document clustering and visualization for large document sets.
- **Tabula** (https://tabula.technology/) — Extract tables from PDFs into spreadsheets. Essential for liberating data from government reports.

#### Data Analysis
- **NICAR (National Institute for Computer-Assisted Reporting)** (https://www.ire.org/nicar/) — IRE's data journalism division. Training, tool guides, database library.
- **DataBasic** (https://www.databasic.io/) — Suite of simple tools for data exploration: word counting, connection mapping, sampling.
- **Google Sheets / Excel** — Core tools for data journalism. NICAR and IRE offer extensive training materials.
- **OpenRefine** (https://openrefine.org/) — Free tool for cleaning messy data. Essential for working with government datasets.

#### FOIA and Public Records
- **MuckRock** (https://www.muckrock.com/) — Platform for filing, tracking, and sharing FOIA requests. Crowdsourced FOIA library.
- **FOIA.gov** (https://www.foia.gov/) — Central federal FOIA portal with agency directories and submission options.
- **Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press (RCFP)** (https://www.rcfp.org/) — Legal resources, open government guide, FOIA templates, legal hotline for journalists.
- **State open records guides** — RCFP maintains state-by-state guides at https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/

#### Digital Verification
- **InVID/WeVerify Plugin** (https://weverify.eu/verification-plugin/) — Browser extension for video and image verification, reverse search, metadata extraction.
- **TinEye** (https://tineye.com/) — Reverse image search to find image origins and manipulations.
- **Jeffrey's Image Metadata Viewer** (http://exif.regex.info/exif.cgi) — Extract EXIF data from images to verify time, location, camera.
- **Wayback Machine** (https://archive.org/web/) — Internet Archive for checking historical versions of websites and verifying deleted content.

#### OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence)
- **Bellingcat Online Investigation Toolkit** (https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/18rtqh8EG2q1xBo2cLNyhIDuK9jrPGwYr9DI2UncoqJQ/) — Comprehensive spreadsheet of OSINT tools categorized by use case.
- **GIJN's Resource Center** (https://gijn.org/resources/) — Curated tools and guides organized by investigation type.
- **IntelTechniques Tools** (https://inteltechniques.com/tools/) — Collection of search tools for social media, email addresses, usernames, etc.
- **Google Advanced Search** (https://www.google.com/advanced_search) — Underutilized tool for precise searches using site:, filetype:, and date filters.

#### Secure Communication
- **Signal** (https://signal.org/) — Encrypted messaging app. Industry standard for source communication.
- **SecureDrop** (https://securedrop.org/) — Open-source whistleblower submission system. Used by major newsrooms.
- **Freedom of the Press Foundation** (https://freedom.press/) — Resources for digital security, encryption, source protection.
- **Electronic Frontier Foundation's Surveillance Self-Defense** (https://ssd.eff.org/) — Guides for protecting communications and sources.

## Using These Resources

### For Curriculum Lessons
Each lesson links to 1-3 resources directly relevant to that lesson's concepts. These should be:
- **Free or widely accessible** (avoid paywalls when possible)
- **Evergreen** (prefer resources that won't disappear or become outdated quickly)
- **Action-oriented** (prefer guides over theory when teaching intermediate students)

### For Independent Learning
Students should explore:
- **IRE Resource Center** — search for tip sheets on topics they're struggling with
- **GIJN Resources** — browse by investigation type (corruption, environment, health, etc.)
- **Tool experimentation** — try DocumentCloud, Tabula, InVID plugin on real documents/images

### For Going Deeper
Advanced students or those specializing should:
- **Attend conferences** — IRE annual conference, GIJN Global Summit, regional IRE trainings
- **Join organizations** — IRE membership ($25-70) unlocks database library and resource center
- **Take specialized courses** — Knight Center MOOCs (free), Poynter courses (paid), university programs
- **Read investigations** — study ProPublica, ICIJ, Marshall Project, The Markup for methodology notes

### For Staying Current
Investigative journalism evolves rapidly. Students should:
- **Follow @IRE_NICAR, @GIJN, @Bellingcat** on social media for new tools and techniques
- **Subscribe to newsletters** — NICAR-L listserv, GIJN newsletter, Nieman Lab
- **Read methodology notes** — when publications like The Markup or ProPublica publish investigations, read the "How we did it" section
- **Experiment with new tools** — verification tech, AI-assisted investigation, satellite imagery analysis

## Final Notes

These resources represent a starting point, not an exhaustive list. Investigative journalism is a practice discipline — the best learning happens by doing. Students should:
1. **Start with IRE and GIJN** — these are the central hubs
2. **Build a personal toolkit** — identify the 5-10 tools they use most and master them
3. **Learn from published investigations** — reverse-engineer how reporters found, verified, and told stories
4. **Connect with practitioners** — join local/regional journalism organizations, attend meetups, find mentors

The resources here support the curriculum, but the curriculum itself — the practice of reporting — is where learning happens.
