# Journalism and investigative reporting — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 11 lessons (39%)
- **Socratic questions** — 5 lessons (18%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 3 lessons (11%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (4%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 29% accessible (1-2), 57% standard (3), 14% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 11: "Why do agencies deny requests, and how do you fight back?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 17: "How do you interview trauma survivors ethically?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 23: "How do you verify documents in the age of deepfakes?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 25: "How do you avoid getting sued while publishing the truth?" (difficulty 4)

This field covers journalism and investigative reporting, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.
