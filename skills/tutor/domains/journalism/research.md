# Research Summary — Journalism and Investigative Reporting

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Source Development and Cultivation
Building and maintaining relationships with sources, protecting confidentiality, navigating power dynamics, developing trust networks, working with whistleblowers.

### 2. Document Analysis and FOIA
Public records research, Freedom of Information Act requests, document verification, database analysis, paper trail investigation, understanding government records systems.

### 3. Data Journalism Fundamentals
Basic statistical literacy for journalists, working with datasets, data visualization, spreadsheet analysis, identifying patterns in structured data, data verification.

### 4. Interview Techniques
Advanced interviewing strategies, hostile interview management, trauma-informed interviewing, extracting information from reluctant sources, phone vs. in-person dynamics.

### 5. Investigative Frameworks
Story structure for investigations, hypothesis testing in journalism, establishing timelines, following the money, connecting disparate information, building narrative from evidence.

### 6. Ethics and Legal Considerations
Shield laws, defamation risk, off-the-record protocols, conflicts of interest, transparency standards, protecting vulnerable subjects, invasion of privacy boundaries.

### 7. Verification and Fact-Checking
Multi-source verification, document authentication, digital verification tools, debunking misinformation, establishing chain of custody for evidence.

### 8. Digital Investigation Tools
Open-source intelligence (OSINT), social media investigation, reverse image search, metadata analysis, digital footprint tracking, secure communication tools.

- **ProPublica's "Reporting Recipe" series** — topic-specific investigative guides

## Gaps and Considerations for Intermediate Level

This intermediate curriculum assumes students already have:
- Basic news writing and reporting skills
- Understanding of journalism ethics fundamentals
- Ability to conduct standard interviews
- Basic research and fact-checking skills

The curriculum will build toward:
- Multi-source long-form investigations
- Advanced FOIA and public records work
- Data-driven investigative projects
- Cross-platform story development
- Collaborative investigative work
