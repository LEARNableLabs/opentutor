# Podcast and Audio Storytelling — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books
- **Reality Radio: Telling True Stories in Sound** edited by John Biewen & Alexa Dilworth — anthology of essays by working audio producers on craft and ethics
- **Out on the Wire: The Storytelling Secrets of the New Masters of Radio** by Jessica Abel — graphic nonfiction breakdown of This American Life's methods
- **Sound Reporting: The NPR Guide to Audio Journalism and Production** by Jonathan Kern — comprehensive textbook from NPR training staff
- **The Podcast Book** by Maeve Adams — production guide covering end-to-end workflow
- **Transom Manifesto** — collection of essays from Transom.org workshops (available online: https://transom.org/topics/manifesto/)

### Online Educational Platforms
- **Transom.org** (https://transom.org) — the definitive resource for audio storytelling: tools, techniques, reviews, and the HowSound podcast
- **NPR Training** (https://training.npr.org) — guides and articles from NPR's internal training program, covering journalism and production
- **AIR (Association of Independents in Radio)** (https://airmedia.org) — professional association with workshops, funding, and community
- **Third Coast International Audio Festival** (https://thirdcoastfestival.org) — annual conference and extensive online audio library
- **PRX (Public Radio Exchange)** (https://www.prx.org) — distribution platform with creator resources and community

### Courses
- **Berklee Online: Introduction to Audio Production** — structured course on recording and mixing fundamentals
- **Coursera: Audio Engineering for Music Production** (University of Rochester) — technical foundation applicable to podcasting
- **LinkedIn Learning / Lynda**: DAW-specific tutorials (Audacity, Reaper, Pro Tools, Hindenburg)
- **MasterClass**: Ira Glass (storytelling), Shonda Rhimes (writing for audio narrative)

### University Syllabi (examples to study)
- NYU Journalism: Audio Storytelling syllabus
- Columbia Journalism School: Radio Documentary course materials
- USC Annenberg: Podcast Production course outline

## Tools and Software

### Digital Audio Workstations (DAWs)
- **Audacity** (https://www.audacityteam.org) — free, open-source, cross-platform. Great for beginners.
- **Reaper** (https://www.reaper.fm) — $60, powerful, scripting-friendly. Excellent value.
- **Hindenburg Journalist** (https://hindenburg.com) — designed specifically for radio/podcast production. $95-$375.
- **Adobe Audition** (https://www.adobe.com/products/audition.html) — professional, part of Creative Cloud. $20.99/month.
- **Pro Tools** (https://www.avid.com/pro-tools) — industry standard for audio post-production. $29.99/month.
- **Descript** (https://www.descript.com) — edit audio by editing text transcript. Innovative workflow. Free tier available.

### Recording (Remote Multi-Track)
- **Zencastr** (https://zencastr.com) — browser-based remote recording. Free and paid tiers.
- **SquadCast** (https://squadcast.fm) — high-quality remote recording. $20-$40/month.
- **Riverside.fm** (https://riverside.fm) — records locally on each participant's device. $15-$24/month.
- **Zoom** — widely available but lower audio quality. Use as fallback, not primary tool.

### Hosting Platforms
- **Buzzsprout** (https://www.buzzsprout.com) — beginner-friendly, excellent tutorials. $12-$24/month.
- **Transistor** (https://transistor.fm) — unlimited shows, analytics. $19-$99/month.
- **Libsyn** (https://libsyn.com) — longest-running podcast host. $5-$75/month.
- **Anchor** (https://anchor.fm) — free, owned by Spotify. Good for beginners, limited control.
- **Simplecast** (https://simplecast.com) — analytics-focused, professional tier. $15+/month.

### Transcription
- **Otter.ai** (https://otter.ai) — AI transcription, free tier available.
- **Descript** (https://www.descript.com) — transcription + editing workflow.
- **Rev.com** (https://www.rev.com) — human transcription, $1.50/minute.
- **Sonix** (https://sonix.ai) — automated transcription with editing tools.

### Music Libraries (Royalty-Free)
- **Free Music Archive** (https://freemusicarchive.org) — curated free music, Creative Commons licensed.
- **Incompetech** (https://incompetech.com) — Kevin MacLeod's extensive library, very permissive licensing.
- **YouTube Audio Library** (https://www.youtube.com/audiolibrary) — free for use in any project.
- **Artlist** (https://artlist.io) — subscription music library. $9.99/month.
- **Epidemic Sound** (https://www.epidemicsound.com) — subscription music and SFX. $15/month personal.
- **Blue Dot Sessions** (https://www.sessions.blue) — unique ambient music, free and premium tiers.

### Sound Effects Libraries
- **Freesound** (https://freesound.org) — community-uploaded sounds, Creative Commons.
- **BBC Sound Effects** (https://sound-effects.bbcrewind.co.uk) — 16,000+ BBC archive sounds, free for personal/educational use.
- **Zapsplat** (https://www.zapsplat.com) — large free library with premium tier.
- **Soundsnap** (https://www.soundsnap.com) — professional sound library, subscription.

### Microphones (Recommendations by Budget)

#### Budget ($50-150)
- **Audio-Technica ATR2100x-USB** — dynamic USB/XLR mic, versatile
- **Samson Q2U** — similar to ATR2100x, great value
- **Blue Yeti** — USB condenser, popular but picks up room noise

#### Mid-Range ($150-400)
- **Shure SM7B** — the podcast standard (requires preamp/interface)
- **Rode PodMic** — dynamic, podcast-specific design
- **Electro-Voice RE20** — broadcast standard, warm sound

#### High-End ($400+)
- **Shure SM7B** (with Cloudlifter preamp) — full professional setup
- **Sennheiser MKH 416** — shotgun mic, film/radio standard
- **Neumann TLM 103** — studio condenser, pristine sound

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Cross-Discipline Links
- **Film editing theory** — Walter Murch's *In the Blink of an Eye* applies to audio editing
- **Oral history methods** — techniques from historians preserve voices with care
- **Music composition** — understanding musical structure informs audio narrative arc
- **Theatre sound design** — creating sonic worlds for live performance
- **Video game audio** — interactive soundscapes and adaptive music
- **ASMR and binaural recording** — intimacy and spatial audio techniques
- **Field recording as art** — phonography and acoustic ecology movements

### Historical Context
- **Golden Age of Radio** (1920s-1950s) — where audio storytelling began (Orson Welles, Norman Corwin)
- **NPR's founding** (1970) — establishing public radio journalism standards
- **This American Life's launch** (1995) — modernizing radio storytelling
- **Serial's release** (2014) — bringing podcasting to mainstream consciousness

### Academic Research
- **Sound Studies** — interdisciplinary field examining culture and sound
- **Phonography** — field recording as creative practice
- **Audio engineering journals** — AES (Audio Engineering Society) papers on psychoacoustics and production
- **Media studies** — research on podcast audiences, listening practices, parasocial relationships
