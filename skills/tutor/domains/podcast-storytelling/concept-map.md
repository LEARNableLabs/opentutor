## Dependencies

### Foundational Concepts
The entire curriculum rests on three foundational concepts that should be established early:
- **Audio-first thinking** underpins narrative, interview, and production choices
- **Sonic narrative** is the lens through which all design decisions are made
- **Intimacy of voice** explains why podcasting works as a medium

### Narrative Chain
The narrative concepts build sequentially:
- Story arc provides structure for tension building
- Tension building requires stakes establishment to be meaningful
- Narrative payoff depends on both tension and established stakes
- Scene construction translates abstract structure into concrete moments

### Interview Chain
Interview concepts depend on both preparation and execution skills:
- Question design requires deep research depth
- Emotional access requires the safety created by open-ended questions
- Interview arc applies story structure to conversation flow

### Sound Design Chain
Sound design concepts layer complexity:
- Environmental sound builds on room tone
- Sonic world-building combines environmental sound with field recording mastery
- Sound effects layering adds to established worlds
- Frequency space and panning create room for all these elements

### Production Chain
Production concepts have strict technical dependencies:
- Equalization must come before compression (order matters in signal chain)
- Vocal processing requires understanding both EQ and compression
- Tonal matching depends on having EQ and processing skills
- Mix coherence is the synthesis of all production techniques

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic DAW operation** — needed for: cutting technique, equalization, panning
- **Fundamental narrative structure** — needed for: story arc, scene construction, interview arc
- **Basic microphone technique** — needed for: field recording, vocal delivery
- **File format understanding** — needed for: RSS mechanics, hosting platforms
- **Active listening skills** — needed for: interview techniques, question design, editing decisions

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are prerequisites for many downstream skills — if students struggle here, it will cascade:

1. **Audio-first thinking** — if students default to text thinking, they'll miss sonic opportunities throughout
2. **Scene construction** — inability to build scenes limits interview use, sound design application, and editing
3. **Frequency space** — without this, mixing becomes muddy and frustrating
4. **Cutting technique** — all editing depends on mechanical proficiency
5. **Question design** — weak questions produce weak interviews, limiting available story material

## Common Misconceptions

### About Narrative
- **"Audio stories need more narration"** — Actually, good tape and sound can often replace voice-over. Students over-narrate when they could show instead.
- **"Music makes everything more emotional"** — Music can overwhelm or manipulate. Silence and natural sound are often more powerful.

### About Interviews
- **"More questions = better interview"** — Quality over quantity. One good question with deep follow-ups beats a rapid-fire list.
- **"You need to fill every silence"** — Pauses let subjects think and often lead to the best responses.

### About Technical Production
- **"Louder is better"** — Loudness wars don't apply to podcasts. Hitting -16 LUFS is the target, not a ceiling.
- **"EQ can fix bad recording"** — Some problems can't be fixed in post. Get good sound at recording time.
- **"Compression makes everything sound professional"** — Over-compression kills dynamics and sounds flat. Use it carefully.

### About Distribution
- **"Being on Spotify/Apple is enough"** — Discoverability requires metadata optimization, social presence, and cross-promotion.
- **"Downloads = listeners"** — Many downloads are never heard. Completion rates and engagement matter more.

## Cross-Concept Connections

- **Intimacy of voice** connects to **conversational tone**, **pause and space**, and **emotional access** — they all leverage the unique closeness of audio
- **Story arc** connects to **interview arc**, **energy management**, and **tension building** — structure repeats across scales
- **Sonic world-building** connects to **field recording**, **sound effects layering**, and **signature sound** — all build immersive audio environments
- **Equalization** connects to **frequency space**, **tonal matching**, and **vocal processing** — EQ is the foundation of mixing
