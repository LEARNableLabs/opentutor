# Research Summary — Podcast and Audio Storytelling

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Narrative Structure
- Story arc design for audio-first storytelling
- Three-act structure adaptations for podcasts
- Serialized vs. episodic narrative formats
- Hooks, cliffhangers, and maintaining engagement
- Scene construction and transitions

### 2. Interview Techniques
- Question design and interview preparation
- Active listening and follow-up questions
- Creating intimacy and trust with subjects
- Remote vs. in-person interview dynamics
- Ethical considerations in documentary work

### 3. Audio Recording Fundamentals
- Microphone selection and placement
- Recording environments and acoustic treatment
- Field recording techniques and portable setups
- Signal chain basics (preamps, interfaces, levels)
- Troubleshooting common recording problems

### 4. Sound Design and Atmosphere
- Layering ambient sound and room tone
- Using music to enhance narrative (not overwhelm it)
- Sound effects: diegetic vs. non-diegetic
- Building sonic worlds and sense of place
- Silence as a storytelling tool

### 5. Editing and Pacing
- Cutting for clarity and rhythm
- Managing energy across an episode
- When to use montage vs. extended scenes
- Removing filler without losing authenticity
- DAW workflows (Audacity, Reaper, Pro Tools, Hindenburg)

### 6. Voice and Delivery
- Developing a unique hosting voice
- Script reading vs. conversational delivery
- Controlling pace, inflection, and emphasis
- Avoiding "NPR voice" and other affectations
- Recording voiceover and narration

### 7. Mixing and Mastering
- EQ, compression, and dynamics processing
- Loudness standards (LUFS) for podcasts
- Creating consistent audio across speakers
- Finalizing for distribution platforms
- Batch processing and automation

### 8. Distribution and Audience
- RSS feeds and podcast hosting platforms
- Metadata optimization and discoverability
- Building audience through social and cross-promotion
- Analytics and measuring engagement
- Monetization strategies

## Pedagogical Notes

At the **intermediate level**, students typically:
- Have basic audio editing experience
- Understand fundamental story structure
- Own or have access to recording equipment
- Have produced at least a few podcast episodes or audio pieces
- Are ready to refine craft and develop unique style

The curriculum should emphasize:
- **Craft over tools** — any DAW works; storytelling technique is what matters
- **Listening deeply** — analyzing professional work to understand choices
- **Iterative refinement** — editing is rewriting; first drafts are never final
- **Finding your voice** — moving beyond imitation to authentic style
- **Ethics and responsibility** — documentary work requires trust and care
