# Hot Sauce Fermentation — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Capsaicin structure** — alkaloid molecule that binds to TRPV1 pain receptors
2. **TRPV1 receptors** — ion channels activated by capsaicin, heat, and acid
3. **Capsaicinoid profiles** — different pepper varieties have different ratios of capsaicin analogs
4. **Scoville scale** — standardized measurement of capsaicin concentration
5. **Capsaicin stability** — how fermentation pH and time affect capsaicin concentration. Depends on: capsaicin structure
6. **Heat perception timing** — how capsaicinoid ratios affect onset and duration of heat. Depends on: capsaicinoid profiles, TRPV1 receptors

7. **Lactic acid bacteria (LAB)** — Lactobacillus species that drive fermentation
8. **Anaerobic fermentation** — metabolic pathway where LAB produce lactic acid without oxygen
9. **LAB metabolic pathways** — conversion of sugars to lactic acid, CO2, and flavor compounds. Depends on: anaerobic fermentation
10. **Lactobacillus species succession** — different species dominate at different stages. Depends on: LAB, fermentation stages
11. **Salt percentage** — controls osmotic pressure and selects for LAB over pathogens. Depends on: LAB
12. **Osmotic pressure** — how salt concentration affects microbial cell water balance. Depends on: salt percentage
13. **Fermentation kinetics** — rate of acid production and microbial growth
14. **Temperature effects on LAB** — optimal 60-75°F; faster at higher temps but may skip complexity. Depends on: fermentation kinetics
15. **Fermentation stages** — lag phase, exponential growth, stationary, decline. Depends on: LAB metabolic pathways
16. **pH curves** — how pH drops over time as lactic acid accumulates. Depends on: fermentation stages, LAB metabolic pathways

17. **Flavor compounds** — volatile organic compounds, organic acids, esters produced during fermentation
18. **Volatile organic compounds** — create aroma profile; change dramatically during fermentation. Depends on: flavor compounds
19. **Organic acids** — lactic, acetic, citric contribute to sourness and preservation. Depends on: flavor compounds, LAB metabolic pathways
20. **Acid balance** — ratio of lactic to acetic acid affects sharpness vs. roundness. Depends on: organic acids
21. **Residual sugars** — unconsumed sugars add sweetness; balance heat and acid. Depends on: LAB metabolic pathways
22. **Amino acids and umami** — protein breakdown by LAB creates savory depth. Depends on: LAB metabolic pathways
23. **Barrel aging chemistry** — oak tannins, oxidation, and long-term flavor evolution. Depends on: organic acids, volatile organic compounds
24. **Flavor pairing** — matching pepper volatiles with complementary fruit/spice volatiles. Depends on: volatile organic compounds
25. **Compound evolution over time** — how flavors change week-to-week during fermentation. Depends on: fermentation stages, flavor compounds

26. **Mold types** — surface molds (kahm yeast, aspergillus) vs. penetrating molds
27. **Mycotoxins** — dangerous compounds from certain mold species
28. **Clostridium botulinum** — deadly pathogen suppressed by low pH
29. **pH thresholds for safety** — pH <4.6 prevents botulism; <4.0 is fermentation target. Depends on: pH curves
30. **Starter cultures vs. wild fermentation** — inoculated consistency vs. ambient microbes. Depends on: LAB species succession
31. **Off-odors and visual inspection** — diagnosing fermentation problems early. Depends on: mold types, LAB metabolic pathways
32. **pH testing protocols** — using pH strips or meters to verify safety. Depends on: pH thresholds
33. **Pasteurization** — heat treatment to stop fermentation and kill remaining microbes. Depends on: LAB, shelf stability
34. **Shelf stability** — how pH, water activity, and processing affect storage life. Depends on: pH thresholds, pasteurization

35. **Tasting analysis** — identifying ingredients, processing, and age by flavor
36. **Ingredient identification** — reverse-engineering recipes from finished product. Depends on: flavor pairing, acid balance
37. **Recipe frameworks** — Louisiana-style, Mexican-style, Caribbean-style archetypes
38. **Adjunct ingredients** — garlic, onions, fruit and their fermentation impacts. Depends on: LAB metabolic pathways, residual sugars
39. **Sugar impact of adjuncts** — how added fruits or sweeteners affect fermentation speed. Depends on: adjunct ingredients, fermentation kinetics
40. **Post-fermentation processing** — blending, straining, thickening, pH adjustment. Depends on: acid balance
41. **Texture control** — chunky vs. smooth, body and viscosity. Depends on: post-fermentation processing
42. **Secondary aging** — continuing flavor development after initial fermentation. Depends on: compound evolution
43. **Recipe synthesis** — combining all knowledge into original creations. Depends on: all previous concepts

## Dependencies

### Foundation Layer (Lessons 1-4)
- **TRPV1 receptors** are the biological mechanism behind **capsaicin structure's** effect
- **Capsaicinoid profiles** determine the complexity of heat beyond simple Scoville numbers
- **Capsaicin stability** during fermentation requires understanding both the molecule and the fermentation pH
- **Heat perception timing** is the first design concept, requiring knowledge of profiles and receptors

### LAF Layer (Lessons 5-9)
- **LAB metabolic pathways** are the engine of fermentation and prerequisite for understanding all downstream effects
- **Salt percentage** and **osmotic pressure** work together to create the right environment for LAB
- **Temperature effects** and **fermentation kinetics** determine the speed and quality of fermentation
- **Fermentation stages** and **pH curves** are the observable manifestations of microbial activity

### Flavor Layer (Lessons 11-15)
- All flavor concepts depend on **LAB metabolic pathways** as the source of transformation
- **Acid balance** requires understanding **organic acids** from fermentation
- **Compound evolution** requires understanding **fermentation stages** timeline
- **Flavor pairing** builds on knowing what **volatile organic compounds** exist in different ingredients
- **Barrel aging** is an advanced extension of **compound evolution**

### Safety Layer (Lessons 17-21)
- **pH thresholds** are the key safety metric, built on understanding **pH curves** from fermentation
- **Clostridium botulinum** risk is prevented by proper **pH thresholds**
- **Off-odor diagnosis** requires baseline knowledge of normal **LAB metabolic pathways**
- **Pasteurization** decision depends on understanding trade-offs with live **LAB** benefits
- **Shelf stability** integrates **pH thresholds**, **pasteurization**, and water activity

### Design Layer (Lessons 23-27)
- **Tasting analysis** requires deep knowledge of **flavor compounds**, **acid balance**, and **aging**
- **Recipe frameworks** organize existing knowledge of regional styles
- **Adjunct ingredients** impact requires understanding their **sugar content** and **microbial effects**
- **Post-fermentation processing** builds on **acid balance** and **texture** goals
- **Recipe synthesis** is the capstone, requiring integration of all previous concepts

## Critical Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts gate significant portions of the curriculum:

1. **LAB metabolic pathways** (Lesson 6) — unlocks all flavor and safety understanding
2. **pH curves** (Lesson 9) — gates safety layer and troubleshooting
3. **Acid balance** (Lesson 12) — required for all flavor design work
4. **pH thresholds for safety** (Lesson 18) — gates all recipe experimentation
5. **Recipe synthesis** (Lesson 27) — integrates everything; student shouldn't reach this until prior concepts are solid

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic chemistry** — needed for: molecular structure (capsaicin), pH, acids/bases, osmotic pressure
- **Basic microbiology** — needed for: bacterial metabolism, anaerobic vs. aerobic, contamination, pathogens
- **Kitchen safety** — needed for: food handling, contamination prevention, safe tasting practices

## Common Misconception Anchors

Certain concepts are frequently misunderstood and require extra attention:

- **Capsaicin stability** (Lesson 3) — students often think fermentation "cooks off" heat
- **Salt percentage** (Lesson 7) — confusion between % by weight of vegetables vs. % of total water
- **Temperature effects** (Lesson 8) — belief that "warmer is always faster and better"
- **Mold types** (Lesson 17) — students often panic at harmless kahm yeast or dump at first white spot
- **Pasteurization** (Lesson 21) — students don't understand the probiotic vs. safety trade-off
- **Adjunct sugar impact** (Lesson 25) — underestimating how fruit sugars speed up and change fermentation

## Learning Sequence Rationale

The curriculum follows this arc:

1. **Hook with familiar sensation** (Lessons 1-4) — start with the burn everyone knows, explain it scientifically
2. **Core mechanism** (Lessons 5-9) — deep dive into how LAF actually works
3. **First review** (Lesson 10) — consolidate before moving to applied topics
4. **Flavor artistry** (Lessons 11-15) — now that they know the mechanism, explore the creative palette
5. **Second review** (Lesson 16) — consolidate flavor concepts before safety
6. **Safety and troubleshooting** (Lessons 17-21) — gate recipe experimentation behind safety knowledge
7. **Third review** (Lesson 22) — consolidate before synthesis
8. **Creative synthesis** (Lessons 23-27) — apply everything to original recipe design

This sequence ensures students understand WHY before HOW, and have safety knowledge before they start experimenting.
