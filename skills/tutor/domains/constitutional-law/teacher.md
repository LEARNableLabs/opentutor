# Constitutional Law — Comparative Systems — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **Socratic questions** — 7 lessons (26%)
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 6 lessons (22%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (19%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (4%)

Primary format: Socratic questions.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 30% accessible (1-2), 44% standard (3), 26% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 8: "Can a president and prime minister share power without constant conflict?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 12: "Should constitutional review be concentrated in one special court or spread across all courts?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 15: "When should courts refuse to answer constitutional questions?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 17: "Can constitutional rights stop private companies from discriminating?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 18: "How do you balance free speech against national security?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers constitutional law — comparative systems, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.
