## Common Failure Modes
1. **"The Pre-Socratics were primitive proto-scientists"**
   - Why students get this wrong: They see questions like "what is everything made of?" as merely scientific.
   - How to correct: Emphasize the metaphysical ambition (seeking ultimate principles) and the role of rational argument over empirical observation. Show how Parmenides used pure logic, not observation.

2. **"Socrates never said anything definitive, he just asked questions"**
   - Why students get this wrong: The elenchus seems purely destructive; students miss Socrates' positive commitments.
   - How to correct: Highlight Socrates' firm beliefs (virtue is knowledge, the examined life, soul > body) that emerge consistently across dialogues. The method is questioning, but the aim is discovering truth.
