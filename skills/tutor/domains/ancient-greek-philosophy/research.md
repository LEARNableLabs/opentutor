# Ancient Greek Philosophy — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Pre-Socratic Philosophy (c. 600-400 BCE)
- Natural philosophy and cosmology (Thales, Anaximander, Anaximenes)
- Pythagoreanism (mathematics, harmony, transmigration)
- Heraclitus (flux, logos, unity of opposites)
- Parmenides and Eleatic school (being, monism, logical argument)
- Pluralists (Empedocles, Anaxagoras, Atomists)

### 2. Sophists and Socrates (c. 450-399 BCE)
- Sophistic movement (rhetoric, relativism, skepticism)
- Protagoras and Gorgias
- Socratic method (elenchus, dialectic)
- Socratic ethics (virtue, knowledge, examined life)

### 3. Plato (c. 428-348 BCE)
- Theory of Forms (metaphysics, epistemology)
- The soul and virtue
- Political philosophy (Republic, ideal state)
- Dialectic and the divided line
- Love and beauty (Symposium)

### 4. Aristotle (384-322 BCE)
- Logic and Categories
- Metaphysics (substance, causation, potentiality/actuality)
- Ethics (virtue ethics, eudaimonia, golden mean)
- Politics and community
- Natural philosophy and biology

### 5. Hellenistic Philosophy (c. 323-31 BCE)
- Stoicism (Zeno, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius) — logic, physics, ethics
- Epicureanism (atomism, pleasure, ataraxia)
- Skepticism (Pyrrho, suspension of judgment)
- Cynicism (Diogenes, living according to nature)

## Level Assessment for Intermediate

**Assumes:**
- Familiarity with basic philosophical concepts (ethics, metaphysics, epistemology)
- Comfort with abstract reasoning
- Some exposure to philosophical argument structure
- Reading stamina for primary sources with guidance

**Appropriate depth:**
- Engage directly with excerpts from primary texts
- Understand historical context and influence
- Compare positions across thinkers
- Apply concepts to contemporary questions
- Basic engagement with scholarly interpretations (not deep philology)

**Avoid:**
- Detailed Greek language study
- Exhaustive coverage of minor figures
- Deep dives into textual scholarship debates
- Advanced metaphysics requiring formal logic background
