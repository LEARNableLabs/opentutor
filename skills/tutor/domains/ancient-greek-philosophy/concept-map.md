# Ancient Greek Philosophy — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Natural Philosophy** — the shift from mythological to rational explanations of nature
2. **Archē** — the fundamental principle or substance from which all things originate
3. **Material Monism** — the view that reality is made of one fundamental substance (Thales: water)
4. **Flux and Logos** — Heraclitus's view that change is fundamental but governed by rational principle
5. **Unity of Opposites** — Heraclitus's idea that opposites are interconnected and interdependent
6. **Being vs. Becoming** — Parmenides' radical distinction between what is (unchanging) and apparent change (illusion)
7. **Atomism** — Democritus's theory that reality consists of indivisible atoms moving in void
8. **Sophistry and Relativism** — the Sophists' challenge to absolute truth and focus on rhetoric
9. **Socratic Method (Elenchus)** — questioning to reveal contradictions and achieve aporia (state of puzzlement)
10. **Virtue as Knowledge** — Socrates' belief that knowing the good leads necessarily to doing the good
11. **The Examined Life** — Socrates' principle that self-knowledge and questioning are essential to virtue
12. **Theory of Forms** — Plato's metaphysical claim that perfect, eternal Forms exist separately from material particulars. Depends on: Parmenides (being), Socratic definitions
13. **Participation** — the relationship between particulars and Forms (how things "share in" Forms)
14. **The Good** — Plato's highest Form, source of truth and being
15. **Allegory of the Cave** — Plato's metaphor for education and the ascent from ignorance to knowledge. Depends on: Theory of Forms
16. **Tripartite Soul** — Plato's division of soul into rational, spirited, and appetitive parts
17. **Justice (Platonic)** — harmony among parts of the soul; each part doing its proper function. Depends on: Tripartite Soul
18. **Philosopher-King** — Plato's ideal ruler who has knowledge of Forms and the Good
19. **Eros and the Ascent** — Plato's ladder of love from physical beauty to knowledge of the Form of Beauty
20. **Immanent Forms** — Aristotle's revision: forms exist within substances, not separately. Depends on: Theory of Forms (rejection)
21. **Substance (Ousia)** — Aristotle's fundamental category: individual things that exist independently
22. **Four Causes** — Aristotle's analysis of why things exist: material, formal, efficient, final. Depends on: Substance
23. **Potentiality and Actuality** — Aristotle's framework for understanding change and development
24. **Virtue Ethics** — Aristotle's focus on character and excellence developed through habituation. Depends on: Socratic virtue
25. **Golden Mean** — Aristotle's principle that virtue lies between extremes. Depends on: Virtue Ethics
26. **Eudaimonia** — human flourishing or well-being as the highest good. Depends on: Virtue Ethics
27. **Political Nature** — Aristotle's claim that humans are naturally social and political animals
28. **Syllogism** — Aristotle's formal logical structure for valid deductive reasoning
29. **Stoicism** — Hellenistic school emphasizing virtue, rationality, and acceptance of fate. Depends on: Socratic virtue, Aristotelian logic
30. **Dichotomy of Control** — Stoic distinction between what is and isn't in our power
31. **Cosmopolitanism** — Stoic view of universal human community
32. **Epicureanism** — Hellenistic school seeking ataraxia (tranquility) through pleasure and absence of pain. Depends on: Atomism
33. **Ataraxia** — tranquility or freedom from disturbance (goal for both Epicureans and Skeptics)
34. **Skepticism (Pyrrhonian)** — suspension of judgment (epochē) to achieve ataraxia
35. **Cynicism** — radical rejection of conventional values to live according to nature

## Dependencies

### Major Conceptual Threads

**Metaphysics: What exists?**
- Archē → Material Monism → Atomism (materialist line)
- Being vs. Becoming → Theory of Forms → Immanent Forms (idealist line evolving)
- Theory of Forms requires understanding of Parmenides' being and Socratic search for definitions

**Epistemology: How do we know?**
- Socratic Method → Platonic Dialectic → Aristotelian Logic
- Sophistic Relativism provides the skeptical challenge that Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle respond to
- Allegory of the Cave depends on Theory of Forms
- Skepticism (epochē) responds to all prior epistemological claims

**Ethics: How should we live?**
- Virtue as Knowledge → Platonic Justice → Aristotelian Virtue Ethics → Stoicism/Epicureanism/Cynicism
- All Hellenistic schools respond to the question "what is the good life?" raised by Socrates
- Golden Mean refines earlier virtue concepts
- Eudaimonia becomes the shared goal, but schools differ on means

**Politics: How should we organize society?**
- Socratic questioning of democracy → Plato's ideal state → Aristotle's political nature → Stoic cosmopolitanism
- Philosopher-King requires Theory of Forms and Tripartite Soul
- Cynicism radically rejects the political project

### Critical Transitions

1. **Pre-Socratics to Socrates** — shift from cosmological questions to ethical/epistemological ones
2. **Socrates to Plato** — addition of metaphysical system (Forms) to ground ethics
3. **Plato to Aristotle** — grounding philosophy in the natural world rather than transcendent realm
4. **Classical to Hellenistic** — shift from theoretical to practical philosophy, focus on individual flourishing in uncertain times

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic philosophical concepts** — needed for: understanding distinctions between metaphysics, epistemology, ethics
- **Logical reasoning** — needed for: following philosophical arguments, understanding Socratic method, Aristotelian syllogisms
- **Abstract thinking** — needed for: grasping Theory of Forms, understanding metaphysical debates
- **Historical context of ancient Greece** — helpful for: understanding Sophists, democracy debates, Hellenistic anxiety

## Bottleneck Concepts

These are especially difficult and require extra attention:

1. **Theory of Forms** — students often struggle to understand why Plato posits a separate realm; critical for understanding Plato's entire system
2. **Participation** — the relationship between Forms and particulars is philosophically murky even for experts
3. **Four Causes** — students conflate causes or miss that formal/final causes are non-mechanical
4. **Potentiality/Actuality** — requires shift from static to dynamic thinking about existence
5. **Skeptical suspension of judgment** — seems self-refuting; hard to distinguish from relativism

## Common Confusions

- **Confusing Socrates with Plato** — hard to separate historical Socrates from Plato's portrayal
- **Thinking all Pre-Socratics are materialists** — Parmenides and Pythagoras challenge this
- **Assuming Stoicism = emotionlessness** — actually about proper emotional response, not suppression
- **Conflating Epicureanism with hedonism** — Epicurus advocated moderate pleasure, not excess
- **Missing the political context** — philosophy emerges in response to Greek city-state politics and later Hellenistic empire
