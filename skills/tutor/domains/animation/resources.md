# Animation — Principles and Techniques: Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Texts
- **The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation** by Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston
  - The definitive reference for the 12 principles
  - Rich with historical context and behind-the-scenes Disney stories
  - Best for: understanding *why* principles were developed, not just what they are
  - Level fit: Intermediate to advanced (dense but essential)

- **The Animator's Survival Kit** by Richard Williams (2001)
  - Comprehensive, practical guide covering timing, walks, runs, weight, and performance
  - Clear diagrams and frame-by-frame breakdowns
  - Available as both book and video series
  - Best for: hands-on learning with specific exercises
  - Level fit: Perfect for intermediate students — actionable and well-structured

- **Timing for Animation** by Harold Whitaker and John Halas
  - Deep dive into frame-by-frame timing decisions
  - Technical but approachable
  - Best for: lessons 8-13 (timing module)
  - Level fit: Intermediate — assumes basic animation knowledge

### University Courses and Curricula
- **Khan Academy — Pixar in a Box**: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar
  - Free, interactive lessons covering animation fundamentals
  - Includes exercises and real Pixar examples
  - Best for: visual learners who want hands-on interaction
  - Modules on character modeling, rigging, and animation

- **Animation Mentor**: https://www.animationmentor.com/
  - Professional online animation school
  - Structured curriculum with mentor feedback
  - Best for: students wanting guided, intensive training
  - Note: Paid courses, but free resources and blogs available

- **CalArts Character Animation** program (reference syllabi)
  - One of the top animation schools globally
  - Student work showcases demonstrate progression
  - Best for: seeing what professional training looks like

## Supplementary Resources

### Video Tutorials and Channels

- **Alan Becker — 12 Principles of Animation**: https://www.youtube.com/user/noogai89
  - Clear, visual breakdown of each principle with examples
  - Short, digestible format perfect for daily lessons
  - Highly recommended for lessons 1-6

- **New Frame Plus**: https://www.youtube.com/c/NewFramePlus
  - Animation analysis in games and film
  - Focus on storytelling through movement
  - Best for: lessons on character performance and advanced integration
  - Great for critical analysis practice

- **Howard Wimshurst**: https://www.youtube.com/c/HowardWimshurst
  - Animation tutorials, career advice, and process breakdowns
  - Practical, industry-focused content
  - Best for: workflow tips and professional context

- **Toniko Pantoja**: https://www.youtube.com/c/TonikoPantoja
  - Animation principles and character design
  - Focus on appeal and personality
  - Best for: character-focused lessons (14-18)

- **The Royal Ocean Film Society** — animation analysis essays
  - Video essays on animation history and technique
  - Best for: context and inspiration, not technical instruction

### Interactive Tools and Software

- **Blender** (free, open-source): https://www.blender.org/
  - Full 3D animation suite
  - Extensive documentation and community tutorials
  - Best for: students interested in 3D animation
  - Grease pencil tool excellent for 2D animation as well

- **Krita** (free, open-source): https://krita.org/
  - 2D animation with frame-by-frame capabilities
  - Great onion skinning and timeline tools
  - Best for: 2D animators on a budget

- **Animatron** (browser-based): https://www.animatron.com/
  - No installation required, works in browser
  - Good for quick tests and experiments
  - Best for: students who can't install software or want immediate access

- **Toon Boom Harmony** (industry standard)
  - Professional 2D animation software
  - Used by most animation studios
  - Note: Expensive, but free trial available

- **Dragonframe** (stop-motion)
  - Industry standard for stop-motion animation
  - Best for: students interested in physical animation

### Online Animation Schools and Platforms

  - High-quality, curated content
  - Best for: character design and advanced animation techniques
  - Note: Paid subscription

- **iAnimate**: https://www.ianimate.net/

- **Animation Collaborative** (formerly AnimationMentor)

### Books — Additional Reading

- **Character Animation Crash Course** by Eric Goldberg
  - Fast-paced, practical guide from Disney veteran
  - Best for: walk cycles, character performance

- **Acting for Animators** by Ed Hooks
  - Focus on performance and storytelling through motion
  - Best for: lessons 14-18 (character performance module)

- **Drawn to Life** by Walt Stanchfield (two volumes)
  - Collected lecture notes from Disney drawing classes
  - Emphasis on gesture, clarity, and appeal
  - Best for: pose and gesture work (lesson 15)

- **Elemental Magic** by Joseph Gilland
  - Specialized guide for effects animation
  - Best for: lesson 21 (water/effects)

## Code and Technical Resources

- **Blender Python API**: https://docs.blender.org/api/current/
  - For students interested in procedural animation or rigging
  - Best for: advanced students exploring animation programming

- **Easing functions reference**: https://easings.net/
  - Visual reference for different easing curves
  - Interactive tool to see timing variations
  - Best for: lessons 5, 8-12 (timing and easing)

- **Physics simulation resources**
  - Box2D, Bullet Physics — open source physics engines
  - Best for: understanding realistic physics for lesson 19-21

## Unexpected Connections

### Animation in Other Domains
- **Game Animation** — interactive constraints, state machines, blend trees
  - Study God of War, The Last of Us, Uncharted for cinematic game animation
  - Understanding how animation adapts to player input

- **UI/UX Animation** — micro-interactions, delightful details in interfaces
  - Principles of animation apply to button clicks, page transitions
  - Study Material Design motion guidelines

- **Data Visualization Animation** — using motion to convey information
  - Hans Rosling's Gapminder presentations
  - Motion design in journalism (New York Times, The Pudding)

### Cross-Disciplinary Inspiration
- **Dance and choreography** — especially for character performance lessons
  - Study Martha Graham, Bob Fosse for movement vocabulary
  - Ballet positions inform strong pose work

- **Martial arts** — for action animation and weight transfer
  - Understanding real fighting physics helps animated combat feel right

- **Theatre and improv** — for character acting and timing
  - Animation is acting — study performance arts

- **Music and rhythm** — timing in animation parallels musical timing
  - Tempo, rhythm, accents all apply to motion
  - Study music videos for rhythm-driven animation

### Historical Context
- **Early animation history** — Winsor McCay, Fleischer Studios, early Disney
  - Understanding where principles came from
  - Study Gertie the Dinosaur (1914) for early character animation

- **The golden age of animation** — 1930s-1960s Warner Bros, MGM, Disney
  - Looney Tunes, Tom & Jerry — masters of exaggeration and timing

- **Animation strikes and labor history** — the human side of the industry
  - Understanding working conditions and artistic struggles

### Philosophy and Theory
- **The Uncanny Valley** — Masahiro Mori's theory on realism and eeriness
- **Norman McLaren's philosophy** — "Animation is not the art of drawings that move, but the art of movements that are drawn"
- **Sergei Eisenstein's writings on Disney** — Soviet film theorist analyzing animation

## Recommended Watching Order (Films/Series for Study)

### For Understanding Principles
1. **Pixar shorts** (especially *Luxo Jr.*, *Geri's Game*, *Piper*) — clear principle application
2. **Looney Tunes classics** — exaggeration and timing mastery
3. **Disney Renaissance films** (*Little Mermaid*, *Beauty and the Beast*, *Lion King*) — character animation peak
4. **Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse** — breaking rules intentionally

### For Character Performance
1. **Kung Fu Panda** series — excellent character movement variety
2. **How to Train Your Dragon** — Toothless is masterclass in non-verbal performance
3. **The Iron Giant** — emotion through mechanical character

### For Effects and Technical
1. **Moana** — water simulation and stylization
2. **Frozen** — snow and ice effects
3. **Ratatouille** — food and material variety

### For Stylized/Limited Animation
1. **Samurai Jack** — limited animation as artistic choice
2. **Into the Spider-Verse** — hybrid technique
3. **Klaus** — 2D/3D hybrid lighting

## Citation Note

When referencing these resources in lessons, always provide proper attribution and encourage students to engage directly with original sources rather than relying solely on secondary interpretation. Animation is learned by *doing* and *seeing*, not just reading about it.
