## Common Failure Modes
1. **"More frames = better animation"**
   - Students often think smooth animation requires animating every frame (on-ones)
   - Reality: Frame timing is a stylistic choice. Most animation is on-twos or even on-threes
   - Correction: Show examples where limited animation (UPA style, anime) creates powerful effects through selective movement

2. **"Squash and stretch means making things wobbly"**
   - Students overuse squash/stretch, making everything feel like rubber
   - Reality: The principle maintains volume and conveys energy, not just deformation
   - Correction: Emphasize the "constant volume" rule and show subtle applications in realistic animation

3. **"Arcs are just curved paths"**
   - Students draw curved motion paths but miss the mechanical reason
   - Reality: Arcs come from rotating joints — any movement constrained by a pivot follows an arc
   - Correction: Have students trace their own arm movement to see natural arcs, then contrast with mechanical/sliding motion
