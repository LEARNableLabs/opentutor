# Animation — Principles and Techniques: Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Squash and stretch** — visual elasticity that conveys weight and energy
2. **Anticipation** — preparatory movement before an action
3. **Staging** — directing viewer attention through composition and clarity
4. **Straight ahead vs pose-to-pose** — two fundamental animation workflows
5. **Follow-through and overlapping action** — parts continue moving after the main body stops
6. **Slow in and slow out** — easing at movement start/end for natural physics
7. **Arcs** — natural movement follows curved paths, not straight lines
8. **Timing and emotion** — frame count determines mood and character. Depends on: slow in/slow out, arcs
9. **Impact frames and smears** — special techniques for ultra-fast motion. Depends on: timing
10. **Frame timing strategies** — on-ones, on-twos, stylistic choices. Depends on: timing and emotion
11. **Weight through timing** — spacing and duration create mass perception. Depends on: slow in/slow out, timing
12. **Personality through timing** — character psychology expressed via movement speed. Depends on: timing and emotion, weight
13. **Walk cycles** — fundamental repeating character motion. Depends on: arcs, weight, timing
14. **Line of action and gesture** — single curved line expressing pose energy. Depends on: staging, arcs
15. **Anticipation in performance** — thought before action in character acting. Depends on: anticipation, staging
16. **Personality in movement** — character traits visible in motion choices. Depends on: timing personality, walk cycles
17. **Facial animation and dialogue** — expression and phonemes. Depends on: staging, timing
18. **Momentum and impact physics** — realistic collision behavior. Depends on: weight through timing
19. **Material properties** — how different substances move (cloth, water, etc.). Depends on: follow-through, overlapping action
20. **Effects animation** — specialized techniques for natural phenomena. Depends on: material properties, physics
21. **Exaggeration** — pushing reality for entertainment and clarity. Depends on: all principles
22. **Appeal** — making characters and motion visually interesting. Depends on: all principles
23. **Secondary action** — supporting actions that add dimension. Depends on: follow-through, staging
24. **Solid drawing/posing** — maintaining volume and form. Depends on: staging, line of action
25. **Artistic license** — knowing when to break rules. Depends on: understanding all principles first
26. **Personal style** — synthesizing principles into unique voice. Depends on: mastery of fundamentals

## Dependencies

### Foundation Layer
- **Squash and stretch** is taught first because it's the most visible, immediate principle
- **Anticipation** and **staging** work together — anticipation needs staging to direct attention
- **Straight ahead vs pose-to-pose** are alternative workflows, not dependent on each other
- **Slow in/slow out** requires understanding arcs to see why natural deceleration follows curves

### Timing Layer
- **All timing concepts** build on the slow in/slow out foundation
- **Weight through timing** cannot be understood without first seeing how spacing creates perception
- **Personality through timing** requires mastering neutral/realistic timing first
- **Frame timing strategies** (ones/twos) become meaningful only after students understand timing's emotional impact

### Character Performance Layer
- **Walk cycles** integrate arcs + weight + timing in one repeating motion
- **Line of action** extends staging principles to full-body poses
- **Performance anticipation** applies the anticipation principle to acting, not just mechanics
- **Personality in movement** synthesizes walk cycles with timing personality
- **Facial animation** is taught late because it requires staging (directing attention) + timing (dialogue rhythm)

### Physics and Integration Layer
- **Physics concepts** depend on weight/timing mastery
- **Material properties** extend follow-through and overlapping to different substances
- **Effects animation** is the most technically demanding, requiring physics + all principles
- **Exaggeration** and **appeal** are meta-principles that modify all others — taught late when students understand what they're exaggerating
- **Artistic license** only makes sense after understanding the rules being broken

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Bottleneck 1: Timing
Almost everything after lesson 7 depends on timing mastery. Students who struggle with timing (lessons 8-13) will struggle with everything else. Consider additional practice or slower pacing through this module.

### Bottleneck 2: Weight perception
If students can't make objects feel heavy or light through timing alone (lesson 11), character performance will feel floaty and unconvincing. This is a make-or-break concept.

### Bottleneck 3: Line of action
Weak posing (lesson 15) undermines all subsequent character work. Students may need to pause and practice gesture drawing alongside animation.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic drawing fundamentals** — needed for solid drawing, staging, gesture
- **Keyframe concepts** — needed for understanding pose-to-pose workflow
- **Software proficiency** — students should be comfortable with their chosen tool before tackling principles
- **Basic physics intuition** — gravity, momentum at a conceptual level (not mathematical)

## Common Concept Clusters

Students should see these concepts as related families:

1. **The Physics Family**: squash/stretch, slow in/out, arcs, weight, momentum — all about real-world motion
2. **The Attention Family**: staging, anticipation, secondary action — directing the viewer's eye
3. **The Personality Family**: timing/emotion, personality through timing, character movement — psychology in motion
4. **The Workflow Family**: straight ahead, pose-to-pose, frame timing strategies — how you make it
5. **The Polish Family**: exaggeration, appeal, solid drawing — refinement and artistry
