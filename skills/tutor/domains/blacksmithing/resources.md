# Blacksmithing — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books
- **The Complete Modern Blacksmith** by Alexander Weygers — The tool-making bible. Covers forge welding, tool design, and heat treatment with detailed illustrations. Ideal for intermediate students because it assumes basic forging competence. Available used; worth owning.
  
- **The Art of Blacksmithing** by Alex Bealer — Historical context and traditional techniques. Excellent for understanding *why* certain methods evolved. Less hands-on than Weygers but stronger on forge welding theory.

- **New Edge of the Anvil** by Jack Andrews — Practical instruction with good forge welding section. Intermediate-friendly with clear explanations of heat management and flux use.

- **The Backyard Blacksmith** by Lorelei Sims — Modern, accessible approach. Good for students transitioning from hobby to serious skill building. Excellent tool-making projects.

- **Metallurgy for the Non-Metallurgist (2nd ed.)** by Harry Chandler (ASM International) — Essential steel science reference. Use selectively for students wanting deeper understanding of phase transformations, grain structure, and heat treatment. Available as PDF from ASM.

### Online Educational Resources
- **Anvilfire iForge Demonstrations** (https://www.anvilfire.com/iForge/) — Extensive step-by-step visual tutorials. Particularly strong on tool making and forge welding. Archive format; browsable by topic.

- **IForgeIron Forums** (https://www.iforgeiron.com/forums/) — Active community with "Lessons in the Craft" section. Search for forge welding and heat treatment threads. Image-heavy; great for visual learners.

- **ABANA (Artist-Blacksmith's Association of North America)** (https://www.abana.org/education/) — Educational resources, conference proceedings, and chapter connections. Intermediate students should explore local ABANA chapters for hands-on workshops.

- **Anvilfire Heat Treating Guide** (https://www.anvilfire.com/FAQs/heat_treating_blades.php) — Detailed heat treatment reference with temperature charts, quench media selection, and troubleshooting.

- **Anvilfire Tool Steel Reference** (https://www.anvilfire.com/21centbs/articles/W1_Tool_Steel.php) — W1 tool steel deep-dive. Use as template for understanding other tool steels.

### Institutional Courses
- **Touchstone Center for Crafts** (Pennsylvania) — Week-long intermediate/advanced blacksmithing courses. Strong on forge welding and tool making.

- **John C. Campbell Folk School** (North Carolina) — Forge welding-specific workshops. Weekend to week-long formats.

- **Penland School of Craft** (North Carolina) — Tool making intensives. Advanced skill level expected.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Video Content
- **Alec Steele** (https://www.youtube.com/@AlecSteele) — High-production YouTube channel. Pattern welding and Damascus series are excellent visual references. Watch for technique, not personality.

- **Black Bear Forge** (https://www.youtube.com/@BlackBearForge) — Traditional techniques, clear instruction. Strong on coal forge management and forge welding fundamentals.

- **Christ Centered Ironworks** (https://www.youtube.com/@ChristCenteredIronworks) — Detailed forge welding tutorials with good failure analysis. Slower pace; ideal for careful study.

- **DF in the Shop** (https://www.youtube.com/c/DFintheshop) — Tool making with strong heat treatment science explanations. Good for students wanting metallurgical depth.

- **Essential Craftsman** (https://www.youtube.com/@essentialcraftsman) — Blacksmith tools in trade context (carpentry, masonry). Shows how tool design serves real work.

### Interactive Tools & Visualizations
- **Iron-Carbon Phase Diagram** — Search "Fe-C phase diagram" for printable versions. Essential for understanding austenite, eutectic melting, and heat treatment temperature ranges. Laminate one for shop reference.

- **Heat Color Chart** — Multiple sources available; print and post near forge. Correlates incandescent color to approximate temperature. Learn to judge color in dim light (not bright sunlight).

- **Hardenability Curves** — ASM handbooks contain Jominy curves for common tool steels. Use to understand quench medium selection. Advanced reference.

### Suppliers with Educational Content
- **Centaur Forge** (https://www.centaurforge.com/) — Major blacksmith supplier. Catalog descriptions include tool use guidance. Video demonstrations on site.

- **Blacksmith's Depot** (https://www.blacksmithsdepot.com/) — Beginner through advanced tools. Good educational blog covering forge welding and heat treatment.

- **New England Forge** (https://www.newenglandforge.com/) — Tool steel supplier with detailed composition charts and heat treatment specs.

### Code/Simulations
- No coding resources directly applicable, but students with programming background might enjoy modeling heat diffusion or building quench rate calculators.

### People to Follow
- **Uri Hofi** — Israeli blacksmith known for ergonomic hammer design and power hammer technique. Look up Hofi-style hammers for tool ergonomics discussion.

- **Francis Whitaker** — Legendary American blacksmith (1906-1999). Historical reference; ABANA's Whitaker competition showcases traditional skills.

- **Tom Clark** — Contemporary bladesmith and educator. Search for published articles on san mai construction and forge welding troubleshooting.

- **Jim Hrisoulas** — Author of *The Complete Bladesmith*. While blade-focused, his forge welding and pattern welding techniques apply to tool making.

## Unexpected Connections

- **Japanese sword polishing** — Shows how surface reading can diagnose internal weld quality. Polishers train for decades to see folding flaws. Relevant for students interested in non-destructive testing.

- **Archaeometallurgy** — Study of ancient metalwork reveals forge welding techniques before modern metallurgy. Look up bloomery iron and pattern-welded Migration Period swords.

- **Jewelry fabrication** — Precious metal soldering is chemically similar to forge welding (diffusion bonding with flux). Techniques transfer at small scale.

- **Welding metallurgy (arc/TIG/MIG)** — Modern fusion welding deals with similar challenges: oxidation, heat-affected zones, grain growth. Forge welding provides intuition for modern welding problems.

- **Biomimetic materials** — Nacre (mother-of-pearl) uses layered structure for toughness, similar to pattern-welded steel. Lesson in how iteration of simple bonds creates emergent properties.

- **Traditional timber framing** — Joinery philosophy parallels forge welding: careful surface preparation, precise geometry, and testing under stress. Cross-craft connection for students with woodworking background.

### Historical References
- **Diderot's Encyclopédie** — 18th-century illustrations of forge work. Available digitized. Shows traditional forge welding in manufacturing context.
  
- **De Re Metallica** by Georgius Agricola (1556) — Historical metalworking text. English translation available. Shows early understanding of fire management and metal properties.

## Tools & Equipment Notes

Students at intermediate level should own or have access to:
- **Solid fuel forge** (coal or charcoal) — forge welding in gas forge is possible but coal gives better atmosphere control
- **Borax flux** — 20 Mule Team from grocery store works; avoid pre-mixed welding flux until you understand plain borax
- **Wire brush** — for surface preparation; bench grinder with wire wheel even better
- **Tongs variety** — appropriate tongs for different stock sizes prevents fumbling at welding heat
- **Proper eye protection** — forge welding spits flux and scale; safety glasses minimum, face shield better

Nice to have:
- **Pyrometer** — removes guesswork from heat treatment; not necessary but builds confidence
- **Magnet** — testing for non-magnetic point (austenite transformation)
- **File set** — for hardness testing; cheap harbor freight files work fine
- **Slack tub** — water for controlled cooling; use if normalizing or annealing

## Notes for Further Study

After completing this intermediate curriculum, students ready for advanced work should explore:

1. **Mokume gane** — Japanese lamination technique using non-ferrous metals (copper, silver, brass)
2. **San mai construction** — Hard core with tough cladding; requires precision forge welding
3. **Differential hardening** — Creating hamon (temper line) through selective quenching
4. **Controlled atmosphere welding** — Argon or vacuum welding for reactive metals
5. **Cable Damascus** — Forge welding wire rope for random pattern effect
6. **Canister Damascus** — Powder steel or layered stock forge-welded in steel can

All of these build on the foundational forge welding and heat treatment skills from this curriculum.
