# Complexity Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Network Science (Barabási)** — Free online textbook at http://networksciencebook.com/. Comprehensive, visual, accessible. Covers chapters 1-10 of the curriculum. Perfect for intermediate students.

- **Introduction to the Theory of Complex Systems (Thurner, Hanel, Klimek)** — Oxford University Press, 2018. Mathematical treatment at intermediate level. Balances rigor with intuition. Covers emergence, networks, information theory, and applications.

- **Complexity: A Guided Tour (Melanie Mitchell)** — Oxford, 2009. Conceptual overview of the field, less mathematical but excellent for building intuition. Good supplementary reading.

- **Santa Fe Institute Complexity Explorer** — https://www.complexityexplorer.org/. Multiple free online courses: Introduction to Complexity, Agent-Based Modeling, Fractals and Scaling, Nonlinear Dynamics. Video lectures, quizzes, certificates.

- **The Origins of Order (Stuart Kauffman)** — Oxford, 1993. Dense but foundational work on self-organization in biology. Advanced but worth dipping into for fitness landscapes and NK models.

## Courses and Lectures

- **MIT OCW 18.S995: Network Science and Models** — https://ocw.mit.edu/. Full course materials: lectures, problem sets, readings. Mathematical approach.

- **Coursera: Introduction to Complexity (Santa Fe Institute)** — Video lectures by Melanie Mitchell. Accessible entry point.

- **Network Science (University of Michigan)** — Coursera course focusing on social networks, with computational labs.

- **Systems Innovation YouTube Channel** — https://www.youtube.com/c/ComplexityLabs. Short videos on complexity concepts applied to sustainability, economics, and social systems.

## Interactive Tools and Visualizations

- **Complexity Explorables** — https://complexity-explorables.org/. Interactive visualizations of emergence, networks, epidemics, evolution, game theory. Essential resource. Created by complexity scientists.

- **NetLogo Models Library** — https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/. Hundreds of agent-based models: flocking, segregation, epidemics, evolution, networks. Download and modify.

- **The Evolution of Trust (Nicky Case)** — https://ncase.me/trust/. Beautiful interactive explanation of game theory and cooperation. Use for lesson 22.

- **Network Science Book Visualizations** — http://networksciencebook.com/ includes interactive network visualizations and data.

- **Oracle of Bacon** — https://oracleofbacon.org/. Explore six degrees of separation in Hollywood. Use for small-world lesson (9).

## Code and Computational Resources

- **NetworkX (Python)** — https://networkx.org/. Industry-standard library for network analysis. Excellent documentation and tutorials.

- **igraph (R/Python/C)** — https://igraph.org/. Fast network analysis library. Good for large networks.

- **Mesa (Python)** — https://mesa.readthedocs.io/. Agent-based modeling framework. Easier than NetLogo for students comfortable with Python.

- **Gephi** — https://gephi.org/. Open-source network visualization software. Drag-and-drop interface, good for exploratory analysis.

- **Netlogo** — https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/. Agent-based modeling environment. Lower barrier to entry than coding from scratch.

## Research Papers (Key Readings)

- **Barabási & Albert (1999)** — "Emergence of scaling in random networks." *Science*. The preferential attachment paper. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.286.5439.509

- **Watts & Strogatz (1998)** — "Collective dynamics of 'small-world' networks." *Nature*. The small-world paper. https://www.nature.com/articles/30918

- **Schelling (1971)** — "Dynamic models of segregation." *Journal of Mathematical Sociology*. Classic agent-based model.

- **Axelrod (1980)** — "Effective choice in the Prisoner's Dilemma." *Journal of Conflict Resolution*. Evolution of cooperation.

- **Bak, Tang, Wiesenfeld (1987)** — "Self-organized criticality." *Physical Review Letters*. The sandpile paper.

- **Newman (2006)** — "Modularity and community structure in networks." *PNAS*. Community detection algorithm. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0601602103

- **Clauset, Shalizi, Newman (2009)** — "Power-law distributions in empirical data." *SIAM Review*. How to test for power laws rigorously. https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062

- **Bettencourt et al. (2007)** — "Growth, innovation, scaling, and the pace of life in cities." *PNAS*. Urban scaling laws. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0610172104

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

- **The Stack Overflow network** — Analyze collaboration patterns in online communities. Real-world network dataset.
  
- **Music and power laws** — Hit songs follow power-law distributions (Matthew effect). Connect to preferential attachment.

- **Mycelial networks** — Fungal networks resemble internet topology. Nature's scale-free networks.

- **Ant colony optimization** — Swarm algorithm inspired by ant foraging. Engineering application of emergence.

- **Wikipedia edit wars** — Social dynamics and conflict resolution as complex system. Real-time data available.

- **Protein interaction networks** — Cellular function emerges from network structure. Network medicine applications.

- **Historical spread of innovations** — Diffusion of agriculture, languages, technologies follows network dynamics.

- **Cryptocurrency networks** — Transaction networks, mining pools, governance. Modern complex adaptive system.

- **Murmuration videos** — Starling flocks as visual poetry. Never gets old.
