# Research Summary — Complexity Science

## Major Subtopics

1. **Emergence & Self-Organization** — how simple rules create complex patterns; phase transitions; criticality; emergent properties in physical, biological, and social systems
2. **Network Science** — graph theory foundations; scale-free networks; small-world phenomena; network dynamics; robustness and vulnerability
3. **Adaptation & Evolution** — evolutionary dynamics; fitness landscapes; genetic algorithms; adaptive agents; co-evolution; learning in complex systems
4. **Information & Computation** — information theory in complex systems; cellular automata; computation at the edge of chaos; algorithmic complexity
5. **Applications** — ecosystems; economies; social systems; cities; the brain; technology networks

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- *Introduction to the Theory of Complex Systems* (Thurner, Hanel, Klimek) — comprehensive mathematical treatment at intermediate level
- *Network Science* (Barabási) — accessible introduction to network theory with interactive online book
- *Complexity: A Guided Tour* (Mitchell) — conceptual overview of the field
- *The Origins of Order* (Kauffman) — self-organization and adaptation in biology

### Courses
- **Santa Fe Institute Complexity Explorer** — multiple free courses on complexity fundamentals, networks, agent-based modeling
- **MIT OCW 18.S995** — Network Science and Models
- **Coursera Network Science** — University of Michigan course

### Interactive Resources
- **Complexity Explorables** (complexity-explorables.org) — interactive visualizations of emergence, networks, epidemics, evolution
- **Network Science Book** (networksciencebook.com) — free online textbook with visualizations
- **NetLogo Models Library** — agent-based simulations for emergence and adaptation

### Research Centers
- Santa Fe Institute (primary hub for complexity research)
- New England Complex Systems Institute
- Center for Complex Network Research (Northeastern)

## Pedagogical Notes

- Highly interdisciplinary — examples from physics, biology, economics, sociology
- Visual and computational — simulations are essential for building intuition
- Intermediate level requires mathematical maturity (calculus, linear algebra, probability) but not advanced formalism
- Balance conceptual understanding with quantitative tools
- Emphasize "more is different" philosophy — collective behavior emerges from interactions
