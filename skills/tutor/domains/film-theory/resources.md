# Film Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks
- **Bordwell & Thompson: "Film Art: An Introduction"** (12th edition) — comprehensive intermediate-level textbook covering formal analysis, mise-en-scene, editing, narrative, genre. The gold standard. [McGraw Hill](https://www.mheducation.com/)
- **Louis Giannetti: "Understanding Movies"** (15th edition) — accessible intro to film analysis with strong visual examples [Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/)
- **Timothy Corrigan & Patricia White: "The Film Experience"** (5th edition) — combines formal analysis with cultural/historical context [Bedford/St. Martin's](https://www.macmillanlearning.com/)

### Primary Theoretical Texts
- **Sergei Eisenstein: "Film Form" and "The Film Sense"** — foundational montage theory, read excerpts for intellectual montage and dialectical editing
- **André Bazin: "What is Cinema?" Volumes I & II** — realist aesthetic, anti-montage, ontology of the photographic image
- **Laura Mulvey: "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema"** (1975 essay) — feminist film theory, male gaze (advanced extension)

### University Courses (Open Access)
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: "The Film Experience"** (21L.011) — full lecture notes, assignments, and readings [https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21l-011-the-film-experience-fall-2013/](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21l-011-the-film-experience-fall-2013/)
- **Yale Open Courses: "Introduction to Theory of Literature"** (has film narrative modules) [https://oyc.yale.edu/](https://oyc.yale.edu/)
- **Coursera: "Film, Images & Historical Interpretation"** (Université de Genève) [https://www.coursera.org/](https://www.coursera.org/)

### Institutional Resources

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Film Theory Meets Digital Media
- **The Kuleshov Effect and YouTube thumbnails** — how juxtaposition creates clickbait meaning
- **TikTok as Soviet montage** — rapid cutting and collision of ideas in 60 seconds
- **Meme culture as intellectual montage** — Eisenstein would recognize meme logic

### Cross-Disciplinary Links
- **Architecture and mise-en-scene** — how space tells story (Bong Joon-ho's Parasite)
- **Music theory and rhythmic editing** — tempo, motif, variation (Baby Driver soundtrack sync)
- **Painting composition and frame design** — Caravaggio's chiaroscuro in film noir lighting
- **Photography and cinematography** — Gregory Crewdson's cinematic still photographs

### Genre Studies
- **Film noir as visual style** — not just crime, but a lighting and composition grammar
- **Westerns and landscape as character** — John Ford's Monument Valley
- **Horror and the uncanny frame** — what's in the frame vs. what's outside it
- **Musicals as montage** — Busby Berkeley's kaleidoscope editing

### Contemporary Debates
- **Digital vs. film stock** — does the medium change the aesthetic? (Tarantino's resistance to digital)
- **Streaming aspect ratios** — how does phone viewing reshape composition?
- **AI-generated imagery and mise-en-scene** — can synthetic images carry the same formal weight?
- **Long-form TV as new narrative canvas** — prestige TV vs. cinematic structure (The Sopranos, Breaking Bad)

## Essential Films for Study (Referenced in Curriculum)

These films are cited across lessons and should be accessible to the student:

- **Battleship Potemkin** (1925, Eisenstein) — Odessa Steps sequence, rhythmic montage
- **Citizen Kane** (1941, Welles) — deep focus, non-linear narrative
- **Breathless** (1960, Godard) — jump cuts
- **Psycho** (1960, Hitchcock) — shower scene editing
- **2001: A Space Odyssey** (1968, Kubrick) — formalist composition
- **The Shining** (1980, Kubrick) — symmetry and dread
- **Pulp Fiction** (1994, Tarantino) — non-linear structure
- **Children of Men** (2006, Cuarón) — long take realism
- **The Grand Budapest Hotel** (2014, Anderson) — production design and color
- **Parasite** (2019, Bong) — spatial composition and class narrative

Available on: Criterion Channel, Kanopy, streaming platforms. Many are in public domain or available via university libraries.
