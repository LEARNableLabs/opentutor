# Ceramics Glazing Chemistry — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Mastering Cone 6 Glazes: Improving Durability, Fit and Aesthetics"** by John Hesselberth & Ron Roy
  - **Coverage**: Mid-range glaze chemistry with deep focus on durability and glaze fit
  - **Why it's good**: Most comprehensive treatment of oxide chemistry for cone 5-6; excellent for intermediate students; includes extensive appendices on materials and testing
  - **URL**: Available from ceramic suppliers and Amazon

- **"The Potter's Dictionary of Materials and Techniques"** (5th edition) by Frank & Janet Hamer
  - **Coverage**: Encyclopedic reference covering all ceramic materials, processes, and terms
  - **Why it's good**: Best single-volume reference; alphabetical format makes it excellent for looking up specific materials or concepts
  - **URL**: https://www.amazon.com/Potters-Dictionary-Materials-Techniques-Hamer/dp/0812238109

- **"Understanding Glazes"** by Richard Eppler & Douglas Mimi Obstler
  - **Coverage**: Deep dive into oxide chemistry, phase diagrams, and glaze calculation
  - **Why it's good**: Most technical of the standard texts; perfect for intermediate students ready for rigor; excellent on eutectic systems
  - **URL**: https://ceramicartsdaily.org/bookstore/understanding-glazes/

- **"Glazes and Glass Coatings"** by Richard Eppler & Douglas Knovel
  - **Coverage**: Industrial perspective on glaze chemistry, including commercial applications
  - **Why it's good**: Bridges art pottery and industrial ceramics; excellent on colorants and opacity
  - **URL**: Available through American Ceramic Society

- **"The Ceramic Spectrum"** by Robin Hopper
  - **Coverage**: Comprehensive guide to clay and glaze technology with extensive color examples
  - **Why it's good**: Beautifully illustrated; great for visual learners; includes testing methodology
  - **URL**: https://www.amazon.com/Ceramic-Spectrum-Robin-Hopper/dp/0873419553

### Interactive Tools & Software

- **GlazeMaster / Insight** — https://digitalfire.com/insight
  - **Type**: Desktop software (Windows/Mac)
  - **Cost**: $135 USD (as of 2026)
  - **Features**: Industry-standard glaze calculation, extensive material database, limit formulas, analysis tools
  - **Best for**: Serious students and professionals; most powerful tool available

- **Glazy** — https://glazy.org
  - **Type**: Web-based (free)
  - **Cost**: Free
  - **Features**: UMF calculation, recipe sharing, material database, visual analysis
  - **Best for**: Beginners and intermediates; no installation needed; community features

- **HyperGlaze** — http://hyperglaze.com
  - **Type**: Windows software (free)
  - **Cost**: Free
  - **Features**: Recipe calculation, UMF conversion, material analysis
  - **Best for**: Free desktop alternative to GlazeMaster

- **GlazeChem** — https://glazechem.com
  - **Type**: Mobile and web app
  - **Cost**: Free basic version, paid pro version
  - **Features**: Recipe management, UMF calculation, test tracking
  - **Best for**: Mobile-friendly glaze management

- **Matrix Glaze Calculator** (by Matt Katz) — https://glazy.org/posts/2309
  - **Type**: Spreadsheet-based (free)
  - **Cost**: Free
  - **Features**: Excel/Google Sheets template for manual calculations
  - **Best for**: Learning the math by seeing formulas; no software needed

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Cross-Discipline Links

- **Materials Science & Glass Research**
  - **Connection**: Glaze is glass; modern glass research (Corning, MIT) uses the same oxide chemistry
  - **Resources**: 
    - "Introduction to Glass Science and Technology" (Royal Society of Chemistry)
    - MIT OpenCourseWare: 3.091 Introduction to Solid State Chemistry

- **Geology & Mineralogy**
  - **Connection**: Ceramic materials are minerals; understanding feldspar requires geology
  - **Resources**:
    - "Manual of Mineralogy" by Klein & Dutrow
    - MinDat.org mineral database — https://www.mindat.org

- **Archaeology & Conservation Science**
  - **Connection**: Analyzing ancient glazes reveals their chemistry and history
  - **Resources**:
    - "Archaeological Chemistry" by Zvi Goffer
    - Winterthur Museum conservation science publications

- **Industrial Ceramics & Refractories**
  - **Connection**: Sanitary ware, tile, and refractories use the same chemistry at larger scale
  - **Resources**:
    - American Ceramic Society publications — https://ceramics.org
    - "Industrial Ceramics" journal

- **Food Science & Leaching**
  - **Connection**: Glaze durability connects to food safety chemistry
  - **Resources**:
    - FDA guidance on ceramic food-contact surfaces
    - "Toxic Glazes" research by Linda Arbuckle and John Hesselberth

- **Optics & Color Science**
  - **Connection**: Color in glazes follows the same physics as gemstones and minerals
  - **Resources**:
    - "Color and Light in Nature" by Lynch & Livingston
    - Crystal field theory in coordination chemistry textbooks

### Historical & Cultural Context

- **Chinese Ceramic History**
  - **Connection**: Celadon, chun, and tenmoku glazes were chemistry achievements centuries ahead
  - **Resources**:
    - "Science and Civilisation in China, Vol. 5: Chemistry and Chemical Technology" by Joseph Needham
    - Metropolitan Museum ceramic collections — https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection

- **Islamic Tilework**
  - **Connection**: Luster glazes and turquoise alkaline glazes are masterpieces of medieval chemistry
  - **Resources**:
    - "Islamic Ceramics" by Oliver Watson
    - V&A Museum Islamic collections — https://www.vam.ac.uk

- **Delftware & Majolica**
  - **Connection**: European tin-glazed earthenware chemistry
  - **Resources**:
    - Rijksmuseum Delft collections — https://www.rijksmuseum.nl
    - "Tin-Glaze Traditions" by Robin Hildyard
