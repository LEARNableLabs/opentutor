# Vintage Computer Restoration — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Assessment and Triage
- Visual inspection techniques
- Power-on safety protocols
- Component identification
- Common failure modes by platform

### 2. Power Supply Restoration
- Capacitor replacement (recapping)
- Voltage regulation testing
- Linear vs switching power supplies
- Safety protocols for high voltage

### 3. Board-Level Repair
- Corrosion and battery leak cleanup
- Trace repair and wire wrapping
- IC socket replacement
- ROM reading and burning

### 4. Display Technology
- CRT monitor restoration and safety
- Composite video repair
- LCD retrofitting
- Color convergence and calibration

### 5. Storage Media
- Floppy drive cleaning and alignment
- Hard drive restoration (MFM, IDE)
- Optical drive repair
- Media imaging and preservation

### 6. Input Devices
- Keyboard restoration (mechanical switches)
- Mouse cleaning and sensor repair
- Joystick and paddle controllers
- Cable fabrication

### 7. Platform-Specific Knowledge
- Commodore 64/128 (SID chip issues, power supplies)
- Apple II series (power supply, keyboard)
- IBM PC/XT/AT (ISA cards, BIOS)
- Amiga (PSU, capacitor plague)
- Early Macintosh (analog board, CRT)

### 8. Preservation and Documentation
- Software imaging (disk, ROM, EPROM)
- Documentation archival
- Ethical restoration practices
- Community contribution

## Intermediate Level Context

At intermediate level, students should have:
- Basic electronics knowledge (voltage, current, resistance)
- Soldering experience (through-hole minimum)
- Computer literacy (file systems, boot process)
- Safety awareness (ESD, high voltage)

The curriculum will focus on:
- Platform-agnostic diagnosis skills
- Common repair patterns across systems
- Component-level troubleshooting
- Building toward advanced repairs (chip-level, custom solutions)
- Not assuming specific platform expertise yet
