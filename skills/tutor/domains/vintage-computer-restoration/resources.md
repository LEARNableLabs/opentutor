# Vintage Computer Restoration — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Technical Documentation
- **Console5 Wiki** (https://console5.com/wiki/) — Platform-specific repair guides, capacitor lists, schematics. Strongest for Commodore, Atari, Sega, Nintendo. The recapping reference.
- **Minuszerodegrees.net** (https://www.minuszerodegrees.net/) — IBM PC/XT/AT technical reference. BIOS, POST codes, interrupt resources, detailed hardware guides.
- **Service Manuals Collection** (https://archive.org/details/bitsavers_dataBooks_02ac9894) — Archive.org's Bitsavers collection. Original equipment service manuals, datasheets, schematics. The authoritative source when it exists.
- **RetroRGB** (https://www.retrorgb.com/) — Video signal tech reference. RGB, sync types, upscaling, mods. Goes deep on video quality.
- **6502.org** (http://www.6502.org/) — 6502 CPU platform resources. Hardware projects, repair guides, community forums.

### Community Forums and Wikis
- **Vintage Computer Federation (VCFed)** (https://www.vcfed.org/) — Premier restoration community. Forums cover every platform. Annual VCF events. Wiki has platform-specific guides.
- **68k Macintosh Liberation Army (68kMLA)** (https://68kmla.org/) — Early Macintosh restoration. Analog board safety guides, compact Mac disassembly, SE/30 recapping.
- **Lemon64** (https://www.lemon64.com/) — Commodore 64 hardware forums. Deep technical discussions, PSU replacement guides, cartridge projects.
- **English Amiga Board** (https://eab.abime.net/) — Amiga restoration, mods, and preservation. Capacitor lists, Kickstart ROM guides.
- **AtariAge** (https://atariage.com/forums/) — Atari 2600, 5200, 7800, 8-bit computers, ST, Jaguar. Hardware mods and repairs.

### Books
- **"Troubleshooting and Repairing Computer Monitors" by Stephen J. Bigelow** — CRT theory, safety, and repair. Out of print but available used. Essential for video work.
- **"The TTL Cookbook" by Don Lancaster** — 7400-series logic fundamentals. Helps with discrete logic troubleshooting in early computers.
- **Platform Service Manuals** — Commodore 64 Service Manual, Apple II Reference Manual, IBM PC Technical Reference. Often available as PDFs on archive.org.
- **"Analog Circuit Design" by Bob Pease** — For understanding linear regulators and analog board design in vintage systems. Advanced but clarifying.

### Academic/Historical
- **Computer History Museum Collection** (https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/) — Archival scans, oral histories, technical documents. Context for "why was this designed this way?"
- **"Commodore: A Company on the Edge" by Brian Bagnall** — Business history that explains engineering decisions (cost-cutting, rapid iteration).
- **"Revolution in The Valley" by Andy Hertzfeld** — Early Macintosh development stories. Insight into compact Mac design philosophy.

### Interactive Tools and Emulators
- **VICE** (https://vice-emu.sourceforge.io/) — Commodore emulator (C64, VIC-20, PET, C128). Use to test software and ROMs.
- **AppleWin** (https://github.com/AppleWin/AppleWin) — Apple II emulator. Helpful for validating disk images and ROM dumps.

- **Falstad Circuit Simulator** (https://www.falstad.com/circuit/) — Visual circuit simulator. Model voltage regulators, filters. Great for understanding power supply behavior.

### Software Tools
- **KryoFlux** (https://www.kryoflux.com/) — USB floppy controller for disk imaging. Reads copy-protected and damaged disks at flux level. Commercial but industry standard.
- **Greaseweazle** (https://github.com/keirf/Greaseweazle) — Open-source flux-level disk imager. DIY hardware option (STM32 board + firmware). Competes with KryoFlux.
- **HxC Floppy Emulator** (https://hxc2001.com/) — Hardware floppy emulator. Use SD cards as floppy drives. Great for software preservation and testing.
- **Mini vMac** (https://www.gryphel.com/c/minivmac/) — Compact Macintosh emulator. Validate ROM dumps and test software.
- **Fritzing** (https://fritzing.org/) — Document your board repairs and mods. Circuit diagrams, PCB layouts.

### Code and Projects
- **Sidekick64** (https://github.com/frntc/Sidekick64) — Raspberry Pi cartridge for C64. Demonstrates modern integration with vintage systems.
- **PiStorm** (https://github.com/captain-amygdala/pistorm) — Raspberry Pi as Amiga accelerator. FPGA-adjacent project for advanced students.
- **Booting the Computer** (https://www.patreon.com/jj_botc) — Visual explanations of CPU and chip internals. Webcomic format makes complex topics accessible.
- **RetroCMP** (http://retrocmp.com/projects) — Custom replacement boards, modern PSU builds, upgrade projects. For students ready to design their own solutions.

### Parts Suppliers
- **Console5** (https://console5.com/store/) — Replacement parts catalog. Capacitor kits by platform, power supplies, belts, switches. Restoration-specific inventory.
- **Amiga Kit** (https://amigakit.amiga.store/) — Amiga parts and upgrades. Capacitor kits, RAM, custom chips.
- **ReAmiga** (https://www.reamiga.info/) — Amiga capacitor kits and repair parts. European source.
- **Mouser Electronics** (https://www.mouser.com/) — General electronics supplier. Capacitors, ICs, resistors. Use for generic parts.
- **Digikey** (https://www.digikey.com/) — Alternative to Mouser. Excellent search and filtering.
- **eBay / Aliexpress** — For unobtainable chips and parts. Caution: fakes exist (especially 6581 SID). Cross-reference with community.

### Chemicals and Consumables
- **MG Chemicals** — Flux removers, contact cleaners, conformal coating. Available through Mouser/Digikey.
- **DeoxIT** (https://caig.com/deoxit-d-series/) — Contact cleaner and rejuvenator. For edge connectors, switches, pots. Not for floppy heads.
- **White vinegar** — Neutralize alkaline battery corrosion. Cheap, effective, available everywhere.
- **Isopropyl alcohol (90%+)** — PCB cleaning. Higher concentration = better. Don't use rubbing alcohol (70%).

## Unexpected Connections (cross-discipline links)

### Vintage Computer Restoration → Synthesizer Repair
- Analog synthesis (Moog, ARP) uses same-era components and design patterns. Power supply recapping, CV troubleshooting, VCA/VCF circuits all translate. Modular synth community overlaps with vintage computing.
- **Resource:** Moog Service Manuals (https://www.moogarchives.com/)

### Vintage Computer Restoration → Arcade Cabinet Repair
- CRT monitors, switching PSUs, JAMMA standard edge connectors, ROM burning. Arcade boards are essentially computers. More physically robust but same core skills.
- **Resource:** Arcade Museum Technical Forums (https://forums.arcade-museum.com/)

### Vintage Computer Restoration → Automotive Electronics (OBD-I era)
- 1980s-90s cars use similar through-hole boards, electrolytic capacitors, and diagnostic approaches. ECU repair techniques (chip tuning, EPROM burning) directly apply.
- **Resource:** MegaSquirt ECU project (http://www.msextra.com/)

### Vintage Computer Restoration → Ham Radio Equipment
- RF circuit troubleshooting, power supply design, signal integrity. Vintage transceivers (1970s-80s) share failure modes with vintage computers. Community knowledge transfer.
- **Resource:** eHam.net forums (https://www.eham.net/)

### Vintage Computer Restoration → Museum Conservation
- Ethical restoration, documentation standards, reversible modifications. Museum-quality preservation practices elevate hobby work to archival standards.
- **Resource:** American Institute for Conservation (https://www.culturalheritage.org/)

### Vintage Computer Restoration → Material Science
- Polymer degradation (yellowing plastic), metal corrosion, solder joint failure, electrolyte chemistry. Understanding why materials fail enables better long-term preservation.
- **Resource:** Retrobrite science (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retr0bright)

## Wild Cards (for sparking curiosity)

### The Demoscene
- Competitive programming for vintage platforms. Demos push hardware beyond spec (raster tricks, sprite multiplexing, cycle-exact timing). Best validation test for restoration quality.
- **Resource:** Pouet.net (https://www.pouet.net/), Demoscene High-Quality Videos (https://www.scene.org/)

### Copy Protection Archaeology
- Understanding how copy protection worked (weak bits, track alignment, encryption) is reverse engineering and digital forensics. Connects to KryoFlux and software preservation.
- **Resource:** Museum of Computer Adventure Game History (https://mocagh.org/)

### Chip Decapping and Die Photography
- Opening ICs to photograph silicon dies. Reveals circuit design, identifies remarked chips, historical research. Requires chemistry but overlaps with restoration authentication.
- **Resource:** Zeptobars (https://zeptobars.com/en/)

### Alternative Platforms (Non-Western Computing)
- Soviet clones (ZX Spectrum → Byte-01, Apple II → Agat), Eastern Bloc computers. Different design constraints, rare parts, geopolitical history.
- **Resource:** Soviet Computer Museum (https://www.sovietcomputers.com/)

### Homebrew Hardware Development
- Building new hardware for vintage platforms (cartridges, accelerators, SD card adapters). Requires PCB design, understanding bus protocols.
- **Resource:** Exxos.com (https://www.exxosforum.co.uk/), Tindie marketplace (https://www.tindie.com/)

### Industrial / Embedded Vintage Systems
- CNC controllers, medical equipment, aviation electronics using vintage chips. Higher stakes, professional restoration standards. Some systems still in production use.
- **Resource:** Industrial maintenance forums, PLCTalk (https://www.plctalk.net/)

## Recommended Learning Sequence

1. **Start with video:** The 8-Bit Guy's "Restoring a [platform]" series for overview and inspiration.
2. **Read:** Platform-specific Console5 guide for your target machine.
3. **Practice:** Recap a junk power supply (follow Adrian's Digital Basement's PSU videos).
4. **Community:** Join VCFed forums, introduce yourself, ask for advice before first project.
5. **Tool up:** ESR meter, desoldering pump, variac, isolation transformer. Buy used when possible.
6. **Document:** Start a blog or forum thread documenting your first restoration. Teaching others cements learning.
7. **Contribute:** Upload ROM dumps, update wiki pages, answer beginner questions. Community knowledge compounds.

## Study Tips for This Topic

- **Photo everything:** Before disassembly, during repair, after completion. Visual documentation aids diagnosis and builds portfolio.
- **Fail cheap:** Practice desoldering on junk boards from recycling centers before touching valuable hardware.
- **One system at a time:** Master a single platform (C64 or Apple II recommended for beginners) before diversifying.
- **Schematic reading is a superpower:** Invest time learning to read schematics. It unlocks all platforms.
- **Safety is not negotiable:** CRT discharge procedure drilled until automatic. No shortcuts.
- **Test, don't guess:** Measure voltages, check continuity, verify signals. Shotgunning parts wastes money and time.
- **Community is the curriculum:** Forums, Discord servers, YouTube comments. Ask questions, share findings.
