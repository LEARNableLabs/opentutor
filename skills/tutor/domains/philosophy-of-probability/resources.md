# Philosophy of Probability and Statistics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Hacking, I. (2001). *An Introduction to Probability and Inductive Logic*** — Accessible, historically informed introduction. Perfect for intermediate students. Covers interpretations, induction, and paradoxes.
- **Gillies, D. (2000). *Philosophical Theories of Probability*** — Comprehensive survey of interpretations (classical, frequentist, logical, subjective, propensity). More philosophical depth.
- **Mayo, D. G. (1996). *Error and the Growth of Experimental Knowledge*** — Defends error-statistical (frequentist) approach. Important counterweight to Bayesian dominance in philosophy.
- **Howson, C. & Urbach, P. (2006). *Scientific Reasoning: The Bayesian Approach*** — Textbook-level introduction to Bayesian philosophy of science. Covers Dutch books, prior selection, confirmation.
- **Pearl, J. (2009). *Causality: Models, Reasoning and Inference*** — The definitive text on causal graphical models. Technical but essential.

### Papers and Articles

- **ASA Statement on P-values (2016)** — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108 — Official guidance on p-value interpretation
- **Gelman, A. & Shalizi, C. (2013). "Philosophy and the practice of Bayesian statistics"** — http://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/published/philosophy.pdf
- **Wasserstein, R. L. et al. (2019). "Moving to a World Beyond p < 0.05"** — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2019.1583913
- **Ioannidis, J. (2005). "Why Most Published Research Findings Are False"** — https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Philosophy

- **Bertrand Russell on induction** — Classic problem: can we justify inferring the future from the past?
- **David Hume on causation** — Skeptical challenge to causal reasoning that still echoes today
- **Karl Popper on falsification** — Philosophy of science connecting to statistical testing

### Mathematics

- **Algorithmic information theory** — Kolmogorov complexity gives alternative foundation for randomness and probability
- **Ergodic theory** — Mathematical justification for frequentist interpretation via long-run behavior
- **Cox's theorem** — Derives probability axioms from consistency requirements (related to Dutch books)

### Other Sciences

- **Quantum mechanics** — Uses non-classical probability (non-commutative), challenges interpretation debates
- **Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics** — Origin of statistical thinking in physics
- **Genetics and evolution** — Where frequentist and Bayesian approaches coexist productively
- **Economics** — Expected utility theory, game theory, rational choice under uncertainty
- **Psychology** — Cognitive biases in probabilistic reasoning (base rate neglect, conjunction fallacy)
- **Law** — How courts should interpret statistical evidence (prosecutor's fallacy, identification evidence)

### Practical Applications

- **Medical diagnosis** — Bayesian reasoning for interpreting test results (sensitivity, specificity, base rates)
- **Climate science** — Uncertainty quantification, detection and attribution
- **Machine learning** — Overfitting as philosophical problem, bias-variance tradeoff
- **A/B testing** — Practical Bayesian vs frequentist debates in tech industry
- **Election forecasting** — Single-case probability in action (Nate Silver, FiveThirtyEight)
- **COVID-19 pandemic** — Real-time statistical reasoning under uncertainty, model assumptions, communication of uncertainty
