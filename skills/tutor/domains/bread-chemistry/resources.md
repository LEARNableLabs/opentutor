# Bread Chemistry — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Harold McGee, *On Food and Cooking: The Science and Lore of the Kitchen*** — the bread, grain and dough chapters give accurate, readable explanations of gluten, fermentation, Maillard browning and staling. Best first reference at the intermediate level.
- **Delcour & Hoseney, *Principles of Cereal Science and Technology*** — the precise technical reference for flour components, gluten chemistry, starch gelatinization and retrogradation. Use for exact claims, not casual reading.
- **Myhrvold & Migoya, *Modernist Bread*** — in-depth volumes on dough structure, fermentation and baking, with cutaway photos of crumb and crust. Use the published excerpts and photos.
- **Shewry, P.R., "Wheat" (2009), *Journal of Experimental Botany*** — https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erp058 — optional deep dive on wheat grain biology and storage proteins.
- **Shewry, P.R. & Halford, N.G., "Cereal seed storage proteins: structures, properties and role in grain utilization" (2002), *Journal of Experimental Botany*** — https://doi.org/10.1093/jexbot/53.370.947 — optional deep dive on gliadin and glutenin structure.
- **Shewry, Napier & Tatham, "Seed storage proteins: structures and biosynthesis" (1995), *The Plant Cell*** — https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.7.7.945 — optional deep dive.
- **Collins, Gerday & Feller, "Xylanases, xylanase families and extremophilic xylanases" (2004), *FEMS Microbiology Reviews*** — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.femsre.2004.06.005 — optional deep dive for the lesson on enzymes and improvers (arabinoxylans).
- **Wikipedia** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch_gelatinization ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch_retrogradation — good for basics and vocabulary; check precise claims against the textbooks above.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

- **Books for bakers**
  - Ken Forkish, *Flour Water Salt Yeast* — practical, percentage-based recipes that make the theory testable.
  - Peter Reinhart, *The Bread Baker's Apprentice* — clear explanations of each stage of bread making.
- **Articles**
  - King Arthur Baking — https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog — science-oriented articles on flour, hydration and gluten.
  - Serious Eats — https://www.seriouseats.com — bread science pieces by J. Kenji López-Alt and Stella Parks.
  - The Perfect Loaf (Maurizio Leo) — https://www.theperfectloaf.com — in-depth sourdough, fermentation and starter guides.
- **Videos**
  - America's Test Kitchen / Cook's Illustrated — https://www.youtube.com/@AmericasTestKitchen — science segments on gluten, browning and baking.
  - Search YouTube for windowpane test and poke test demonstrations to see dough development firsthand.
- **Tools**
  - A kitchen scale and an instant-read thermometer, if the student bakes.
  - A spreadsheet or online baker's-percentage calculator for hydration and ratio work.
- **Unexpected connections (wild cards)**
  - Polymer physics: gluten as a natural elastomer, comparable to rubber.
  - Archaeology: the roughly 14,000-year-old flatbread remains found at the Shubayqa 1 site in Jordan (Arranz-Otaegui et al., 2018, *PNAS*), which predate agriculture.
  - Microbial ecology of sourdough starters: yeast and lactic acid bacteria communities.
  - Maillard chemistry in other foods: coffee roasting and seared steak.

## Lesson by lesson

Found by web search; every link was checked.

### Lesson 1: Why can wheat flour make a risen loaf when rice flour can't?
- ▶ [What is Gluten Anyway? (America's Test Kitchen Kids)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3g7sGpYUieg) — A recipe tester makes 'webbed proteins' easy to picture, so you can see why only wheat dough can trap gas like a balloon.
- 📄 [Science of Bread: Bread Science 101 (Exploratorium)](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/bread/bread_science.html) — It compares gluten to the rubber of a balloon and shows how starch reinforces it, which explains in a few minutes why rice flour falls flat.
- 📄 [Gluten free rice-flour bread could revolutionize global bread production (ScienceDaily)](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/03/170322092653.htm) — Researchers got rice-flour bread to rise with no gluten at all, and how they did it shows exactly what gluten normally does.
- 🕹 [Baking Bread: The Chemistry of Bread-Making (Compound Interest)](https://www.compoundchem.com/2016/01/13/bread/) — A one-page infographic that shows flour proteins, water, enzymes and yeast all working toward a risen loaf.

### Lesson 2: Why does wheat need two different gluten proteins instead of one?
- ▶ [The Only Video You'll Ever Need to Watch About Gluten (Reactions)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-JVO62Vzhqc) — The American Chemical Society's channel breaks gluten into its two proteins and explains, molecule by molecule, why dough both stretches and snaps back.
- 📄 [Gluten: How Does It Work? (Modernist Cuisine)](https://modernistcuisine.com/mcah/gluten-how-does-it-work/) — The rubber-band image (gliadin lets dough stretch, glutenin makes it snap back) will change how you think about every dough you knead.
- 📑 [The structure and properties of gluten: an elastic protein from wheat grain (Shewry et al., Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-structure-and-properties-of-gluten:-an-elastic-Shewry-Halford/8ef16ac3eab3c11c6add37d509b7ce49711df922) — This is the classic primary source for the 'elastic backbone' idea, and its 'loop and train' model of how glutenin stores springiness is easy to picture.

### Lesson 3: If flour is mostly starch, what is all that starch doing in dough?
- ▶ [How a Chemist Makes the Softest Bread You'll Ever Eat](https://youtube.com/watch?pp=0gcJCdgAo7VqN5tD&v=3ziMBDPMuP8) — The tangzhong trick (pre-cooking some flour with water) shows starch grabbing water and turning it into a softer loaf.
- ▶ [What is Starch Gelatinization?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMOgr-Buiag) — A short look at what happens inside starch granules when dough turns into crumb in the oven.
- 📄 [Damaged Starch (BAKERpedia)](https://bakerpedia.com/processes/damaged-starch/) — Granules broken in the mill soak up 2–3 times more water and feed the yeast, so the way flour is milled quietly changes your dough.
- 📑 [The Chemistry of Breadmaking (open-access review)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9238825/) — A free review that follows amylose and amylopectin from dough through baking to staling, for when you want the full story.

### Lesson 4: What are the minor players (arabinoxylans, lipids and enzymes) doing in your flour?
- ▶ [Thursday Thoughts: Baking With Enzymes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7M1X4UFgn4w) — A baker explains how the enzymes already in flour (and the ones mills add) change dough before the yeast even gets going.
- 📄 [Enzymatic Activity (Alpha-Amylase) in Flour & Its Impact on Dough (Pizza Blab)](https://www.pizzablab.com/learning-and-resources/flour/enzymatic-activity-in-flour/) — Too much native amylase, from wheat that sprouted in the field, gives a gummy crumb that seems underbaked, and the 'falling number' test catches it.
- 📄 [Enzymes in Bread Baking: What They Are, How They Work (BAKERpedia e-book)](https://bakerpedia.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Enzymes-ebook_2020.pdf) — A free, plain-language guide to amylases and the xylanases that break down arabinoxylans, the 'minor' fibre that holds up to a quarter of the water in dough.
- 📄 [Bread chemistry on the rise (Chemistry World)](https://www.chemistryworld.com/features/bread-chemistry-on-the-rise/3004720.article) — A chemist's tour of the less-famous parts of flour, such as the lipids that sit at bubble surfaces and keep gas cells from merging.

### Lesson 5: Why does bread flour behave so differently from cake flour?
- ▶ [Expert's Guide to Flour Types (America's Test Kitchen)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=otTGwL5RQn4) — Jack Bishop shows side by side how a few points of protein take you from tender cake to chewy loaf.
- 📄 [What is baker's math, and how can I use it in my everyday bread baking? (King Arthur)](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2022/04/28/what-is-bakers-math-and-how-can-i-use-it-in-my-everyday-bread-baking) — Once flour is always 100%, you can compare any two recipes at a glance and see why each flour needs a different amount of water.
- 📄 [Science of Cooking: Ask the Inquisitive Cooks! (Exploratorium)](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/icooks/4-21-03.html) — Whole wheat flour has more protein yet makes denser bread, because sharp flakes of bran cut the gluten strands as they form.
- 📄 [Wheat flour (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_flour) — A handy reference for how hard and soft wheats are graded into flour types and protein ranges in different countries.

### Lesson 6: If gluten is 'in' flour, why can't you find any in a dry bag?
- ▶ [Science: What happens when you wash a Dough? Here's how to see and feel gluten](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXjvlYKsXgA) — Watch a ball of dough rinsed under water until the starch runs off and only a stretchy, gummy lump of gluten is left.
- 🕹 [Science of Bread: Great Balls of Gluten Activity (Exploratorium)](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/bread/activity-gluten.html) — A kitchen experiment you can do in 20 minutes that proves gluten only exists once water and kneading build it.
- 🕹 [Which Wheat Flour Has the Most Gluten? (Science Buddies)](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/project-ideas/FoodSci_p040/cooking-food-science/which-wheat-flour-has-the-most-gluten) — Turns the dough-washing test into a real experiment: weigh the gluten balls from different flours and check them against the labels.
- 📄 [A beginner's guide to gluten (King Arthur)](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2021/08/18/a-beginners-guide-to-gluten) — It explains why dry flour holds only the two proteins and water is what turns them into gluten.
