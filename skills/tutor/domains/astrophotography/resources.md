# Astrophotography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books
- **"The Deep-sky Imaging Primer" by Charles Bracken** — comprehensive guide to DSLR deep-sky imaging, covers tracking, stacking, and processing workflows. Excellent for intermediate learners making the leap to multi-hour integrations.
- **"Astrophotography" by Thierry Legault** — technical reference covering equipment, techniques, and processing from beginner to advanced. Strong on the physics and optics.
- **"The Astrophotography Manual" by Chris Woodhouse** — practical, project-based approach with detailed equipment recommendations and processing tutorials.
- **"Making Every Photon Count" by Steve Richards** — deep dive into exposure optimization, noise theory, and signal extraction. Advanced but worth reading even at intermediate level.
- **"Digital SLR Astrophotography" by Michael Covington** — classic text, slightly dated but excellent fundamentals on sensor behavior and calibration.

### Online Tutorial Sites
- **Lonely Speck** (https://www.lonelyspeck.com/) — Ian Norman's comprehensive Milky Way and nightscape tutorials. Best resource for wide-field imaging foundations. Start with "Milky Way Photography 101."
- **AstroBackyard** (https://astrobackyard.com/) — Trevor Jones's extensive library of beginner to intermediate deep-sky imaging guides. Covers equipment reviews, techniques, and processing workflows. His "Beginner's Guide to Deep-Sky Astrophotography" is essential.
- **Clarkvision** (https://clarkvision.com/articles/astrophotography.image.processing/) — Roger Clark's technical articles on sensor physics, noise theory, and processing. More advanced but authoritative.
- **Sky & Telescope** (https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/astrophotography-tips/) — monthly technique articles and equipment reviews from professional astronomers and experienced amateurs.

### Courses
- **Lonely Speck's "Milky Way Mastery"** — paid course covering wide-field nightscape photography from planning to final processing.
- **"Deep-Sky Imaging" on CloudyNights** — community-driven tutorials and threads covering every aspect of deep-sky imaging.

### Interactive Tools & Software

#### Planning & Simulation
- **Stellarium** (https://stellarium.org/) — FREE planetarium software for desktop (Windows, Mac, Linux). Essential for target planning and understanding celestial mechanics. Mobile version also available.
- **PhotoPills** (https://www.photopills.com/) — mobile app (iOS/Android) for Milky Way planning, includes augmented reality overlay, sun/moon calculator, and exposure planner. Paid but worth it.
- **The Photographer's Ephemeris** (https://photoephemeris.com/) — planning tool for sun, moon, and Milky Way positions. Desktop and mobile versions.
- **SkySafari** (https://skysafariastronomy.com/) — comprehensive sky atlas and planning app (iOS/Android). More detailed than Stellarium mobile.
- **Clear Sky Chart** (https://www.cleardarksky.com/) — weather forecasting specifically for astronomers. Shows cloud cover, transparency, and seeing predictions.
- **Light Pollution Map** (https://www.lightpollutionmap.info/) — interactive map showing Bortle scale values worldwide. Essential for finding dark sky sites.
- **Dark Site Finder** (https://darksitefinder.com/) — North America focused light pollution map with cleaner interface.

#### Acquisition & Control
- **BackyardEOS** (https://www.backyardeos.com/) — camera control software for Canon DSLRs (paid). Enables intervalometer, bulb ramping, and live view focus.
- **NINA** (Nighttime Imaging 'N' Astronomy) (https://nighttime-imaging.eu/) — FREE open-source acquisition suite. Supports camera control, autoguiding, sequencing, and planetarium integration. Rapidly becoming the standard.
- **APT** (AstroPhotography Tool) (https://www.astrophotography.app/) — FREE camera control and planning software. Simpler than NINA but very capable.
- **SharpCap** (https://www.sharpcap.co.uk/) — camera control focused on polar alignment, live stacking, and planetary imaging. Free version is excellent; Pro unlocks advanced features.
- **PHD2** (https://openphdguiding.org/) — FREE autoguiding software. Essential once you move to guided imaging (beyond this intermediate curriculum but worth knowing about).

#### Stacking & Calibration
- **DeepSkyStacker** (http://deepskystacker.free.fr/english/index.html) — FREE Windows stacking software. Industry standard for DSLR deep-sky stacking. Simple interface, excellent results.
- **Siril** (https://siril.org/) — FREE cross-platform stacking and processing software (Windows, Mac, Linux). Good alternative to DSS, especially for Mac users.
- **Sequator** (https://sites.google.com/view/sequator/) — FREE Windows tool for stacking Milky Way foreground+sky composites. Handles landscape blending automatically.
- **Starry Landscape Stacker** (https://sites.google.com/site/starrylandscapestacker/) — Mac-only stacking tool (paid). Excellent for wide-field nightscapes.
- **ASTAP** (https://www.hnsky.org/astap.htm) — FREE stacking and plate-solving software. Lightweight and fast.

#### Processing
- **PixInsight** (https://pixinsight.com/) — Commercial processing software ($300). Industry standard for serious astrophotography. Steep learning curve but unmatched power.
- **Adobe Photoshop** (https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop.html) — Standard image editor. Excellent for final processing, layer masking, and compositing. Subscription-based.
- **GIMP** (https://www.gimp.org/) — FREE open-source alternative to Photoshop. Fully capable for astrophotography processing.
- **darktable** (https://www.darktable.org/) — FREE open-source RAW processor (alternative to Lightroom). Good for linear stretching and initial adjustments.
- **GraXpert** (https://github.com/Steffenhir/GraXpert) — FREE AI-powered gradient removal tool. Works as standalone or Photoshop plugin. Game-changer for light pollution removal.
- **StarTools** (https://www.startools.org/) — Commercial processing software focused on simplicity and non-destructive workflow. Good middle ground between DSS and PixInsight.

#### Planetary Imaging
- **AutoStakkert** (https://www.autostakkert.com/) — FREE lucky imaging software for planetary and lunar stacking. Analyzes video frames and stacks best percentile.
- **RegiStax** (https://www.astronomie.be/registax/) — FREE planetary processing software. Wavelet sharpening and alignment.
- **PIPP** (Planetary Imaging PreProcessor) (https://sites.google.com/site/astropipp/) — FREE preprocessing for planetary video (cropping, debayering, format conversion).

### Communities & Forums
- **CloudyNights** (https://www.cloudynights.com/) — largest astrophotography forum. Sections for imaging, equipment, processing, and beginners. Incredibly helpful community.
- **r/astrophotography** (https://www.reddit.com/r/astrophotography/) — active Reddit community with weekly challenges, image sharing, and critique threads. Read the wiki and posting guidelines.
- **r/LandscapeAstro** (https://www.reddit.com/r/LandscapeAstro/) — focused on wide-field Milky Way and nightscape imaging.
- **Stargazers Lounge** (https://stargazerslounge.com/) — UK-based forum with active imaging section and equipment classifieds.
- **Astrobin** (https://www.astrobin.com/) — image sharing platform with full EXIF data. Browse by target, equipment, or integration time to see what's possible with your gear.
- **PixInsight Forum** (https://pixinsight.com/forum/) — official support and techniques forum for PixInsight users.

### Data Sources & Example Images
- **Astrobin** (https://www.astrobin.com/) — search by target name to see acquisition details (exposure, ISO, integration time, equipment) for thousands of images. Learn what's possible.
- **NASA APOD** (Astronomy Picture of the Day) (https://apod.nasa.gov/) — daily featured astrophoto with description. Archive goes back to 1995.
- **European Southern Observatory Image Gallery** (https://www.eso.org/public/images/) — professional quality deep-sky images for reference and inspiration.
- **Free Sample Data** — many YouTubers (Nebula Photos, Chuck's Astrophotography) provide sample light/dark/flat frames for practice processing.

## Tools & Hardware

### Star Trackers (intermediate level)
- **iOptron SkyGuider Pro** — popular mid-range tracker, 11 lb payload, built-in polar scope ($400-500)
- **Sky-Watcher Star Adventurer 2i** — feature-rich tracker with WiFi control, illuminated polar scope ($400-500)
- **Sky-Watcher Star Adventurer Mini (SAM)** — ultra-portable tracker for lightweight setups ($350-400)
- **Move Shoot Move** — modular tracker system with time-lapse capability ($300-600 depending on config)

### Accessories
- **Intervalometers** — wired or wireless remote shutter release for long exposure sequences
  - Canon: Canon TC-80N3 or cheap 3rd party equivalents
  - Nikon: Nikon MC-36 or 3rd party
  - Sony: wireless via app or 3rd party cables
- **Bahtinov Masks** — available on Amazon/eBay for $20-50, or DIY templates online
- **Dew Heaters** — prevent lens fogging in humid conditions (Dew-Not strips, powered dew shields)
- **Red Headlamps** — preserve night vision during field sessions
- **Power Banks** — high-capacity USB power banks or 12V battery packs for all-night sessions

### Recommended Budget Setup (intermediate)
- **Camera:** Used Canon 6D, Nikon D750, or Sony A7 II (~$600-900 used)
- **Lens:** Rokinon/Samyang 135mm f/2 (~$450) for tracked deep-sky, or Rokinon 24mm f/1.4 (~$600) for wide-field
- **Tracker:** Sky-Watcher Star Adventurer 2i ($450)
- **Tripod:** Sturdy photo tripod ($100-200)
- **Accessories:** intervalometer ($20), Bahtinov mask ($30), dew heater ($40)
- **Software:** DeepSkyStacker (free), Siril (free), GIMP (free)
- **Total:** ~$2,000-2,500 for a complete capable setup

## Unexpected Connections (rabbit holes for curious students)

### Astrophotography ↔ Computer Vision
- Image stacking algorithms are related to HDR imaging, super-resolution, and image registration in computer vision
- Star detection = blob detection in CV
- Plate solving (astrometry) uses pattern matching and catalog databases

### Astrophotography ↔ Signal Processing
- Noise reduction techniques borrow from radar and audio DSP
- Fourier analysis for periodic noise removal (amplifier glow, walking noise)
- Deconvolution for sharpening (same math as audio de-reverb)

### Astrophotography ↔ Climate Science
- Same sensors (scientific CMOS) used in Earth observation satellites
- Calibration frame techniques adapted from remote sensing
- Atmospheric correction methods shared between astrophotography and satellite imagery

### Astrophotography ↔ Neuroscience
- Calcium imaging in neuroscience uses identical stacking and SNR improvement techniques
- Both deal with faint signals in noisy environments
- Processing pipelines share mathematical foundations

### DIY & Maker Culture
- 3D-printed telescope parts, custom filter wheels, Arduino-based focusers
- Open-source hardware designs (OnStep telescope controller)
- Citizen science projects (AAVSO variable star observations using astrophotography)

### Historical Astrophotography
- Study glass plate photography from the early 1900s — fundamentals haven't changed
- Harvard Plate Collection — 500,000 plates, some now digitized
- Palomar Sky Survey — historical all-sky survey, compare modern amateur images to professional work from 1950s
