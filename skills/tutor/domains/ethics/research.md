# Ethics Research Summary

## Topic Overview

Ethics (normative ethics specifically) addresses the question "How should we act?" Three major frameworks dominate modern moral philosophy:

1. **Utilitarianism** — Consequentialist theory: actions are right if they maximize overall happiness/well-being
2. **Deontology** — Duty-based theory: actions are right if they follow universal moral rules/duties
3. **Virtue Ethics** — Character-based theory: actions are right if they reflect good character and promote human flourishing

## Major Subtopics

### Foundations
- Normative vs. descriptive ethics
- Moral reasoning and justification
- The relationship between theory and practice
- Meta-ethical questions (what makes something "good"?)

### Utilitarianism
- Classical utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill)
- Act vs. rule utilitarianism
- Hedonic vs. preference utilitarianism
- Common objections (rights, justice, demandingness)
- Modern developments (Singer, effective altruism)

### Deontology
- Kantian ethics (categorical imperative, autonomy, dignity)
- Moral absolutism vs. prima facie duties (Ross)
- Rights-based theories
- Common objections (conflicting duties, consequences)

### Virtue Ethics
- Aristotelian foundations (eudaimonia, virtues, practical wisdom)
- Character development and moral education
- Moral exemplars
- Common objections (relativism, action guidance)

### Applied Ethics
- Comparing frameworks on real-world cases
- Trolley problems and moral dilemmas
- Professional ethics, bioethics, environmental ethics
- Integration and pluralism

## Key Sources

### Academic Encyclopedias
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (https://plato.stanford.edu) — gold standard for technical entries
- Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (https://iep.utm.edu) — more accessible, pedagogically oriented
- PhilPapers (https://philpapers.org) — research database

### Textbooks & Courses
- Rachels & Rachels, *The Elements of Moral Philosophy* — standard undergraduate text
- Shafer-Landau, *The Fundamentals of Ethics* — clear, accessible
- MIT OpenCourseWare: 24.231 Ethics (https://ocw.mit.edu)
- Yale Open Courses: Phil 181 (Justice with Michael Sandel)

### Video & Interactive
- Crash Course Philosophy (YouTube) — episodes 32-40 cover ethics
- Wireless Philosophy (https://www.wi-phi.com) — short animated philosophy videos
- The Trolley Problem Game and other thought experiment simulators
- Justice with Michael Sandel (Harvard/edX)

### Primary Texts (intermediate-accessible)
- Mill, *Utilitarianism* (1861) — short, readable
- Kant, *Grounding for the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785) — challenging but essential
- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* — Books I-II for virtue ethics foundations
- Singer, "Famine, Affluence, and Morality" (1972) — applied utilitarian argument

## Learning Considerations for Intermediate Level

- Students likely have some exposure to philosophical argumentation
- Can handle primary texts with guidance
- Ready for formal argument analysis (premises, validity, soundness)
- Need scaffolding for Kant (notoriously difficult)
- Benefit from real-world applications to motivate theory
- Misconception risk: reducing theories to caricatures (e.g., "utilitarianism = do whatever makes you happy")
