# Ethics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks (intermediate level)
- **Rachels & Rachels, *The Elements of Moral Philosophy* (9th ed., 2018)** — Clear, accessible introduction to major ethical theories. Excellent for intermediate students. Covers utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, plus social contract theory and feminist ethics. [Oxford University Press]
- **Shafer-Landau, *The Fundamentals of Ethics* (5th ed., 2021)** — Engaging textbook with cases and discussion questions. Less technical than Rachels. Good for students who want accessibility. [Oxford University Press]
- **Driver, *Ethics: The Fundamentals* (2nd ed., 2013)** — Shorter, focused on the big three theories. Good supplementary text. [Wiley-Blackwell]

### Online Courses & Lecture Notes
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 24.231 Ethics** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-231-ethics-fall-2009/ — Complete course materials including lecture notes, readings, and assignments. Intermediate/advanced level.
- **Yale Open Courses: Justice with Michael Sandel (Phil 181)** — https://oyc.yale.edu/political-science/plsc-118 — Famous course on justice and ethics. Video lectures, transcripts, readings. Very accessible and engaging.
- **Coursera: Introduction to Philosophy (University of Edinburgh)** — https://www.coursera.org/learn/philosophy — Weeks 3-4 cover ethics. Free to audit.
- **edX: Justice (Harvard, Michael Sandel)** — https://www.edx.org/course/justice — Same material as Yale course, different platform.

### Primary Texts (read with guidance)
All three are challenging but essential for intermediate students:

- **John Stuart Mill, *Utilitarianism* (1861)** — Short (~60 pages), readable. Chapters 2 and 4 are essential. Free at Project Gutenberg: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11224
- **Immanuel Kant, *Grounding for the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785)** — Difficult but foundational for deontology. Focus on Section 2 (formulations of categorical imperative). Free translations online.
- **Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*** — Books I-II for virtue ethics foundations (eudaimonia, virtues, doctrine of mean). Free at MIT Classics: http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.html

### Academic Encyclopedias (gold standard for reference)
- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy** — https://plato.stanford.edu — Authoritative, peer-reviewed articles. Key entries:
  - Utilitarianism: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/utilitarianism-history/
  - Deontological Ethics: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/
  - Virtue Ethics: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/
  - Kant's Moral Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-moral/
  - Aristotle's Ethics: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-ethics/

- **Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy** — https://iep.utm.edu — More pedagogically oriented, slightly less technical:
  - Ethics: https://iep.utm.edu/ethics/
  - Utilitarianism: https://iep.utm.edu/util-a-r/

## Cross-Disciplinary Connections

### Psychology
- **Moral Development** — Kohlberg, Gilligan (stages of moral reasoning)
- **Trolley Problems & Neural Correlates** — Greene's fMRI studies
- **Moral Foundations Theory** — Haidt (5-6 moral foundations across cultures)

### Economics
- **Game Theory** — Prisoner's dilemma, tragedy of the commons (connections to consequentialism)
- **Behavioral Economics** — How people actually make moral/altruistic decisions (vs. what theories predict)
- **Welfare Economics** — Utility, social choice theory, Pareto optimality

### Political Philosophy
- **John Rawls** — *A Theory of Justice* (combines Kantian and consequentialist elements)
- **Robert Nozick** — Libertarian ethics, rights theory
- **Amartya Sen** — Capabilities approach (alternative to utilitarianism)

### Neuroscience
- **Moral Decision-Making** — What brain regions activate during moral judgments?
- **Psychopathy & Moral Agency** — Can someone be evil if their brain is broken?

### AI & Technology
- **Machine Ethics** — How should AI make moral decisions? (trolley problem for self-driving cars)
- **Alignment Problem** — How to encode human values in AI?
- **Surveillance & Privacy** — Applied ethics of technology

### Biology
- **Evolutionary Ethics** — Did morality evolve? What does that mean for moral realism?
- **Altruism in Animals** — Do other species have morality?

### Literature & Film
- **Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov*** — "If God doesn't exist, is everything permitted?"
- **Ursula K. Le Guin, *The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas*** — Utilitarianism short story
- **The Good Place (TV)** — Explores all three ethical theories with humor

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Effective Altruism Movement
Modern utilitarian-inspired social movement focused on doing the most good with your resources. Organizations:
- **80,000 Hours** — https://80000hours.org — Career advice for doing good
- **GiveWell** — https://www.givewell.org — Charity evaluation
- **The Life You Can Save** — https://www.thelifeyoucansave.org — Peter Singer's organization

### Stoicism (Modern Resurgence)
Ancient virtue ethics school (Epictetus, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius) experiencing popularity surge:
- **Daily Stoic** — https://dailystoic.com
- **Massimo Pigliucci** — https://platofootnote.wordpress.com — Modern Stoic philosopher

### Buddhist Ethics
Non-Western ethical framework with overlaps to virtue ethics:
- Emphasis on compassion, wisdom, mindfulness
- Middle way (similar to Aristotle's mean?)
- Jay Garfield's work on Buddhist philosophy

### Ethics Bowls & Competitions
- **National High School Ethics Bowl** — https://nhseb.unc.edu
- **Intercollegiate Ethics Bowl** — Case-based ethics competitions
- Great for practicing applied ethics reasoning

### Moral Uncertainty
New area: What should you do when you're not sure which moral theory is correct?
- **MacAskill, Bykvist, & Ord, *Moral Uncertainty*** (2020)

### Population Ethics & Longtermism
- Should we care about future generations? How much?
- Existential risk and the long-term future
- Toby Ord, *The Precipice*

## For Students Who Want to Go Deeper

### Next Topics to Explore
1. **Meta-Ethics** — What does "good" mean? Moral realism vs. anti-realism
2. **Care Ethics** — Feminist alternative to the big three
3. **Social Contract Theory** — Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Rawls
4. **Bioethics** — Abortion, euthanasia, cloning, genetic engineering
5. **Animal Ethics** — Do animals have rights? (Singer, Regan, Korsgaard)
6. **Environmental Ethics** — Intrinsic value of nature, climate obligations

### Advanced Texts
- Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (1984) — Challenging but mind-blowing
- T.M. Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other* (1998) — Contractualism
- Bernard Williams, *Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy* (1985) — Critique of moral theory
- Philippa Foot, *Virtues and Vices* (1978) — Modern virtue ethics

### Research Skills
- Learn to use PhilPapers for finding academic articles
- Read book reviews to find new work in ethics
- Follow journals: *Ethics*, *Philosophy & Public Affairs*, *Journal of Moral Philosophy*
- Attend philosophy talks (many universities live-stream)
