# Medical Imaging — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Radiation Physics & X-ray Production
- Electromagnetic radiation basics
- X-ray generation (Bremsstrahlung, characteristic radiation)
- Interaction with matter (photoelectric effect, Compton scattering)
- Radiation safety and dose considerations

### 2. X-ray Radiography
- Image formation and geometry
- Contrast and spatial resolution
- Scatter reduction (grids, air gaps)
- Digital radiography (CR, DR)
- Mammography

### 3. Computed Tomography (CT)
- Projection data and reconstruction algorithms (filtered back-projection)
- Hounsfield units and tissue characterization
- Multi-slice CT and helical scanning
- CT artifacts
- Dose optimization (iterative reconstruction)

### 4. Ultrasound Imaging
- Acoustic wave physics
- Piezoelectric transducers
- Pulse-echo imaging and time-of-flight
- Doppler ultrasound (blood flow)
- Image artifacts (shadowing, reverberation)

### 5. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
- Nuclear magnetic resonance fundamentals
- T1, T2, and proton density contrast
- Pulse sequences (spin echo, gradient echo)
- K-space and frequency encoding
- Fast imaging techniques (EPI, turbo spin echo)
- Functional MRI (fMRI) basics

### 6. Nuclear Medicine
- Radiotracer principles
- SPECT (Single Photon Emission CT)
- PET (Positron Emission Tomography)
- PET/CT fusion imaging

### 7. Image Processing & Analysis
- Noise and filtering
- Segmentation
- Image registration
- Quantitative imaging biomarkers
- AI/deep learning in medical imaging

### 8. Clinical Applications & Safety
- Choosing the right modality
- Image interpretation basics
- Radiation safety (ALARA principle)
- Contrast agents and safety
- Emerging technologies

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **"The Essential Physics of Medical Imaging" by Bushberg et al.** — Gold standard textbook, comprehensive coverage from basic physics through all modalities
- **"Physics of Radiology" by Hendee & Ritenour** — Strong physics foundation
- **"MRI: The Basics" by Ray Hashemi** — Accessible MRI introduction

### Online Courses & Lectures
- **NIBIB (National Institute of Biomedical Imaging and Bioengineering)** — Educational modules on all imaging modalities
- **Radiopaedia.org** — Extensive case library and educational articles
- **AAPM (American Association of Physicists in Medicine)** — Educational resources and webinars

### Interactive Tools
- **XRayPhysics.com** — Interactive X-ray physics simulations
- **MRI Questions** — Question bank for MRI physics
- **DICOM viewers** (OsiriX, 3D Slicer) — Hands-on image analysis

### Video Resources
- **Radiology Tutorials** on YouTube (various channels)
- **Khan Academy** — Basic radiation physics
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** — HST.583 Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging

## Target Audience Considerations

For **intermediate** level:
- Assumes basic physics (mechanics, electricity & magnetism, waves)
- Assumes basic anatomy
- May have some programming background (for image processing)
- Goal: understand the physics behind each modality, recognize artifacts, appreciate clinical tradeoffs
- Depth: quantitative but not highly mathematical (conceptual understanding of equations, not derivations)
