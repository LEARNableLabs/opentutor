# Medical Imaging — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"The Essential Physics of Medical Imaging" by Bushberg, Seibert, Leidholdt, Boone** — The gold standard textbook. Comprehensive coverage of all modalities from physics fundamentals through clinical applications. Dense but authoritative. Best for intermediate to advanced learners. [Publisher link](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/ovid/the-essential-physics-of-medical-imaging-2370)

- **"Physics of Radiology" by Hendee & Ritenour** — Strong physics foundation, more mathematically rigorous than Bushberg. Excellent for students with solid physics background.

- **"MRI: The Basics" by Ray Hashemi, William Bradley, Christopher Lisanti** — Accessible introduction to MRI physics. Good balance of conceptual explanations and mathematical detail. Ideal for intermediate learners tackling MRI for the first time.

- **"Diagnostic Ultrasound: Physics and Equipment" by Peter Hoskins, Kevin Martin, Abigail Thrush** — Comprehensive ultrasound physics textbook. Covers transducers, beam formation, Doppler, and quality assurance.

- **XRayPhysics.com** — Interactive demonstrations of X-ray production, attenuation, and scatter. [http://www.xrayphysics.com](http://www.xrayphysics.com) *(Note: site availability varies)*

- **MRI simulator tools:**
  - **MRISIMUL** — Browser-based MRI pulse sequence simulator. Adjust TR, TE, and see resulting image contrast. 
  - **MRIcro / MRIcroGL** — Free software for viewing and analyzing MRI data. [https://www.nitrc.org/projects/mricron](https://www.nitrc.org/projects/mricron)

- **OsiriX (Mac) / Horos (Mac, free) / 3D Slicer (cross-platform, free)** — DICOM viewers for exploring real medical imaging datasets. 3D Slicer is particularly powerful for segmentation and 3D reconstruction. [https://www.slicer.org](https://www.slicer.org)

- **The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA)** — Public repository of medical images (CT, MRI, PET) with clinical data. Download real datasets for exploration. [https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net)

- **Visible Human Project (National Library of Medicine)** — CT, MRI, and anatomical cross-sections of entire human bodies. [https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/visible/visible_human.html](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/visible/visible_human.html)

### Code & Computational Tools

- **SimpleITK / ITK** — Image processing libraries (Python, C++, R, Java) for medical imaging. Segmentation, registration, filtering. [https://simpleitk.org](https://simpleitk.org)

- **DICOM Python (pydicom)** — Read, modify, and write DICOM files in Python. Essential for medical imaging programming. [https://pydicom.github.io](https://pydicom.github.io)

- **TorchIO** — PyTorch library for medical image preprocessing and augmentation. Good for deep learning on medical images. [https://torchio.readthedocs.io](https://torchio.readthedocs.io)

- **MONAI (Medical Open Network for AI)** — PyTorch-based framework for deep learning in medical imaging. Developed by NVIDIA, King's College London, et al. [https://monai.io](https://monai.io)

- **FreeSurfer** — Brain MRI analysis software (segmentation, surface reconstruction, cortical thickness). Gold standard in neuroimaging research. [https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu](https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu)

- **Jupyter notebooks on medical imaging:**
  - Search GitHub for "medical imaging" + "jupyter notebook" for hundreds of educational notebooks on reconstruction, segmentation, ML.

## Historical Documents & Papers (for deeper dives)

- **Röntgen's original X-ray paper (1895)** — "On a New Kind of Rays" — remarkably readable, short, and exciting. Available in translation from many sources.
- **Lauterbur's original MRI paper (1973)** — "Image Formation by Induced Local Interactions: Examples Employing Nuclear Magnetic Resonance" in *Nature*. The birth of MRI.
- **Hounsfield's original CT paper (1973)** — "Computerized transverse axial scanning (tomography): Part I. Description of system" in *British Journal of Radiology*. Nobel Prize-winning work.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Medical imaging meets art
- **"X-ray vision: The Art of Seeing Inside"** — Museums have used X-ray, CT, and MRI to analyze paintings, mummies, and sculptures non-destructively. The Louvre, British Museum, and Getty all have imaging labs.
- **Nick Veasey** — Artist who creates stunning large-scale X-ray images of everyday objects (flowers, cars, fashion). Beautiful and educational.

### Imaging in veterinary medicine
- The same physics applies to animals! Veterinary radiology uses identical equipment. Challenges: patient positioning (try getting a cat to hold still for an MRI), dose concerns for small animals, anatomical variation.
- **Zoo CT/MRI** — Major zoos use medical imaging to diagnose elephants, pandas, etc. Same scanners, different patients.

### Astronomy and medical imaging share math
- **Image reconstruction** techniques (filtered back-projection, iterative methods) are used in radio astronomy to create images from interferometric data (e.g., ALMA, Event Horizon Telescope black hole image).
- **Compressed sensing** is used in both MRI and astronomical imaging to reduce acquisition time/data volume.

### Security screening
- **Airport baggage scanners** are essentially CT scanners (dual-energy CT can distinguish materials by atomic number, detecting explosives or liquids).
- **Backscatter X-ray** (controversial full-body scanners) uses Compton scatter to image surface features without transmission.

### Industrial imaging (NDT — Non-Destructive Testing)
- X-ray and CT are used extensively to inspect welds, castings, composite materials (aircraft wings, turbine blades) without destroying them.
- Ultrasound is used to detect cracks in pipelines, railway tracks, and structural components.
- Same physics, different applications.

### Geophysics
- **Seismic tomography** (imaging Earth's interior from earthquake waves) uses the same math as medical CT — just different waves (elastic vs electromagnetic) and vastly different scales.
