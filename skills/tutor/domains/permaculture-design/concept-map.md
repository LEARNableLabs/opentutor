# Permaculture Design — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Three Ethics** — Earth Care, People Care, Fair Share; foundation for all design decisions
2. **Systems Thinking** — understanding interconnected elements, feedback loops, and emergent properties
3. **Observation** — protracted observation as primary design tool; reading patterns and collecting data
4. **Pattern Recognition** — identifying natural patterns and applying them to design
5. **Zone Planning** — organizing space by frequency of use and energy input (zones 0-5)
6. **Sector Analysis** — mapping external energies acting on site (sun, wind, fire, water, etc.). Depends on: Observation
7. **Design Principles** — Holmgren's 12 principles as design framework. Depends on: Three Ethics, Systems Thinking
8. **Stacking Functions** — each element serves multiple purposes; each function served by multiple elements. Depends on: Design Principles, Systems Thinking
9. **Edge Effect** — maximizing productive interfaces between systems. Depends on: Pattern Recognition, Design Principles
10. **Water Flow & Catchment** — understanding site hydrology and water movement. Depends on: Observation, Sector Analysis
11. **Swales & Earthworks** — passive water harvesting and landscape regeneration. Depends on: Water Flow, Zone Planning
12. **Soil Food Web** — understanding biological soil systems and fertility cycling. Depends on: Systems Thinking
13. **Soil Building** — no-dig methods, sheet mulching, composting. Depends on: Soil Food Web
14. **Plant Guilds** — companion planting for mutual benefit and function stacking. Depends on: Stacking Functions, Soil Food Web
15. **Forest Gardens** — perennial polyculture systems with vertical layering. Depends on: Plant Guilds, Edge Effect, Succession
16. **Animal Integration** — animals as system elements with multiple functions. Depends on: Stacking Functions, Soil Building
17. **IPM (Integrated Pest Management)** — ecological pest control through biodiversity. Depends on: Systems Thinking, Animal Integration
18. **Scale of Permanence** — Yeomans framework for design sequencing. Depends on: All observation concepts
19. **Pattern to Details** — working from large patterns down to specific placements. Depends on: Pattern Recognition, Scale of Permanence
20. **Whole System Design** — integrating all elements into coherent plan. Depends on: All previous concepts
21. **Phased Implementation** — prioritizing and timing construction/planting over years. Depends on: Whole System Design, Scale of Permanence
22. **Adaptive Maintenance** — observing and adjusting mature systems. Depends on: Observation, Systems Thinking, Feedback Loops

## Dependencies

### Observation Foundation
- **Zone Planning** requires **Observation** because you must understand how you actually use space and where energy is needed
- **Sector Analysis** requires **Observation** because identifying external forces demands careful attention to site conditions over time
- **Water Flow** requires **Observation** because understanding hydrology means watching water movement in different weather conditions

### Systems Thinking Web
- **Stacking Functions** builds on **Systems Thinking** because recognizing how elements interact is prerequisite to designing multi-functional relationships
- **Plant Guilds** require **Systems Thinking** and **Stacking Functions** because guilds are about beneficial interactions and shared functions
- **Animal Integration** requires **Stacking Functions** because animals must serve multiple roles (fertility, pest control, food)
- **IPM** requires **Systems Thinking** because ecological pest management depends on understanding predator-prey and habitat relationships

### Design Principles as Bridge
- **Design Principles** connect **Ethics** to **Practice** — they translate values into actionable patterns
- **Edge Effect** applies Design Principle 11 (use edges and value the marginal)
- **Stacking Functions** applies Design Principle 4 (apply self-regulation and accept feedback)
- **Small, Slow Solutions** (Principle 9) informs **Phased Implementation**

### Water & Earth Systems
- **Swales** require **Water Flow** understanding because placement depends on contour and catchment
- **Earthworks** require **Zone Planning** and **Sector Analysis** because large infrastructure must be placed according to permanence and external forces
- **Soil Building** requires **Soil Food Web** knowledge because effective composting and mulching depend on understanding biological processes

### Biological Systems Integration
- **Forest Gardens** require **Plant Guilds**, **Edge Effect**, and **Succession** because they integrate companion planting with vertical structure and temporal change
- **Plant Guilds** require **Soil Building** because understanding nutrient cycling helps select complementary plants (nitrogen fixers, dynamic accumulators)

### Design Methodology
- **Scale of Permanence** integrates all observation concepts because it sequences design from most permanent (climate, landform) to least (plants, animals)
- **Pattern to Details** requires **Pattern Recognition** and **Scale of Permanence** because it's about working from landscape-level patterns down through placement details
- **Whole System Design** requires ALL previous concepts because it synthesizes observation, principles, and systems into coherent plans
- **Phased Implementation** requires **Whole System Design** and **Scale of Permanence** because prioritization depends on understanding the complete system and what elements must come first

## Critical Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are prerequisites for many downstream ideas:

1. **Systems Thinking** — required for almost all advanced concepts; students who don't grasp interconnection and feedback will struggle with integration
2. **Observation** — without this skill, all analysis is guesswork; foundational for zone/sector planning and water systems
3. **Stacking Functions** — central to permaculture efficiency; required for guilds, animal integration, and whole system design
4. **Water Flow** — water is the primary design driver in most climates; misunderstanding hydrology leads to failed earthworks and erosion

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Permaculture is just organic gardening"** — misses systems integration, design methodology, and broader application
- **"Zones are physical areas"** — zones are about energy/frequency, not fixed boundaries; they shift with users and needs
- **"More diversity is always better"** — need *appropriate* diversity; random polycultures without guild logic create competition and confusion
- **"Permaculture means no intervention"** — permaculture designs systems but requires strategic intervention; not passive rewilding
- **"Design happens once"** — design is iterative; observation and adaptation continue throughout implementation and maintenance

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic Ecology** — needed for: Systems Thinking, Soil Food Web, IPM, Plant Guilds
- **Gardening/Land Management** — needed for: Observation skills, understanding plant needs, soil building
- **Sustainability Concepts** — needed for: Three Ethics, understanding resource limits and closed-loop systems
- **Basic Mapping/Spatial Thinking** — helpful for: Zone Planning, Sector Analysis, site design

## Integration Points

Where concepts come together:

- **Guild Design** = Plant Guilds + Stacking Functions + Soil Building + Edge Effect
- **Site Analysis** = Observation + Zone Planning + Sector Analysis + Water Flow
- **Earthworks Design** = Water Flow + Scale of Permanence + Zone Planning + Pattern Recognition
- **Food Forest** = Plant Guilds + Forest Gardens + Edge Effect + Succession + Soil Building
- **Whole System** = ALL concepts integrated into coherent, adaptive design
