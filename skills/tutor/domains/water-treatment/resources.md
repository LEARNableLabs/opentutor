# Water Treatment and Environmental Engineering — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Water Quality & Treatment: A Handbook on Drinking Water** (AWWA, 6th edition) — The definitive reference for drinking water treatment processes. Comprehensive coverage of coagulation, filtration, disinfection, and advanced treatment. Ideal for intermediate students who need both theory and practical design guidance.
  - Publisher: McGraw-Hill
  - https://www.awwa.org/Store/Water-Quality-Treatment/ProductDetail/65650

- **Wastewater Engineering: Treatment and Resource Recovery** (Metcalf & Eddy, 5th edition) — Industry standard for wastewater treatment. Excellent on biological processes, nutrient removal, and activated sludge. Strong on design calculations and real-world constraints.
  - Publisher: McGraw-Hill
  - https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/wastewater-engineering-treatment-resource-recovery-metcalf-eddy-inc-tchobanoglous/M9780073401188.html

- **Environmental Engineering: Fundamentals, Sustainability, Design** (Mihelcic & Zimmerman, 3rd edition) — Integrates sustainability throughout. Good for students who want broader environmental context beyond just unit operations.
  - Publisher: Wiley
  - https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Environmental+Engineering%3A+Fundamentals%2C+Sustainability%2C+Design%2C+3rd+Edition-p-9781119494379

- **Chemistry for Environmental Engineering and Science** (Sawyer, McCarty, Parkin, 5th edition) — Strong foundation in aquatic chemistry, kinetics, and microbiology. Essential for understanding reaction mechanisms and water chemistry.
  - Publisher: McGraw-Hill
  - https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/chemistry-environmental-engineering-science-sawyer-mccarty/M9780072480665.html

### University Courses (Open Access)

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 1.77 Water Quality Control** — Complete lecture notes, problem sets, and exams from MIT's water treatment course. Excellent for quantitative problem-solving practice.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-77-water-quality-control-spring-2006/

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 1.018 Fundamentals of Ecology** — Provides ecological context for biological treatment processes. Covers microbial ecology and nutrient cycling.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-018j-ecology-i-the-earth-system-fall-2009/

- **TU Delft: Wastewater Treatment (edX)** — Online course covering biological wastewater treatment with process modeling. Includes quizzes and design exercises.
  - https://www.edx.org/learn/environmental-science/delft-university-of-technology-wastewater-treatment

- **Coursera: Water Supply and Sanitation Policy in Developing Countries** (University of Manchester) — Broader policy context for water systems. Good for understanding real-world constraints beyond engineering.
  - https://www.coursera.org/specializations/water-supply

### Interactive Tools and Simulators

- **EPANET** — Water distribution system modeling software. Free from EPA. Useful for understanding hydraulics and water quality in distribution networks.
  - https://www.epa.gov/water-research/epanet

- **EPA SWMM (Storm Water Management Model)** — Models stormwater runoff and combined sewer systems. Free software for urban drainage analysis.
  - https://www.epa.gov/water-research/storm-water-management-model-swmm

- **Desmos Graphing Calculator** — Online graphing tool. Excellent for visualizing kinetic models (Monod, Chick-Watson, adsorption isotherms). Students can manipulate parameters and see immediate effects.
  - https://www.desmos.com/calculator

- **BioWin (EnviroSim)** — Wastewater treatment process simulator. Commercial software but offers educational licenses. Models activated sludge, nutrient removal, and more.
  - https://envirosim.com/products/biowin

### Regulatory and Technical Resources

- **EPA Office of Water** — All federal drinking water and wastewater regulations, technical guidance documents, and design manuals.
  - https://www.epa.gov/environmental-topics/water-topics
  - Drinking water regulations: https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/national-primary-drinking-water-regulations
  - Wastewater regulations: https://www.epa.gov/npdes

- **WHO Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality** — International standards for drinking water. Good for comparing US vs. global approaches.
  - https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549950

- **Water Environment Federation (WEF)** — Professional organization for wastewater professionals. Excellent technical manuals and practice-oriented resources.
  - https://www.wef.org/

- **American Water Works Association (AWWA)** — Professional organization for drinking water. Standards, manuals of practice, and journal articles.
  - https://www.awwa.org/

### Code and Data

- **EPA WaterFX Data Tool** — Interactive tool for exploring water quality data from US drinking water systems.
  - https://www.epa.gov/waterdata

- **USGS Water Quality Data** — Real-time and historical water quality monitoring data for US rivers, lakes, and groundwater.
  - https://waterdata.usgs.gov/nwis

- **GitHub: wastewater-treatment** — Open-source code examples for treatment process calculations in Python and R. Good for students who want to automate calculations.
  - Search GitHub for "wastewater treatment python" or "activated sludge model"

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **The Martian and water reclamation** — Andy Weir's novel features detailed water recycling on Mars. Shows extreme closed-loop systems. Great for motivation.

- **Biomimicry in water treatment** — Mussels' natural filtration, mangrove roots for nutrient removal, bacterial biofilms. Nature as design inspiration.
  - https://biomimicry.org/ (search for water treatment examples)

- **Water treatment in brewing and distilling** — Breweries are obsessive about water quality. Connect to lesson on water characterization and specific ion removal.

- **Aquarium filtration systems** — Home aquariums use biological filters, activated carbon, UV sterilizers. Miniature wastewater treatment plants that students can observe.

- **Historical water treatment disasters** — Milwaukee cryptosporidiosis outbreak (1993), Walkerton E. coli outbreak (2000), Flint lead crisis (2014). Sobering reminders of why this work matters.

- **Space station water recycling** — ISS recycles 93% of water including urine. Distillation + catalytic oxidation achieve extreme reuse.
  - https://www.nasa.gov/mission_pages/station/research/benefits/water_purification.html

- **Ancient water systems** — Roman aqueducts, Mayan cisterns, Persian qanats. Historical engineering shows longstanding human need for clean water.
