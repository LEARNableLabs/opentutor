# Water Treatment and Environmental Engineering — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Water Quality and Characterization
- Physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of water
- Water quality standards and regulations (WHO, EPA)
- Sampling and analytical methods
- Contaminant types: suspended solids, dissolved organics, inorganics, pathogens, emerging contaminants

### 2. Drinking Water Treatment Processes
- Coagulation and flocculation
- Sedimentation and clarification
- Filtration (rapid sand, slow sand, membrane)
- Disinfection (chlorination, UV, ozonation)
- Advanced treatment (activated carbon, ion exchange, reverse osmosis)

### 3. Wastewater Treatment
- Primary treatment (screening, grit removal, sedimentation)
- Secondary treatment (activated sludge, trickling filters, biological nutrient removal)
- Tertiary treatment and advanced processes
- Sludge treatment and disposal
- Decentralized systems (septic tanks, constructed wetlands)

### 4. Physical-Chemical Treatment Principles
- Mass transfer and reaction kinetics
- Reactor design and hydraulics
- Adsorption and ion exchange
- Membrane processes
- Chemical precipitation and oxidation

### 5. Biological Treatment Principles
- Microbial growth kinetics
- Biofilm and suspended growth systems
- Nitrogen and phosphorus removal
- Anaerobic digestion
- Pathogen removal and indicator organisms

### 6. Sustainable Water Management
- Water reuse and reclamation
- Source water protection
- Green infrastructure and low-impact development
- Climate change impacts on water systems
- Energy considerations in treatment

## Key Educational Sources

### Textbooks
- **Water Quality & Treatment** (AWWA, 6th edition) — comprehensive reference for drinking water treatment
- **Wastewater Engineering: Treatment and Resource Recovery** (Metcalf & Eddy) — industry standard for wastewater treatment
- **Environmental Engineering: Fundamentals, Sustainability, Design** (Mihelcic & Zimmerman) — integrated approach
- **Chemistry for Environmental Engineering and Science** (Sawyer, McCarty, Parkin) — foundational chemistry principles

## Key Concepts for Intermediate Level
Students at this level should have basic chemistry and biology background. The curriculum focuses on:
- Quantitative analysis of treatment processes (mass balances, kinetics)
- Design calculations for unit operations
- Integration of multiple treatment steps into complete systems
- Real-world constraints (cost, energy, regulations)
- Sustainability and resource recovery perspectives
