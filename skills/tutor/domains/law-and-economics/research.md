# Law and Economics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Property Law & Economics** — how property rights shape incentives; the Coase theorem; tragedy of the commons; takings and eminent domain
2. **Contract Law & Economics** — efficient breach; reliance vs. expectation damages; incomplete contracts; default rules
3. **Tort Law & Economics** — optimal deterrence; negligence vs. strict liability; Hand formula; products liability
4. **Criminal Law & Economics** — optimal sanctions; deterrence theory; crime as rational choice; punishment vs. rehabilitation
5. **Litigation & Legal Procedure** — settlement vs. trial; fee structures; discovery costs; class actions
6. **Corporate Law & Governance** — agency problems; fiduciary duties; shareholder vs. stakeholder models
7. **Antitrust & Regulation** — market power; predatory pricing; merger analysis; regulatory capture
8. **Behavioral Law & Economics** — bounded rationality; default rules and nudges; framing effects in legal decisions
9. **Constitutional Law & Economics** — federalism; separation of powers; constitutional constraints on economic policy

## Key Sources

### Primary Textbooks
- **Cooter & Ulen, "Law & Economics"** (6th ed.) — standard intermediate text, covers all major areas with mathematical formalism
- **Posner, "Economic Analysis of Law"** (9th ed.) — comprehensive, policy-oriented, less mathematical
- **Shavell, "Foundations of Economic Analysis of Law"** — rigorous graduate-level treatment
- **Polinsky, "An Introduction to Law and Economics"** — accessible, concise primer

### Academic Programs & Syllabi
- **University of Chicago Law School** — birthplace of law and economics movement
- **Yale Law School** — strong program with emphasis on empirical work
- **Harvard Law School** — joint JD/PhD program in law and economics
- **George Mason (Antonin Scalia Law School)** — influential center for law and economics
