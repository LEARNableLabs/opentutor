# Law and Economics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Robert Cooter & Thomas Ulen, "Law & Economics" (6th ed., 2016)** — The standard intermediate textbook. Clear exposition, formal models with intuition, covers all major areas. Best for students comfortable with calculus. Available: Pearson. [Publisher link](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/law-and-economics/P200000005824)

- **Richard Posner, "Economic Analysis of Law" (9th ed., 2014)** — Comprehensive, policy-oriented, less mathematical than Cooter & Ulen. Influential and opinionated. Good for students who want breadth over depth. [Publisher link](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/aspenlaw)

- **Steven Shavell, "Foundations of Economic Analysis of Law" (2004)** — Graduate-level rigor. Formal, complete, systematic. Use for advanced students or to look up specific topics in depth. [Harvard link](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674011496)

- **A. Mitchell Polinsky, "An Introduction to Law and Economics" (4th ed., 2011)** — Concise, accessible primer. Good for quick overviews or students without strong math backgrounds. [Stanford link](https://law.stanford.edu/directory/a-mitchell-polinsky/)

- **Ejan Mackaay & Stéphane Rousseau, "Analysis of Law Through an Economic Lens"** — European perspective, integrates civil law examples. Good for comparative context. [Publisher link](https://www.e-elgar.com/)

### Academic Journals

- **Journal of Law and Economics** (University of Chicago) — The foundational journal, published since 1958. Includes many seminal papers (Coase, Stigler, Becker). [Link](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/toc/jle/current)

- **Journal of Legal Studies** (University of Chicago) — Broader than JLE, includes empirical work and policy analysis. [Link](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/toc/jls/current)

- **American Law and Economics Review** (Oxford) — Official journal of the American Law and Economics Association. More applied than JLE/JLS. [Link](https://academic.oup.com/aler)

- **International Review of Law and Economics** (Elsevier) — Global perspective, includes comparative and developing-country work. [Link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/international-review-of-law-and-economics)

- **Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization** (Oxford) — Focuses on organizational and institutional issues. [Link](https://academic.oup.com/jleo)

### Classic Papers (Must-Read)

- **Ronald Coase, "The Problem of Social Cost" (1960)** — The Coase theorem. [Link](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/466560)

- **Guido Calabresi & A. Douglas Melamed, "Property Rules, Liability Rules, and Inalienability" (1972)** — Framework for choosing legal remedies. [Link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/795467)

- **Steven Shavell, "Strict Liability Versus Negligence" (1980)** — Formal analysis of tort liability regimes. [Link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/724402)

- **Gary Becker, "Crime and Punishment: An Economic Approach" (1968)** — Foundational paper on economics of crime. [Link](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1830482)

- **George Stigler, "The Theory of Economic Regulation" (1971)** — Regulatory capture. [Link](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/466865)

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

- **Biology and law** — Evolutionary explanations for legal norms (altruism, reciprocity, punishment). See Robert Ellickson, "Order Without Law" on cattle ranchers resolving disputes without courts. [Link](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674641693)

- **Neuroscience and criminal law** — Brain imaging evidence on culpability, free will, and insanity defense. See law review articles on neurolaw.

- **Computational law** — Machine learning for predicting case outcomes, contract analysis, regulatory compliance. See Stanford CodeX. [Link](https://law.stanford.edu/codex-the-stanford-center-for-legal-informatics/)

- **Philosophy of law** — Tensions between efficiency (consequentialism) and rights (deontology). See Ronald Dworkin vs. Richard Posner debates.

### Policy Applications

- **Climate change and law** — Carbon pricing, liability for emissions, international treaties. Combines environmental econ, public goods, and international law.

- **Cryptocurrency and law** — Property rights in digital assets, regulation of DeFi, smart contracts as self-enforcing agreements.

- **Pandemic response** — Lockdowns as takings, liability for disease transmission, vaccine mandates. Timely application of tort, constitutional law, and public health economics.

- **AI and liability** — Who's liable when an autonomous vehicle crashes? How to regulate algorithmic bias? Tort law meets tech policy.

### Historical Deep Dives

- **Origins of law and economics at Chicago** — Aaron Director, Ronald Coase, Gary Becker, Richard Posner. See Edmund Kitch, "The Fire of Truth: A Remembrance of Law and Economics at Chicago, 1932-1970."

- **Legal realism and economics** — How legal realism (1920s-1930s) paved the way for economic analysis of law.

- **Public choice theory** — James Buchanan, Gordon Tullock. Virginia/George Mason school. How politics shapes law.

### Fun & Quirky

- **The economics of dueling** — Why did gentlemen risk death over insults? Signaling models of honor. See papers by Steven Levitt.

- **Pirate codes** — How stateless actors create legal systems. Peter Leeson, "The Invisible Hook: The Hidden Economics of Pirates."

- **Medieval law merchant** — How European merchants created contract enforcement without states. See Avner Greif's work on institutions.

- **Economic analysis of dating and marriage** — Gary Becker's "A Treatise on the Family." Matching models, household production, divorce.
