# Condensed Matter Physics — Research Summary

## Topic Overview

Condensed matter physics studies the physical properties of matter in its condensed phases (solid and liquid), where quantum effects and collective behavior of many particles produce emergent phenomena. This is the largest subfield of physics, connecting fundamental theory to materials science and technology.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Crystal Structure & Symmetry
- Lattices, unit cells, Bravais lattices
- Symmetry operations and space groups
- Reciprocal lattice and Brillouin zones
- X-ray diffraction and structure determination

### 2. Lattice Dynamics & Phonons
- Classical vibrations in crystals
- Quantization and phonon modes
- Debye and Einstein models
- Thermal properties and heat capacity

### 3. Electronic Structure & Band Theory
- Free electron model (Drude, Sommerfeld)
- Nearly free electron approximation
- Tight-binding model
- Band structure and Fermi surfaces
- Metals, insulators, and semiconductors

### 4. Electron-Electron Interactions
- Screening and dielectric response
- Hartree-Fock approximation
- Correlation effects and Hubbard model
- Mott transitions

### 5. Magnetism
- Diamagnetism and paramagnetism
- Exchange interactions and ferromagnetism
- Antiferromagnetism and spin waves
- Magnetic domains and hysteresis

### 6. Superconductivity
- Historical discovery and phenomenology
- London equations and Meissner effect
- Cooper pairs and BCS theory
- Type I vs Type II superconductors
- High-temperature superconductivity

### 7. Phase Transitions & Critical Phenomena
- Order parameters and broken symmetry
- Mean field theory
- Critical exponents and universality
- Renormalization group concepts

### 8. Topological Phases
- Berry phase and geometric phases
- Quantum Hall effect
- Topological insulators
- Chern numbers and topological invariants

## Pedagogical Approach for Intermediate Level

For intermediate students (post quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics):
- Balance microscopic theory with emergent phenomena
- Use semi-classical models before full many-body theory
- Emphasize physical intuition alongside formalism
- Connect to real materials and applications
- Defer full field theory treatment to advanced courses
- Focus on canonical models (Ising, Hubbard, BCS) that capture essential physics
