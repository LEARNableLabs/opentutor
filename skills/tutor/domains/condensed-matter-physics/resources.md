# Condensed Matter Physics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Ashcroft & Mermin** — *Solid State Physics* (1976)
  - Comprehensive, rigorous graduate-level text. Excellent for band theory, phonons, and superconductivity. Dated notation but timeless physics.
  - Best for: deep dives, formal derivations

- **Kittel** — *Introduction to Solid State Physics* (9th ed.)
  - Standard undergraduate text. Broad coverage, less depth. Good for first pass on each topic.
  - Best for: overview, crystallography, experimental context

- **Marder** — *Condensed Matter Physics* (2nd ed., 2010)
  - Modern intermediate text. Balances theory and phenomenology. Strong on magnetism and phase transitions.
  - Best for: intermediate students, connecting topics

- **Girvin & Yang** — *Modern Condensed Matter Physics* (2019)
  - Recent text including topological phases, quantum Hall, Dirac materials. Assumes strong background.
  - Best for: advanced topics (lessons 28-30), current research

- **Altland & Simons** — *Condensed Matter Field Theory* (2nd ed., 2010)
  - Many-body methods, field theory, renormalization group. Advanced but clear.
  - Best for: students continuing to graduate work

### Open Course Materials

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 8.231** — *Physics of Solids I*
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-231-physics-of-solids-i-fall-2006/
  - Lecture notes, problem sets, exams. Covers crystal structure through band theory.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 8.511** — *Theory of Solids I*
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-511-theory-of-solids-i-fall-2004/
  - Graduate level, many-body approach.

- **David Tong's Cambridge Lecture Notes** — *Solid State Physics*
  - https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/solidstate.html
  - Excellent free notes covering most topics. Clear derivations, good physical insight. Highly recommended.

- **Chetan Nayak's Lectures** — *Topological Phases of Matter*
  - KITP and ICTP lectures (available on YouTube)
  - Advanced but accessible introduction to topological insulators and superconductors

## People to Follow (Key Researchers & Educators)

### Classic Figures
- **Philip Anderson** — Nobel laureate; localization, broken symmetry, emergent phenomena
- **Lev Landau** — Landau theory of phase transitions, Fermi liquid theory
- **John Bardeen, Leon Cooper, Robert Schrieffer** — BCS theory of superconductivity
- **Klaus von Klitzing** — Discovery of quantum Hall effect (Nobel 1985)

### Contemporary Leaders

- **Liang Fu** (MIT) — Topological materials, superconductivity
- **Ashvin Vishwanath** (Harvard) — Topological phases, quantum magnetism
- **Charles Kane** (UPenn) — Topological insulators (predicted Z₂ invariant)
- **Xiao-Gang Wen** (MIT) — Topological order, string-net condensation
- **Subir Sachdev** (Harvard) — Quantum phase transitions, high-Tc superconductivity
- **Andrea Damascelli** (UBC) — ARPES, correlated electron systems
- **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero** (MIT) — Twisted bilayer graphene, moiré materials

### Science Communicators
- **David Tong** — Cambridge lecturer, excellent online notes
- **Steve Simon** — Oxford, quantum Hall and anyons

## Recommended Reading Path

1. **Start**: Kittel (overview) + David Tong notes (theory)
2. **Deepen**: Marder for intermediate depth
3. **Specialize**:
   - Band theory & electrons: Ashcroft & Mermin chapters 2-15
   - Magnetism: Marder chapters 10-12
   - Superconductivity: Tinkham *Introduction to Superconductivity*
   - Topology: Girvin & Yang chapters 10-13, or Bernevig & Hughes *Topological Insulators*
4. **Advanced**: Altland & Simons for many-body methods
