# Paper Folding Mathematics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Geometric Folding Algorithms: Linkages, Origami, Polyhedra** — Erik Demaine & Joseph O'Rourke (Cambridge, 2007)
  - The definitive reference for computational origami
  - Comprehensive coverage of flat-foldability, complexity results, and universality theorems
  - Advanced undergraduate to graduate level
  - Freely available online: https://erikdemaine.org/papers/FoldingPaper_CGTA/

- **Origami Design Secrets: Mathematical Methods for an Ancient Art** — Robert J. Lang (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2003)
  - Bridges practical origami design and mathematical foundations
  - Detailed treatment of circle packing and TreeMaker algorithm
  - Accessible to intermediate students with strong geometric intuition
  - Second edition (2011) adds more advanced material

- **Project Origami: Activities for Exploring Mathematics** — Thomas Hull (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2012)
  - Activities-based approach connecting K-12 through undergraduate
  - Strong pedagogical framing; good for self-learners
  - Covers Maekawa, Kawasaki, Huzita axioms with hands-on exercises

### Academic Papers & Surveys

- **"A Survey of Folding and Unfolding in Computational Geometry"** — Demaine & O'Rourke (2001)
  - arXiv: cs/0107024
  - Excellent starting point for the field's scope
  - Covers flat folding, rigid folding, unfolding polyhedra

- **"Computational Origami"** — Robert J. Lang (1996)
  - Early foundational paper on algorithmic design
  - Available at: https://langorigami.com/article/computational-origami/

- **"Map Folding"** — Thomas Hull
  - Pedagogical treatment of the classic map folding problem
  - Shows NP-completeness in accessible way

### Course Materials

- **MIT 6.849: Geometric Folding Algorithms** — Erik Demaine (Fall 2012 and later)
  - Graduate-level course; lecture notes available
  - Homepage: https://erikdemaine.org/class/6849/
  - Covers cutting-edge research and open problems
  - Video lectures may be available through MIT OpenCourseWare

- **"Origami Mathematics"** — Thomas Hull (various workshops)
  - Workshop materials often shared on Hull's faculty page
  - Intermediate level with strong hands-on component

### Interactive Tools & Software

#### Web-Based Tools

- **Origami Simulator** — Amanda Ghassaei
  - https://origamisimulator.org/
  - Simulate crease pattern folding in real-time
  - Supports import/export, physics-based folding
  - Essential tool for testing flat-foldability

- **Wolfram Demonstrations** — Map Folding, Flat-Foldable Vertices
  - https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/
  - Search "origami" or "paper folding"
  - Interactive visualizations of Maekawa and Kawasaki theorems

- **GeoGebra Origami Apps** — various creators
  - https://www.geogebra.org/
  - Search "Kawasaki", "Maekawa", "origami"
  - Build-your-own crease patterns and test flat-foldability

#### Downloadable Software

- **TreeMaker** — Robert J. Lang
  - http://www.langorigami.com/article/treemaker/
  - Design origami bases from stick figure input
  - Implements circle-packing algorithm
  - Windows/Mac/Linux versions

- **Freeform Origami** — Tomohiro Tachi
  - https://origami.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~tachi/software/
  - Rigid origami simulator with interactive folding
  - Advanced tool for exploring degrees of freedom

- **Rigid Origami Simulator** — Tomohiro Tachi
  - https://origami.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~tachi/cg/
  - Simulate deployable structures and mechanical origami

### Unexpected Connections & Cross-Disciplinary Links

#### Engineering

- **Self-Assembly Lab** (MIT) — Skylar Tibbits
  - https://selfassemblylab.mit.edu/
  - Programmable materials, 4D printing, self-folding structures
  - Shows future applications of origami mathematics

- **Larry Howell** (BYU) — compliant mechanisms, origami-inspired engineering
  - Deployable structures, medical devices using origami principles

#### Biology

- **Protein Folding** — while not identical to origami, shares computational complexity
  - Levinthal's paradox: why doesn't protein folding take exponential time?
  - Connection: both involve local constraints leading to global structures

- **DNA Origami** — Paul Rothemund (Caltech)
  - Folding DNA strands into prescribed shapes
  - Uses base-pairing as "creases"

#### Art & Culture

- **Between the Folds** (documentary)
  - Profiles origami artists and mathematicians
  - Available on Kanopy, some clips on YouTube
  - Inspiring look at the intersection of art and math

- **Origami USA** — organization, conventions, publications
  - https://origamiusa.org/
  - Community of folders; mix of artistic and mathematical focus

#### Computer Science

- **Graph Coloring** — map coloring connects to mountain-valley assignment
- **Constraint Satisfaction Problems** — layer ordering as CSP
- **Computational Complexity** — map folding as classic NP-complete problem

## How to Use These Resources

### For Students

1. **Start with videos** — Numberphile, Robert Lang TED talks for motivation
2. **Read selected chapters** from Hull's *Project Origami* (accessible entry point)
3. **Use Origami Simulator** to test every lesson's concepts hands-on
4. **Dive into Demaine & O'Rourke** for rigorous treatment of theorems
5. **Explore TreeMaker** to see design algorithms in action
6. **Follow Erik Demaine's papers** for cutting-edge research

### For Lesson Preparation

- Each lesson's resources field points to 1-3 specific links
- Use videos for visual learners, papers for deeper dives
- Interactive tools (Origami Simulator, GeoGebra) for hands-on exploration
- Primary sources (Demaine & O'Rourke) for rigorous proofs

### For Going Deeper

- After completing curriculum, explore Demaine's course (MIT 6.849)
- Read original papers on specific topics of interest
- Join Origami USA or attend a conference (OSME — Origami in Science, Math, and Education)
- Try designing your own origami using TreeMaker
