# Research Summary: Combinatorics and Counting

## Major Subtopics

1. **Fundamental Counting Principles** — multiplication principle, addition principle, one-to-one correspondences
2. **Permutations and Combinations** — arrangements with/without repetition, binomial coefficients
3. **Binomial Theorem and Identities** — Pascal's triangle, combinatorial proofs of binomial identities
4. **Bijective Proofs** — showing two sets have the same cardinality through explicit bijections
5. **Inclusion-Exclusion Principle** — counting with overlapping sets, derangements
6. **Pigeonhole Principle** — simple and generalized versions, applications to existence proofs
7. **Generating Functions** — ordinary generating functions for solving counting problems
8. **Recursion and Recurrence Relations** — recursive counting, solving recurrences
9. **Special Sequences** — Catalan numbers, Stirling numbers, partitions, compositions
10. **Graph Counting** — counting trees, paths, colorings

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Art of Problem Solving: Intermediate Counting & Probability** (David Patrick) — covers one-to-one correspondences, inclusion-exclusion, generating functions, distributions, pigeonhole principle, and constructive counting. Excellent for intermediate learners.
- **Combinatorics: The Art of Counting** (Bruce Sagan) — gentle introduction to enumerative combinatorics at advanced undergraduate level
- **Open Textbook: Combinatorics** (University of Lethbridge) — comprehensive free resource covering basic counting through generating functions
- **An Introduction to Combinatorics and Graph Theory** (David Guichard, Whitman College) — includes graph theory applications

### Online Courses
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: The Art of Counting** (18.S66) — focuses on bijective proofs
- **Coursera: Combinatorics and Probability** (UC San Diego) — interactive course with graded assignments
- **FutureLearn: Combinatorics** — systematic approach to translating problems into counting reference structures
- **Po-Shen Loh: Module 4 - Combinatorics Tools** — competition-oriented, systematic introduction

### Interactive Resources
- **Learn Math Class** — interactive visual tools for permutations, combinations, partitions, Pascal's triangle with animated explorers
- **SAGE** — open source mathematical software for symbolic computation and combinatorial algorithms
- **FindStat** — database for studying statistics on combinatorial objects

## Available Resources

## Level Assessment

For **intermediate** level:
- Assumes basic discrete math, proof techniques (induction, contradiction)
- Should include formal treatment of generating functions
- Emphasize bijective and algebraic proof techniques
- Bridge from computational ("how many?") to theoretical ("why does this formula work?")
- Include applications to graph theory and algorithm analysis
