# Combinatorics and Counting — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Combinatorics: The Art of Counting** by Bruce Sagan (Michigan State University)
  - Level: Advanced undergraduate to beginning graduate
  - Coverage: Comprehensive enumerative combinatorics with bijective emphasis
  - PDF: https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/Books/Aoc/final.pdf
  - Why: Gentle introduction, excellent for intermediate learners, proof-focused

- **Combinatorics (Open Textbook)** — University of Lethbridge
  - Level: Undergraduate
  - Coverage: Basic counting through generating functions and recursion
  - PDF: https://opentext.uleth.ca/PDF/Combinatorics.pdf
  - Why: Free, comprehensive, clear examples, good exercises

- **An Introduction to Combinatorics and Graph Theory** by David Guichard (Whitman College)
  - Level: Undergraduate
  - Coverage: Counting principles, graph theory applications
  - PDF: https://www.whitman.edu/mathematics/cgt_online/cgt.pdf
  - Why: Bridges counting and graphs, includes computational tools

- **Intermediate Counting & Probability** by David Patrick (Art of Problem Solving)
  - Level: Advanced high school / early undergraduate
  - Syllabus: https://data.artofproblemsolving.com/course-docs/syllabi/intermediate-counting-syllabus.pdf
  - Why: Problem-solving focused, excellent for building intuition

- **UConn Probability Course Chapter 1: Combinatorics**
  - Level: Undergraduate
  - PDF: https://probability.oer.math.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2187/2018/01/prob3160ch1.pdf
  - Why: Connects counting to probability applications

### Online Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: The Art of Counting (18.S66)**
  - Focus: Bijective proofs and enumerative techniques
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s66-the-art-of-counting-spring-2003/pages/syllabus/

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Algebraic Combinatorics (18.212)**
  - Focus: Advanced algebraic methods
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-212-algebraic-combinatorics-spring-2019/
  - Why: For students ready to go deeper into partitions, symmetric functions

- **Coursera: Combinatorics and Probability** (UC San Diego)
  - Focus: Interactive problem-solving
  - URL: https://www.coursera.org/learn/combinatorics
  - Why: Graded assignments, structured learning path

- **FutureLearn: Combinatorics**
  - Focus: Systematic problem translation
  - URL: https://www.futurelearn.com/courses/combinatorics-strategies-and-methods-for-counting
  - Why: Teaches method for converting word problems into counting structures

## Unexpected Connections

- **Combinatorics ↔ Probability** — every counting problem becomes a probability if you normalize by the total
- **Combinatorics ↔ Algebra** — symmetric functions, representation theory, Hopf algebras all encode combinatorial structures
- **Combinatorics ↔ Computer Science** — algorithm analysis (running time), data structures (trees), cryptography (key counting)
- **Combinatorics ↔ Physics** — statistical mechanics uses partition functions (generating functions for energy states)
- **Combinatorics ↔ Biology** — phylogenetic trees, sequence alignment, protein folding pathways all involve counting structures
- **Combinatorics ↔ Number Theory** — partition functions connect to modular forms (Ramanujan's work)
