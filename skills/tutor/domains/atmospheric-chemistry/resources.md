# Atmospheric Chemistry and Air Quality — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Introduction to Atmospheric Chemistry (Daniel Jacob)** — Free online textbook, accessible for intermediate level, excellent for self-study. Covers tropospheric and stratospheric chemistry with clear explanations.
  - http://acmg.seas.harvard.edu/people/faculty/djj/book/

- **Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics (Seinfeld & Pandis)** — Comprehensive reference, more quantitative and detailed. Graduate-level but excellent for deep dives.
  - University libraries or ISBN: 978-1118947401

- **Chemistry of the Upper and Lower Atmosphere (Finlayson-Pitts & Pitts)** — Detailed treatment of reaction mechanisms, particularly strong on laboratory studies and kinetics.
  - ISBN: 978-0122570605

### Online Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 12.806 — Atmospheric Chemistry** — Video lectures, problem sets, exams. Covers full semester of graduate atmospheric chemistry.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/12-806-atmospheric-chemistry-fall-2005/

- **Coursera — Air Pollution courses** — Various universities offer air quality courses at different levels.
  - https://www.coursera.org/

- **NCAR/UCAR Educational Resources** — Atmospheric science tutorials, visualizations, and teaching materials.
  - https://scied.ucar.edu/

### Research Organizations

- **NOAA Chemical Sciences Laboratory (CSL)** — Atmospheric chemistry research, measurement techniques, and data.
  - https://csl.noaa.gov/

- **EPA Air Quality Science** — Policy-relevant research, emissions inventories, air quality modeling.
  - https://www.epa.gov/air-research

- **NASA Atmospheric Science** — Satellite missions, data portals, and global observations.
  - https://science.nasa.gov/earth-science/focus-areas/atmosphere/

## People (Researchers and Practitioners to Follow)

### Pioneers and Historical Figures

- **Sydney Chapman** — Developed Chapman cycle for stratospheric ozone (1930s)
- **Arie Jan Haagen-Smit** — Discovered photochemical smog mechanism in Los Angeles (1950s)
- **Paul Crutzen** — Nobel Prize for ozone depletion chemistry (1995)
- **Susan Solomon** — Led research on Antarctic ozone hole chemistry
- **Mario Molina & F. Sherwood Rowland** — Nobel Prize for CFC-ozone depletion theory (1995)

### Contemporary Leaders

- **Daniel Jacob** (Harvard) — Atmospheric chemistry modeling, author of free textbook
- **Jose L. Jimenez** (Colorado Boulder) — Aerosol mass spectrometry and secondary organic aerosols
- **Barbara Finlayson-Pitts** (UC Irvine) — Laboratory studies of atmospheric reactions
- **Paul Wennberg** (Caltech) — Atmospheric chemistry measurements and kinetics
- **Colette Heald** (MIT) — Biogenic emissions and air quality modeling
- **Joost de Gouw** (Colorado/NOAA) — Urban air quality and VOC chemistry

### Science Communicators

- **Kate Marvel** (NASA/Columbia) — Climate-chemistry connections, excellent communicator
- **Michael Mann** (Penn State) — Climate science including atmospheric composition
- **Katharine Hayhoe** — Climate communication with atmospheric science expertise

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Art and Atmospheric Chemistry

- **"The Scream" by Edvard Munch** — The vivid red-orange sky may have been inspired by aerosols from the 1883 Krakatoa eruption, demonstrating stratospheric aerosol transport and optical effects.
  - https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/145820/the-true-colors-of-volcanic-skies

- **Impressionist painting and industrial revolution** — Turner's late paintings show increasing haziness, possibly documenting early industrial air pollution in London.

### Literature

- **"The Worst Hard Time" by Timothy Egan** — Dust Bowl history, demonstrates massive aerosol loading and transport.
- **"The Big Smoke" by Peter Brimblecombe** — History of air pollution in London from medieval to modern times.
- **"Under a White Sky" by Elizabeth Kolbert** — Geoengineering and atmospheric intervention proposals.

### Chemistry in Popular Culture

- **"Dark Waters" (2019 film)** — PFAS contamination, demonstrates persistent organic pollutants and atmospheric transport.
- **"The Simpsons" ozone hole episodes** — Pop culture understanding (and misunderstanding) of atmospheric chemistry.

### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

- **Forensic science** — Atmospheric chemistry used to trace pollution sources, date materials, analyze wildfire origins.
- **Wine chemistry** — Atmospheric oxidation affects wine aging; vintners care about ozone and aerosols.
- **Art conservation** — Museum air quality and ozone damage to paintings and artifacts.
- **Urban planning** — Street canyon effects, green infrastructure for air quality, building design.
- **Public health** — Epidemiology of air pollution, exposure assessment, environmental justice.
