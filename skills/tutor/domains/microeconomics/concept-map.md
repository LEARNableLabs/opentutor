# Microeconomics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Scarcity** — fundamental constraint that forces choice and creates opportunity costs
2. **Opportunity cost** — value of the next-best alternative forgone
3. **Markets** — institutions that coordinate decentralized decision-making through prices
4. **Demand** — relationship between price and quantity buyers are willing to purchase. Depends on: scarcity, opportunity cost
5. **Supply** — relationship between price and quantity sellers are willing to produce. Depends on: opportunity cost, marginal cost
6. **Market equilibrium** — price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Depends on: demand, supply
7. **Elasticity** — responsiveness of quantity to price changes. Depends on: demand, supply
8. **Comparative statics** — analyzing how equilibrium changes when conditions shift. Depends on: market equilibrium, elasticity
9. **Utility** — representation of preferences over bundles of goods
10. **Preferences** — ordering of consumption bundles from most to least preferred
11. **Budget constraint** — limit on affordable consumption based on income and prices. Depends on: scarcity, opportunity cost
12. **Utility maximization** — choosing the best affordable bundle. Depends on: utility, budget constraint
13. **Marginal rate of substitution (MRS)** — willingness to trade one good for another. Depends on: utility, preferences
14. **Substitution effect** — change in consumption from relative price changes. Depends on: utility maximization, MRS
15. **Income effect** — change in consumption from purchasing power changes. Depends on: utility maximization, budget constraint
16. **Revealed preference** — inferring preferences from observed choices. Depends on: utility maximization
17. **Production function** — mapping from inputs to outputs
18. **Marginal product** — additional output from one more unit of input. Depends on: production function
19. **Cost curves** — relationship between output and cost of production. Depends on: production function, input prices
20. **Marginal cost (MC)** — cost of producing one more unit. Depends on: cost curves, marginal product
21. **Average cost (AC)** — cost per unit of output. Depends on: cost curves
22. **Profit maximization** — choosing output to maximize profit. Depends on: marginal cost, market structure
23. **Perfect competition** — market with many price-taking firms. Depends on: profit maximization
24. **Monopoly** — market with single price-setting firm. Depends on: profit maximization, demand
25. **Market power** — ability to influence prices. Depends on: market structure
26. **Deadweight loss** — welfare lost from inefficient allocation. Depends on: market power, equilibrium
27. **Price discrimination** — charging different prices to different customers. Depends on: monopoly, market power
28. **Oligopoly** — market with few strategic firms. Depends on: profit maximization, game theory
29. **Monopolistic competition** — many firms with differentiated products. Depends on: profit maximization, market power
30. **Pareto efficiency** — allocation where no one can be made better off without making someone worse off. Depends on: utility maximization, market equilibrium
31. **Market failures** — situations where markets don't achieve efficient outcomes
32. **Externalities** — costs or benefits affecting third parties. Depends on: market failures
33. **Pigouvian taxes** — taxes to correct externalities. Depends on: externalities, deadweight loss
34. **Public goods** — non-excludable and non-rival goods. Depends on: market failures
35. **Free-rider problem** — incentive to consume public goods without paying. Depends on: public goods
36. **Asymmetric information** — when one party knows more than another. Depends on: market failures
37. **Adverse selection** — bad products drive out good ones. Depends on: asymmetric information
38. **Moral hazard** — hidden actions reduce incentives for care. Depends on: asymmetric information
39. **Game theory** — analysis of strategic interactions. Depends on: utility maximization
40. **Nash equilibrium** — outcome where no player wants to deviate. Depends on: game theory
41. **Dominant strategy** — best choice regardless of others' actions. Depends on: game theory
42. **Prisoner's dilemma** — individually rational actions lead to collectively bad outcomes. Depends on: Nash equilibrium
43. **Repeated games** — strategic interactions over time. Depends on: game theory, Nash equilibrium

## Dependencies

### Foundation Layer
- **Scarcity → Opportunity cost → Markets** — the fundamental economic problem creates tradeoffs, which markets help resolve through price signals

### Consumer Theory Chain
- **Utility/Preferences → MRS** — preferences determine willingness to substitute between goods
- **Budget constraint + Utility → Utility maximization** — constraints and objectives combine to determine choices
- **Utility maximization → Demand** — optimal choices at different prices generate the demand curve
- **Demand + Budget → Income/Substitution effects** — understanding how demand responds to price changes requires decomposing the effects

### Producer Theory Chain
- **Production function → Marginal product** — technology determines productivity
- **Production function + Input prices → Cost curves** — technology and input costs determine production costs
- **Cost curves → Marginal cost** — cost structure determines marginal cost
- **Marginal cost + Revenue → Profit maximization** — costs and revenues determine optimal output choice
- **Profit maximization → Supply** — optimal choices at different prices generate the supply curve

### Market Structure Chain
- **Profit maximization + Market characteristics → Market structures** — number of firms and entry barriers determine market form
- **Market structure → Pricing behavior** — competitive vs monopolistic markets generate different pricing
- **Market power → Deadweight loss** — departure from perfect competition creates inefficiency
- **Monopoly + Information → Price discrimination** — market power plus ability to segment markets enables discrimination

### Market Failures Chain
- **Perfect competition → Pareto efficiency** — competitive markets achieve efficiency under ideal conditions
- **Departures from competition → Market failures** — externalities, public goods, information problems break efficiency
- **Externalities → Pigouvian taxes** — correcting external costs requires internalizing them
- **Asymmetric information → Adverse selection/Moral hazard** — information problems create specific distortions

### Game Theory Chain
- **Strategic interaction → Game theory** — analyzing situations where outcomes depend on others' choices
- **Game theory → Nash equilibrium** — predicting behavior in strategic settings
- **Nash equilibrium → Prisoner's dilemma** — canonical example of conflict between individual and collective rationality
- **Prisoner's dilemma + Time → Repeated games** — repeated interaction can sustain cooperation

## Critical Bottlenecks

### 1. Marginal vs. Average Thinking
**Bottleneck**: Confusing marginal and average concepts (cost, product, utility)
**Why it matters**: Optimization requires marginal analysis, not average
**Unlocks**: Profit maximization, utility maximization, production efficiency
**Teaching tip**: Emphasize "next unit" vs. "typical unit" distinction; use concrete examples

### 2. Graphical Reasoning
**Bottleneck**: Reading and manipulating supply/demand diagrams, indifference curves, isoquants
**Why it matters**: Graphs are the primary language of microeconomics
**Unlocks**: Equilibrium analysis, comparative statics, welfare analysis
**Teaching tip**: Build graphs step-by-step; connect algebraic and geometric representations

### 3. Constrained Optimization
**Bottleneck**: Setting up and solving max/min problems with constraints
**Why it matters**: Core of consumer and producer theory
**Unlocks**: Utility maximization, cost minimization, profit maximization
**Teaching tip**: Start with intuition (tangency = "bang for buck"), then formalize; use Lagrangian when needed

### 4. Strategic Reasoning
**Bottleneck**: Thinking through opponents' incentives and best responses
**Why it matters**: Essential for game theory and oligopoly
**Unlocks**: Nash equilibrium, collusion, repeated games
**Teaching tip**: Use concrete games; emphasize backward induction; connect to real-world situations

### 5. Welfare Analysis
**Bottleneck**: Identifying winners and losers from policy changes; understanding surplus
**Why it matters**: Core of normative economics and policy evaluation
**Unlocks**: Deadweight loss, tax incidence, market failure corrections
**Teaching tip**: Always draw triangles; connect to "what people are willing to pay" vs. "what they actually pay"

## Prerequisite Topics

### Essential
- **Algebra** — needed for solving equilibria, optimization problems, cost functions
- **Basic calculus** — needed for marginal analysis, optimization, deriving supply/demand from fundamentals
- **Graphing** — needed for visualizing supply/demand, indifference curves, cost curves
- **Logic** — needed for game theory, proof of welfare theorems

### Helpful but not required
- **Multivariable calculus** — useful for Lagrangian optimization, partial derivatives in comparative statics
- **Linear algebra** — helpful for production functions, input-output analysis
- **Statistics** — useful for understanding empirical applications, elasticity estimation
- **Programming** — helpful for simulations, computational experiments

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Demand = want"** — Demand requires both willingness AND ability to pay
2. **"Supply/demand curves shift with price changes"** — Price changes cause movement ALONG curves, not shifts
3. **"Monopolies charge the highest possible price"** — Monopolies charge the profit-maximizing price, which balances markup against lost quantity
4. **"Sunk costs matter"** — Sunk costs should not influence future decisions (but psychologically they do)
5. **"Normal profit means zero profit"** — Economic profit differs from accounting profit; normal profit means zero economic profit
6. **"Demand curves always slope down"** — Giffen goods and Veblen goods can have upward-sloping demand
7. **"Price discrimination is unfair"** — It can increase total welfare by serving more customers, though distribution effects vary
8. **"Externalities always require government intervention"** — Coase theorem shows private bargaining can work with low transaction costs
9. **"Public goods require government provision"** — Some public goods are privately provided (open-source software, Wikipedia)
10. **"Nash equilibrium is the best outcome"** — It's stable, not necessarily optimal (prisoner's dilemma shows this)

## Learning Pathways

### Fast track (strong math background)
Start with constrained optimization → derive consumer theory → derive producer theory → analyze market structures → study failures and corrections

### Visual track (graphical reasoning strength)
Build intuition with supply/demand → add consumer/producer surplus → introduce indifference curves → add cost curves → analyze market structures graphically

### Applied track (real-world focused)
Start with market examples → motivate theory as tools for understanding → emphasize elasticities and empirical magnitudes → focus on policy applications

### Game-theoretic track
Introduce strategic thinking early → use games to motivate market structures → analyze oligopoly and cooperation → connect to mechanism design
