# Microeconomics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

**Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach* (9th ed.)**
- Gold standard for intermediate micro
- Clear, rigorous treatment with excellent examples
- Strong on consumer and producer theory
- Mathematical appendices for deeper dives
- **Best for**: Core theory, formal derivations, practice problems

**N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (9th ed.)**
- Most accessible intro/intermediate text
- Abundant real-world applications
- "In the News" boxes connect theory to current events
- Less mathematical than Varian, more intuitive
- **Best for**: Motivation, policy applications, accessible explanations

**Robert Pindyck & Daniel Rubinfeld, *Microeconomics* (9th ed.)**
- Strong on applications and empirical examples
- Excellent chapter-end problems
- Good coverage of uncertainty and time
- More applied than Varian
- **Best for**: Real-world applications, empirical magnitudes, policy analysis

**Walter Nicholson & Christopher Snyder, *Intermediate Microeconomics and Its Application* (13th ed.)**
- Thorough mathematical treatment
- Excellent "Extensions" sections for rabbit holes
- Strong on general equilibrium and welfare
- **Best for**: Math-forward students, deeper theory

### Open Textbooks & Online Courses

**Core-Econ, *The Economy***
- https://www.core-econ.org/the-economy/
- Modern, empirical approach with real-world data
- Excellent unit structure (scarcity → firms → markets → inequality → environment)
- Interactive graphs and empirical exhibits
- Free, open-access, regularly updated
- **Best for**: Contemporary applications, inequality focus, empirical emphasis

**MIT OpenCourseWare 14.01: Principles of Microeconomics (Fall 2018)**
- https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2018/
- Complete course materials: lecture notes, problem sets, exams with solutions
- Professor Jonathan Gruber (well-known labor/health economist)
- Rigorous intermediate treatment
- **Best for**: Problem sets, exam practice, lecture notes

**Stanford Online: Economics**
- https://online.stanford.edu/explore?topics=economics
- Various micro courses at different levels
- Video lectures from Stanford faculty
- **Best for**: Video content, expert instruction

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Behavioral Economics
- **Dan Ariely, *Predictably Irrational*** — accessible intro to behavioral econ
- **Richard Thaler, *Misbehaving*** — history of behavioral economics, nudges
- **Kahneman & Tversky's original papers** — prospect theory, heuristics and biases
- **Why explore**: Shows systematic departures from rational choice theory

### Matching Markets
- **Alvin Roth's work** — kidney exchange, residency matching, school choice
- **Blog**: http://marketdesigner.blogspot.com/
- **Nobel lecture**: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2012/roth/lecture/
- **Why explore**: Markets without prices; stability vs. efficiency

### Network Economics
- **Shapiro & Varian, *Information Rules*** — network effects, digital goods
- **Platform economics** — two-sided markets, Uber/Airbnb
- **Why explore**: Modern markets with increasing returns, winner-take-all dynamics

### Experimental Economics
- **Vernon Smith (Nobel laureate)** — founder of experimental economics
- **EconPort experiments**: http://www.econport.org/
- **Why explore**: Lab tests of theory; behavioral departures from predictions

### Environmental & Climate Economics
- **William Nordhaus (Nobel laureate)** — climate change economics
- **Carbon pricing debates** — tax vs. cap-and-trade
- **Social cost of carbon** — valuing future damages
- **Why explore**: Largest externality problem; intergenerational ethics

### Auction Theory
- **Paul Milgrom & Robert Wilson (Nobel laureates)** — auction design
- **FCC spectrum auctions** — practical mechanism design
- **Google AdWords auctions** — everyday application
- **Why explore**: Mechanism design, revenue equivalence, information revelation

### Industrial Organization
- **Jean Tirole (Nobel laureate)** — regulation, two-sided markets, competition policy
- **Tech antitrust** — Google, Amazon, Facebook cases
- **Why explore**: Real-world application of market structure theory

### Inequality & Distribution
- **Thomas Piketty, *Capital in the Twenty-First Century*** — wealth inequality trends
- **Raj Chetty's Opportunity Atlas** — https://www.opportunityatlas.org/
- **Top income shares data** — World Inequality Database
- **Why explore**: Efficiency says nothing about fairness; equity-efficiency tradeoffs

### Cryptocurrencies & Digital Goods
- **Bitcoin and blockchain** — trust without intermediaries
- **NFTs and digital scarcity** — creating excludability
- **Information goods** — zero marginal cost, IP challenges
- **Why explore**: New markets, interesting excludability questions

### History of Economic Thought
- **Adam Smith, *Wealth of Nations* (1776)** — invisible hand, division of labor
- **Alfred Marshall, *Principles of Economics* (1890)** — supply and demand, marginalism
- **Leon Walras** — general equilibrium
- **Why explore**: See how ideas developed; appreciate intellectual history
