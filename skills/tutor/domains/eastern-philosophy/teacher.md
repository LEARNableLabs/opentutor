# Eastern philosophy — Zen, Taoism, Vedanta — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 8 lessons (28%)
- **Socratic questions** — 5 lessons (17%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (17%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 5 lessons (17%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (7%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 31% accessible (1-2), 45% standard (3), 24% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 8: "If everything is Brahman, why does the world appear multiple and changing?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 9: "How does Shankara's non-dualism differ from Western idealism?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 15: "Why does Zhuangzi dream of being a butterfly?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 20: "What does 'direct pointing to mind' mean if not words?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "Why give a student an unsolvable riddle?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers eastern philosophy — zen, taoism, vedanta, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.
