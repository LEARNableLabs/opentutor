# Eastern Philosophy — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Vedanta
- **Upanishads** (multiple translations)
  - http://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/upan/ — Complete collection of major Upanishads
  - Translations by Eknath Easwaran (accessible) and Patrick Olivelle (scholarly)
- **Bhagavad Gita** — http://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/gita/
  - Central Vedantic text, dialogue format, good for intermediate students
- **Shankara's works**
  - Vivekachudamani (Crest-Jewel of Discrimination)
  - Commentaries on Upanishads and Brahma Sutras (advanced)
- **Modern expositions**
  - Eliot Deutsch, *Advaita Vedanta: A Philosophical Reconstruction*
  - Arvind Sharma, *The Philosophy of Religion and Advaita Vedanta*

### Taoism
- **Tao Te Ching** (Laozi)
  - https://terebess.hu/english/tao/gia.html — Gia-fu Feng translation
  - https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm — Legge translation
  - Compare: Stephen Mitchell (poetic), D.C. Lau (scholarly), Red Pine (commentary-rich)
- **Zhuangzi** (Chuang Tzu)
  - https://terebess.hu/english/chuangtzu.html — Complete Burton Watson translation
  - Inner chapters (1-7) are core philosophical material
  - Outer and miscellaneous chapters show later development
- **Liezi**
  - Lesser-known Taoist text, useful for comparative reading
  - A.C. Graham translation recommended

### Zen Buddhism
- **Platform Sutra** (Huineng)
  - http://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/zen/platform.htm
  - Foundational Chan/Zen text, accessible narrative
- **Mumonkan** (The Gateless Gate)
  - http://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/zen/mumonkan.htm
  - Classic koan collection with commentary, 48 cases
- **Shobogenzo** (Dogen)
  - Treasury of the True Dharma Eye, advanced but profound
  - Start with "Genjokoan" chapter (reality becoming manifest)
  - https://www.thezensite.com/ZenTeachings/Dogen_Teachings/shobogenzo_intro.html
- **Zen Flesh, Zen Bones** compiled by Paul Reps
  - Accessible koan collection and teaching stories

## Cautions & Critiques

### Resources to approach critically:
- **Deepak Chopra** — mixes quantum physics and Vedanta inaccurately
- **"The Secret" and Law of Attraction** — pseudoscientific appropriation
- **Some popular "Zen" books** — often not actual Zen philosophy
- **New Age syntheses** — often flatten differences between traditions

### Important critical perspectives:
- **Luis Gómez** — critiques of Western Zen romanticism
- **Bernard Faure** — critical history of Chan/Zen
- **Robert Sharf** — "Buddhist Modernism" and Western misconceptions
- **David McMahan** — *The Making of Buddhist Modernism*
- **Donald Lopez** — *Prisoners of Shangri-La* (Tibetan Buddhism romanticization)

## For Your Own Teaching Preparation

1. **Read primary sources first** — don't rely only on secondary interpretations
2. **Compare translations** — reveals interpretive choices and ambiguities
3. **Engage scholarly debate** — SEP and IEP entries for current academic understanding
4. **Know the history** — how these texts were used, who had access, what was at stake
5. **Practice humility** — if you're teaching outside your cultural tradition, acknowledge limitations
6. **Use video/audio** — hearing chanting, seeing temples, experiencing aesthetic expressions enriches abstract concepts
7. **Prepare for resistance** — students may find paradox frustrating or dismiss as mystical nonsense; have responses ready
8. **Cross-reference** — when teaching Vedanta, point forward to how Zen approaches similar issues differently

## Best Entry Points by Student Interest

- **Philosophy of mind → Vedanta** (consciousness, self)
- **Ethics/politics → Taoism** (wu wei governance, natural order)
- **Epistemology → Zen** (direct knowing, limits of concepts)
- **Metaphysics → all three** (but start Vedanta for most systematic)
- **Practice-oriented → Zen** (meditation central to philosophy)
- **Comparative philosophy → start here with this curriculum**
- **Skeptical of mysticism → Zhuangzi** (philosophical Taoism less devotional)
