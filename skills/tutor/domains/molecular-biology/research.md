# Research Summary — Molecular Biology: DNA to Protein

## Overview

The DNA-to-protein pathway (central dogma) is fundamental to molecular biology. At the intermediate level, students should understand not just the "what" but the "how" and "why" — mechanisms, regulation, and biological context.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. DNA Structure and Chromatin Organization
- Double helix structure, base pairing rules
- Chromatin packaging and accessibility
- How DNA structure enables information storage

### 2. Transcription
- RNA polymerase mechanism
- Promoters and transcription factors
- Prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic transcription
- Transcription regulation

### 3. RNA Processing (Eukaryotes)
- 5' capping and 3' polyadenylation
- Splicing mechanisms (spliceosome)
- Alternative splicing and isoforms
- RNA editing

### 4. Translation
- Genetic code and tRNA charging
- Ribosome structure and mechanism
- Initiation, elongation, termination
- Post-translational modifications

### 5. Regulation and Integration
- Gene expression regulation (transcriptional and post-transcriptional)
- Epigenetics
- Mutations and their consequences
- Clinical/research applications

## Key Educational Resources

### Primary Textbooks
- **Molecular Biology of the Cell** (Alberts et al.) — gold standard for comprehensive coverage
- **Lehninger Principles of Biochemistry** — excellent for biochemical mechanisms
- **Molecular Biology** (Weaver) — detailed molecular mechanisms

### Online Courses
- **MIT OCW 7.016: Introductory Biology** — foundational molecular biology
- **Khan Academy: Central Dogma** — accessible video explanations
- **iBiology** — research seminar talks and educational videos

### Interactive Tools
- **NCBI Structure Database** — protein and nucleic acid structures
- **RCSB PDB** — 3D structure visualization
- **DNA Learning Center (DNALC)** — interactive animations
- **Protein Data Bank** — molecular visualization

### Video Resources
- **iBiology YouTube Channel** — research talks and primers
- **Nature Video** — mechanism animations
- **Science Communication Lab** — molecular animations

### Primary Literature
- Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology — review articles
- Annual Review of Biochemistry — comprehensive reviews
- PNAS, Science, Nature — landmark papers

## Pedagogical Approach

For intermediate learners:
- Assume basic biology/chemistry background
- Focus on mechanisms, not just descriptions
- Include structural biology perspective
- Connect to disease/research applications
- Emphasize regulation and complexity
- Use problem-solving and case studies

## Common Student Challenges

1. Visualizing 3D molecular structures from 2D diagrams
2. Understanding the energetics of processes
3. Keeping track of many proteins/factors involved
4. Connecting microscopic events to cell behavior
5. Understanding evolutionary/comparative perspectives
6. Grasping the speed and scale of cellular processes
