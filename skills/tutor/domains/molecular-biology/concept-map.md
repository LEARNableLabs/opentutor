# Molecular Biology: DNA to Protein — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **DNA double helix** — antiparallel sugar-phosphate backbone with complementary base pairing
2. **Base pairing rules** — A-T and G-C pairing through hydrogen bonds
3. **Genetic information storage** — sequence of nucleotides encodes biological instructions
4. **Chromatin structure** — DNA packaging into nucleosomes affects accessibility. Depends on: DNA double helix
5. **Gene architecture** — promoters, coding sequences, and regulatory elements define functional units. Depends on: genetic information storage
6. **RNA polymerase** — enzyme that synthesizes RNA from DNA template. Depends on: DNA double helix, base pairing rules
7. **Transcription initiation** — promoter recognition and assembly of transcription machinery. Depends on: gene architecture, RNA polymerase
8. **Transcription elongation** — processive synthesis of RNA transcript. Depends on: RNA polymerase, base pairing rules
9. **Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic transcription** — key differences in regulation and coupling. Depends on: transcription initiation, chromatin structure
10. **Transcriptional regulation** — control of gene expression through activators and repressors. Depends on: transcription initiation, gene architecture
11. **5' capping and 3' polyadenylation** — mRNA modifications for stability and translation. Depends on: transcription elongation
12. **RNA splicing** — removal of introns and joining of exons by the spliceosome. Depends on: transcription elongation
13. **Alternative splicing** — generation of protein diversity from single genes. Depends on: RNA splicing
14. **Mature mRNA** — fully processed transcript ready for translation. Depends on: 5' capping, 3' polyadenylation, RNA splicing
15. **Genetic code** — triplet codon system mapping nucleotides to amino acids
16. **tRNA structure and function** — adaptor molecules linking codons to amino acids. Depends on: genetic code
17. **Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases** — enzymes charging tRNAs with correct amino acids. Depends on: tRNA structure, genetic code
18. **Ribosome structure** — large and small subunits with rRNA catalytic core and tRNA binding sites. Depends on: tRNA structure
19. **Translation initiation** — ribosome assembly at start codon. Depends on: mature mRNA, ribosome structure, genetic code
20. **Translation elongation** — sequential addition of amino acids to growing peptide. Depends on: translation initiation, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases
21. **Translational fidelity** — proofreading mechanisms ensuring accuracy. Depends on: translation elongation, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases
22. **Translation termination** — recognition of stop codons and release of polypeptide. Depends on: translation elongation, genetic code
23. **Protein folding** — acquisition of functional 3D structure. Depends on: translation termination
24. **Post-translational modifications** — chemical modifications diversifying protein function. Depends on: protein folding
25. **Multi-level gene regulation** — coordinated control at transcription, RNA processing, and translation. Depends on: transcriptional regulation, RNA splicing, translation initiation
26. **Mutations and disease** — how changes in DNA sequence affect protein function. Depends on: genetic code, protein folding, multi-level regulation

## Dependencies

### Foundational Relationships
- **Chromatin structure** requires understanding **DNA double helix** because nucleosomes wrap around DNA, and packaging affects which regions are accessible for transcription
- **Gene architecture** builds on **genetic information storage** because promoters and regulatory sequences are specific DNA sequences that control where and when genes are read
- **Transcription initiation** depends on both **gene architecture** (to locate promoters) and **RNA polymerase** (the enzyme that will synthesize RNA)

### Transcription Dependencies
- **Transcription elongation** follows from **RNA polymerase** mechanism and **base pairing rules** because RNA synthesis uses complementary base pairing
- **Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic transcription** requires understanding both **transcription initiation** (different mechanisms) and **chromatin structure** (only relevant in eukaryotes)
- **Transcriptional regulation** builds on **transcription initiation** and **gene architecture** because regulatory proteins bind to specific DNA sequences near promoters

### RNA Processing Dependencies
- **5' capping and 3' polyadenylation** occur co-transcriptionally, so they depend on **transcription elongation**
- **RNA splicing** also occurs during/after **transcription elongation**, removing introns from pre-mRNA
- **Alternative splicing** requires understanding basic **RNA splicing** mechanisms but adds regulatory complexity
- **Mature mRNA** is the product of all three processing steps working together

### Translation Dependencies
- **tRNA structure** must be understood in context of the **genetic code** because tRNAs decode codons
- **Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases** charge specific **tRNAs** with amino acids matching the **genetic code**
- **Ribosome structure** provides binding sites for **tRNAs** and catalyzes peptide bond formation
- **Translation initiation** requires the **mature mRNA**, **ribosome structure**, and understanding of the **genetic code** (start codon)
- **Translation elongation** depends on **translation initiation** to establish the reading frame and **aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases** to provide charged tRNAs
- **Translational fidelity** involves proofreading during both **aminoacyl-tRNA charging** and **elongation**

### Integration Dependencies
- **Protein folding** begins as the polypeptide emerges during **translation termination**
- **Post-translational modifications** modify the folded protein structure
- **Multi-level gene regulation** integrates control points across **transcription**, **RNA processing**, and **translation**
- **Mutations and disease** understanding requires knowledge of how changes propagate through the entire pathway from DNA sequence to protein function

## Critical Bottlenecks

These concepts are essential gateways to understanding later material:

1. **Base pairing rules** — fundamental to both transcription and replication; students who don't grasp complementarity struggle with all downstream concepts
2. **Genetic code** — central to translation; confusion about the triplet code impairs understanding of mutations, tRNAs, and reading frames
3. **RNA splicing** — key difference between prokaryotes and eukaryotes; essential for understanding protein diversity and regulation
4. **Ribosome structure** — understanding the tRNA binding sites (A, P, E) is crucial for following the elongation cycle
5. **Multi-level regulation** — integration point showing how all prior concepts work together in living cells

## Common Conceptual Confusions

### Directionality Issues
Students often confuse:
- DNA strand polarity (5'→3' vs 3'→5')
- Which strand is template vs coding
- Direction of RNA synthesis vs DNA template reading

**Resolution:** Emphasize that polymerases always synthesize 5'→3', but read template 3'→5'

### Central Dogma Oversimplification
The "DNA → RNA → Protein" formulation obscures:
- RNA processing steps between transcription and translation
- Regulation at each step
- Reverse transcription, RNA replication (viruses)

**Resolution:** Present central dogma as a framework, then add complexity incrementally

### Prokaryote-Eukaryote Distinctions
Students mix up which organisms have:
- Introns/splicing (eukaryotes)
- Coupled transcription-translation (prokaryotes)
- Chromatin (eukaryotes)
- Polycistronic mRNA (prokaryotes)

**Resolution:** Create comparison tables and use specific examples (E. coli vs human)

### Code vs Codon vs Anticodon
Confusion between:
- The genetic code (the mapping)
- Codons (on mRNA)
- Anticodons (on tRNA)

**Resolution:** Use concrete examples, emphasize complementarity between codon-anticodon

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic chemistry** — needed for hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic interactions, pH effects (protein folding, DNA stability)
- **Cell structure** — needed for understanding nucleus vs cytoplasm, organelles, compartmentalization (where transcription and translation occur)
- **Protein structure basics** — needed for understanding why amino acid sequence matters (primary → secondary → tertiary structure)
- **Energy and thermodynamics** — needed for understanding GTP/ATP hydrolysis, spontaneity (many steps are energetically unfavorable without coupling)

## Extensions and Connections

- **Epigenetics** — chemical modifications affecting chromatin without changing DNA sequence
- **RNA world hypothesis** — evolutionary perspective on catalytic RNA
- **Synthetic biology** — engineering genetic circuits using these principles
- **Molecular diagnostics** — PCR, sequencing, gene expression profiling
- **Evolution** — mutation rates, selection, molecular clocks
- **Pharmacology** — many drugs target transcription, translation, or protein folding
