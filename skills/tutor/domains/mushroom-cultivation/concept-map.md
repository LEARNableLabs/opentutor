# Mushroom Cultivation — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Fungal life cycle** — spore germination → mycelium → primordia → fruiting body → sporulation
2. **Saprophytic nutrition** — fungi decompose dead organic matter using extracellular enzymes
3. **Lignocellulose breakdown** — how fungi digest wood and plant material. Depends on: saprophytic nutrition
4. **Species ecology** — different mushrooms evolved for different substrates and conditions
5. **C:N ratio** — carbon-to-nitrogen balance determines mycelial growth rate and contamination resistance
6. **Substrate preparation** — pasteurization vs. sterilization methods. Depends on: C:N ratio, contamination risk
7. **Pasteurization** — heat treatment to 160-180°F to kill competitors while preserving beneficial microbes
8. **Sterilization** — pressure-cooking at 15 PSI to kill all organisms for enriched substrates
9. **Competitive exclusion** — fast mycelial colonization prevents mold/bacteria takeover. Depends on: substrate preparation
10. **Moisture content** — optimal 55-65% for most substrates. Depends on: substrate preparation
11. **Supplementation** — adding nitrogen (bran, soy) to boost yields, with increased contamination risk. Depends on: C:N ratio, sterilization
12. **Grain spawn** — colonized grain used to inoculate bulk substrate. Depends on: sterilization
13. **Inoculation density** — spawn-to-substrate ratio affects colonization speed. Depends on: grain spawn
14. **Spawn run** — mycelial colonization phase before fruiting. Depends on: inoculation density, competitive exclusion
15. **Contamination ID** — recognizing Trichoderma (green), Aspergillus (black), bacteria (slime). Depends on: spawn run
16. **Pinning triggers** — environmental changes (temp drop, light, evaporation) signal fruiting. Depends on: spawn run completion
17. **Temperature shock** — 5-10°F drop to initiate pinning. Depends on: pinning triggers
18. **CO₂ concentration** — high CO₂ (>1000 ppm) during colonization, low (<800 ppm) for fruiting. Depends on: pinning triggers
19. **Fresh air exchange (FAE)** — ventilation system to reduce CO₂ and trigger normal morphology. Depends on: CO₂ concentration
20. **Relative humidity** — 85-95% RH for fruiting, controlled by evaporation and misting. Depends on: pinning triggers
21. **Evaporative cooling** — humidity loss from substrate surface triggers pinning. Depends on: relative humidity
22. **Mushroom morphology** — cap shape, stem thickness affected by light, CO₂, humidity. Depends on: FAE, relative humidity
23. **Species requirements** — oyster (easy, wide range), shiitake (lower temp), lion's mane (low CO₂), reishi (high humidity)
24. **Chamber design** — enclosure with humidity, FAE, and temperature control. Depends on: all environmental parameters
25. **Environmental monitoring** — sensors for temp, RH, CO₂ to track and adjust conditions. Depends on: chamber design
26. **Biological efficiency** — (fresh mushroom weight / dry substrate weight) × 100%. Depends on: all cultivation factors
27. **Flush dynamics** — multiple harvests from same substrate, declining yields. Depends on: biological efficiency
28. **Substrate depletion** — nutrients consumed over successive flushes. Depends on: flush dynamics
29. **Rehydration** — soaking substrate between flushes to restore moisture. Depends on: flush dynamics
30. **Diagnostic protocol** — systematic troubleshooting of contamination, pinning failures, morphology issues. Depends on: all concepts
31. **Scaling strategy** — moving from home cultivation to commercial production. Depends on: all concepts

## Dependencies

- **Substrate preparation requires understanding C:N ratio** because the carbon-to-nitrogen balance determines whether you need aggressive sterilization (high-nitrogen enriched substrates attract contaminants) or can use gentler pasteurization (balanced substrates resist invasion)

- **Competitive exclusion builds on substrate preparation** because proper heat treatment creates a clean slate where fast-growing mushroom mycelium can outcompete slower contaminants

- **Supplementation builds on C:N ratio and sterilization** because adding nitrogen-rich supplements (bran, soy) increases yields but also contamination risk, requiring pressure-sterilization rather than pasteurization

- **Pinning triggers depend on completed spawn run** because mycelium must fully colonize substrate before environmental signals (temp drop, light, evaporation) can trigger fruiting body formation

- **FAE and CO₂ concentration are inversely related** because fresh air exchange physically removes CO₂ from the fruiting chamber, signaling the mycelium to form normal-shaped mushrooms rather than leggy, etiolated ones

- **Species requirements integrate all environmental parameters** because each mushroom species evolved in specific ecological niches (oyster: dead hardwood in temperate forests; shiitake: aging oak logs; lion's mane: wounded trees) with distinct temperature, humidity, and gas exchange patterns

- **Biological efficiency integrates all cultivation factors** because yield depends on substrate nutrition (C:N ratio, supplementation), colonization quality (spawn density, contamination control), and fruiting environment (temperature, humidity, FAE)

## Bottleneck Concepts

**1. C:N ratio** — this determines the entire substrate strategy (prep method, contamination risk, yield potential). Students who don't grasp this make random substrate choices without understanding tradeoffs.

**2. Pinning triggers** — the transition from vegetative growth to fruiting is controlled by environmental cues. Students who skip this think "just wait long enough" rather than actively manipulating temperature, humidity, and air exchange.

**3. CO₂ and FAE relationship** — high CO₂ during colonization (sealed bags), low CO₂ during fruiting (ventilation). Students often confuse these phases and either dry out colonizing substrate or grow deformed mushrooms.

**4. Species-specific parameters** — cultivators must translate generic principles to each mushroom's ecology. Treating all mushrooms the same leads to failures (e.g., using oyster parameters for shiitake).

## Common Prerequisite Gaps

- **Sterile technique fundamentals** — students need to understand aseptic transfer, flame sterilization, and working in still air or flow hoods before attempting spawn production
- **Basic biology (cell respiration)** — understanding why fungi need oxygen helps explain FAE requirements and why sealed containers work during colonization but not fruiting
- **Fungal life cycle** — knowing the dikaryotic phase and spore germination clarifies why you can't just "plant" mushroom spores like seeds

## Misconceptions to Address

See teaching-notes.md for detailed list — key ones include:
- "More spawn = faster fruiting" (colonization speed ≠ fruiting speed)
- "All mushrooms need the same conditions" (species-specific requirements vary drastically)
- "Contamination is random bad luck" (it's predictable based on substrate C:N, prep method, and technique)
- "Higher humidity = bigger mushrooms" (excessive humidity can prevent pinning via lack of evaporation)
