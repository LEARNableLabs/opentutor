# History of Mathematics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Ancient Mathematics (3000 BCE - 500 CE)
- **Babylonian mathematics**: Base-60 number system, algebraic methods, astronomy
- **Egyptian mathematics**: Practical geometry, fraction systems, papyrus texts
- **Greek mathematics**: Foundations of proof, Euclidean geometry, number theory, early calculus concepts
- **Chinese and Indian mathematics**: Decimal system, zero, negative numbers, early algebra

### 2. Medieval and Islamic Golden Age (500 - 1400)
- **Islamic mathematics**: Algebra systematization, trigonometry, algorithm development
- **Indian advances**: Hindu-Arabic numerals, sophisticated astronomy
- **European preservation**: Monastery scholarship, Arabic text translation

### 3. Renaissance and Early Modern (1400 - 1650)
- **Symbolic algebra**: Viète, Descartes, development of notation
- **Perspective and art**: Mathematical influence on Renaissance art
- **Early probability**: Cardano, Fermat, Pascal on games of chance

### 4. The Scientific Revolution (1650 - 1800)
- **Calculus**: Newton vs. Leibniz, foundations and applications
- **Analytic geometry**: Merging algebra and geometry
- **Number theory**: Fermat, Euler's contributions
- **Probability theory**: Formalization and applications

### 5. 19th Century Expansion (1800 - 1900)
- **Non-Euclidean geometry**: Gauss, Bolyai, Lobachevsky
- **Abstract algebra**: Groups, rings, fields
- **Rigor and foundations**: Cauchy, Weierstrass, real analysis
- **Set theory**: Cantor's revolutionary work on infinity

### 6. Modern Mathematics (1900 - Present)
- **Formalism and logic**: Hilbert's program, Gödel's incompleteness
- **Topology and abstraction**: Rise of pure mathematics
- **Computing**: Turing, von Neumann, algorithmic mathematics
- **Contemporary trends**: Category theory, computational mathematics

## Pedagogical Notes from Research

- **Narrative approach**: History lends itself to storytelling — mathematical ideas emerged from real problems
- **Cultural diversity**: Important to represent non-European contributions authentically
- **Evolution of notation**: Showing how symbols developed helps understanding
- **Failed ideas**: Understanding wrong turns deepens conceptual grasp
- **Connections to modern practice**: Students should see how historical developments inform current mathematics
