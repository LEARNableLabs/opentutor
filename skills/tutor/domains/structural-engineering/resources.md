# Structural Engineering — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks (Intermediate Level)

- **Structural Analysis by R.C. Hibbeler** (Pearson) — clear explanations, excellent diagrams, thorough coverage of statics and structural analysis. Best for understanding force analysis and equilibrium. ISBN: 978-0134610672

- **Design of Concrete Structures by Nilson, Darwin, and Dolan** (McGraw-Hill) — comprehensive treatment of reinforced concrete behavior and design. Good for material properties and composite systems. ISBN: 978-0073397948

- **Steel Structures: Design and Behavior by Salmon, Johnson, and Malhas** (Pearson) — focuses on steel structural systems, connections, and behavior. Excellent diagrams of steel bridge and building frames. ISBN: 978-0131885561

- **Bridge Engineering Handbook** edited by Wai-Fah Chen and Lian Duan (CRC Press) — encyclopedic reference covering all bridge types. Technical but accessible sections on load paths and structural systems. ISBN: 978-1439852071

- **Tall Building Structures: Analysis and Design by Bryan Stafford Smith and Alex Coull** (Wiley) — classic text on skyscraper structural systems. Clear explanations of tube structures, outriggers, and lateral load resistance. ISBN: 978-0471512370

### Online Courses

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 1.050 Engineering Mechanics I** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-050-solid-mechanics-fall-2004/ — foundational statics, stress/strain, beam bending. Full lecture notes and problem sets.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 1.054 Mechanics and Design of Concrete Structures** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-054-mechanics-and-design-of-concrete-structures-spring-2004/ — reinforced concrete behavior and design principles.

- **Coursera: Engineering Mechanics Specialization (Georgia Tech)** — https://www.coursera.org/specializations/engineering-mechanics — statics, dynamics, and mechanics of materials sequence.

- **edX: Delft University Structural Engineering Courses** — https://www.edx.org/school/delftx — multiple courses on structural analysis, design, and materials.

- **Stanford Online: Structures and Materials** — https://online.stanford.edu/explore?keywords=structures — various short courses on structural concepts.

### Academic Resources

- **StructureMag (Structure Magazine)** — https://www.structuremag.org/ — monthly publication with case studies, technical articles, and practice insights. Accessible to intermediate learners.

- **ASCE Journal of Structural Engineering** — https://ascelibrary.org/journal/jsendh — research papers (more advanced, but abstracts and case studies are accessible).

- **Engineering Structures (Elsevier journal)** — https://www.journals.elsevier.com/engineering-structures — peer-reviewed research on analysis and design.

### Interactive Tools & Simulations

- **West Point Bridge Designer** — https://bridgecontest.usma.edu/ — free software for designing and testing truss bridges. Students compete nationally. Makes load paths visceral.

- **SkyCiv Structural Analysis Software (Educational)** — https://skyciv.com/structural-software/ — cloud-based structural analysis with free educational tier. Model beams, frames, and trusses; visualize bending moments and shear forces.

- **MASTAN2** — https://www.mastan2.com/ — free educational software for matrix structural analysis. Designed for teaching; excellent for frame analysis and visualization.

- **Algodoo (2D Physics Sandbox)** — http://www.algodoo.com/ — create and test structures in a physics simulator. Intuitive way to explore equilibrium and failure modes.

- **Finite Element Method Magnets** — https://fem.oit.duke.edu/ — Duke University's interactive FEM tutorials with visualizations.

### Case Study Collections

- **Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat (CTBUH) Case Studies** — https://www.ctbuh.org/resource/case-studies — detailed analyses of supertall buildings with structural system diagrams and design rationale.

- **Historic Bridge Foundation Database** — http://historicbridges.org/ — photos and technical descriptions of thousands of bridges worldwide.

## People (Researchers & Practitioners to Follow)

### Historical Figures

- **Fazlur Rahman Khan (1929-1982)** — Bangladeshi-American structural engineer who revolutionized skyscraper design. Invented the tube structure concept. His systems enabled the modern supertall building era (Sears Tower, John Hancock Center).

- **Othmar Ammann (1879-1965)** — Swiss-American bridge engineer who designed the George Washington Bridge, Bayonne Bridge, and Verrazano-Narrows Bridge. Master of long-span suspension bridges.

- **Gustave Eiffel (1832-1923)** — French engineer famous for the Eiffel Tower, but he was primarily a bridge engineer. Pioneered iron truss bridges and understood wind loading before aerodynamics was formalized.

- **John Roebling (1806-1869)** — German-American engineer who designed the Brooklyn Bridge (completed by his son Washington Roebling). Invented wire rope manufacturing techniques that made long-span suspension bridges possible.

### Contemporary Figures

- **Leslie E. Robertson (1928-2021)** — Designed the structural systems for the original World Trade Center towers, US Bank Tower, and Shanghai World Financial Center. Known for innovative tall building systems.

- **Santiago Calatrava (1951-)** — Spanish architect-engineer known for sculptural bridges and buildings. Combines structural efficiency with artistic expression. Controversial but influential.

- **Cecil Balmond (1943-)** — Sri Lankan-British structural engineer who collaborated with architects on radical designs. Known for non-linear structural thinking and the Serpentine Gallery Pavilions.

- **Hanif Kara (1958-)** — Structural engineer and professor at Harvard GSD. Advocates for integrated design where structure and architecture develop together.

### Researchers & Educators

- **Connor Jerome** — MIT professor emeritus, expert on structural dynamics and tall buildings.

- **Sarah Billington** — Stanford professor researching sustainable structural materials and systems.

- **Maria Garlock** — Princeton professor specializing in structural mechanics, masonry structures, and historical analysis.

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Biomimicry in Structures

- **The Gherkin (30 St Mary Axe, London)** — diagrid structure inspired by sea sponges' skeletal geometry. Uses 50% less steel than conventional bracing.
  - Resource: https://www.arup.com/projects/30-st-mary-axe

- **Eden Project Biomes** — lightweight geodesic domes based on soap bubble geometry and pollen grain structures.
  - Resource: https://www.edenproject.com/learn/for-everyone/what-are-biomes

- **Eastgate Centre (Harare, Zimbabwe)** — natural ventilation system based on termite mound airflow. Not structural per se, but shows nature-inspired engineering.
  - Resource: https://www.arup.com/projects/eastgate-centre

### Music & Structures

- **Resonance phenomena** — bridges and buildings can act like musical instruments. The Millennium Bridge in London vibrated laterally when pedestrians' footsteps synchronized.
  - Resource: https://www.arup.com/projects/millennium-bridge

- **Singing cables** — cable-stayed bridges sometimes produce eerie tones when wind flows over cables (Rama VIII Bridge in Bangkok, Auckland Harbour Bridge).

### Art & Structural Form

- **Frei Otto's tensile structures** — German architect-engineer who pioneered lightweight membrane structures. His hanging chain models found optimal compression forms (inverted = tension structures).
  - Resource: https://www.arup.com/projects/munich-olympic-stadium

- **Antonio Gaudí's funicular models** — Gaudí used weighted strings to find optimal arch shapes for the Sagrada Família. Pure geometry derived from physical forces.
  - Resource: https://calatravasantiago.wordpress.com/2011/04/05/gaudi-and-his-use-of-catenary-curve/

### Extreme Engineering

- **Millau Viaduct (France)** — tallest bridge in the world (343m tower height), cable-stayed design by Michel Virlogeux and Norman Foster.
  - Resource: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millau_Viaduct

- **Akashi Kaikyo Bridge (Japan)** — longest suspension bridge span in the world (1991m). Designed to withstand typhoons and earthquakes.
  - Resource: https://www.jb-honshi.co.jp/english/bridgeworld/bridge.html

- **Burj Khalifa (Dubai)** — tallest building in the world (828m). Bundled tube structure with setbacks based on Hymenocallis flower geometry.
  - Resource: https://www.burjkhalifa.ae/en/the-tower/design-construction/

### Forensic Engineering

- **Why Buildings Fall Down by Matthys Levy and Mario Salvadori** (book) — accessible case studies of structural failures. Engaging narratives with technical explanations. ISBN: 978-0393311525

- **To Engineer Is Human by Henry Petroski** (book) — philosophical exploration of failure as essential to engineering progress. ISBN: 978-0679734161

- **ASCE forensic engineering resources** — https://www.asce.org/communities/institutes-and-technical-groups/forensic-engineering — technical committee focused on learning from failures.

## Additional Reading

### Popular Science Books

- **The New Science of Strong Materials by J.E. Gordon** — accessible material science with structural applications. Explains why materials behave as they do.

- **Structures: Or Why Things Don't Fall Down by J.E. Gordon** — classic popular science book on structural mechanics. Witty and insightful.

- **The Tower and the Bridge by David Billington** — history of structural art, arguing that great structures are simultaneously efficient, economical, and elegant.

- **Building the Impossible (Discovery Channel series)** — megaproject documentaries including bridges, tunnels, and skyscrapers.

## Notes for Intermediate Students

- Start with **MIT OCW courses** and **Practical Engineering videos** to build intuition before diving into textbooks.
- Use **West Point Bridge Designer** to make load paths tangible — you'll quickly learn what works and what collapses.
- **SkyCiv** is excellent for visualizing internal forces (bending moment diagrams, shear diagrams) that are invisible in real structures.
- Read **Why Buildings Fall Down** early — understanding failure modes builds better intuition than only studying successes.
- Follow **CTBUH** for cutting-edge tall building design and **FHWA** for bridge engineering case studies.
- Don't skip the historical figures — knowing who invented tube structures (Khan) or modern suspension bridges (Roebling) humanizes the field.
