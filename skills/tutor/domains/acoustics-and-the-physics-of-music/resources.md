# Acoustics and the Physics of Music — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Donald E. Hall, "Musical Acoustics" (3rd edition, Brooks-Cole, 2002)** — Standard textbook for intermediate-level courses. Covers wave physics through psychoacoustics with balance of theory and application. Excellent for lessons on harmonics, timbre, and perception. Used at University of Vermont, TCU, and other institutions.

- **Neville H. Fletcher & Thomas D. Rossing, "The Physics of Musical Instruments" (Springer-Verlag, 1998)** — Comprehensive reference on instrument acoustics. Detailed treatment of strings, air columns, percussion, and brass. Best for lessons 14-20 (Musical Instruments module). Used at UIUC Physics 406.

- **Thomas D. Rossing (ed.), "The Science of Sound" (Addison-Wesley, 2001)** — Broad coverage from fundamentals through applications. Accessible mathematics, excellent diagrams. Good for wave fundamentals and resonance lessons.

## People to Follow

### Historical Figures

- **Hermann von Helmholtz (1821-1894)** — Pioneering work on acoustics, timbre perception, consonance theory. "On the Sensations of Tone" (1863) is foundational.

- **Joseph Fourier (1768-1830)** — Developed Fourier analysis (originally for heat flow), which became essential for understanding complex tones.

- **Lord Rayleigh (John William Strutt, 1842-1919)** — "The Theory of Sound" (1877) remains a classic reference on wave physics and acoustics.

- **Harry F. Olson (1902-1982)** — Acoustic engineer at RCA, pioneered microphone design and acoustics measurement.

### Contemporary Researchers & Educators

- **Thomas D. Rossing** — Prolific author on musical acoustics and instrument physics. Authored multiple textbooks and hundreds of papers.

- **Neville H. Fletcher** — Australian physicist specializing in musical instrument acoustics. Co-author of definitive text on instrument physics.

- **Daniel Levitin** — Neuroscientist and musician, author of "This Is Your Brain on Music" — bridges acoustics, perception, and neuroscience.

- **Diana Deutsch** — Psychologist known for auditory illusions (tritone paradox, scale illusion). Demonstrates complexity of pitch perception.

- **Curtis Roads** — Computer music researcher, author of "The Computer Music Tutorial" — connects acoustics to synthesis and digital audio.

### YouTube Educators

- **3Blue1Brown** — Grant Sanderson's videos on Fourier transforms provide excellent visual intuition (search "But what is the Fourier Transform?")

- **Physics Videos by Eugene Khutoryansky** — Animated explanations of wave phenomena, interference, and resonance with excellent visualizations

- **Minute Physics** — Short, clear explanations of physics concepts including sound and waves

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Mathematics

- **Differential equations** — The wave equation (∂²u/∂t² = c²∂²u/∂x²) governs all linear wave phenomena. Connects acoustics to heat flow, quantum mechanics, and electromagnetism.

- **Group theory** — Symmetry in waveforms and spectral patterns. Connects to crystallography and particle physics.

- **Number theory** — Frequency ratios (3:2, 4:3, 5:4) connect harmonics to rational approximations and continued fractions.

### Neuroscience

- **Cochlear mechanics** — The ear performs a biological Fourier transform, with different locations responding to different frequencies. Connects to signal processing and filter design.

- **Neural plasticity** — Perfect pitch development, auditory scene analysis, and musical training effects on brain structure.

### Architecture & Engineering

- **Concert hall design** — Reverberation time calculations (Sabine equation), diffusion, and absorption. Connects to materials science and civil engineering.

- **Noise control** — Active noise cancellation uses destructive interference. Connects to control systems and signal processing.

### Biology

- **Animal communication** — Bird songs use harmonic structures; whale songs use infrasound; bats use ultrasound. Connects to evolutionary biology and ecology.

- **Biomechanics of voice** — Vocal folds as vibrating membranes, vocal tract as resonator. Connects to physiology and speech pathology.

### Computer Science

- **Audio compression** — MP3, AAC use psychoacoustic masking to discard inaudible information. Connects to information theory and data compression.

- **Speech recognition** — Uses spectral features (MFCCs), pitch tracking, formant analysis. Connects to machine learning and AI.

- **Music information retrieval** — Beat tracking, chord recognition, genre classification from acoustic features. Connects to pattern recognition and data science.

### Philosophy

- **What is music?** — Physical vibrations vs. perceptual experience vs. cultural construct. Connects to philosophy of mind and aesthetics.

- **The hard problem of qualia** — Why does 440 Hz *feel* like "A"? Connects to consciousness studies and phenomenology.

### History

- **Tuning system evolution** — Pythagorean → just intonation → meantone → equal temperament reflects changing mathematical and aesthetic priorities across cultures.

- **Instrument invention** — How technological advances (metallurgy, precision machining, electronics) enabled new instruments and sounds.

### Cultural Studies

- **Musical universals** — Are octave equivalence, consonance preferences, and rhythmic entrainment universal? Connects to anthropology and ethnomusicology.

- **Technology and aesthetics** — How recording technology shaped musical styles (close-miking, multitracking, autotune).

## Wild Cards & Enrichment

- **Cymatics** — Visualizing sound with sand patterns on vibrating plates (Chladni figures). Beautiful connection between sound and geometry.

- **Acoustic levitation** — Using standing waves to suspend objects in mid-air. Demonstrates acoustic pressure and radiation forces.

- **The "brown note"** — Myth vs reality of infrasound causing physiological effects. Good discussion of psychoacoustics and critical thinking.

- **Musical roads** — Grooves in pavement creating melodies when driven over. Engineering meets music composition.

- **Acoustic metamaterials** — Engineered materials with unusual acoustic properties (negative refraction, cloaking). Cutting-edge research connecting to materials science.

- **The Shepard tone** — Auditory illusion of infinitely ascending pitch. Demonstrates circularity in pitch perception.

- **Synesthesia** — Cross-modal perception (seeing sounds as colors). Connects neuroscience, perception, and artistic experience.
