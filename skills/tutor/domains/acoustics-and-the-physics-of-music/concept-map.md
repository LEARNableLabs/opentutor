# Acoustics and the Physics of Music — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Longitudinal waves** — sound propagates as compressions and rarefactions in a medium
2. **Frequency and amplitude** — determine pitch and loudness respectively
3. **Superposition principle** — waves add algebraically at every point. Depends on: longitudinal waves
4. **Interference** — constructive and destructive patterns from superposition. Depends on: superposition principle
5. **Standing waves** — specific patterns where nodes and antinodes remain fixed. Depends on: interference, superposition
6. **Fundamental frequency** — lowest resonant frequency of a system. Depends on: standing waves
7. **Reflection and transmission** — wave behavior at boundaries. Depends on: longitudinal waves
8. **Phase relationships** — relative timing between waves determines interference. Depends on: superposition, interference
9. **Beats** — amplitude modulation from slightly different frequencies. Depends on: phase relationships, interference
10. **Resonance** — dramatic amplitude increase at natural frequencies. Depends on: frequency, standing waves
11. **Natural frequencies** — frequencies at which systems preferentially vibrate. Depends on: resonance, fundamental frequency
12. **Harmonic series** — integer multiples of fundamental frequency. Depends on: standing waves, fundamental frequency
13. **Partials and overtones** — frequency components above the fundamental. Depends on: harmonic series
14. **Mode shapes** — spatial patterns of standing waves at different frequencies. Depends on: standing waves, harmonic series
15. **Timbre** — quality of sound determined by spectral content and evolution. Depends on: harmonic series, partials
16. **Spectral envelope** — distribution of energy across frequency bands. Depends on: timbre, partials
17. **Wave speed on strings** — determined by tension and mass density. Depends on: fundamental frequency, standing waves
18. **Air column resonance** — standing waves in tubes (open/closed). Depends on: standing waves, resonance
19. **Body resonance** — instrument body amplifies certain frequencies. Depends on: resonance, timbre
20. **Formants** — resonant peaks in spectrum characteristic of resonator shape. Depends on: resonance, spectral envelope
21. **Inharmonic partials** — partials not in integer ratios (bells, drums). Depends on: partials, harmonic series
22. **Consonance and dissonance** — perceptual quality related to frequency ratios. Depends on: harmonic series, partials
23. **Critical bandwidth** — frequency range within which roughness occurs. Depends on: consonance and dissonance
24. **Pitch perception** — how the auditory system extracts pitch from complex sounds. Depends on: fundamental frequency, harmonic series
25. **Spectral centroid** — center of mass of spectrum, relates to brightness. Depends on: spectral envelope, timbre
26. **Equal-loudness contours** — frequency-dependent sensitivity of hearing. Depends on: frequency, amplitude
27. **Auditory masking** — one sound preventing perception of another. Depends on: critical bandwidth, pitch perception
28. **Virtual pitch** — perception of missing fundamental from overtones alone. Depends on: pitch perception, harmonic series
29. **Reverberation** — persistence of sound from multiple reflections. Depends on: reflection and transmission
30. **Fourier analysis** — decomposition of complex sound into frequency components. Depends on: superposition, harmonic series
31. **Sampling theorem** — minimum sampling rate to capture frequencies. Depends on: frequency, Fourier analysis

## Dependencies

- **Standing waves** require understanding superposition and interference because they arise from the superposition of forward and backward traveling waves
- **Harmonic series** builds on standing waves and fundamental frequency because harmonics are the allowed standing wave patterns
- **Timbre** depends on harmonic series and partials because the spectral content (which harmonics are present and their relative strengths) defines tone quality
- **Resonance** connects to natural frequencies because resonance occurs when driving frequency matches a natural frequency
- **Body resonance and formants** build on general resonance but add complexity of coupled resonators
- **Consonance/dissonance** requires harmonic series understanding because simple frequency ratios produce overlapping harmonics
- **Pitch perception** builds on fundamental frequency and harmonic series because the brain uses harmonic patterns to extract pitch
- **Virtual pitch** extends pitch perception by showing pitch can be inferred even when fundamental is absent
- **Fourier analysis** synthesizes many earlier concepts (superposition, harmonics, spectral envelope) into a mathematical framework

## Bottleneck Concepts

1. **Superposition principle** — everything else about wave interference, standing waves, and complex tones depends on this
2. **Standing waves** — critical for understanding all musical instruments and resonance
3. **Harmonic series** — foundational for timbre, pitch perception, consonance, and spectral analysis
4. **Timbre vs. pitch** — students must separate these perceptual dimensions to understand instrument differences
5. **Resonance** — unifies string vibrations, air columns, room acoustics, and perception

## Mind-Blowing Moments

1. **Lesson 5 (Beats)** — hearing the "wah-wah" of interference between two close frequencies makes superposition visceral
2. **Lesson 10 (Harmonic series)** — realizing every musical note is actually a stack of frequencies explains so much about music theory
3. **Lesson 15 (Violin vs guitar)** — same strings, same notes, completely different sound because of body resonance
4. **Lesson 26 (Missing fundamental)** — your brain invents a pitch that isn't physically present in the sound
5. **Lesson 30 (Fourier analysis)** — ANY sound, no matter how complex, can be built from simple sine waves

## Common Misconceptions

1. **Sound is transverse like light** — Sound in air is longitudinal (compression waves), not transverse
2. **Pitch = frequency** — While related, pitch is perceptual and can be manipulated (missing fundamental, octave illusions)
3. **Loudness = amplitude** — Loudness is perceptual and frequency-dependent (equal-loudness contours)
4. **All partials are harmonic** — Only true for ideal strings and air columns; real percussion has inharmonic partials
5. **Resonance makes things louder** — Resonance amplifies specific frequencies, often while suppressing others
6. **Octaves are universal** — The 2:1 frequency ratio is physical, but octave equivalence is perceptual
7. **Instruments produce single frequencies** — All real instruments produce complex tones with many partials
8. **Tuning is objective** — Equal temperament vs just intonation shows tuning involves compromises
9. **Bigger = lower pitch always** — Depends on whether you change length, tension, or mass density
10. **Fourier analysis is just math** — It's a profound physical truth: superposition allows ANY waveform to be decomposed

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Algebra and trigonometry** — needed for wave equations, frequency ratios, phase relationships
- **Basic physics (mechanics)** — needed for oscillations, energy, restoring forces
- **Wave concepts** — needed for wavelength, frequency, speed relationships (often covered in intro physics)
- **Basic music literacy** — needed to connect physical concepts to musical experience (notes, intervals, scales)
- **Logarithms** — helpful for decibel scale and frequency ratios (can be taught as needed)

## Cross-Connections to Other Domains

- **Signal processing** — Fourier analysis, sampling theory, digital filters
- **Neurophysiology** — how the cochlea performs spectral analysis, neural coding of pitch
- **Architecture** — room acoustics, reverberation, absorption materials
- **Musical instrument design** — engineering for desired timbres and responses
- **Audio engineering** — recording, mixing, mastering, effects processing
- **Mathematics** — differential equations (wave equation), Fourier series, complex numbers
- **Psychophysics** — measurement of perceptual dimensions, just-noticeable differences
