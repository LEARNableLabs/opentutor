# Research Summary: Acoustics and the Physics of Music

## Major Subtopics

1. **Wave Fundamentals** — Physical principles of wave phenomena, oscillations, superposition, interference, standing waves
2. **Sound Production & Resonance** — How vibrations create sound, resonance in physical systems, harmonic series, overtones
3. **Musical Instruments** — Physics of strings, air columns, percussion, electronic synthesis
4. **Psychoacoustics & Perception** — How humans perceive pitch, timbre, loudness; consonance and dissonance; auditory masking
5. **Room Acoustics & Digital Audio** — Reverberation, reflection, absorption, digital sampling, Fourier analysis

## Key Sources Found

### University Courses

- **UIUC Physics 406: Acoustical Physics of Music** — comprehensive course covering acoustical physics with emphasis on music and musical instruments
  - URL: https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Course_Info/Phys406POM_Course_Syllabus.pdf
  
- **Northern Illinois University Physics 180: Acoustics, Music and Hearing** — covers fundamentals through perception
  - URL: https://www.niu.edu/clas/physics/_pdf/spring-2019/Phys-180-syllabus.pdf

- **University of Maryland Physics 102: Physics of Music** — study of physical basis of sound, acoustical properties, human ear and voice, reproduction of sound, electronic music, acoustical properties of auditoriums
  - URL: https://www.umdphysics.umd.edu/academics/courses/989-physics-102-physics-of-music.html

- **MIT OCW: Acoustics of Speech and Hearing** — graduate-level course on physical processes in production, propagation, and reception of human speech
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-551j-acoustics-of-speech-and-hearing-fall-2004/pages/syllabus/

- **MIT OCW: Physics III - Vibrations and Waves** — includes video lectures on sound waves
  - URL: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/resources/lecture-11-video-1/

### Textbooks

- **Donald E. Hall, "Musical Acoustics" (3rd edition, Brooks-Cole, 2002)** — standard textbook used at University of Vermont, TCU, and other institutions
  
- **Neville H. Fletcher & Thomas D. Rossing, "The Physics of Musical Instruments" (Springer-Verlag)** — comprehensive reference on instrument physics, used at UIUC

### Interactive Tools & Simulators

- **PhET Sound Waves Simulation** — interactive research-based simulation for exploring sound waves with audio playback
  - URL: https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/sound-waves

- **Acoustics Apps** — interactive e-learning platform with virtual experiments for acoustics principles, covering wave phenomena to musical instrument acoustics
  - URL: https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/149/2/1175/600803/Acoustics-Apps-Interactive-simulations-for-digital

- **MySIMULATOR Sound & Music** — 3D simulations bridging physics and music theory, including room acoustics and standing-wave resonators
  - URL: https://www.mysimulator.uk/categories/sound/

- **Explore Sound Simulators** — collection of acoustic simulators including sound wave visualization
  - URL: https://exploresound.org/acoustics-activities/acoustic-simulators/

- **Room Acoustics Simulator (Persona 500)** — interactive tool for reverberation time, room modes, and material absorption
  - URL: https://persona500.com/room-acoustics-simulator

### Video Resources

## Available Educational Resources

- Rich collection of university-level courses at intermediate level
- Strong emphasis on both theoretical foundations and practical applications
- Excellent interactive tools for visualization (wave behavior, instrument physics, room acoustics)
- Balance between physics rigor and musical applications
- Coverage spans from fundamental wave physics through psychoacoustics to digital audio

## Pedagogical Approach Informed by Sources

- **Engineering + music perspective** — problems explored from physical principles through to real musical instruments
- **Interactive experimentation** — many courses emphasize hands-on work with simulators and real instruments
- **Interdisciplinary connections** — bridges physics, music theory, perception, and engineering
- **Visual + auditory learning** — topic naturally supports both seeing and hearing phenomena
