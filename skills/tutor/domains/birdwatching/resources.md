# Birdwatching — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Educational Organizations

- **Cornell Lab of Ornithology** — https://www.birds.cornell.edu/
  - World-leading ornithological research institution with extensive public education programs
  - Houses eBird, Merlin, Macaulay Library, and Bird Academy
  - Covers all topics at all levels; outstanding intermediate resources

- **All About Birds** — https://www.allaboutbirds.org/
  - Comprehensive species guide with photos, sounds, ID tips, maps, and life history
  - Excellent learning articles on ID skills, behavior, and conservation
  - Best single reference for North American species

- **National Audubon Society** — https://www.audubon.org/
  - Conservation-focused with strong educational content
  - Regional chapters provide local birding guides and field trips
  - Good for ethics, conservation context, and habitat-based birding

### Field Guides & References

- **The Sibley Guide to Birds** — https://www.sibleyguides.com/
  - Comprehensive North American guide with 6,600+ detailed illustrations
  - Excellent for visual learners; shows variation within species
  - Blog has superb ID articles on difficult species pairs

- **Peterson Field Guide to Birds of North America** — https://www.petersonguides.com/
  - Classic guide using the Peterson Identification System (arrows pointing to field marks)
  - Longstanding standard; many birders grew up with Peterson

- **The Crossley ID Guide** — https://www.crossleybooks.com/
  - Photo-based guide showing birds in realistic contexts
  - Helps build "search image" for birds in habitat
  - Excellent complement to illustration-based guides

### Online Courses

- **Bird Academy** — https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/
  - Cornell Lab's paid course platform ($10-50 per course)
  - Recommended courses: Joy of Birdwatching, Be a Better Birder: Learning Bird Songs, Hawk Watch, Bird Biology
  - High-quality video instruction with quizzes and certificates

## Interactive Tools & Apps

### Essential Apps (All Free)

- **Merlin Bird ID** — https://merlin.allaboutbirds.org/
  - AI-powered ID using photos, sound recordings, or questionnaire
  - Includes bird pack downloads for offline use
  - Sound ID feature is revolutionary for learning vocalizations
  - **Required for this curriculum**

- **eBird Mobile** — https://ebird.org/
  - Submit checklists in the field; view real-time sightings nearby
  - Access species maps, bar charts, and alerts
  - **Required by Lesson 20**

- **BirdNET** — https://birdnet.cornell.edu/
  - Real-time sound ID for passive monitoring
  - Good complement to Merlin for vocal practice

### Citizen Science Platforms

- **eBird** — https://ebird.org/
  - Global bird observation database (900M+ observations)
  - Personal checklist management, species lists, range maps, abundance data
  - Powers scientific research and conservation
  - Explore page: https://ebird.org/explore — essential tool for learning local species

- **iNaturalist** — https://www.inaturalist.org/
  - Community-driven species identification (not just birds)
  - Upload photos for expert verification
  - Excellent for learning visual ID and getting feedback

- **BirdCast** — https://birdcast.info/
  - Migration forecasting using weather radar
  - Live migration maps show real-time bird movements
  - Local migration alerts help time field trips
  - **Essential for Lessons 16-18**

### Media Archives

- **Macaulay Library** — https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/
  - World's largest archive of wildlife audio, video, and photos (2M+ recordings)
  - Search by species, location, recordist quality
  - **Primary resource for Lessons 11-15 (vocalizations)**

- **Xeno-canto** — https://xeno-canto.org/
  - Community-contributed bird sound recordings worldwide
  - 750,000+ recordings; global coverage beyond North America
  - Includes spectrograms; downloadable

## Code, Data & Tools

### eBird Data Access

- **eBird Status and Trends** — https://ebird.org/science/status-and-trends
  - Visualize abundance, range changes, and population trends
  - Data products downloadable for analysis
  - Used in 100+ peer-reviewed publications

- **eBird API** — https://documenter.getpostman.com/view/664302/S1ENwy59
  - Programmatic access to observations, taxonomy, hotspots, and regional data
  - For students with coding skills (Python, R, JavaScript)

### Data Analysis Tools

- **R package: auk** — https://cornelllabofornithology.github.io/auk/
  - Extract and analyze eBird data in R
  - For advanced students interested in data science applications

- **GBIF (Global Biodiversity Information Facility)** — https://www.gbif.org/
  - Aggregates biodiversity data including eBird
  - Download species occurrence data for analysis

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Physics & Engineering

- **Biomimicry in aviation** — https://asknature.org/
  - Bird wing design inspires drones and aircraft (winglets, slotted primaries)
  - Barn Owl silent flight studied for noise reduction in aviation

- **Feather nanostructures** — Structural coloration (blues, greens) uses photonic crystals
  - https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/the-fascinating-physics-of-iridescent-feathers-180975427/

### Neuroscience & Cognition

- **Bird song learning and human language** — Neural pathways for vocal learning parallel human speech development
  - https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1256846
  - Zebra Finches used as model organisms for language acquisition research

- **Quantum biology in navigation** — Cryptochrome proteins may enable magnetoreception
  - https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03618-9

### Computer Science & AI

- **Merlin Bird ID tech** — Convolutional neural networks trained on citizen science photos
  - Cornell Lab AI page: https://www.birds.cornell.edu/home/ai-for-ornithology/
  - Same architectures used in facial recognition and medical imaging

- **Bioacoustics and signal processing** — Spectrograms, automated call detection
  - BirdNET uses TensorFlow deep learning: https://github.com/kahst/BirdNET-Analyzer

### Climate Science

- **Birds as bioindicators** — Phenology shifts (migration timing, nesting dates) track climate change
  - https://www.audubon.org/climate/survivalbydegrees
  - eBird data reveals range shifts in real time

- **Acoustic monitoring for biodiversity** — Soundscape ecology measures ecosystem health
  - Bernie Krause's "The Great Animal Orchestra" — https://www.wildsanctuary.com/

### Mathematics & Statistics

- **Species distribution modeling** — MaxEnt and other algorithms predict habitat suitability
  - Used by eBird Status and Trends to create range maps from sparse data
  - https://ebird.org/science/status-and-trends/methods

- **Optimal foraging theory** — Mathematical models predict where birds feed
  - Connects to economics (cost-benefit analysis) and game theory

### Psychology & Behavior

- **Tool use in corvids** — New Caledonian Crows craft tools, showing problem-solving comparable to primates
  - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982216309009

- **Social learning and culture** — Bird songs evolve culturally (e.g., White-crowned Sparrow dialects)
  - Parallels to human cultural transmission

### Art & Humanities

- **John James Audubon** — "Birds of America" (1827-1838), iconic natural history art
  - https://www.audubon.org/birds-of-america

- **Roger Tory Peterson** — Revolutionized field guides with visual comparison system
  - Influence on scientific illustration and nature education

- **Bird symbolism across cultures** — Corvids in mythology, owls as wisdom, doves as peace
  - Interdisciplinary link to anthropology, literature, religion

## Gear & Equipment (Optional)

### Binoculars (Beginner-Intermediate)

- **Nikon Monarch 7 8x42** (~$350) — Excellent value; bright, sharp, durable
- **Vortex Diamondback HD 8x42** (~$200) — Budget-friendly, good optics
- **Celestron Nature DX 8x42** (~$150) — Entry-level, solid for getting started

*Note: 8x magnification is easier to use and brighter than 10x for most birding*

### Field Guides (Physical Books)

- Choose ONE to start: Sibley, Peterson, or National Geographic. Don't collect them all until you've used one thoroughly.

### Smartphone Accessories

- **Phone scope adapter** — Attach phone to binoculars or spotting scope for digiscoping (photos through optics)
- **External microphone** — Improves sound recording quality for Merlin Sound ID

- **Policy advocacy** — Migratory Bird Treaty Act, habitat protection
  - Audubon Advocacy: https://www.audubon.org/conservation/advocacy
