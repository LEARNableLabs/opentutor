# Tracking and Trailing — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Mammal Tracks & Sign: A Guide to North American Species" by Mark Elbroch** (2003, Stackpole Books)
  - Covers: Comprehensive track morphology, gaits, scats, feeding signs for all North American mammals
  - Why it's good for intermediate level: Detailed species accounts with measurements, clear illustrations, includes behavioral ecology
  - URL: https://www.stackpolebooks.com/mammal-tracks-sign

- **"A Field Guide to Mammal Tracking in North America" by James Halfpenny** (2008, Johnson Books)
  - Covers: Gait analysis, trailing methodology, substrate effects, aging techniques
  - Why it's good: Emphasizes interpretation over identification; strong on systematic tracking
  - URL: https://tracknature.com (author's site with field guide info)

- **"Peterson Field Guide to Animal Tracks" by Olaus Murie & Mark Elbroch** (3rd edition, 2005)
  - Covers: Classic illustrated guide, track patterns, scat, sign
  - Why it's good: Portable field reference, excellent illustrations, time-tested accuracy
  - URL: https://www.hmhbooks.com/peterson-field-guides

- **"The Art of Tracking: The Origin of Science" by Louis Liebenberg** (1990, David Philip Publishers)
  - Covers: Tracking methodology, cognitive aspects, evolutionary theory, San bushmen tracking culture
  - Why it's good: Philosophical and methodological depth; reframes tracking as scientific reasoning
  - URL: https://www.cybertracker.org/tracking-books

### Academic & Technical Resources

- **"Behavior of North American Mammals" by Mark Elbroch & Kurt Rinehart** (2011, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt)
  - Connects sign to behavior ecology; essential for interpreting what tracks mean, not just what they are

- **Track and Sign Journals** — CyberTracker and Tracker Certification publish ongoing research, case studies, and technique refinements
  - URL: https://www.cybertracker.org/resources

- **WildTrack Footprint Identification Technology (FIT)** — https://wildtrack.org

## Unexpected Connections (cross-discipline links)

### Tracking → Forensic Science
- Footwear analysis in criminal investigations uses identical methodology (pattern matching, gait analysis, substrate effects)
- Resources: FBI footwear database, "Forensic Gait Analysis" by Ivan Birch (book)
- When to drop in: Lesson 20 (aging), 26 (reconstruction)

### Tracking → Robotics & Biomechanics
- Boston Dynamics uses animal gait research for robot locomotion (Spot, BigDog)
- URL: https://www.bostondynamics.com/blog — search "gait" or "locomotion"
- When to drop in: Lesson 7-8 (gait efficiency)

### Tracking → Archaeology & Paleoanthropology
- Hominin trackways (Laetoli footprints, 3.6 million years old) reveal bipedal gait evolution
- Resources: "The Fossil Footprint Maker website" — https://www.fossilfootprints.org
- When to drop in: Lesson 1 (what footprints preserve), lesson 8 (gait as evolutionary signal)

### Tracking → Machine Learning
- Computer vision challenges: how to handle substrate variation, partial tracks, occlusion
- Parallel to human perception — what features are diagnostic?
- Resources: Papers on automated species identification from camera traps (search "footprint recognition wildlife" on Google Scholar)
- When to drop in: Lesson 5 (pattern recognition), lesson 22 (substrate as a confound)

### Tracking → Philosophy of Science
- Abductive reasoning (inference to best explanation) — trackers generate hypotheses from incomplete data
- Connection to medical diagnosis, historical reconstruction, detective work
- Resource: "Clues, Myths, and the Historical Method" by Carlo Ginzburg (essay on evidential reasoning)
- When to drop in: Lesson 18, 26 (narrative reconstruction)

### Tracking → Indigenous Knowledge Systems
- Traditional ecological knowledge (TEK) includes millennia of observational tracking data
- Example: Inuit knowledge of caribou migration predicted from track aging and sign density
- Resource: "Arctic Trails" database — https://www.trackingproject.org
- When to drop in: Lesson 20 (aging), 24 (trailing methodology)

### Tracking → Non-Invasive Wildlife Monitoring
- Modern ecology relies on sign surveys (track plates, scat transects, hair snares) because direct observation is often impossible
- Resource: "Noninvasive Survey Methods for Carnivores" (USDA Forest Service, free PDF)
- URL: https://www.fs.usda.gov/psw/publications/zielinski/psw_2006_zielinski001.pdf
- When to drop in: Lesson 13 (scat as data), 17 (incidental sign for science)

### Tracking → Citizen Science
- iNaturalist, eMammal, and regional tracking projects crowdsource wildlife distribution data
- Students can contribute observations during field practice
- When to drop in: Lesson 5 (community learning), lesson 26 (contributing to science)

## Regional & Specialized Resources

### Desert Tracking
- "Tracking and the Art of Seeing" by Paul Rezendes — includes sand and hard-ground techniques
- "Desert Tracking" by David Diaz (out of print but available used)

### Snow Tracking
- "Wildlife of the Rocky Mountains: Tracking and Tracking Ecology" by James Halfpenny
- Advantages: High-fidelity substrate, reveals gaits clearly
- Challenges: Rapid weathering (sun cups, wind), depth variation

### Coastal/Wetland Tracking
- "Tracker's Field Guide" by James Lowery — includes mudflat and beach tracking
- Species specialization: shorebirds, otters, raccoons, herons

### European Tracking
- "Animal Tracks and Signs" by Preben Bang & Preben Dahlstrøm (Oxford University Press)
- Different species (red deer vs. elk, European badger vs. American), similar principles

## Tools & Equipment

- **Tracking stick** — adjustable measuring tool for stride/straddle
  - DIY instructions: https://www.wildernessawareness.org/tracking-stick-guide

- **Field journal** — waterproof notebook (Rite in the Rain brand)

- **Camera** — phone cameras work; DSLR with macro lens ideal for detail

- **Measuring tape** — flexible fabric or metal, 150cm minimum

- **Plaster or dental stone** — for making track casts
  - Guide: https://www.nps.gov/articles/track-casting.htm

- **Hand lens (10x magnification)** — for examining scat content, hair

- **Field guides** — laminated pocket guides for quick reference
  - Quick Reference Publishing "Animal Tracks" series (regional options)
