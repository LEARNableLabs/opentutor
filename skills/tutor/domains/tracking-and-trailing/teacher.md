## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Tracks always look like field guide illustrations"**
   - Why students believe this: Field guides show idealized tracks in perfect substrate under optimal conditions
   - Reality: Substrate, slope, speed, and weather drastically alter appearance. A fox track in wet sand looks nothing like a fox track in snow
   - How to correct: Early exposure to substrate variation (lesson 4, then reinforced in 22). Show same species in mud/snow/dust side-by-side

2. **"Bigger track = bigger animal (within a species)"**
   - Why: Intuitive size correlation
   - Reality: Front vs. hind feet differ in size; speed affects track size (running spreads toes); substrate compression varies
   - Correction: Teach measurement of corresponding feet only; discuss gait effects on apparent size (lesson 8-9)

3. **"Scat shape alone identifies the species"**
   - Why: Beginner guides emphasize morphology
   - Reality: Diet drastically changes scat appearance. A coyote eating berries produces scat resembling a b
