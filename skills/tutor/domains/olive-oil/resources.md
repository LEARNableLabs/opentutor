# Olive Oil — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Academic References

- **Boskou, D. (ed.) — *Olive Oil: Chemistry and Technology*** (2nd ed., AOCS Press)
  - Comprehensive technical reference covering chemistry, processing, analysis, and quality
  - Intermediate to advanced level; strong on fatty acids and phenolic compounds
  - Best for: chemistry module (Lessons 10-14)
  - https://www.aocs.org/olive-oil-chemistry-technology

- **Harwood, J. & Aparicio, R. (eds.) — *Handbook of Olive Oil: Analysis and Properties*** (Springer)
  - Deep dive into analytical methods and quality parameters
  - Advanced level; focus on laboratory techniques
  - Best for: quality grading (Lesson 2), chemical analysis (Lessons 12-13)
  - https://link.springer.com/book/olive-oil-handbook

- **Mueller, Tom — *Extra Virginity: The Sublime and Scandalous World of Olive Oil*** (W.W. Norton)
  - Accessible narrative covering history, fraud, quality, and sensory analysis
  - Intermediate/popular level; excellent storytelling
  - Best for: foundations (Lessons 1-3), fraud rabbit hole
  - https://www.wwnorton.com/books/Extra-Virginity/

- **International Olive Council (IOC) — Trade Standard Applying to Olive Oils and Olive-Pomace Oils**
  - Official quality standards and limits (free fatty acids, peroxide value, sensory requirements)
  - Technical/regulatory document
  - Best for: quality grades (Lesson 2), chemical standards (Lesson 12)
  - https://www.internationaloliveoil.org/what-we-do/chemistry-standardisation-unit/standards-and-methods/

### Unexpected Connections

#### Wine and Olive Oil Parallels
- Both use terroir concepts, varietal character, professional tasting panels
- Wine Folly's comparison: https://winefolly.com/deep-dive/wine-olive-oil/
- Best for: terroir lesson (Lesson 6), sensory methodology (Lesson 16)

#### Oleocanthal and Ibuprofen
- Gary Beauchamp's discovery that oleocanthal triggers same throat receptors as ibuprofen
- Nature paper and follow-up research
- Best for: pungency lesson (Lesson 14), health rabbit hole
- https://www.nature.com/articles/oleocanthal-inflammation

#### Ancient Trade Routes and Amphorae
- Archaeological evidence of olive oil trade in Roman Empire
- Amphora markings, Monte Testaccio (Rome's "pottery mountain" of broken oil jars)
- Best for: historical lesson (Lesson 3), cultural rabbit hole
- https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/amphorae

#### Climate Change and Olive Belt Migration
- Shifting viable growing zones, heat stress, new regions (England, northern Europe)
- Xylella fastidiosa impact on Italian olive groves
- Best for: terroir (Lesson 6), harvest timing (Lesson 22)
- Research from climate science journals

#### Olive Oil Fraud Economics
- Tom Mueller's investigations, Italian police raids, soft deodorization scandal
- Economic incentives for adulteration (price differences, low detection rates)
- Best for: quality grades (Lesson 2), sensory defect detection (Lesson 18)
- https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/slippery-business

#### Supercritical Fluid Extraction
- Novel processing using CO2 instead of mechanical pressing
- Preserves phenolics, different flavor profile
- Best for: production methods (Lesson 3), chemistry rabbit hole
- Food engineering journals

## Reading Order Recommendation

For students working through the curriculum:

1. **Start with**: Tom Mueller's *Extra Virginity* (engaging entry point)
2. **During cultivar module**: UC Davis cultivar profiles + Olive Oil Source directory
3. **During chemistry module**: Boskou textbook chapters on fatty acids and phenolics + Olive Wellness Institute articles
4. **During sensory module**: COOC sensory materials + IOC standards + practice with tasting kit
5. **During integration**: UC Davis webinars on harvest timing and quality factors

## Notes on Resource Selection

- **Prioritize IOC and UC Davis** — these are authoritative and free/low-cost
- **Supplement with physical tasting** — sensory concepts cannot be learned from reading alone; purchase small tasting set
- **Balance technical and accessible** — alternate between research papers (Boskou, journals) and popular sources (Mueller, videos)
- **Check dates on chemical research** — phenolic compound research is evolving; prefer sources from 2015+
- **For fraud/adulteration**: prioritize investigative journalism and regulatory sources over industry claims
