# Neuroplasticity and Brain Rehabilitation — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Foundations of Neuroplasticity
The biological basis of brain change across the lifespan. Core concepts include synaptic plasticity, structural plasticity, critical periods, and the historical shift from viewing the brain as fixed to understanding it as dynamic. Key figures: Donald Hebb, Eric Kandel, Michael Merzenich.

### 2. Cellular and Molecular Mechanisms
How plasticity works at the cellular level: long-term potentiation (LTP), long-term depression (LTD), Hebbian learning, neurogenesis, dendritic spine remodeling, neurotrophic factors (BDNF, NGF), and activity-dependent gene expression.

### 3. Clinical Applications in Neurorehabilitation
Stroke recovery, traumatic brain injury, spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, and neurodegenerative diseases. Focus on constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT), motor imagery, brain-computer interfaces, and evidence-based rehabilitation protocols.

### 4. Rehabilitation Techniques and Technologies
Non-invasive brain stimulation (rTMS, tDCS), virtual reality therapy, robotic-assisted therapy, enriched environments, task-specific training, and the role of timing, intensity, and specificity in rehabilitation.

### 5. Special Populations and Emerging Research
Neuroplasticity in aging, pediatric populations, pharmacological enhancement of plasticity, optogenetics, closed-loop neuromodulation, and personalized rehabilitation based on neuroimaging biomarkers.
