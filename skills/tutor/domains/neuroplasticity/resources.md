# Neuroplasticity and Brain Rehabilitation — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks
- **Neuroplasticity** by Moheb Costandi (MIT Press Essential Knowledge, 2016) — Accessible 200-page introduction covering history, mechanisms, and applications. Perfect for intermediate students who want a readable overview before diving into technical literature.
- **The Brain That Changes Itself** by Norman Doidge (2007) — Popular science classic. Use for motivation and case studies, but supplement with rigorous sources for mechanisms. Good for lessons 1-2 (foundations).
- **Principles of Neural Science** (6th ed.) by Kandel, Schwartz, Jessell, Siegelbaum, Hudspeth — Chapters 53-55 on learning, memory, and synaptic plasticity. Gold standard for LTP mechanisms (lesson 7). Available in most university libraries.
- **Stroke Recovery and Rehabilitation** (2nd ed.) edited by Joel Stein et al. (2020) — Clinical textbook covering evidence-based rehabilitation. Essential for lessons 12-23.

### University Courses (Open Access)
- **MIT 9.01 Introduction to Neuroscience** (OpenCourseWare) — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-01-introduction-to-neuroscience-fall-2007/ — Lectures 19-21 cover learning, memory, and plasticity. Video lectures, notes, and exams available.
- **Stanford Bio 150 Human Behavioral Biology** (YouTube) — https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL848F2368C90DDC3D — Lectures by Robert Sapolsky. Lecture 15 covers neuroplasticity in development and adulthood.
- **Coursera: Synapses, Neurons and Brains** (Hebrew University) — https://www.coursera.org/learn/synapses — 5-week course covering synaptic transmission, LTP, and plasticity mechanisms. Audit for free.
- **Coursera: Understanding the Brain** (University of Chicago) — https://www.coursera.org/learn/neurobiology — Includes modules on neuroplasticity and neural development.

### Review Articles (Open Access)
- **Nature Reviews Neuroscience** — https://www.nature.com/subjects/neuroplasticity — Curated collection of reviews on plasticity mechanisms, clinical applications, and emerging technologies.
- **Kolb et al. (2016) "Brain plasticity in the developing brain"** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5854417/ — Comprehensive review of developmental and adult plasticity. Good for lesson 4 (critical periods) and lesson 25 (pediatric).
- **Cramer et al. (2011) "Harnessing neuroplasticity for clinical applications"** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21228773/ — Translational review linking mechanisms to rehabilitation. Excellent for lessons 12-23.
- **Malenka & Bear (2004) "LTP and LTD: an embarrassment of riches"** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3753797/ — Classic review of LTP/LTD mechanisms. Dense but authoritative for lesson 7.

### Clinical Guidelines
- **AHA/ASA Stroke Rehabilitation Guidelines** — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000098 — Evidence-based recommendations for stroke rehabilitation. Reference for lessons 12-17.
- **Cochrane Reviews on Rehabilitation** — https://www.cochranelibrary.com/ — Systematic reviews of CIMT, mirror therapy, virtual reality, and other interventions. Use for lessons 13, 19, 21.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos
- **Michael Merzenich TED Talk: "Growing evidence of brain plasticity"** — https://www.ted.com/talks/michael_merzenich_growing_evidence_of_brain_plasticity — 23 minutes. Use in lesson 2. Pioneer discusses his research on cortical reorganization.
- **Jill Bolte Taylor TED Talk: "My stroke of insight"** — https://www.ted.com/talks/jill_bolte_taylor_s_powerful_stroke_of_insight — 18 minutes. First-person account of stroke and recovery. Use in lesson 12.
- **V.S. Ramachandran TED Talk: "The neurons that shaped civilization"** — https://www.ted.com/talks/vs_ramachandran_the_neurons_that_shaped_civilization — Includes mirror neurons and phantom limb. Rabbit hole for lesson 2 or 14.
- **Khan Academy Neuroscience** — https://www.khanacademy.org/science/health-and-medicine/nervous-system-and-sensory-infor — Short videos on neuron structure, synaptic transmission, and plasticity. Good for prerequisite review.
- **Crash Course Anatomy & Physiology: Nervous System** — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qPix_X-9t7E — Engaging 10-minute overview. Use for students who need foundational review before lesson 1.
- **Nature Video: "The brain that changes itself"** — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELpfYCZa87g — Documentary-style explainer on neuroplasticity mechanisms.

### Interactive Tools
- **3D Brain (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory)** — https://www.3dbraincsh.org/ — Interactive 3D brain model with information on structures and functions. Use to orient students on brain anatomy.
- **Backyard Brains: Virtual Neurophysiology Lab** — https://backyardbrains.com/ — Simulations of action potentials, synaptic transmission, and plasticity. Hands-on learning for lesson 6-7.
- **The Brain Atlas** — https://www.brainfacts.org/3d-brain — Interactive atlas from the Society for Neuroscience. Good for stroke lesion mapping (lesson 12).
- **PubMed Central (PMC)** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/ — Open-access repository of biomedical literature. Teach students to search for and read primary research.

### Code and Data
- **NeuroSynth** — https://neurosynth.org/ — Meta-analysis platform for fMRI data. Students can look up brain activation patterns for "motor imagery," "stroke," "plasticity," etc.
- **OpenNeuro** — https://openneuro.org/ — Open neuroimaging datasets. Advanced students can explore stroke recovery data or brain stimulation studies.
- **BCI Competition** — http://www.bbci.de/competition/ — Datasets and benchmarks for brain-computer interface research. Rabbit hole for lesson 15 or 17.
- **Allen Brain Atlas** — https://portal.brain-map.org/ — Gene expression, connectivity, and electrophysiology data. Advanced resource for students interested in molecular mechanisms.

### People to Look Up
- **Eric Kandel** — Nobel laureate for work on molecular basis of memory and LTP. Read his autobiography *In Search of Memory*.
- **Michael Merzenich** — Pioneer of cortical plasticity research; co-founded Posit Science (brain training software).
- **V.S. Ramachandran** — Known for work on phantom limbs, synesthesia, and mirror neurons.
- **Edward Taub** — Developed constraint-induced movement therapy; controversial animal research led to clinical breakthrough.
- **Álvaro Pascual-Leone** — Leader in non-invasive brain stimulation (TMS, tDCS) for rehabilitation.
- **Randolph Nudo** — Primate research on stroke recovery and motor cortex reorganization.
- **Leonardo Cohen** — NIH researcher on motor learning, plasticity, and brain stimulation.
- **Steven Zeiler & John Krakauer** — Critical voices questioning assumptions in rehabilitation research; emphasize rigorous experimental design.

### Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

#### Neuroplasticity and Music
- **Musical training changes brain structure** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3957486/ — Longitudinal studies showing structural and functional changes in musicians. Parallels to motor learning in rehabilitation.

#### Neuroplasticity and Meditation
- **Mindfulness and cortical thickness** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1361002/ — Studies linking meditation practice to gray matter changes. Raises questions about mechanism: is it attention training, stress reduction, or something else?

#### Neuroplasticity and Language Learning
- **Bilingualism and cognitive reserve** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4341987/ — Bilingual individuals show delayed dementia onset, possibly due to enhanced cognitive reserve from lifelong language switching.

#### Neuroplasticity and Sleep
- **Sleep and memory consolidation** — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3768102/ — Sleep-dependent reactivation of neural patterns strengthens learning. Implications for rehabilitation: should we time interventions around sleep?

#### Neuroplasticity in the Wild
- **Seasonal neurogenesis in songbirds** — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.7403907 — Some species grow and shrink brain regions seasonally. Extreme plasticity in nature; what can we learn?

## Recommended Learning Path

1. **Start with a textbook**: Read Costandi's *Neuroplasticity* or relevant chapters from Kandel for structured overview.
2. **Watch foundational videos**: Merzenich TED talk, Khan Academy neuroscience, MIT OCW lectures.
3. **Dive into mechanisms**: Work through MIT 9.01 lectures 19-21 and Malenka & Bear review on LTP.
4. **Explore clinical applications**: Read AHA/ASA guidelines and Cochrane reviews; watch Jill Bolte Taylor TED talk.
5. **Browse primary literature**: Use PubMed to find recent papers on topics of interest; practice reading methods and results.
6. **Engage with tools**: Try 3D Brain, Backyard Brains, NeuroSynth to visualize and interact with concepts.
7. **Follow the frontier**: Browse *Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation* and *Nature Neuroscience* for cutting-edge research.
