# Evolutionary Biology and Phylogenetics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Core Evolutionary Biology
- Mechanisms of evolution (natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, mutation)
- Population genetics and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium
- Quantitative genetics and fitness
- Adaptation and constraint
- Speciation processes (allopatric, sympatric, parapatric)
- Macroevolutionary patterns
- Molecular evolution and neutral theory

### Phylogenetics
- Phylogenetic tree reading and construction
- Character-based methods (parsimony)
- Distance-based methods (neighbor-joining, UPGMA)
- Statistical methods (maximum likelihood, Bayesian inference)
- Molecular clocks (strict and relaxed)
- Comparative methods
- Applications to epidemiology, conservation, and systematics

## Key Educational Resources Found

### Textbooks
- **"Evolution" by Douglas J. Futuyma and Mark Kirkpatrick** (4th Edition, Sinauer Associates, 2017) — most widely used textbook, appears in syllabi from Georgia Tech, UAF, and other universities
- **"An Introduction to Molecular Evolution and Phylogenetics"** by Lindell Bromham — focuses on how genomes record evolutionary information

### University Courses
- **Yale EEB 122** (Ecology and Evolutionary Biology) — comprehensive lecture series including phylogeny and systematics
- **Emory BIOL 241** (Evolutionary Biology) — covers phylogenetics concepts and tree construction
- **Georgia Tech Evolutionary Biology** — quantitative approach to fitness, adaptation, and phylogenetics
- **University of Alaska Fairbanks BIOL 481** — principles of evolution with phylogenetics integration
- **University of Illinois IB 467** (Principles of Systematics) — dedicated systematics course

### Video Lectures
- **Khan Academy** — "Understanding and building phylogenetic trees" video series covering cladogram construction and reading
- **MIT OCW 6.047** (Computational Biology Fall 2015) — Lecture 18 on Molecular Evolution and Phylogenetics
- **MIT OCW 18.417** (Introduction to Computational Molecular Biology) — phylogenetics lecture materials
- **MIT CompBio Lecture 19** (Fall 2019) by Prof. Manolis Kellis — computational phylogenetics on YouTube

### Interactive Tools and Software
- **MEGA (Molecular Evolutionary Genetics Analysis)** — free, beginner-friendly software for phylogenetic analysis with good visualization and built-in models
- **BEAST (Bayesian Evolutionary Analysis Sampling Trees)** — sophisticated Bayesian MCMC tool for time-calibrated phylogenies, best for epidemic dynamics
- **RAxML (Randomized Axelerated Maximum Likelihood)** — fast, scalable maximum likelihood inference
- **IQ-TREE 2** — modern maximum likelihood tool
- **iTOL (Interactive Tree of Life)** — online browser-based tool for tree visualization and annotation
- **BEAUti** — interactive application for designing BEAST analyses

### Open Educational Resources
- **Yale Open Courses** — full lecture videos and transcripts for EEB 122
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** — lecture notes, problem sets, and slides for computational biology courses

## Available Online Tutorials

## Key Concepts for Intermediate Level
- Students need to understand BOTH the biological theory (mechanisms of evolution, population genetics) AND the computational methods (tree construction algorithms)
- Balance between conceptual understanding and practical tool use
- Important to cover both classical approaches (parsimony) and modern statistical methods (ML, Bayesian)
- Real-world applications provide motivation: viral evolution tracking, conservation genetics, biodiversity studies

## Research Gaps Identified
- Need to connect molecular clock methods to real epidemic tracking examples
- Importance of showing limitations of each phylogenetic method
- Should emphasize when to use which tool/method based on dataset characteristics
