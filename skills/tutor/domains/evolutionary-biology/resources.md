# Resources — Evolutionary Biology and Phylogenetics

## Primary Textbooks

### Comprehensive Evolutionary Biology
**"Evolution" by Douglas J. Futuyma and Mark Kirkpatrick** (4th Edition, Sinauer Associates, 2017)
- Most widely used evolution textbook in university courses
- Comprehensive coverage from population genetics to macroevolution
- Excellent figures and examples
- Used at: Georgia Tech, University of Alaska Fairbanks, many others
- **Best for:** Lessons 1-12 (evolutionary mechanisms, speciation, molecular evolution)
- Available: University libraries, Amazon (~$80-120)

### Phylogenetics Focus
**"An Introduction to Molecular Evolution and Phylogenetics" by Lindell Bromham** (2nd Edition, Oxford University Press, 2016)
- Specifically designed for students learning phylogenetics
- Bridges biological concepts and computational methods
- Excellent on molecular clocks and substitution models
- **Best for:** Lessons 11, 13-28 (molecular evolution through phylogenetic methods)
- Available: University libraries, Oxford University Press website

## Software & Tools

### MEGA (Molecular Evolutionary Genetics Analysis)
- **Type:** Desktop application (Windows, Mac, Linux)
- **Cost:** Free
- **Best for:** Beginners learning parsimony and distance methods
- **Features:** GUI-based, good visualization, built-in tutorials
- **Use in:** Lessons 18-20 (parsimony, NJ, UPGMA)
- **Download:** megasoftware.net
- **Tutorial:** Medium guide "MEGA Software for Phylogenetic Analysis: Beginner to Advanced"

### IQ-TREE 2
- **Type:** Command-line (web server also available)
- **Cost:** Free, open-source
- **Best for:** Maximum likelihood inference
- **Features:** Fast, automatic model selection, good documentation
- **Use in:** Lesson 22 (maximum likelihood)
- **Download:** iqtree.org
- **Tutorial:** Official documentation and quickstart guide

### BEAST (Bayesian Evolutionary Analysis Sampling Trees)
- **Type:** Command-line + GUI (BEAUti for setup)
- **Cost:** Free, open-source
- **Best for:** Bayesian inference and molecular clock dating
- **Features:** Time-calibrated trees, relaxed clocks, viral dynamics
- **Use in:** Lessons 24-25, 26-28 (Bayesian inference and molecular clocks)
- **Download:** beast.community
- **Tutorials:** Excellent tutorial ecosystem on BEAST website
  - "First Steps" tutorial for beginners
  - "Measurably Evolving Populations" for viral data
  - "Relaxed Molecular Clocks" tutorial

### RAxML (Randomized Axelerated Maximum Likelihood)
- **Type:** Command-line
- **Cost:** Free, open-source
- **Best for:** Large datasets, bootstrap analysis
- **Features:** Very fast, standard tool in phylogenomics
- **Use in:** Lessons 20, 22 (bootstrap, maximum likelihood)
- **Download:** GitHub (raxml-ng is the modern version)

### iTOL (Interactive Tree of Life)
- **Type:** Web-based
- **Cost:** Free for academic use
- **Best for:** Tree visualization and annotation
- **Features:** Multiple tree layouts, publication-quality figures, interactive
- **Use in:** All tree-based lessons, especially 13-16, 28-30
- **Link:** itol.embl.de
- **Tutorial:** Built-in help and examples on website

### Nextstrain
- **Type:** Web-based, real-time phylogenetic tracking
- **Cost:** Free
- **Best for:** Viral evolution and epidemiology applications
- **Features:** Real-time SARS-CoV-2, influenza, Ebola tracking
- **Use in:** Lessons 25, 30 (viral outbreak tracking)
- **Link:** nextstrain.org

## Scientific Literature (Entry Points)

### Key Review Papers
- **Felsenstein (2004)** "Inferring Phylogenies" — comprehensive textbook, technical
- **Bromham & Penny (2003)** "The modern molecular clock" *Nature Reviews Genetics*
- **Yang & Rannala (2012)** "Molecular phylogenetics: principles and practice" *Nature Reviews Genetics*
- **Nascimento et al. (2017)** "A biologist's guide to Bayesian phylogenetic analysis" *Nature Ecology & Evolution*

### Tree Thinking
- **Baum et al. (2005)** "The Tree-Thinking Challenge" *Science*
- Addresses common misconceptions about reading phylogenetic trees
- **Best for:** Lesson 16

### Applications Papers (Examples for Real-World Lessons)
- **Viral evolution:** Nextstrain publications on SARS-CoV-2 evolution
- **Conservation:** Faith (1992) "Conservation evaluation and phylogenetic diversity" *Biological Conservation*
- **Speciation:** Seehausen (2006) "African cichlid fish: a model system in adaptive radiation research" *Proc. R. Soc. B*

## Datasets for Practice

### Beginner-Friendly
- **Primate cytochrome b sequences** — small, well-studied, clear relationships
  - Available: GenBank, included in MEGA example files
  - **Use in:** Lessons 18-20, 22

- **Great ape phylogeny** — human, chimp, gorilla, orangutan
  - Clear relationships, good for tree reading practice
  - **Use in:** Lessons 13-16

### Intermediate
- **Influenza H3N2 hemagglutinin sequences** — temporal data, measurably evolving
  - Available: Nextstrain, NCBI Influenza Database
  - **Use in:** Lessons 24-25, 26-27, 30

- **SARS-CoV-2 sequences** — real-time pandemic data
  - Available: GISAID, Nextstrain
  - **Use in:** Lessons 25, 30

### Advanced (Student's Choice)
- Encourage students to find datasets in their domain of interest:
  - Conservation: endangered species for phylogenetic diversity
  - Ecology: community phylogenetics
  - Medicine: pathogen evolution, cancer phylogenies
  - **Use in:** Lessons 29-31

## Notes on Resource Quality

- **Futuyma & Kirkpatrick** is the gold standard for evolution theory
- **Bromham** is excellent for bridging biology and computation
- **BEAST tutorials** are exceptionally well-written and pedagogically sound
- **Khan Academy** is best for absolute beginners; use for foundational concepts only
- **MIT OCW** materials assume stronger math/CS background than other resources
- **Nextstrain** is the best real-world phylogenetics application to show students

## Accessibility

All software listed is free and open-source except where noted. Most textbooks are available through university libraries. Open educational resources (Yale, MIT OCW, Khan Academy, Berkeley Understanding Evolution) are completely free and accessible.
