# Mycology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **OpenStax Microbiology** — Free comprehensive textbook with strong mycology coverage including bacteriology, virology, parasitology context. Excellent for intermediate students with clinical correlations. [Access through OpenStax library resources]

- **Introductory Mycology (Alexopoulos, Mims, Blackwell)** — Classic comprehensive mycology textbook covering all major groups, updated with molecular phylogenetics. Gold standard for university mycology courses.

- **The Fifth Kingdom (Bryce Kendrick)** — Readable introduction to fungal diversity with excellent illustrations and engaging writing. Good for students finding traditional texts too dry.

- **Fungi: Biology and Applications (Kevin Kavanagh)** — Strong applied mycology focus covering biotechnology, medical, and industrial applications. Perfect for students interested in practical applications.

### University Course Materials

- **UT Austin BIO341 Mycology** (http://www.sbs.utexas.edu/mycology/bio341/bio341_topic_01.htm) — Comprehensive online lecture series covering fungal classification, structure, reproduction. Excellent diagrams and organization for self-paced learning.

- **Utah Valley University Mycology Course** (https://gzahn.github.io/mycology/) — Modern web-based mycology resource with interactive elements, current taxonomy, good visual content.

- **Colorado State BZ333 Introductory Mycology** — Course syllabus and materials covering classification, major phyla (Ascomycota, Basidiomycota), mycorrhizae. [PDF syllabi available through university sites]

- **Cornell Mycology Teaching Resources** (http://mycology.cornell.edu/fteach.html) — Teaching materials, illustrated guides, fungal identification resources, and educational content from leading mycology program.

### Online References

- **MycoBank** (https://www.mycobank.org/) — Fungal nomenclature database, taxonomic information, and species descriptions. Essential for current taxonomy.

- **Index Fungorum** (http://www.indexfungorum.org/) — Comprehensive database of fungal names and taxonomic changes. Useful for tracking nomenclatural updates.

- **Mushroom Observer** (https://mushroomobserver.org/) — Community science platform with fungal observations, photos, and identifications. Great for seeing diversity in the wild.

## Interactive Tools & Simulations

### Virtual Labs

- **Labster Fungi Labs** (https://www.labster.com/blog/fungi-labs) — Seven virtual lab experiments including fungal identification, microscopy, biotechnology applications, and pathogen diagnostics. Excellent for students without access to physical labs.

### Learning Platforms

- **EasyBiologyClass Fungi Resources** (https://www.easybiologyclass.com/fungi-mycology-online-tutorials-lecture-notes-study-materials/) — PowerPoint presentations, video lectures, online tutorials, and practice materials. Good for review and visual learning.

### Identification Tools

- **Mycologue** — Computerized identification keys for fungi using morphological characteristics. [Available through mycology education sites]

- **Mycology Online** — Searchable illustrated guide to fungal pathogens of humans with detailed photos and descriptions.

- **iNaturalist** (https://www.inaturalist.org/) — Community identification of fungal observations with AI suggestions and expert verification. Great for field identification practice.

## Code & Data Resources

### Databases

- **UNITE** (https://unite.ut.ee/) — Molecular database for fungal ITS sequences, enabling DNA-based identification. Essential for molecular mycology.

- **FungiDB** (https://fungidb.org/) — Genomics resource for fungal genomes, gene annotations, and comparative genomics. For advanced students interested in molecular aspects.

### Phylogenetic Resources

- **Tree of Life Web Project - Fungi** (http://tolweb.org/Fungi/) — Phylogenetic relationships, evolutionary context, and branch descriptions.

- **MycoCosm** (https://mycocosm.jgi.doe.gov/) — Fungal genomics portal with hundreds of sequenced genomes, comparative tools, and metabolic pathway reconstructions.

## Repositories & Field Guides

- **Mushroom Collecting and Identification** (r/mycology subreddit) — Active community for identification help, foraging tips, and mycology discussion. Use critically with expert verification.

- **National Audubon Society Field Guide to Mushrooms** — Comprehensive North American mushroom field guide with photos and descriptions. Essential for field work.

- **All That the Rain Promises and More (David Arora)** — Pocket field guide with personality, covering Western North American fungi. Accessible and entertaining.

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Computer Science & Network Theory

- **Mycelial networks as distributed computing** — Fungal networks solve optimization problems similar to algorithms (shortest path, load balancing). Research using slime molds to recreate Tokyo rail system.
- *Relevant papers:* Nakagaki et al. (2000) "Intelligence: Maze-solving by an amoeboid organism" in Nature
- Connection to lessons 4, 21, 22 on mycelial structure and networks

### Philosophy & Cognition

- **Fungal intelligence and consciousness** — What constitutes intelligence without a brain? Decision-making in fungi challenges assumptions about cognition.
- *Books:* *Entangled Life* (Merlin Sheldrake), *The Web of Meaning* (Jeremy Lent)
- Connection to lessons 21-22 on fungal ecology and decision-making

### Architecture & Design

- **Mycelium-based materials** — Growing building materials from fungal mycelium. Companies like Ecovative using fungi for packaging, leather alternatives, construction.
- *Resources:* Ecovative Design (ecovativedesign.com), MycoWorks (mycoworks.com)
- Connection to lessons 30-31 on biotechnology applications

### Art & Culture

- **Fungal art and bioart** — Artists growing sculptures from fungi, using spores as printing material, exploring fungal aesthetics.
- *Artists:* Jae Rhim Lee (Infinity Burial Project), Carsten Höller (mushroom installations), Philip Ross (mycelium furniture)
- Connection throughout curriculum as aesthetic engagement point

### Economics & Food Systems

- **Fungal economics** — Truffle markets, mushroom cultivation industry, mycological services valuation. Ecosystem services provided by mycorrhizal fungi worth billions.
- *Resources:* "Economics of Mushroom Production" studies, truffle market analyses
- Connection to lessons 14, 27-28, 30 on applied mycology

### Climate Science

- **Fungi and carbon cycling** — Mycorrhizal fungi storing significant atmospheric carbon underground. Climate change impacts on fungal communities affecting carbon sequestration.
- *Relevant papers:* Averill et al. (2014) on mycorrhizal fungi and soil carbon, Field et al. on fungal responses to climate change
- Connection to lessons 21-22, 25 on decomposition and ecological roles

### Music & Sound

- **Fungal bioelectric signals converted to music** — Projects translating fungal electrical activity into sound. "Mushroom concerts" exploring fungal communication.
- *Artists:* Tarun Nayar, MycoLyco project
- Engagement hook for students interested in music/sound

### Medicine & Neuroscience

- **Psychedelics and mental health** — Psilocybin research for depression, PTSD, addiction treatment. Renaissance in psychedelic-assisted therapy.
- *Research centers:* Johns Hopkins Center for Psychedelic and Consciousness Research, Imperial College London
- Connection to lesson 28, handle with appropriate context and ethics discussion

### Materials Science

- **Fungal enzymes in industry** — Laccases for bioremediation, lignin-degrading enzymes for biofuel production, cellulases for textile processing.
- *Applications:* Paper industry, textile industry, wastewater treatment
- Connection to lessons 3, 25, 30-31 on unique enzymes and applications

## Cautions & Critical Evaluation

**Commercial claims:** Supplement industry makes many unverified claims about medicinal mushrooms. Teach students to evaluate evidence critically.

**Foraging safety:** NEVER consume wild mushrooms without expert verification. Deadly species exist and field guides are insufficient for beginners.

**Popular science vs. research:** Books like *Fantastic Fungi* are engaging but sometimes overstate fungal "intelligence" or consciousness. Balance with peer-reviewed sources.

**Taxonomic instability:** Fungal taxonomy changes rapidly with molecular data. Check current names in MycoBank/Index Fungorum.

**Cultural appropriation:** Indigenous mycological knowledge should be credited and respected, not appropriated without attribution.

## Accessibility Notes

- **Color blindness:** Many fungal identification guides rely on color. Provide shape/texture alternatives.
- **Field work limitations:** Virtual alternatives (Labster, photo galleries) for students unable to access field sites.
- **Microscopy access:** Digital microscopy images widely available when physical microscopes unavailable.
- **Text alternatives:** Video captions, audio descriptions for visual content when possible.
