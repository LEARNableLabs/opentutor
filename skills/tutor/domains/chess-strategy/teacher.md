# Chess strategy — from openings to endgame theory — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 9 lessons (33%)
- **Socratic questions** — 7 lessons (26%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 2 lessons (7%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (4%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 41% accessible (1-2), 41% standard (3), 19% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 10: "How do you create a plan from your pawn structure?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 16: "How do you calculate forcing sequences without missing defensive resources?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 18: "Can you sacrifice material for purely positional advantages?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 23: "How do you win or draw the Lucena and Philidor positions?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 26: "Can you combine opening preparation, middlegame strategy, and endgame knowledge in one complete game?" (difficulty 5)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers chess strategy — from openings to endgame theory, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: center control, piece mobility, development principles, development tempo, piece coordination, useful squares, opening to middlegame transition, strategic themes, typical plans, open games, closed games, opening repertoire building, king safety, timing in chess, center tension (and 63 more).
