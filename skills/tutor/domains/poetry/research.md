# Poetry — Forms, Meter, and Close Reading — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Prosody & Scansion
- Metrical feet (iamb, trochee, anapest, dactyl, spondee, pyrrhic)
- Scansion notation and practice
- Stress patterns and syllable emphasis
- Relationship between natural speech and metrical verse

### 2. Poetic Forms
- Closed/fixed forms: sonnet, villanelle, sestina, pantoum, terza rima, ghazal, haiku
- Open forms and free verse
- Rhyme schemes and stanza patterns
- Form as meaning-making device

### 3. Close Reading Methodology
- Breaking down poems to understand craft decisions
- Analyzing speaker, dramatic situation, imagery
- Examining metaphor, irony, figures of speech
- Diction, syntax, and word choice analysis
- Sound devices (alliteration, assonance, consonance)
- Line breaks and enjambment

### 4. Historical Evolution
- Classical prosody to contemporary forms
- Relationship between form and cultural/historical context
- Evolution from strict meter to free verse

## Learning Sequence Insights

From syllabi analysis:
- Start with **listening and natural rhythm** before notation
- Progress from **simple meters** (iambic) to complex (mixed/variable)
- Introduce **form** after meter foundation is solid
- Use **close reading throughout** — not just at the end
- **Create alongside analysis** — writing reinforces understanding
- Schedule **regular review** of terminology and skills
- Connect **form to meaning** — why poets choose constraints
