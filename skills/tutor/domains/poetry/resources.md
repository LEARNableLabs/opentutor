# Poetry — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Interactive Learning Tools

- **For Better For Verse** (https://prosody.lib.virginia.edu/instructions/)
  - Interactive scansion trainer with self-validation
  - Best for: deliberate practice in lessons 3-7
  - Intermediate-friendly; immediate feedback on stress and foot marking

- **Versecraft** (https://versecraft.app/)
  - Interactive timeline of verse evolution, meter identification quizzes, syllable-level reading practice
  - Best for: lessons 1-8, especially meter identification practice
  - Gamified progression from beginner to expert

- **Poem Analysis Scansion Tool** (https://poemanalysis.com/poetry-plus/scansion-tool/)
  - Automated scansion with interactive refinement
  - Best for: self-checking in lessons 4-8
  - Shows multiple interpretations; good for discussing scansion as interpretive

- **Poem Analysis Study Tools** (https://poemanalysis.com/poetry-plus/study-tools/)
  - Suite of poetry analysis resources
  - Best for: synthesis lessons 20-24

### Course Materials & Textbooks

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Reading Poetry (21L-004)** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21l-004-reading-poetry-spring-2018/pages/syllabus/)
  - Full syllabus with close reading emphasis
  - Intermediate level; excellent model for integrated analysis
  - Use for: lesson structure reference, sample close readings

- **Open Oregon: Intro to Poetry** (https://openoregon.pressbooks.pub/bockovenpoetry/chapter/chapter-8-poetic-form-open-and-closed/)
  - Open textbook chapter on closed vs. open form
  - Best for: lessons 9-14 (forms)
  - Clear explanations of sonnet, villanelle, free verse

- **Writers.com: Crafting Poems in Form** (https://writers.com/course/crafting-poems-in-form-rhyme-meter-fixed-forms-and-more)
  - Workshop-style course on fixed forms (rhyme, meter, sonnet, villanelle, sestina, ghazal, haiku, terza rima)
  - Best for: students who want to write alongside analysis
  - Use in lessons 9-14 for form deep-dives

### Academic Guides

- **Poetry at Harvard: Guide to Prosody** (https://poetry.harvard.edu/guide-prosody)
  - Authoritative outline of prosody basics and notation systems
  - Best for: lessons 1-8 (meter and scansion)
  - Intermediate-appropriate; clear terminology

- **The English Major's Prosody Cheat Sheet** (https://jpellegrino.com/cheatsheets/TheEnglishMajorsProsodyCheatSheet-6x9.pdf)
  - Quick reference for metrical feet, scansion symbols, common meters
  - Best for: keeping open during lessons 3-8
  - PDF download; print or keep in tab

- **Washington State University: Tips for Scanning Poetry** (https://public.archive.wsu.edu/campbelld/public_html/amlit/prostips.htm)
  - Practical advice for scansion practice
  - Best for: lesson 2-4 (learning to hear and mark stress)
  - Emphasizes reading aloud and tapping rhythm

## People to Explore

- **Derek Attridge** — Prosody theorist; *The Rhythms of English Poetry*, *Poetic Rhythm: An Introduction*
- **Paul Fussell** — *Poetic Meter and Poetic Form*; classic intermediate-level text
- **Robert Pinsky** — Poet and prosody advocate; *The Sounds of Poetry: A Brief Guide*
- **Mary Oliver** — *A Poetry Handbook*; accessible craft guide
- **Don Paterson** — *The Poem*; contemporary British poet on close reading
- **Helen Vendler** — Close reading critic; *Poems, Poets, Poetry*
- **Brooks & Warren** — *Understanding Poetry* (New Critical approach; historical but influential)

## Unexpected Connections

- **Rap and hip-hop prosody** — Contemporary evolution of metrical practice; internal rhyme, complex rhythms. See Adam Bradley's *Book of Rhymes*.
- **Song lyrics as poetry** — Bob Dylan's Nobel Prize; when does a song become a poem? Boundary questions.
- **Digital/code poetry** — Computational generation, hypertext poetry, Twitter bots. Contemporary formal constraints.
- **Concrete/visual poetry** — When typography becomes form (E.E. Cummings, George Herbert's pattern poems).
- **Translation studies** — What gets lost/gained when translating metered verse across languages?
- **Oulipo constraints** — Mathematical/algorithmic poetry (N+7, snowball, lipogram). Extreme formal constraint as generative practice.
- **Neuroscience of rhythm** — Why humans entrain to beat; pattern recognition in poetry. Oliver Sacks' *Musicophilia*.
- **Disability poetics** — How d/Deaf poets work with sound; visual prosody. See work of John Lee Clark, Ilya Kaminsky.
