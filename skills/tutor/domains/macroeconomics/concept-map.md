# Macroeconomics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **GDP and national income accounting** — measuring aggregate economic activity
2. **Real vs. nominal variables** — adjusting for price changes. Depends on: GDP
3. **Unemployment rate** — measuring labor market slack
4. **Economic growth** — long-run increases in output per capita
5. **Capital accumulation** — building productive capacity. Depends on: growth
6. **Savings rate** — share of income saved for investment. Depends on: capital accumulation
7. **Technological progress** — increases in total factor productivity. Depends on: growth, capital accumulation
8. **Endogenous growth** — self-sustaining innovation. Depends on: technological progress
9. **Business cycles** — short-run output fluctuations
10. **Consumption function** — relationship between income and spending. Depends on: business cycles
11. **Investment volatility** — fluctuations in capital spending. Depends on: business cycles
12. **Multiplier effect** — amplification of demand shocks. Depends on: consumption, investment
13. **Aggregate supply** — economy's production at different price levels. Depends on: business cycles
14. **Price stickiness** — slow adjustment of prices and wages. Depends on: aggregate supply
15. **Natural rate of unemployment** — unemployment in long-run equilibrium. Depends on: unemployment rate
16. **Job search and matching** — process of workers finding jobs. Depends on: natural unemployment
17. **Efficiency wages** — wages above market-clearing. Depends on: unemployment
18. **Money and money supply** — medium of exchange and store of value
19. **Inflation** — sustained increase in price level. Depends on: money, real vs. nominal
20. **Quantity theory of money** — relationship between money growth and inflation. Depends on: money, inflation
21. **Phillips curve** — inflation-unemployment trade-off. Depends on: inflation, unemployment
22. **Monetary policy** — central bank control of interest rates. Depends on: inflation, money supply
23. **Fiscal policy** — government spending and taxation for stabilization
24. **Government debt** — accumulated deficits. Depends on: fiscal policy
25. **Ricardian equivalence** — debt neutrality hypothesis. Depends on: fiscal policy, debt
26. **Exchange rates** — relative price of currencies
27. **Exchange rate regimes** — fixed vs. floating systems. Depends on: exchange rates
28. **Capital flows** — international investment movements. Depends on: exchange rates
29. **Balance of payments** — record of international transactions. Depends on: capital flows, exchange rates
30. **Financial crises** — breakdown of credit intermediation. Depends on: capital flows, monetary policy

## Dependencies

### Critical Paths

**Growth theory path:**
- GDP → Real variables → Growth → Capital accumulation → Savings → Technological progress → Endogenous growth
- This builds the foundation for understanding long-run prosperity differences across countries

**Business cycle path:**
- GDP → Cycles → Consumption → Investment → Multiplier → Aggregate supply → Price stickiness
- This explains short-run fluctuations and why economies deviate from potential output

**Policy path:**
- Money → Inflation → Phillips curve → Monetary policy
- Fiscal policy → Government debt → Ricardian equivalence
- These converge in understanding stabilization policy trade-offs

**Open economy path:**
- Exchange rates → Regimes → Capital flows → Balance of payments
- This extends closed-economy models to international linkages

### Key Dependencies

- **Aggregate supply requires understanding business cycles** — you can't grasp why AS slopes upward without knowing why we care about short-run fluctuations
- **Phillips curve requires both inflation and unemployment concepts** — it's the nexus of these two key variables
- **Monetary policy effectiveness requires price stickiness** — without sticky prices, money is neutral even in the short run
- **Open economy analysis requires understanding closed economy first** — Mundell-Fleming builds on IS-LM logic
- **Financial crises connect everything** — they involve credit (monetary), fiscal response, international flows, and real effects on output and unemployment

## Bottlenecks

### Conceptual Bottlenecks

1. **Real vs. nominal distinction** — students who don't internalize this will struggle with inflation, growth accounting, and real exchange rates
2. **Short-run vs. long-run** — the fundamental organizing principle. Growth is long-run, cycles are short-run, policy bridges both
3. **Stock vs. flow** — capital is a stock, investment is a flow; debt is a stock, deficit is a flow. Confusion here derails understanding
4. **Expectations** — rational expectations revolutionized macro but are hard to grasp. Critical for Phillips curve, consumption smoothing, Ricardian equivalence

### Technical Bottlenecks

1. **Solow model steady state** — the algebra can obscure the economics. Need to emphasize the intuition: diminishing returns → convergence
2. **Multiplier algebra** — 1/(1-MPC) looks simple but requires understanding circular flow logic
3. **IS-LM framework** — if using it, students often memorize curve shifts without understanding equilibrium concept

## Common Misconceptions

### Measurement
- **"GDP measures welfare"** — it doesn't capture inequality, environmental degradation, home production, leisure
- **"Unemployment rate = % of population unemployed"** — it's % of labor force, which excludes discouraged workers

### Growth
- **"Saving more always increases welfare"** — golden rule shows you can over-save
- **"Technology is exogenous"** — endogenous growth shows innovation responds to incentives
- **"Poor countries will automatically catch up"** — convergence requires similar institutions and technology access

### Cycles
- **"Recessions are caused by supply shocks"** — most modern recessions are demand-driven
- **"The multiplier is infinite"** — it converges because MPC < 1
- **"Prices adjust instantly"** — empirically, prices and wages are sticky

### Policy
- **"Inflation is always bad"** — moderate inflation greases the wheels (downward wage rigidity)
- **"Government debt is like household debt"** — governments can roll over debt indefinitely, print money, have much longer horizons
- **"Monetary policy is ineffective at the zero lower bound"** — unconventional policy (QE, forward guidance) can still work
- **"Fiscal stimulus always causes inflation"** — depends on output gap and monetary policy response

### Open Economy
- **"Trade deficits are bad"** — they're often the flip side of capital inflows financing investment
- **"Fixed exchange rates eliminate volatility"** — they shift volatility to reserves and output
- **"Devaluation always improves trade balance"** — J-curve dynamics mean short-run worsening

## Learning Sequence Logic

The curriculum follows this arc:

1. **Measure first** (lessons 1-3) — establish the variables we care about (GDP, unemployment, inflation)
2. **Long run, then short run** (lessons 4-17) — growth theory gives the benchmark, then cycles show deviations
3. **Mechanisms before policy** (lessons 10-17) — understand consumption, investment, labor markets before studying how to manipulate them
4. **Money after real economy** (lessons 19-22) — classical dichotomy: real variables determined first, then nominal
5. **Closed economy before open** (lessons 1-26 then 27-30) — master domestic mechanics before adding international linkages
6. **Historical capstone** (lesson 30) — 2008 crisis integrates finance, policy, and international dimensions

This sequencing respects conceptual dependencies while building intuition progressively.
