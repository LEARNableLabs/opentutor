# Macroeconomics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Mankiw, N. Gregory. *Macroeconomics* (9th ed.)** — Gold standard intermediate text. Clear exposition, extensive policy applications, US-focused. Best for traditional IS-LM approach.
  - https://www.macmillanihe.com/page/detail/Macroeconomics/?k=9781319106058

- **Blanchard, Olivier and David R. Johnson. *Macroeconomics* (7th ed.)** — Alternative to Mankiw, stronger on European perspective and open economy. More emphasis on dynamics.
  - https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/macroeconomics/P200000005835

- **Jones, Charles I. *Macroeconomics* (4th ed.)** — Modern treatment emphasizing growth and long-run. Light on traditional IS-LM, strong on microfoundations.
  - https://wwnorton.com/books/9780393923902

- **Acemoglu, Daron, David Laibson, and John List. *Economics* (2nd ed.)** — Recent text integrating behavioral insights, strong on empirics and causality.
  - https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/economics/P200000005925

- **CORE Team. *The Economy*** — Open-access textbook with interactive elements, empirical focus, heterodox perspectives. Excellent for engaged learners who want alternatives to mainstream views.
  - https://www.core-econ.org/the-economy/

### Open Courseware

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 14.05 Intermediate Macroeconomics** — Full course materials (lecture notes, problem sets, exams) from Ricardo Caballero. Rigorous but accessible.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-05-intermediate-macroeconomics-spring-2013/

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 14.06 Macroeconomic Theory IV** — Advanced undergraduate/early graduate. For students who want to go deeper.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-06-intermediate-macroeconomics-spring-2004/

- **Stanford Online Economics Courses** — Various macro courses with video lectures.
  - https://online.stanford.edu/

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Video Lectures and Series

- **Khan Academy Macroeconomics** — Complete sequence from basics through intermediate topics. Excellent for visual learners; Sal Khan's explanations are patient and clear.
  - https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/macroeconomics

- **Marginal Revolution University (MRU)** — Tyler Cowen and Alex Tabarrok's video courses. Principles, development, and macro topics with high production value.
  - https://mru.org/

- **Federal Reserve Educational Videos** — Short, high-quality explainers on monetary policy, inflation, financial crises.
  - https://www.federalreserveeducation.org/resources/video

- **EconplusDal** — YouTube channel with whiteboard-style macro lessons, exam prep.
  - https://www.youtube.com/c/EconplusDal

- **Economic Synopses (St. Louis Fed)** — Short video explainers on current economic topics.
  - https://research.stlouisfed.org/publications/economic-synopses

### Interactive Tools and Simulations

- **FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data)** — The essential tool. 800,000+ time series, graphing, data downloads. Students should explore freely.
  - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/

- **Trading Economics** — Real-time macro indicators, forecasts, historical data for 196 countries.
  - https://tradingeconomics.com/

- **IMF Data Mapper** — Interactive visualizations of global economic data.
  - https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/

- **OECD Data** — Comparative statistics across OECD countries.
  - https://data.oecd.org/

- **World Bank Open Data** — Development indicators, poverty, inequality.
  - https://data.worldbank.org/

- **Penn World Table** — Cross-country GDP, productivity, growth data going back to 1950.
  - https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/productivity/pwt/

- **Our World in Data — Economic Growth** — Beautiful long-run visualizations of growth, productivity, living standards.
  - https://ourworldindata.org/economic-growth

- **Gapminder** — Animated visualizations of development indicators over time (Hans Rosling's legacy).
  - https://www.gapminder.org/

- **ECONlib Macroeconomics** — Interactive graphs and simulations for teaching.
  - https://www.econlib.org/

### Code and Notebooks

- **QuantEcon** — Open-source code library for economic modeling in Python and Julia. Includes notebooks on growth, dynamic programming.
  - https://quantecon.org/

- **FRED API and Python** — Programmatic access to FRED data via Python (fredapi package).
  - https://github.com/mortada/fredapi

- **Macroeconomic Models in Python** — GitHub repo with implementations of Solow, RBC, New Keynesian DSGE.
  - https://github.com/jesusfv/econ-models

- **Notebook Economics** — Jupyter notebooks for teaching macro concepts.
  - https://github.com/rstudio/teaching-economics

## People (to look up)

### Foundational Figures
- **John Maynard Keynes** — *The General Theory*; founding father of modern macroeconomics
- **Milton Friedman** — Monetarism, permanent income hypothesis, natural rate of unemployment
- **Robert Solow** — Solow growth model (Nobel 1987)
- **Paul Samuelson** — Neoclassical synthesis, first American econ Nobel (1970)
- **Robert Lucas** — Rational expectations revolution, RBC theory (Nobel 1995)

### Contemporary Leading Macroeconomists
- **Olivier Blanchard** — Former IMF chief economist, influential textbook author, public debt researcher
- **Ben Bernanke** — Former Fed chair, financial crisis researcher (Nobel 2022)
- **Janet Yellen** — Former Fed chair and Treasury Secretary, labor economist
- **Larry Summers** — Former Treasury Secretary, secular stagnation hypothesis
- **Christina Romer** — Economic historian, Great Depression and fiscal policy expert
- **Paul Krugman** — International trade and macro, New Keynesian models (Nobel 2008)
- **Kenneth Rogoff** — Exchange rates, sovereign debt, *This Time is Different*
- **Carmen Reinhart** — Financial crises, debt, co-author with Rogoff
- **Stephanie Kelton** — Leading MMT proponent, deficit owl
- **Daron Acemoglu** — Institutions and growth, technical change (Nobel 2024 for institutions)
- **Esther Duflo** — Development, RCTs (Nobel 2019) — connects micro to macro
- **Thomas Piketty** — Inequality, *Capital in the Twenty-First Century*

### Central Bankers and Policymakers
- **Jerome Powell** — Current Fed chair
- **Christine Lagarde** — ECB president
- **Jay Powell, Janet Yellen, Ben Bernanke, Alan Greenspan, Paul Volcker** — Fed chairs across eras

## Unexpected Connections (wild cards)

### Macroeconomics and Climate
- **Stern Review on the Economics of Climate Change** — Discount rates, intergenerational equity, growth and sustainability
  - https://www.lse.ac.uk/granthaminstitute/publication/the-economics-of-climate-change-the-stern-review/

- **Nordhaus Integrated Assessment Models** — DICE/RICE models linking climate and economy (Nobel 2018)
  - https://williamnordhaus.com/

### Macro and Psychology
- **Behavioral Macroeconomics** — Animal spirits, over-optimism, narrative economics (Shiller, Akerlof)
  - Shiller's *Narrative Economics*: https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691182292/narrative-economics

### Macro and History
- **Economic History** — Great Depression, hyperinflations, financial crises offer natural experiments
  - Eichengreen, *Hall of Mirrors* (Great Depression vs. Great Recession)
  - Reinhart & Rogoff, *This Time is Different* (800 years of financial crises)

### Macro and Complexity Science
- **Agent-based macro models** — Heterogeneous agents, networks, emergence
  - Santa Fe Institute economics: https://www.santafe.edu/research/economics

### Macro and Demographics
- **Aging and secular stagnation** — Declining population growth → slower demand, investment
  - Goodhart & Pradhan, *The Great Demographic Reversal*

### Macro and Technology
- **Automation and inequality** — AI, robots, labor share, future of work
  - Acemoglu & Restrepo on automation and tasks
  - Brynjolfsson & McAfee, *The Second Machine Age*

### Macro and Institutions
- **Why Nations Fail** (Acemoglu & Robinson) — Institutions determine long-run prosperity
  - https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/209838/why-nations-fail-by-daron-acemoglu-and-james-a-robinson/

### Macro and Finance
- **Asset pricing and macro** — Equity premium puzzle, consumption-based CAPM
  - Campbell, *Financial Decisions and Markets*
