# Optical Illusions — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Vision Science: Photons to Phenomenology" by Stephen Palmer** (MIT Press, 1999)
  - Comprehensive, research-level treatment of visual perception
  - Excellent chapters on Gestalt principles, color, motion, depth
  - Ideal for intermediate students wanting depth without full neuroscience background
  - https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262161831/vision-science/

- **"Eye and Brain: The Psychology of Seeing" by Richard L. Gregory** (5th ed., Princeton, 1997)
  - Classic introduction, highly readable
  - Emphasizes perception as hypothesis-testing
  - Strong on illusions, ambiguous figures, impossible objects
  - Good for historical context and intuition-building
  - https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691048376/eye-and-brain

- **"Sensation and Perception" by Wolfe, Kluender, Levi, et al.** (6th ed., Sinauer, 2021)
  - Standard undergraduate textbook, well-illustrated
  - Balances physiology, psychophysics, and real-world applications
  - Each chapter has "illusion" sidebars with explanations
  - https://academic.oup.com/book/43877

- **"Perception" by Robert Sekuler & Randolph Blake** (5th ed., McGraw-Hill, 2005)
  - More advanced than Wolfe, closer to graduate level
  - Strong on neural mechanisms, single-cell recordings, brain imaging
  - Detailed treatment of motion (MT/V5), color (V4), and form (ventral stream)
  - https://www.mheducation.com/

- **"Purves Neuroscience" edited by Dale Purves et al.** (6th ed., Oxford, 2017)
  - Chapter 12 (Central Visual Pathways) and Chapter 24 (Vision) are essential
  - Covers retinal circuitry, LGN, V1 organization, higher visual areas
  - Authoritative, medical-school level
  - https://global.oup.com/ushe/product/neuroscience-9781605353807

### Academic Papers & Reviews

- **Purves, D., & Lotto, R. B. (2011). "Why We See What We Do Redux: A Wholly Empirical Theory of Vision." Sinauer.**
  - Empirical approach to illusions: perception reflects natural scene statistics
  - Challenges depth-cue theories (Gregory)
  - Covers Müller-Lyer, Ebbinghaus, color/lightness constancy
  - https://global.oup.com/ushe/product/why-we-see-what-we-do-redux-9780878936595

- **Ramachandran, V. S., & Gregory, R. L. (1991). "Perceptual filling in of artificially induced scotomas in human vision." Nature, 350(6320), 699-702.**
  - Classic paper on perceptual completion
  - Shows brain fills in missing regions (blind spot, scotomas)
  - https://www.nature.com/articles/350699a0

- **Kitaoka, A., & Ashida, H. (2003). "Phenomenal characteristics of the peripheral drift illusion." VISION, 15, 261-262.**
  - Original paper on rotating snakes and peripheral drift
  - Mechanism: motion energy from luminance gradients + eye movements
  - https://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/~akitaoka/

- **Friston, K. (2010). "The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?" Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 11(2), 127-138.**
  - Predictive coding framework
  - Perception as Bayesian inference minimizing prediction error
  - Dense but foundational for modern theories
  - https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2787

- **Adelson, E. H. (2000). "Lightness perception and lightness illusions." In M. Gazzaniga (Ed.), The New Cognitive Neurosciences (2nd ed., pp. 339-351). MIT Press.**
  - Definitive treatment of lightness constancy and checkerboard shadow illusion
  - http://web.mit.edu/persci/people/adelson/

- **Geisler, W. S., Perry, J. S., Super, B. J., & Gallogly, D. P. (2001). "Edge co-occurrence in natural images predicts contour grouping performance." Vision Research, 41(6), 711-724.**
  - Bayesian formalization of Gestalt continuity principle
  - Natural scene statistics predict grouping behavior
  - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0042698900002774

- **Lafer-Sousa, R., Hermann, K. L., & Conway, B. R. (2015). "Striking individual differences in color perception uncovered by 'the dress' photograph." Current Biology, 25(13), R545-R546.**
  - The Dress explained: individual differences in assumed illuminant
  - Ties to color constancy and prior assumptions
  - https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(15)00502-5

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Illusion Databases & Interactive Sites

- **Michael Bach's Optical Illusions & Visual Phenomena**
  - 100+ illusions with explanations, interactive demos
  - Organized by category (color, motion, size, Gestalt)
  - Gold standard for web-based illusions
  - https://michaelbach.de/ot/

- **Akiyoshi Kitaoka's Illusion Pages**
  - Novel motion and color illusions (rotating snakes, café wall, etc.)
  - Cutting-edge, many unpublished
  - High-resolution images for making posters/slides
  - https://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/~akitaoka/index-e.html

- **The Exploratorium's Illusions Gallery**
  - Hands-on museum exhibits adapted for web
  - Good for younger/general audiences
  - "Snacks" format: build-your-own instructions
  - https://www.exploratorium.edu/snacks/subject/vision

- **Optical Illusions & Visual Phenomena (Archimedes Lab)**
  - Curated collection, puzzle/game format
  - Good for warm-ups or breaks
  - https://www.archimedes-lab.org/illusions.html

- **Mighty Optical Illusions**
  - Daily illusion blog, user submissions
  - Mix of classic and novel, some explanations
  - https://www.moillusions.com/

### Interactive Tools & Demos

- **Wolfram Demonstrations Project**
  - Mathematica notebooks for illusions (Müller-Lyer, Hermann grid, motion)
  - Interactive parameter sliders
  - Free Wolfram Player to run
  - https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/ (search "optical illusions")

- **Paul Bourke's Illusion Gallery**
  - Ray-traced Ames rooms, impossible objects
  - 3D models and rendering code
  - https://paulbourke.net/

- **Shadertoy**
  - WebGL shaders for real-time illusion generation
  - Search for "optical illusion", "Moiré", "peripheral drift"
  - Code examples you can modify
  - https://www.shadertoy.com/

- **Three.js Examples**
  - WebGL 3D graphics library
  - Examples: Penrose triangle, impossible cube, forced perspective
  - https://threejs.org/examples/

- **Observable: "Optical Illusions Collection"**
  - JavaScript notebooks with D3.js visualizations
  - Editable, forkable, shareable
  - https://observablehq.com/@d3/gallery (search "illusion")

- **PhET Interactive Simulations: Color Vision**
  - University of Colorado Boulder
  - Interactive simulation of photoreceptors, color mixing
  - https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/color-vision

### Code & Experimental Tools

- **PsychoPy (Python)**
  - Open-source for building psychology/neuroscience experiments
  - Pre-built demos for Stroop, motion aftereffects, Gabor patches
  - Builder interface (GUI) + Coder (Python scripting)
  - https://psychopy.org/

- **jsPsych (JavaScript)**
  - Run experiments in the browser
  - Plugins for visual stimuli, reaction time, surveys
  - Good for online data collection
  - https://www.jspsych.org/

- **Psychtoolbox (MATLAB)**
  - Industry standard for vision research
  - Precise timing, calibration, low-level graphics
  - Steeper learning curve but powerful
  - http://psychtoolbox.org/

- **Processing / p5.js**
  - Creative coding for artists/designers
  - Easy to make animated illusions (moiré, peripheral drift)
  - p5.js runs in browser, great for sharing
  - https://processing.org/ and https://p5js.org/

- **GitHub: Illusion Code Repositories**
  - Search "optical illusions python" or "peripheral drift illusion"
  - Example: https://github.com/mathisonian/illusions (interactive JavaScript)
  - Example: https://github.com/akitaoka-lab/ (Kitaoka's illusion code)

### Books for Deeper Dives

- **"The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception" by James J. Gibson** (1979)
  - Contrarian view: perception is direct, not inferential
  - Challenges illusion-as-error framing
  - Useful counterpoint to Bayesian/predictive coding
  - https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9781315740218/ecological-approach-visual-perception-james-gibson

- **"Visual Intelligence: How We Create What We See" by Donald D. Hoffman** (1998)
  - Accessible pop-science, focuses on constructive nature of vision
  - Many illusions used as examples
  - https://wwnorton.com/books/9780393319675

- **"Phantoms in the Brain" by V.S. Ramachandran & Sandra Blakeslee** (1998)
  - Neurological case studies (phantom limbs, Capgras, neglect)
  - Illusions as windows into brain organization
  - https://www.harpercollins.com/products/phantoms-in-the-brain-v-s-ramachandrans-blakeslee

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

### Camouflage & Military Deception
- Dazzle camouflage (WWI ships) used high-contrast patterns to confuse motion perception
- Modern military camouflage exploits Gestalt grouping and texture segmentation
- Links to evolutionary arms races in nature (cuttlefish, octopus, katydid)

### Magic & Misdirection
- Magicians empirically discovered attention limits, change blindness, motion cueing
- Gustav Kuhn's research on magic and neuroscience
- "Sleights of Mind" by Macknik & Martinez-Conde

### Accessibility & Design for Low Vision
- Understanding contrast illusions improves text readability
- Motion sensitivity varies across retina — affects UI animation choices
- Color blindness and The Dress: designing for perceptual diversity

### Psychedelic Research & Consciousness
- Geometric hallucinations (form constants) from serotonergic psychedelics
- REBUS model (Carhart-Harris & Friston): psychedelics relax high-level priors
- Ties illusions to altered states, predictive coding, and consciousness studies

### Cross-Cultural Perception
- Müller-Lyer illusion weaker in non-carpentered environments (Segall et al., 1966)
- Debated whether perception is universal or shaped by experience
- Links to developmental vision science (critical periods, perceptual learning)

### Synesthesia
- Grapheme-color synesthesia: seeing numbers as colored
- Cross-activation between adjacent cortical areas (V4 and number area)
- Are we all mildly synesthetic? (McGurk effect as audio-visual cross-talk)

### Video Games & Virtual Reality
- VR sickness from visual-vestibular mismatch
- Game designers exploit depth cues, motion parallax, forced perspective (Mario 64)
- Impossible spaces in Portal, Monument Valley — playable Escher worlds
