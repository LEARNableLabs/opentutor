# Research Summary — Optical Illusions & Neuroscience

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Early Visual Processing
- **Retinal mechanisms** — lateral inhibition, center-surround receptive fields, adaptation
- **Primary visual cortex (V1)** — orientation selectivity, spatial frequency filtering, edge detection
- **Hermann grid, Mach bands** — classical lateral inhibition illusions

### 2. Gestalt Principles & Perceptual Organization
- **Grouping laws** — proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, common fate
- **Figure-ground segregation** — Rubin's vase, ambiguous figures
- **Perceptual completion** — Kanizsa triangle, amodal completion

### 3. Motion Perception
- **Apparent motion** — phi phenomenon, motion aftereffects (waterfall illusion)
- **Motion-induced blindness** — attentional suppression
- **Peripheral drift** — Kitaoka's rotating snakes
- **Biological motion** — point-light walkers

### 4. Depth & Size Perception
- **Monocular cues** — linear perspective, occlusion, texture gradient
- **Size constancy failures** — Ponzo, Müller-Lyer, Ebbinghaus illusions
- **Ames room** — distorted spaces exploiting perspective
- **Impossible objects** — Penrose triangle, Escher drawings

### 5. Color & Lightness
- **Color constancy** — The Dress, checkerboard shadow illusion
- **Simultaneous contrast** — context-dependent lightness
- **Chromatic adaptation** — afterimages, negative aftereffects
- **Luminance vs. perceived brightness** — Dale Purves' empirical approach

### 6. Top-Down Processing & Prediction
- **Predictive coding** — hierarchical Bayesian inference
- **Context effects** — ambiguous stimuli resolved by expectation
- **Attention modulation** — change blindness, inattentional blindness
- **Multisensory integration** — McGurk effect, rubber hand illusion

## Key Sources

### Academic Textbooks
- **"Vision Science" by Stephen Palmer** — comprehensive treatment of perception, Gestalt principles, illusions
- **"Eye and Brain" by Richard Gregory** — classic introduction, hypothesis-testing view of perception
- **"Sensation and Perception" by Wolfe et al.** — standard undergraduate text with excellent illusion coverage
- **"Perception" by Sekuler & Blake** — research-oriented, covers neural mechanisms in depth

### Research Papers & Reviews
- **Dale Purves et al.** — empirical approach to lightness, color, and geometrical illusions (Duke)
- **V.S. Ramachandran & Richard Gregory** — perceptual filling-in, ambiguous figures
- **Akiyoshi Kitaoka** — motion illusions, peripheral drift
- **Mark Changizi** — evolutionary explanations for illusions
- **Karl Friston** — predictive coding framework

### Online Collections
- **Michael Bach's illusion pages** — exhaustive catalog with neural explanations
- **Exploratorium exhibits** — hands-on demonstrations, educational context
- **Akiyoshi Kitaoka's website** — gallery of novel motion/color illusions
- **Psychophysics toolbox demos** — MATLAB code for classic illusions

## Recommended Learning Path (Intermediate Level)

1. **Start with low-level mechanisms** — retinal processing, receptive fields (concrete, measurable)
2. **Move to mid-level organization** — Gestalt, grouping, segmentation (computational principles)
3. **Explore domain-specific systems** — motion, depth, color (specialized pathways)
4. **Finish with high-level cognition** — prediction, attention, multisensory (abstract, still debated)

This progression moves from well-established physiology to cutting-edge theory, building confidence before entering contested territory.
