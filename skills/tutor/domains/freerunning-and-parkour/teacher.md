## Domain Hooks
- **Parkour and evolutionary biomechanics** — How modern parkour mirrors ancestral movement patterns (persistence hunting, arboreal locomotion). Drop this in during movement efficiency discussion. Connection to barefoot running mechanics and natural movement.

- **Parkour as controlled falling** — Frame parkour as continuous gravitational potential energy management. Every movement is a negotiation with gravity. Connects to orbital mechanics, roller coaster design, and cliff diving physics.

- **The cat-righting reflex and aerial awareness** — Biomechanics of how cats (and trained humans) reorient mid-air without external forces. Angular momentum conservation in freerunning flips. Connects to space station rotations and gymnastics.

- **Parkour in microgravity** — Thought experiment: how would parkour work on the Moon or Mars? Forces scale with gravity, but mass (inertia) doesn't. Reveals deep understanding of force vs mass. Connection to astronaut training.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Soft landings are always safer"** — Students conflate "soft" with "safe," but overly compliant landings increase joint travel and time under load. Reality: optimal landing uses controlled stiffness appropriate to drop height and terrain. Teach force-time curve manipulation instead of binary soft/hard thinking.

2. **"Rolling dissipates all the force"** — Rolls are incredibly effective but don't eliminate impact — they redistribute it across time and body surface area. Students need to understand that roll entry still requires proper landing mechanics. Bad landing → roll = injury. Good landing → roll = optimal.

3. **"More muscle = better parkour performance"** — Absolute strength has diminishing returns; relative strength (strength-to-weight ratio) and reactive strength matter more. Excessive muscle mass increases impact forces and energy cost. Emphasize functional strength and power-to-weight optimization.
