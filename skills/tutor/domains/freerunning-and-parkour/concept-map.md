# Freerunning and Parkour — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Force production and absorption** — how the body generates and manages forces during movement
2. **Ground reaction forces** — Newton's third law applied to parkour movements
3. **Center of mass** — the balance point that determines trajectory and stability
4. **Momentum and impulse** — how mass in motion transfers through movements and impacts
5. **Joint kinematics** — how joints move through ranges of motion. Depends on: force production
6. **Joint loading and torque** — rotational forces and stress on joints. Depends on: force production, joint kinematics
7. **Impact forces** — forces experienced during landings. Depends on: momentum, ground reaction forces
8. **Force-time curves** — temporal distribution of impact forces. Depends on: impact forces
9. **Energy dissipation** — spreading impact across time and body structures. Depends on: force-time curves, momentum
10. **Roll mechanics** — using rotation to distribute force. Depends on: energy dissipation, momentum
11. **Landing strategies** — stiff vs compliant landings. Depends on: impact forces, energy dissipation
12. **Eccentric muscle action** — lengthening under load to absorb force. Depends on: landing strategies, joint loading
13. **Kinetic chain** — sequential joint action working together. Depends on: joint kinematics, eccentric muscle action
14. **Vault biomechanics** — mechanics of clearing obstacles. Depends on: force production, momentum, center of mass
15. **Approach velocity and take-off angle** — optimizing vault entry. Depends on: vault biomechanics, momentum
16. **Upper body force transmission** — how arms transfer force during vaults. Depends on: force production, vault biomechanics
17. **Triple extension** — simultaneous ankle-knee-hip extension for power. Depends on: kinetic chain, force production
18. **Stretch-shortening cycle** — elastic energy storage and release. Depends on: eccentric muscle action, plyometric mechanics
19. **Power generation** — rate of force production. Depends on: force production, triple extension
20. **Force vectors** — direction and magnitude of applied forces. Depends on: force production, vault biomechanics
21. **Economy of motion** — minimizing energy waste. Depends on: movement efficiency, force production
22. **Transition mechanics** — maintaining flow between obstacles. Depends on: momentum, center of mass, economy of motion
23. **Proprioception** — body position awareness in space. Depends on: joint kinematics, spatial awareness
24. **Motor learning** — skill acquisition through practice. Depends on: proprioception, movement planning
25. **Strength requirements** — force production capacity needed for parkour. Depends on: force production, power generation
26. **Relative strength** — strength-to-bodyweight ratio. Depends on: strength requirements
27. **Reactive strength** — rapid eccentric-concentric coupling. Depends on: stretch-shortening cycle, eccentric muscle action
28. **Tissue adaptation** — how connective tissue responds to loading. Depends on: progressive loading, impact forces
29. **Injury mechanisms** — how injuries occur biomechanically. Depends on: joint loading, impact forces, landing strategies
30. **Progressive overload** — gradually increasing training stress. Depends on: tissue adaptation, training variables
31. **Periodization** — structured training phases. Depends on: progressive overload, skill acquisition
32. **Movement efficiency** — optimizing technique for performance. Depends on: economy of motion, biomechanical principles

## Dependencies

### Foundational Chain
- **Ground reaction forces** and **center of mass** are the foundation for understanding all parkour movements — every movement involves managing these two elements
- **Momentum and impulse** depends on understanding forces and mass, enabling analysis of impacts and transitions
- **Joint kinematics** and **joint loading** build on force concepts to understand how movements stress the body

### Landing Mechanics Chain
- **Impact forces** require understanding momentum and ground reaction forces
- **Energy dissipation** strategies (rolls, absorption) depend on understanding force-time relationships
- **Eccentric muscle action** is the primary biological mechanism for absorbing forces safely
- **Kinetic chain** integrates multiple joints working together to distribute force

### Vaulting and Power Chain
- **Vault biomechanics** combines force production, momentum management, and center of mass control
- **Triple extension** and **stretch-shortening cycle** are the mechanisms for explosive power
- **Upper body force transmission** requires understanding how force vectors work in vaulting contexts

### Efficiency and Flow Chain
- **Economy of motion** depends on understanding force production and movement mechanics
- **Transition mechanics** requires mastering momentum conservation and center of mass control
- **Proprioception** and **motor learning** enable adaptive, efficient movement

### Training and Adaptation Chain
- **Strength requirements** build on understanding force production and power generation
- **Tissue adaptation** requires progressive overload principles
- **Injury mechanisms** depend on understanding joint loading, impact forces, and landing mechanics
- **Periodization** integrates all concepts into structured long-term development

## Bottlenecks

Students often struggle with these pivotal concepts:

1. **Force-time curves** — abstract concept that unlocks understanding of both impact management and power generation
2. **Eccentric muscle action** — counterintuitive (muscles creating force while lengthening) but essential for landing safety
3. **Kinetic chain** — understanding sequential vs simultaneous joint action is critical for both power and safety
4. **Force vectors** — spatial reasoning about force direction is necessary for vault optimization
5. **Stretch-shortening cycle** — integrates elastic properties with muscle mechanics, essential for explosive power

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic physics** (forces, momentum, energy) — needed for force production, momentum, impact forces, energy dissipation
- **Fundamental anatomy** (major joints, muscle groups) — needed for joint kinematics, kinetic chain, eccentric action
- **Basic physiology** (muscle contraction, tissue properties) — needed for tissue adaptation, strength requirements, motor learning

## Common Misconception Pathways

- Students often learn **landing strategies** without understanding **force-time curves**, leading to technique application without comprehension of why certain strategies work
- **Triple extension** is sometimes taught as pure technique rather than a biomechanical power optimization principle
- **Economy of motion** appears simple but requires deep understanding of force production, momentum, and center of mass
- **Progressive overload** is often understood for strength but not for skill acquisition and tissue adaptation in parkour context
