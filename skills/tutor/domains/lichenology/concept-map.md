# Lichenology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Symbiosis** — multi-organism partnership as basis for lichen existence
2. **Mycobiont** — fungal partner, provides structure and protection
3. **Photobiont** — algal or cyanobacterial partner, provides photosynthate
4. **Holobiont** — integrated lichen organism with emergent properties
5. **Mutualism** — reciprocal benefits driving symbiotic stability. Depends on: symbiosis, mycobiont, photobiont
6. **Nutrient exchange** — carbon, nitrogen, and mineral flows between partners. Depends on: mutualism
7. **Tripartite lichens** — three-partner systems with cyanobacteria for nitrogen fixation. Depends on: symbiosis, photobiont
8. **Controlled parasitism** — alternative interpretation of lichen symbiosis. Depends on: mutualism, nutrient exchange
9. **Specificity** — degree of partner selectivity in symbiosis formation. Depends on: symbiosis, co-evolution
10. **Growth forms** — crustose, foliose, fruticose morphologies reflecting ecological adaptation. Depends on: holobiont
11. **Microhabitat preferences** — substrate chemistry, moisture, light gradients. Depends on: growth forms
12. **Asexual propagules** — soredia, isidia, fragmentation for clonal reproduction. Depends on: holobiont
13. **Secondary chemistry** — lichen-specific metabolites for defense and competition. Depends on: mycobiont
14. **Primary succession** — lichen role as pioneer species on bare substrates. Depends on: growth forms, stress tolerance
15. **Poikilohydry** — passive water balance enabling extreme desiccation tolerance. Depends on: holobiont
16. **Succession patterns** — lichen community change over ecological time. Depends on: primary succession, competitive displacement
17. **Dispersal limitation** — propagule movement constraints shaping biogeography. Depends on: asexual propagules
18. **Substrate continuity** — temporal habitat availability affecting species persistence. Depends on: succession patterns
19. **Bioindication** — using lichen presence/absence to assess environmental conditions. Depends on: physiological sensitivity
20. **Pollution sensitivity** — differential species tolerance to air pollutants. Depends on: bioindication
21. **Diversity indices** — quantitative measures of lichen community composition. Depends on: bioindication
22. **Sampling design** — field methods for biomonitoring studies. Depends on: diversity indices
23. **Heavy metal accumulation** — lichen ability to concentrate atmospheric pollutants. Depends on: bioindication
24. **Climate sensitivity** — lichen response to temperature and moisture regimes. Depends on: poikilohydry, biogeography
25. **Morphological characters** — traits used for field identification. Depends on: growth forms, reproductive structures
26. **Chemical taxonomy** — spot test reagents revealing secondary chemistry. Depends on: secondary chemistry
27. **Taxonomic diversity** — patterns of species richness across regions and substrates. Depends on: biogeography, specificity
28. **Conservation priorities** — rare species documentation and habitat protection. Depends on: taxonomic diversity, substrate continuity

## Dependencies

### Foundation Layer (Lessons 1-5)
- **Mutualism** requires understanding **symbiosis**, **mycobiont**, and **photobiont** because the nature of reciprocal benefits cannot be grasped without knowing the partners and their baseline relationship
- **Tripartite lichens** build on **symbiosis** and **photobiont** concepts because adding a third partner (cyanobacteria) requires understanding the two-partner baseline
- **Specificity** depends on **symbiosis** and **co-evolution** because partner selectivity only makes sense in evolutionary context

### Form and Function (Lessons 6-10)
- **Microhabitat preferences** require understanding **growth forms** because morphology constrains where lichens can persist
- **Asexual propagules** depend on **holobiont** concept because reproductive structures must maintain the symbiotic unit
- **Secondary chemistry** builds on **mycobiont** knowledge because the fungus produces most defensive compounds

### Ecology (Lessons 11-16)
- **Primary succession** requires **growth forms** and **stress tolerance** because pioneering ability stems from morphological and physiological traits
- **Succession patterns** build on **primary succession** and add **competitive displacement** because community change involves both arrival order and biotic interactions
- **Substrate continuity** depends on **succession patterns** because temporal habitat availability shapes which species persist through successional stages

### Bioindication (Lessons 17-22)
- **Pollution sensitivity** requires **bioindication** foundation because differential tolerance is the mechanism underlying bioindicator value
- **Diversity indices** build on **bioindication** because quantitative measures formalize qualitative sensitivity patterns
- **Sampling design** depends on **diversity indices** because field protocols must produce data compatible with analytical methods
- **Climate sensitivity** requires **poikilohydry** understanding because water balance physiology determines temperature and moisture responses

### Identification and Conservation (Lessons 23-26)
- **Chemical taxonomy** depends on **secondary chemistry** knowledge because spot tests detect specific metabolites
- **Morphological characters** require **growth forms** and **reproductive structures** understanding because identification relies on these traits
- **Conservation priorities** depend on **taxonomic diversity** and **substrate continuity** because rare species protection requires knowing distribution patterns and habitat needs

## Bottlenecks

### Critical Concept: Holobiont
Understanding lichens as integrated organisms (not just fungi with algae inside) is essential for grasping:
- Reproductive strategies
- Ecological functions
- Physiological responses
- Evolutionary dynamics

Students who think of lichens as "fungus + algae" rather than emergent entities will struggle with why dispersal, succession, and stress tolerance patterns differ from free-living organisms.

### Critical Concept: Bioindication Logic
The move from "some lichens are sensitive" to "we can use lichens to quantify pollution" requires understanding:
1. Physiological mechanisms of sensitivity
2. Community-level response gradients
3. Sampling and statistical inference
4. Confounding factors (climate, substrate, etc.)

This is a multi-step inferential chain that students often shortcut, leading to oversimplified biomonitoring interpretations.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic biology** — cellular structure, photosynthesis, respiration (needed for: mycobiont, photobiont, nutrient exchange)
- **Ecology fundamentals** — succession, competition, niche, dispersal (needed for: primary succession, succession patterns, microhabitat preferences)
- **Introductory mycology or botany** — fungal life cycles, algal diversity (needed for: mycobiont, photobiont, reproduction, taxonomy)

## Common Confusions

- **Lichen = fungus with algae vs. lichen as emergent organism** — affects understanding of all ecological and physiological properties
- **All lichens are nitrogen-fixers** — only cyanobacterial partners fix nitrogen; many lichens lack cyanobacteria
- **Lichen sensitivity = universal pollution indicator** — different species respond to different pollutants; must match sensitivity to target pollutant
- **Crustose = primitive, fruticose = advanced** — growth forms reflect ecology, not evolutionary grade
- **Chemical spot tests identify species** — chemistry aids identification but requires morphological context
