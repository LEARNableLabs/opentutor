# Lichenology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Symbiosis and Organismal Biology
- Fungal-algal/cyanobacterial partnerships (mycobiont-photobiont relationships)
- Symbiotic architecture and metabolic exchange
- Co-evolution and specificity
- Lichen thalli structure and growth forms (crustose, foliose, fruticose)

### 2. Ecology and Succession
- Substrate colonization patterns
- Pioneer species in primary succession
- Competition and niche partitioning
- Microclimatic preferences and stress tolerance
- Dispersal mechanisms (soredia, isidia, fragmentation, sexual spores)

### 3. Bioindication and Environmental Monitoring
- Air quality assessment using lichen diversity and abundance
- Sensitivity to sulfur dioxide, nitrogen compounds, heavy metals
- Lichen monitoring protocols (transplant studies, diversity indices)
- Climate change indicators (range shifts, phenological changes)

### 4. Biodiversity and Identification
- Major lichen families and genera
- Morphological, chemical (TLC), and molecular identification
- Regional diversity patterns
- Substrate specificity (corticolous, saxicolous, terricolous)

### 5. Applied Lichenology
- Bioprospecting for secondary metabolites
- Cultural uses (dyes, medicines, food)
- Conservation challenges

## Key Sources

### Academic Textbooks
- **Lichens of North America** (Brodo, Sharnoff, Sharnoff 2001) — comprehensive field guide with photography
- **Lichen Biology** (Nash, ed. 2008) — authoritative academic treatment
- **Introduction to Lichenology** (Hale 1983) — classic beginner text

### Educational Resources
- British Lichen Society educational materials and field guides
- American Bryological and Lichenological Society resources
- USDA Forest Service FIA Lichen Indicator database
- Consortium of Lichen Herbaria specimen portal

### Field Guides and Identification
- Regional guides (e.g., *Lichens of the North Woods*, *Pacific Northwest Lichens*)
- iNaturalist lichen observation database
- Macrolichens of the Pacific Northwest interactive key

### Online Courses and Lectures
- OSU Ecampus Lichenology course materials
- Field naturalist society webinars and workshops
- YouTube channels featuring lichen ecology and identification

## Available Resource Types

- **Visual atlases** — high-resolution photos of growth forms, reproductive structures, substrate associations
- **Interactive keys** — dichotomous and multi-access identification tools
- **Chemical spot tests** — K, C, KC, P reagent guides for chemotaxonomy
- **Field protocols** — transect methods, voucher collection, photography standards
- **Monitoring datasets** — long-term lichen community change studies
- **Molecular phylogenies** — understanding relationships beyond morphology
