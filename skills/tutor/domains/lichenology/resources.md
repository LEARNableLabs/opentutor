# Lichenology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Lichens of North America** (Brodo, Sharnoff, Sharnoff, 2001) — The definitive field guide with 800+ species, stunning photography, and accessible descriptions. Excellent for morphological identification and natural history context. Intermediate-friendly.
  - Yale University Press, ISBN 978-0300082494

- **Lichen Biology** (Thomas H. Nash III, ed., 2008) — Comprehensive academic treatment covering symbiosis physiology, ecology, secondary chemistry, and molecular methods. Advanced but essential reference for mechanistic understanding.
  - Cambridge University Press, ISBN 978-0521871624

- **Introduction to Lichenology** (Mason E. Hale, 1983) — Classic beginner text with clear diagrams, readable prose, and foundational concepts. Dated on molecular taxonomy but still useful for morphology and ecology.
  - Smithsonian Institution Press

### Online Courses and Syllabi

- **Oregon State University Ecampus — Lichenology Course** — University-level online course with modules on symbiosis, ecology, identification, and biomonitoring. Check for open educational resources.
  - https://ecampus.oregonstate.edu

- **British Lichen Society — Educational Resources** — Training modules, identification guides, and field course listings. Excellent for UK/European species but concepts are universal.
  - https://www.britishlichensociety.org.uk/learning

- **USDA Forest Service FIA Lichen Program** — Protocols, training materials, and monitoring data for North American bioindication studies. Free and comprehensive.
  - https://www.fs.usda.gov/fia/topics/lichen-monitoring/

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos and Lectures

- **"Lichen: The Remarkable Organism That Made Life on Land Possible"** (PBS Eons) — 10-minute animated overview of lichen evolution and ecology. Great hook for Lesson 1.
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HFX9kKpl1JA (check YouTube for PBS Eons lichens)

- **"The Hidden Kingdom of Fungi" (BBC/PBS)** — Episode 2 features lichen symbiosis with high-quality cinematography. Use clips for Lessons 2-3.

- **Lichenology lectures by Bruce McCune (Oregon State)** — Recorded guest lectures covering biomonitoring, diversity gradients, and field methods. Search YouTube or OSU open courseware.

- **iNaturalist Lichen Observation videos** — Community members demonstrate field photography and microhabitat observation techniques.
  - https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/54743-Lichens

### Interactive Tools

- **Consortium of Lichen Herbaria (formerly CNALH)** — Searchable database of 2+ million lichen specimens with photos, locality data, and identification keys. Essential for Lessons 23-26.
  - https://lichenportal.org/cnalh/

- **iNaturalist — Lichens** — Crowdsourced observation platform with AI-assisted identification suggestions. Students can upload photos and get community feedback. Gamifies learning.
  - https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/54743-Lichens

- **Macrolichens of the Pacific Northwest — Interactive Key** — Multi-access key for 300+ species using growth form, color, chemistry, substrate filters. Teaches key logic and identification workflow.
  - https://lichenportal.org/portal/checklists/checklist.php?clid=14 (check for regional keys)

- **British Lichen Society Spot Test Guide** — Photos and videos demonstrating K, C, KC, P reagent tests with color change examples.
  - https://www.britishlichensociety.org.uk/chemical-spot-tests

### Citizen Science and Monitoring Projects

- **North American Lichen Project** — Community science initiative documenting lichen diversity across the continent. Students can contribute observations.
  - https://lichenportal.org/cnalh/projects/

- **USFS FIA Lichen Air Quality Gradient Projects** — Access to long-term monitoring data for student data analysis exercises (Lesson 19).
  - https://www.fs.usda.gov/research/products/dataandtools/datasets

- **City Nature Challenge** — Annual global bioblitz includes lichen observations. Motivates urban biomonitoring (Lesson 20).
  - https://citynaturechallenge.org

### Code and Data Analysis

- **R package: `vegan`** — Community ecology analysis (diversity indices, ordination). Use for Lesson 19 (sampling design) and Lesson 22 (review).
  - https://cran.r-project.org/package=vegan

- **USFS FIA Lichen Community Diversity Indices** — Downloadable datasets and R scripts for calculating community metrics (Lesson 17, 21).
  - https://www.fs.usda.gov/rds/archive/Catalog/RDS-2022-0001

- **GIS lichen mapping tutorials** — ESRI and QGIS workflows for bioindication spatial analysis. Optional extension for advanced students.

### Field Guides (Regional)

- **Lichens of the North Woods** (Joe Walewski, 2007) — Field guide for Great Lakes region with excellent macrophotography and dichotomous keys.
  - Kollath+Stensaas Publishing, ISBN 978-0967011486

- **California Lichens** (Sharnoff, Sharnoff, 2017) — Regional flora with habitat notes and conservation status.

- **Common Lichens of Northeastern North America** (Hinds & Hinds, 2007) — Pocket guide format, beginner-friendly.
  - New York Botanical Garden Press

### People (Researchers and Practitioners to Follow)

- **Trevor Goward** — Canadian lichenologist known for elegant writing and "The Lichen-Forming Ascomycetes of Yukon." Philosophical approach to lichen biology.

- **Toby Spribille** — Researcher who discovered basidiomycete yeasts as third partners in many lichens (2016 paper shook the field). Excellent for Lesson 3 (tripartite lichens).
  - Selected paper: *Science* 353(6298), 2016. DOI: 10.1126/science.aaf8287

- **Linda Geiser** — USFS ecologist specializing in lichen biomonitoring and air quality. Led development of FIA lichen protocols.

- **Irwin Brodo** — Senior author of *Lichens of North America*. Decades of taxonomic and ecological research.

- **Bruce McCune** — Quantitative ecologist at Oregon State, developed lichen diversity metrics and monitoring frameworks.

### Unexpected Connections

- **Lichens and music** — Composer Jóhann Jóhannsson's album *Orphée* was inspired by Icelandic lichen-covered lava fields. Use for ambient study music or interdisciplinary discussion.

- **Lichen motifs in Indigenous art** — Tlingit and Haida artists use lichen patterns in formline design. Connects lichenology to Pacific Northwest cultural heritage.

- **Lichen-eating snails** — Gastropods in genus *Pallifera* specialize on lichens; radula adaptations for scraping thalli. Neat example of coevolution and trophic specialization.

- **Beatrix Potter's lichen drawings** — Before *Peter Rabbit*, Potter was an accomplished lichen illustrator and amateur researcher who challenged Victorian mycologists. Gender and science history angle.
  - https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/beatrix-potter-pioneering-scientist.html

- **Lichen green roofs** — Some green roof designs intentionally cultivate *Cladonia*, *Cetraria*, and other lichens for stormwater management and urban biodiversity. Ties ecology to green infrastructure.
