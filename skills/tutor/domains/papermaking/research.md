# Papermaking Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Fiber Science
- **Plant fiber chemistry** — cellulose structure, lignin, hemicellulose, and their roles in paper properties
- **Fiber sources** — bast fibers (flax, hemp, kozo, gampi), leaf fibers (abaca), seed fibers (cotton), recycled fibers
- **Fiber processing** — retting, cooking (alkaline vs. acidic), beating, refining, hydration
- **Fiber morphology** — fiber length, diameter, flexibility, bonding potential
- **Water chemistry** — pH, mineral content, conductivity, and their effects on formation

### Sheet Formation & Engineering
- **Pulp preparation** — consistency, fiber distribution, additives (sizing, fillers, retention aids)
- **Sheet formation techniques** — Western mould & deckle, Japanese nagashi-zuki, cylinder mold machines
- **Drainage and pressing** — water removal, fiber alignment, density control
- **Drying methods** — air drying, restraint drying, loft drying, surface texture control
- **Paper properties** — strength (wet/dry), opacity, surface texture, permanence

### Artistic Techniques
- **Pulp painting** — colored pulp application, stenciling, direct drawing
- **Embedments & inclusions** — botanicals, threads, metallic elements
- **Layering & lamination** — multiple sheets, translucent effects
- **Surface treatments** — sizing (gelatin, methylcellulose), watermarking, embossing
- **Experimental approaches** — alternative fibers (banana, pineapple, denim), mixed media integration

### Historical & Contemporary Practice
- **Historical paper traditions** — Chinese origins, Japanese washi, European watermarks, Islamic decorative papers
- **Contemporary artists** — Winifred Lutz, Kathryn & Howard Clark, Aimee Lee, Helen Hiebert
- **Conservation science** — archival properties, permanence testing, deacidification
- **Sustainable practices** — local fiber sourcing, waste reduction, natural dyes

## Curriculum Approach

Given the intermediate level, the curriculum will:
- Assume basic chemistry knowledge (molecular structure, pH, polymers)
- Assume some craft/studio experience (measurement, material handling)
- Balance scientific understanding with hands-on technique
- Progress from controlled Western methods to more fluid Japanese techniques
- Integrate artistic applications throughout (not separated into "science" vs. "art" modules)
- Include both reproducible engineering and experimental exploration

Total estimated lessons: 25 (enough to cover both fiber science depth and artistic range without excessive padding)
