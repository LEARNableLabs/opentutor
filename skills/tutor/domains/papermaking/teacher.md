## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Paper strength comes from compression during pressing"**
   - *Why students think this*: They see pressing as forceful, so assume it "squeezes" fibers together
   - *Correction*: Strength comes from hydrogen bonding between hydrated, fibrillated cellulose fibers. Pressing removes water so bonds can form, but excessive pressure doesn't create more bonds. Demonstrate with two sheets: one well-beaten and lightly pressed, one poorly beaten and heavily pressed. The first is stronger.

2. **"More beating always makes stronger paper"**
   - *Why students think this*: Beating = more processing = better results (industrial thinking)
   - *Correction*: Beating fibrillates fibers (good for bonding) but also cuts them (bad for strength). There's an optimal point. Show over-beaten pulp: it looks silky but makes weak, dense paper. Teach students to watch for the shift from "opening up" to "cutting down."
