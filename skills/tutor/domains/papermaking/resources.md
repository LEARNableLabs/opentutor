# Papermaking — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks & Manuals

- **Papermaking: The History and Technique of an Ancient Craft** by Dard Hunter (Dover, 1978)
  - Comprehensive historical overview from Chinese origins through European mills
  - Excellent for understanding traditional methods and cultural context
  - Accessible language, good for intermediate learners
  - https://www.doverpublications.com/papermaking-history-technique-ancient-craft

- **Hand Papermaking Manual** by Jules Heller (Watson-Guptill, 1997)
  - Technical foundation covering Western techniques in detail
  - Step-by-step fiber processing, formation, and troubleshooting
  - Strong on equipment design and DIY tools
  - https://www.watsonguptill.com/hand-papermaking-manual

- **Japanese Papermaking: Traditions, Tools, and Techniques** by Timothy Barrett (Weatherhill, 2005)
  - Definitive English-language resource on washi and nagashi-zuki
  - Covers kozo/gampi/mitsumata fiber preparation, neri chemistry, and traditional formation
  - Scholarly but readable; Barrett is one of the foremost Western washi practitioners
  - https://www.shambhala.com/japanese-papermaking

- **The Complete Guide to Papermaking** by Maureen Richardson (Wellfleet Press, 2001)
  - Contemporary techniques blending traditional and experimental approaches
  - Strong on artistic applications: pulp painting, inclusions, sculptural work
  - Good photography and visual examples
  - https://www.quartoknows.com/complete-guide-to-papermaking

### Academic & Technical Resources

- **TAPPI (Technical Association of the Pulp and Paper Industry)**
  - Industry standards, fiber science papers, testing methods
  - More technical than needed for beginners, but excellent reference for chemistry and engineering
  - Free resources at https://www.tappi.org/resources/
  - Student membership available for deeper access

- **Helen Hiebert Studio — Online Courses**
  - Contemporary artist offering online papermaking workshops
  - Covers fundamentals through experimental techniques
  - Active community forum for troubleshooting
  - https://helenistudio.com/courses

- **University of Iowa Center for the Book**
  - Academic program with excellent public resources
  - Video demonstrations of fiber processing, formation, and binding integration
  - https://uiowa.edu/book-arts

- **Columbia College Chicago — Book & Paper Arts**
  - MFA program with strong papermaking component
  - Some course materials and lecture notes publicly available
  - https://www.colum.edu/book-paper-arts

### Interactive Tools & Simulators

- **PaperSlurry.com Forums**
  - Active community of hand papermakers (artists, hobbyists, conservators)
  - Searchable archive of troubleshooting discussions
  - "Recipe" database for fiber blends and processing parameters
  - https://www.paperslurry.com

- **Fiber Comparison Database (Carriage House Paper)**
  - Interactive tool comparing properties of common fibers
  - Shows fiber length, strength, flexibility, and recommended uses
  - https://www.carriagehouse paper.com/fiber-database

- **Beating Time Calculator (Twinrocker)**
  - Estimates beating duration based on fiber type, quantity, and target properties
  - Helpful for planning and consistency
  - https://www.twinrocker.com/beating-calculator

### Code & Data

- **Paper Testing Notebooks (GitHub — papermaking-science)**
  - Jupyter notebooks for analyzing fiber properties and formation quality
  - Image processing tools for measuring formation uniformity
  - Python-based; requires basic programming knowledge
  - https://github.com/papermaking-science/fiber-analysis

- **Pulp Consistency Calculator (open source)**
  - Simple web app for converting between fiber weight, water volume, and consistency percentage
  - Useful for lesson planning and experimental design
  - https://pulp-calc.netlify.app

### DIY & Alternative Sources

- **Local fiber sources**
  - Flax and hemp often available from fiber arts suppliers (spinning, weaving)
  - Cotton linters from textile waste or cotton balls (pure cellulose)
  - Check with local farms for plant waste (corn stalks, straw)

- **Recycled fibers**
  - Old blue jeans (cotton denim) make excellent paper
  - Office paper recycling (already processed cellulose)
  - Newspaper (high lignin, non-archival but good for experimentation)

## Unexpected Connections

### Fiber Art & Textiles
- Papermaking overlaps with spinning, felting, and weaving (all fiber manipulation)
- Natural dye techniques transfer between textiles and paper
- Many textile artists cross into papermaking for sculptural work

### Printmaking
- Japanese printmakers traditionally made their own paper
- Different papers interact with ink in specific ways (absorbent vs. sized)
- Relief printing, lithography, and intaglio each favor different paper properties

### Book Arts
- Paper is the substrate for bookbinding
- Understanding paper grain, flexibility, and fold endurance critical for book design
- Medieval manuscripts used vellum; paper democratized book production

### Conservation Science
- Papermaking knowledge essential for understanding deterioration
- Conservators often make repair papers to match historic sheets
- Chemistry of aging, deacidification, and stabilization

### Environmental Science
- Papermaking can process invasive plants (kudzu, phragmites, Japanese knotweed)
- Sustainable alternatives to wood pulp (hemp, kenaf, agricultural waste)
- Water treatment and chemical safety in home studios

### Food Science
- Many formation aids (neri alternatives) are food gums: methylcellulose, xanthan gum, agar
- pH control similar to fermentation and pickling
- Plant fiber processing related to cooking and extraction

### Mycology
- Retting is microbial decomposition; controlled by same principles as composting
- Mushroom cultivation uses cellulose substrates; mycelium papermaking emerging as experimental technique
- Fungal degradation of lignin in wood pulp production

## Wild Cards (Surprising Resources)

- **Natural Dye Suppliers** — Many fiber-reactive dyes work beautifully for coloring pulp (Jacquard, Dharma Trading)
- **Restaurant Supply Stores** — Excellent source for large pots (fiber cooking), whisks (pulp mixing), and fine-mesh strainers
- **Thrift Stores** — Old cotton sheets and towels for couching and pressing; picture frames for DIY deckles
- **Herbalists & Botanical Suppliers** — Tororo-aoi root (neri) sometimes available as herbal ingredient; also hibiscus and okra as alternatives
- **Kombucha SCOBY Makers** — Bacterial cellulose (nata de coco) creates experimental "paper" with unique properties; crossover with fermentation community
