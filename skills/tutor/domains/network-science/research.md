# Network Science Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Graph Theory Foundations
- Graph representations (adjacency matrices, edge lists)
- Types of networks (directed/undirected, weighted, bipartite, temporal)
- Basic metrics (degree, paths, connectivity)

### 2. Network Structure and Properties
- Degree distributions and power laws
- Clustering coefficients and transitivity
- Small-world phenomenon and six degrees of separation
- Centrality measures (degree, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector)

### 3. Network Models
- Random networks (Erdős-Rényi)
- Small-world networks (Watts-Strogatz)
- Scale-free networks (Barabási-Albert, preferential attachment)
- Configuration models

### 4. Dynamics on Networks
- Epidemic spreading (SIS, SIR, SEIR models)
- Percolation theory and network resilience
- Threshold models and cascades
- Synchronization

### 5. Social Networks
- Community detection and modularity
- Information diffusion and viral content
- Influence and opinion dynamics
- Homophily and triadic closure

### 6. Applications
- Epidemiology (COVID-19, flu, STDs)
- Social media platforms (Twitter, Facebook, TikTok)
- Biological networks (protein interactions, neural networks)
- Infrastructure networks (power grids, internet)

## Key Educational Sources

### Textbooks
- **Network Science** by Albert-László Barabási (http://networksciencebook.com/) — comprehensive, free online book with excellent visualizations
- **Networks** by Mark Newman — rigorous treatment with mathematical depth
- **Networks, Crowds, and Markets** by Easley & Kleinberg — economics and social network focus

### Online Courses
- **Stanford CS224W** (http://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224w/) — machine learning on networks
- **Complexity Explorer** (https://www.complexityexplorer.org/) — Santa Fe Institute courses including Introduction to Dynamical Systems and Chaos
- **Network Science course** on Coursera by Barabási

### Tools and Platforms
- **NetworkX** (https://networkx.org/) — Python library for network analysis
- **Gephi** (https://gephi.org/) — interactive visualization platform
- **SNAP** (https://snap.stanford.edu/) — Stanford Network Analysis Platform
- **igraph** — R and Python library for complex networks

### Video Lectures
- Stanford Online lectures on network analysis
- Santa Fe Institute complexity series
- 3Blue1Brown visualizations of graph algorithms

### Interactive Resources
- Network Science book interactive demos
- Complexity Explorer simulations
- NetLogo models for epidemics and diffusion

## Research Gaps Identified
- None for intermediate curriculum — abundant high-quality resources available
- Strong coverage from computational, mathematical, and applied perspectives
- Real-world datasets readily available for hands-on learning
