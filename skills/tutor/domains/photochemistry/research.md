# Photochemistry — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Light-Matter Interaction Fundamentals
- Electromagnetic radiation and photon energy
- Beer-Lambert law and absorption spectroscopy
- Selection rules and transition moments
- Chromophores and auxochromes

### 2. Electronic Structure and Excited States
- Molecular orbital theory for excited states
- Singlet and triplet states
- Jablonski diagram
- Electronic configurations and term symbols

### 3. Photophysical Processes
- Absorption and emission
- Internal conversion and intersystem crossing
- Fluorescence and phosphorescence
- Quantum yields and lifetimes
- Radiative vs. non-radiative decay

### 4. Energy Transfer Mechanisms
- Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET)
- Dexter electron exchange
- Triplet-triplet annihilation
- Sensitization and quenching

### 5. Photochemical Reactions
- Norrish Type I and Type II reactions
- Photoisomerization (cis-trans, electrocyclic)
- Photocycloadditions ([2+2], [4+4])
- Photoredox chemistry
- Photofragmentation and photosubstitution

### 6. Applications
- Natural photosynthesis (light-harvesting, reaction centers)
- Photovoltaics and solar energy conversion
- Photocatalysis and environmental remediation
- Photopolymerization
- Phototherapy and photomedicine
- Photochromic materials

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Modern Molecular Photochemistry of Organic Molecules** (Turro, Ramamurthy, Scaiano) — comprehensive graduate-level text
- **Photochemistry of Organic Compounds** (Klán & Wirz) — excellent for mechanisms and applications
- **Principles of Molecular Photochemistry** (Turro) — classic undergraduate introduction
- **Photochemistry and Photophysics of Coordination Compounds** (Balzani et al.) — inorganic focus

## Available Learning Pathways

**For intermediate students** (assumes general chemistry, organic chemistry I, basic quantum mechanics):
- Start with absorption/emission fundamentals
- Build intuition through Jablonski diagram
- Connect to organic reaction mechanisms
- Introduce computational tools for excited state visualization
- Emphasize applications (photosynthesis, solar cells) to maintain engagement
- Balance theory (quantum mechanics, kinetics) with experimental techniques (spectroscopy)

**Key prerequisites needed:**
- Molecular orbital theory
- Reaction mechanisms (organic)
- Basic quantum mechanics (wavefunctions, energy levels)
- Spectroscopy fundamentals
- Chemical kinetics

**Common challenges:**
- Visualizing excited states (not just ground state structures)
- Understanding spin multiplicity and selection rules
- Connecting photophysical processes to timescales
- Distinguishing energy transfer mechanisms
- Reconciling orbital theory with practical photochemistry
