# Photochemistry — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Wave-particle duality** — light behaves as both electromagnetic wave and discrete photons
2. **Photon energy** — E = hν relates frequency to energy; determines which electronic transitions are accessible
3. **Beer-Lambert law** — quantitative relationship between light absorption and concentration (A = εbc)
4. **Chromophores** — molecular structures that absorb light (conjugated systems, carbonyls, aromatic rings)
5. **HOMO-LUMO gap** — energy difference between highest occupied and lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals; determines absorption wavelength. Depends on: chromophores
6. **Selection rules** — quantum mechanical rules governing allowed vs forbidden transitions. Depends on: photon energy
7. **Transition dipole moment** — determines intensity of electronic transitions. Depends on: selection rules
8. **Franck-Condon principle** — electronic transitions occur on timescales faster than nuclear motion (vertical excitation). Depends on: photon energy
9. **Singlet states** — excited states with paired electron spins (S = 0). Depends on: electronic transitions
10. **Triplet states** — excited states with unpaired electron spins (S = 1); lower energy than corresponding singlet. Depends on: singlet states
11. **Exchange energy** — energy difference between singlet and triplet due to electron-electron repulsion. Depends on: singlet states, triplet states
12. **Jablonski diagram** — visual roadmap of all photophysical pathways available to an excited molecule. Depends on: singlet states, triplet states
13. **Vibrational relaxation** — rapid (~ps) energy loss within an electronic state. Depends on: Franck-Condon principle
14. **Internal conversion** — non-radiative transition between states of same spin multiplicity. Depends on: Jablonski diagram
15. **Intersystem crossing** — spin-forbidden transition between singlet and triplet manifolds. Depends on: Jablonski diagram, triplet states
16. **Fluorescence** — radiative decay from S₁ → S₀ (spin-allowed, fast ~ns). Depends on: singlet states, internal conversion
17. **Phosphorescence** — radiative decay from T₁ → S₀ (spin-forbidden, slow μs-s). Depends on: triplet states, intersystem crossing
18. **Stokes shift** — energy difference between absorption and emission maxima. Depends on: fluorescence, vibrational relaxation
19. **Quantum yield** — efficiency of a photophysical or photochemical process (ratio of events to photons absorbed). Depends on: competing pathways in Jablonski diagram
20. **Fluorescence lifetime** — average time a molecule spends in S₁ before decay. Depends on: fluorescence, quantum yield
21. **Spin-orbit coupling** — mechanism enabling intersystem crossing; enhanced by heavy atoms. Depends on: intersystem crossing
22. **Heavy atom effect** — enhancement of triplet formation and phosphorescence by heavy elements. Depends on: spin-orbit coupling
23. **Energy transfer** — non-radiative transfer of excitation energy from donor to acceptor molecule. Depends on: excited states
24. **Spectral overlap** — degree of overlap between donor emission and acceptor absorption; required for efficient energy transfer. Depends on: energy transfer, fluorescence
25. **Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET)** — dipole-dipole energy transfer mechanism (long-range, R⁻⁶ distance dependence). Depends on: energy transfer, spectral overlap
26. **Dexter energy transfer** — electron exchange mechanism (short-range, requires orbital overlap). Depends on: energy transfer, triplet states
27. **Photofragmentation** — bond cleavage in excited state (Norrish Type I). Depends on: excited state reactivity
28. **Norrish Type I** — α-cleavage of carbonyl compounds producing radicals. Depends on: photofragmentation
29. **Norrish Type II** — intramolecular hydrogen abstraction followed by cleavage. Depends on: excited state reactivity
30. **Photoisomerization** — light-induced change in molecular geometry (cis-trans, electrocyclic). Depends on: excited state potential surfaces
31. **Photocycloaddition** — light-driven formation of cyclic structures ([2+2], [4+4]). Depends on: excited state reactivity, Woodward-Hoffmann rules
32. **Photoredox catalysis** — use of light-absorbing catalysts to drive single-electron transfer reactions. Depends on: excited state redox potentials
33. **Singlet oxygen** — highly reactive ¹O₂ generated by energy transfer from triplet sensitizer. Depends on: triplet states, Dexter energy transfer

## Dependencies

### Linear Prerequisites
- **HOMO-LUMO gap** requires understanding of **chromophores** because conjugation and functional groups determine orbital energies
- **Selection rules** depend on **photon energy** because transitions must match energy and symmetry requirements
- **Singlet/triplet states** require knowing about **electronic transitions** to understand what "excited state" means
- **Exchange energy** builds on **singlet and triplet states** to explain why they differ in energy
- **Jablonski diagram** synthesizes **singlet states, triplet states, and various decay pathways** into one framework

### Branching Pathways
- **Fluorescence** and **phosphorescence** both emerge from the **Jablonski diagram** but represent different routes (S₁→S₀ vs T₁→S₀)
- **FRET** and **Dexter transfer** are alternative **energy transfer mechanisms** that operate under different distance and spin regimes
- **Photochemical reactions** (Norrish I, Norrish II, isomerization, cycloaddition) all stem from **excited state reactivity** but differ in mechanism

### Critical Bottlenecks
- **Jablonski diagram** (concept 12) is the conceptual hub — students must master this before understanding photophysics
- **Spin multiplicity** (concepts 9-11) is essential for distinguishing fluorescence from phosphorescence and for understanding triplet chemistry
- **Quantum yield** (concept 19) requires understanding all competing pathways in the Jablonski diagram

### Key Integrations
- **Heavy atom effect** connects **spin-orbit coupling** to practical phosphorescence enhancement
- **Photoredox catalysis** integrates **excited state formation** with **redox chemistry** and **synthetic applications**
- **Photosynthesis** demonstrates **antenna systems** (FRET networks) and **charge separation** in a biological context

## Prerequisite Topics (external)

- **Molecular orbital theory** — needed for understanding HOMO-LUMO gaps, π→π* transitions, n→π* transitions
- **Quantum mechanics basics** — wavefunctions, energy quantization, Pauli exclusion principle (for singlet/triplet)
- **Organic reaction mechanisms** — radical chemistry, carbocation/carbanion intermediates (for photochemical reactions)
- **Spectroscopy fundamentals** — UV-vis absorption, emission, Beer-Lambert law
- **Chemical kinetics** — rate laws, lifetimes, competing reactions (for quantum yields)
- **Thermodynamics** — energy levels, Boltzmann distribution, equilibrium (for understanding excited state populations)

## Common Conceptual Barriers

### Barrier 1: Visualizing Excited States
Students often struggle to move beyond ground-state molecular structure thinking. **Solution**: Heavy use of orbital diagrams, potential energy surfaces, and Jablonski diagrams as visual scaffolds.

### Barrier 2: Spin Multiplicity
The abstraction of "spin pairing" and its consequences for state energies and transition probabilities is difficult. **Solution**: Concrete examples (oxygen triplet ground state), electron configuration diagrams, analogy to bar magnets.

### Barrier 3: Timescales
Photophysical processes span 15 orders of magnitude (fs to seconds). Students lose intuition. **Solution**: Explicit timescale comparisons, kinetic competition diagrams, "what happens first" exercises.

### Barrier 4: Energy Transfer vs Electron Transfer
Confusing FRET (energy moves, electrons stay) with photoredox (electrons move). **Solution**: Molecular orbital diagrams showing electron movement vs energy flow, donor/acceptor schemes.

### Barrier 5: Selection Rules
Treating selection rules as absolute rather than probabilistic. **Solution**: Emphasize "allowed" means "probable" and "forbidden" means "improbable but possible with mechanisms like spin-orbit coupling."
