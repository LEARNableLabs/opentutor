## Domain Hooks
- **The Chinese traction trebuchet** — appears centuries before European counterweight versions; why did technology transfer take so long? Drops into lessons 9-10 when discussing traction engines.

- **Scaling laws in biology** — why elephants can't jump and mice can fall from any height. The same square-cube law governs both animals and siege engines. Great comparison for lesson 4.

- **Brunelleschi's dome construction** — uses similar timber engineering and scaling considerations, but in reverse (supporting weight from above vs. throwing weight forward). Can reference during material selection discussion (lesson 15).

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Bigger is always better"** — Students assume larger engines are strictly superior. In reality, scaling introduces structural problems (square-cube law), increases construction time and material cost, reduces mobility, and makes tuning harder. Emphasize that engine choice depends on tactical objectives: a smaller, faster-firing engine might be preferable to a massive, slow one.

2. **"Medieval engineers were primitive"** — Modern students often underestimate medieval engineering sophistication. Counter this by showing the empirical optimization methods they used, the precision of their construction, and the complexity of their designs. The lack of calculus didn't prevent them from achieving optimal release angles through trial and error.
