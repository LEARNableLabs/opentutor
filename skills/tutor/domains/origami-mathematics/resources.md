# Resources — Origami Mathematics

## 1. Primary Sources

### Textbooks

**Origametry: Mathematical Methods in Paper Folding** — Thomas C. Hull (Cambridge University Press, 2020)
- URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/origametry/F1221B0998585B16C87B7AD8DDC05358
- Author site: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/origametry/F1221B0998585B16C87B7AD8DDC05358
- Level: Upper undergraduate / graduate. The single most complete mathematical reference on origami. Four parts: geometric constructions (Huzimi-Justin axioms, Galois theory of origami numbers), flat folding (Kawasaki-Justin theorem, NP-hardness), 3D folding, and applications. Over 180 figures.
- Best for: All modules. Core reference throughout the course — flat-foldability theorems, constructibility, algebraic origami numbers, and rigidity.
- Cost: **Paid** (~$45 paperback).

**How to Fold It: The Mathematics of Linkages, Origami, and Polyhedra** — Joseph O'Rourke (Cambridge University Press, 2011)
- Publisher page: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/how-to-fold-it/9780521145473
- Level: High school algebra/geometry and up. 200 color figures, 50 exercises with solutions, companion animations.
- Best for: Early modules (flat-foldability, basic constructibility). The best bridge from intuitive to rigorous material for an intermediate student.
- Cost: **Paid** (~$35 paperback).

**Project Origami: Activities for Exploring Mathematics** (2nd ed.) — Thomas Hull (CRC Press, 2012)
- URL: https://www.routledge.com/Project-Origami-Activities-for-Exploring-Mathematics-Second-Edition/Hull/p/book/9781466567917
- Level: College / advanced high school. 30 hands-on activities connecting origami to geometry, calculus, abstract algebra, number theory, combinatorics, and topology.
- Best for: Supplementary exercises and physical exploration across all modules. Especially strong for flat-foldability and modular origami activities.
- Cost: **Paid** (~$50).

**Origami Design Secrets: Mathematical Methods for an Ancient Art** (2nd ed.) — Robert J. Lang (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2011)
- Level: Intermediate to advanced. Bridges practical origami design with mathematical foundations. Covers tree theory, circle/river packing, uniaxial bases, and the algorithms behind TreeMaker.
- Best for: Computational design modules (circle-packing, base design algorithms, TreeMaker theory).
- Cost: **Paid** (~$55).

**Geometric Folding Algorithms: Linkages, Origami, Polyhedra** — Erik Demaine, Joseph O'Rourke (Cambridge University Press, 2007)
- URL: https://erikdemaine.org/papers/
- Level: Graduate / research. The foundational monograph on computational origami. 587 citations. Hundreds of results and 60+ open problems.
- Best for: Reference for advanced modules (computational complexity, universality results, polyhedra). An intermediate student will use selected chapters rather than reading cover to cover.
- Cost: **Paid** (~$60).

**Twists, Tilings, and Tessellations: Mathematical Methods for Geometric Origami** — Robert J. Lang (CRC Press, 2017)
- Level: Advanced. Focuses on twist folds, tessellations, and their mathematical underpinnings.
- Best for: Tessellation and rigid-origami modules.
- Cost: **Paid** (~$65).

**Introduction to Computational Origami** — Ryuhei Uehara (Springer, 2020)
- Level: Graduate. Computational complexity perspective: flat-foldability NP-hardness, map folding, algorithmic problems.
- Best for: Complexity theory modules (Bern-Hayes NP-hardness, parameterized complexity).
- Cost: **Paid** (~$55).

### Key Papers

Listed in recommended reading order for an intermediate student.

- T. C. Hull, "On the Mathematics of Flat Origamis" (1994), 142 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e2aeb0a6efb8351452ed5d209e5e980b59159af — Foundational graph-theoretic model for flat origami. Start here for flat-foldability.
- T. C. Hull, "The Combinatorics of Flat Folds: a Survey" (2013) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1065 — Survey of Maekawa's and Kawasaki's theorems, counting valid fold assignments.
- M. W. Bern, B. Hayes, "The Complexity of Flat Origami" (1996), 160 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7dfa56beb0c2643112718562832692df83727a7 — NP-hardness of mountain-valley assignment. Core complexity result.
- R. Alperin, "A Mathematical Theory of Origami Numbers and Constructions" (1999) — https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9912039 — Hierarchy of origami number fields connecting folding to algebraic number theory.
- R. C. Alperin, R. J. Lang, "One-, Two-, and Multi-Fold Origami Axioms" (2006), 56 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3177bcabb27ddfd82ea6783cf6aea2e9b192d451 — Extends Huzimi's axioms to multi-fold operations.
- R. J. Lang, "A Computational Algorithm for Origami Design" (1996), 241 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1006c0ab0295b41e5f3ec96ba0b5025a731c73c4 — First complete algorithm for designing arbitrary origami bases.
- E. Demaine, T. Tachi, "Origamizer: A Practical Algorithm for Folding Any Polyhedron" (2017), 108 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcc00dc59c7458119d12a600c28da4ea25eeb497 — Universal polyhedron-folding algorithm.
- T. C. Hull, I. Zakharevich, "Flat Origami is Turing Complete" (2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07932 — Flat origami mappings simulate any Turing machine. Frontier result.
- D. Eppstein, "Computational Complexities of Folding" (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07666 — Parameterized complexity under the exponential time hypothesis.

### Applied / Engineering Papers

- J. L. Silverberg et al., "Using Origami Design Principles to Fold Reprogrammable Mechanical Metamaterials" (2014), 998 citations — https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1252876 — Bistable Miura-ori unit cells with tunable compressive modulus.
- M. Schenk, S. D. Guest, "Geometry of Miura-folded Metamaterials" (2013), 963 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22e3963e0aaba110efc3128d7157e4d2c094666b — Negative in-plane Poisson's ratio, positive out-of-plane.
- E. T. Filipov, T. Tachi, G. H. Paulino, "Origami Tubes Assembled into Stiff, yet Reconfigurable Structures" (2015), 663 citations — https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1509465112 — Coupled Miura-ori tubes for deployable architecture.
- T. Tachi, "Generalization of Rigid Foldable Quadrilateral Mesh Origami" (2009), 264 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab401d7e479b2b0875c53bd53fd57a0ac2eb75d2 — Rigid-foldability conditions for quadrilateral meshes.
- T. Tachi, "Origamizing Polyhedral Surfaces" (2010), 196 citations — https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46ad6b56e0ba8f75e1f3890498b175937aecaa4 — Crease patterns that fold into arbitrary polyhedral surfaces.

## 6. Unexpected Connections

### Category Theory
The student's background in category theory provides deep structural lenses for origami mathematics:
- **Groupoids model reversible folds.** Each fold is an invertible morphism; fold-unfold sequences at different crease vertices form a groupoid rather than a group. This directly formalizes the "undo a fold" operation the student performs at the table.
- **Functorial folding.** The map from crease patterns to folded geometric realizations is structure-preserving: composition of folding operations maps to composition of spatial embeddings. This makes the folding process itself a functor between categories.
- **Mountain-valley duality as categorical duality.** Swapping all mountain and valley assignments produces a valid dual folding, mirroring how every theorem in a category has a free dual theorem in the opposite category.
- **Wallpaper groups and origami tessellations.** The 17 wallpaper groups classifying origami tessellation symmetries are exactly the groups arising from restricted origami constructions (Chari and Macauley, 2025).
- **Universal properties and Lang's universal molecule.** Lang's theorem that every tree has a unique optimal base (the "universal molecule") is a universality result in the categorical sense.

### Optimal Transport
The student's background in optimal transport creates bridges at several points:
- **Circle-packing as constrained optimization.** Lang's TreeMaker algorithm solves a constrained optimization problem (circle-packing for uniaxial bases) with LP/QP structure analogous to the Kantorovich relaxation the student already knows.
- **Isometric deformation.** Origami's fundamental constraint — paper is inextensible, so folding preserves intrinsic distances — is a discrete analog of structure-preserving deformations in Wasserstein space. Rigid-origami continuous folding paths are discrete geodesics in a constrained configuration space.
- **Voronoi/power diagrams.** Both semidiscrete OT and origami tessellations partition planar regions into cells governed by distance-based constraints. Understanding Laguerre diagrams enriches intuition for Miura-ori and related tessellations.

### Mathematics of Juggling
- **Local constraints determine global validity.** Juggling's average property (the average of a siteswap equals the number of balls) is structurally analogous to Kawasaki's theorem (alternate angles around a vertex sum to pi) and Maekawa's theorem (mountain-valley count differs by two). In both fields, simple local constraints at each vertex/beat determine whether a global pattern is valid.
- **State-transition graphs.** Juggling state diagrams (which states are reachable from which) parallel the dual graph structure of crease patterns, where both require analyzing reachability subject to local constraints.

### Engineering and Materials Science
- Miura-ori tessellations produce mechanical metamaterials with programmable negative Poisson's ratio — meaning they get narrower when compressed, unlike almost all natural materials. The student will encounter the same variational and optimization thinking from OT applied to designing tunable mechanical properties.
- Deployable space structures (solar arrays, antenna reflectors) use rigid origami to pack large surfaces into small launch volumes. NASA's Starshade project uses Lang's origami algorithms directly.

### Physics (Statistical Mechanics)
- Hull, Michelen, and Yap (2025) developed a theory of random flat-foldable origami using Markov chains on mountain-valley assignments, connecting origami combinatorics to spin-glass models and partition functions. Nakajima (2024) mapped flat-foldability onto spin glass ground-state search on random graphs. This is a live research frontier bridging origami and statistical physics.

### Journalism (Accessible Deep Dives)
- **Quanta Magazine, "The Atomic Theory of Origami"** — https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-atomic-theory-of-origami-20171031/ — Research-level context in accessible prose. Covers Hull's and Demaine's work on the fundamental building blocks of origami mathematics.
