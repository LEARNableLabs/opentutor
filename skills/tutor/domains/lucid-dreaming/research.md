# Lucid Dreaming — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Neuroscience of Consciousness and Sleep
- REM sleep architecture and physiology
- Neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) in dreaming
- Frontal cortex reactivation during lucid dreams
- Gamma band activity (40 Hz) as marker of lucidity
- Default mode network and metacognition during sleep

### 2. Phenomenology of Lucid Dreaming
- Subjective experience and first-person reports
- Degrees of lucidity (pre-lucid, semi-lucid, fully lucid)
- Dream control vs. awareness
- Sensory vividness and stability
- False awakenings and hybrid states

### 3. Induction Techniques
- **MILD** (Mnemonic Induction of Lucid Dreams) - Tholey & LaBerge
- **WILD** (Wake-Initiated Lucid Dreams) - hypnagogic transition
- **WBTB** (Wake-Back-to-Bed) - circadian optimization
- **Reality testing** - state testing and critical awareness
- **Dream journaling** - recall enhancement and pattern recognition
- **tDCS and light stimulation** - external neurostimulation
- **Galantamine and acetylcholine** - pharmacological enhancement

### 4. Applications and Research Methods
- Therapeutic applications (PTSD, nightmares, anxiety)
- Motor learning and skill rehearsal
- Creativity and problem-solving
- Communication from lucid dreams (eye-signal paradigm)
- EEG/fMRI validation studies

## Key Sources

### Academic Research
- **LaBerge & Rheingold (1990)** - "Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming" (Stanford foundational work)
- **Voss et al. (2009)** - Nature Neuroscience paper on gamma activity during lucid REM sleep
- **Baird et al. (2019)** - PLOS ONE meta-analysis of induction techniques
- **Konkoly et al. (2021)** - Current Biology paper on real-time communication during lucid dreams
- **Stumbrys et al. (2012)** - International Journal of Dream Research review of induction methods
