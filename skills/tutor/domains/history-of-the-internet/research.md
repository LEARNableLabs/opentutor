# History and Theory of the Internet — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Historical Development
- **Pre-Internet Era (1940s-1960s)** — packet switching theory, ARPANET precursors, time-sharing systems
- **ARPANET (1969-1983)** — first networked communication, TCP/IP development, email invention
- **Network Expansion (1980s-1990s)** — NSFNET, commercialization, WWW invention, browser wars
- **Modern Internet (1990s-present)** — search engines, social media, mobile internet, Web 2.0/3.0

### Theoretical Foundations
- **Network Architecture** — end-to-end principle, packet switching vs circuit switching, layered protocols
- **Protocol Design** — TCP/IP stack, DNS, HTTP/HTTPS, routing protocols
- **Network Effects** — Metcalfe's law, critical mass, platform economics
- **Governance & Standards** — IETF, W3C, ICANN, net neutrality debates
- **Social & Cultural Theory** — digital divides, online communities, information architecture

### Key Technical Milestones
- Packet switching (Paul Baran, Donald Davies)
- TCP/IP standardization (Vint Cerf, Bob Kahn)
- Domain Name System (Paul Mockapetris)
- World Wide Web (Tim Berners-Lee)
- HTTP, HTML, URL standards
- Search algorithms (PageRank)
- Mobile internet protocols

## Learning Challenges for Intermediate Students

1. **Historical vs. Technical Balance** — students may gravitate toward one; both are essential
2. **Abstraction Levels** — understanding both high-level architecture and packet-level detail
3. **Evolution vs. Design** — internet wasn't planned as a whole; emergent properties matter
4. **Social Context** — military origins, academic culture, commercialization tensions
5. **Technical Debt** — why certain "mistakes" (IPv4 address space) persist
