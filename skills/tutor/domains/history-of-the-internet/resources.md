# History and Theory of the Internet — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks & Books

- **"Where Wizards Stay Up Late: The Origins of the Internet"** by Katie Hafner & Matthew Lyon — The definitive narrative history of ARPANET and the early internet. Accessible storytelling for intermediate students. Strong on people and context.
  - Best for: Lessons 1-10 (ARPANET era)
  - https://www.amazon.com/Where-Wizards-Stay-Up-Late/dp/0684832674

- **"Weaving the Web"** by Tim Berners-Lee — First-person account of inventing the World Wide Web. Explains design decisions and philosophy behind open standards.
  - Best for: Lessons 13-14 (WWW invention)
  - https://www.w3.org/People/Berners-Lee/Weaving/

- **"The Master Switch"** by Tim Wu — History of information empires and internet regulation. Essential for understanding governance and net neutrality debates.
  - Best for: Lessons 23-24 (governance, net neutrality)
  - https://timwu.org/the-master-switch/

- **"Tubes: A Journey to the Center of the Internet"** by Andrew Blum — Physical infrastructure of the internet: data centers, submarine cables, exchange points. Makes abstract networks concrete.
  - Best for: Lesson 22 (infrastructure review)
  - https://www.andrewblum.net/tubes

- **"The Innovators"** by Walter Isaacson — Broader computing history with strong sections on internet pioneers. Good biographical context.
  - Best for: Background reading across all modules
  - https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Innovators/Walter-Isaacson/9781476708706

### Online Courses

- **Khan Academy: Computers and the Internet** — Excellent visual explanations of packet switching, routing, TCP/IP, DNS. Perfect for intermediate students.
  - Best for: Lessons 2-3, 8-9, 17-21 (technical foundations)
  - https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/computers-and-internet

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 6.829 Computer Networks** — Advanced technical course, but lecture notes and slides are excellent reference material
  - Best for: Lessons 17-21 (protocol deep dive)
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/electrical-engineering-and-computer-science/6-829-computer-networks-fall-2002/

- **Coursera: Internet History, Technology, and Security** by Charles Severance — Comprehensive course covering history and protocols. Good video lectures.
  - Best for: All modules
  - https://www.coursera.org/learn/internet-history

- **Stanford CS144: Introduction to Computer Networking** — Strong on protocol design and implementation
  - Best for: Lessons 8-9, 17-21
  - https://cs144.github.io/

### Historical Archives

- **Computer History Museum** — Primary source documents, oral histories, timeline. Excellent for finding original ARPANET materials.
  - https://www.computerhistory.org/internethistory/

- **Internet Hall of Fame** — Biographies of internet pioneers (Cerf, Kahn, Tomlinson, Berners-Lee). Great for humanizing the history.
  - https://www.internethalloffame.org/

- **Living Internet** — Comprehensive historical reference covering all aspects of internet history. Well-organized, reliable.
  - https://www.livinginternet.com/

- **RFC Editor** — Archive of all RFCs (Request for Comments). Read original protocol specifications.
  - Key RFCs: 791 (IP), 793 (TCP), 2324 (HTCPCP - April Fools), 1945 (HTTP/1.0)
  - https://www.rfc-editor.org/

- **W3C Archives** — World Wide Web Consortium historical documents, including Berners-Lee's original 1989 proposal
  - https://www.w3.org/History.html

### Interactive Tools

- **How DNS Works** — Beautiful visual explanation of DNS resolution process
  - https://howdns.works/

- **Submarine Cable Map** — Interactive map of undersea internet cables. Makes internet infrastructure tangible.
  - https://www.submarinecablemap.com/

- **Cloudflare Learning Center** — Excellent explanations of DNS, CDN, routing, DDoS, security. Clear diagrams.
  - https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/

- **What Happens When** (GitHub) — Detailed answer to "what happens when you type google.com and press enter?"
  - https://github.com/alex/what-happens-when

- **Internet Archive** — Wayback Machine shows web history. Good for showing how websites evolved.
  - https://archive.org/

- **RIPE Atlas** — Real-time internet measurement platform. Students can run traceroutes, see global routing.
  - https://atlas.ripe.net/

### Code & Experiments

- **Wireshark** — Packet capture tool. Students can see TCP handshakes, DNS queries, HTTP requests in real traffic.
  - https://www.wireshark.org/

- **Packet Tracer** (Cisco) — Network simulation tool. Build virtual networks and experiment with protocols.
  - https://www.netacad.com/courses/packet-tracer

- **Simple HTTP server projects** — Build a basic web server to understand HTTP internals
  - Python: https://docs.python.org/3/library/http.server.html
  - Node.js: https://nodejs.org/en/docs/guides/anatomy-of-an-http-transaction/

### Organizations & Standards Bodies

- **Internet Society (ISOC)** — Global organization for internet policy and development. Excellent educational resources.
  - https://www.internetsociety.org/

- **IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force)** — Where internet standards are developed. Read about the RFC process.
  - https://www.ietf.org/

- **ICANN (Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers)** — Manages DNS root and IP address allocation
  - https://www.icann.org/

- **W3C (World Wide Web Consortium)** — Web standards organization. HTML, CSS, HTTP specifications.
  - https://www.w3.org/

- **Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF)** — Digital rights advocacy. Great for net neutrality, privacy, governance debates.
  - https://www.eff.org/

## People to Look Up

### Internet Pioneers

- **Vint Cerf & Bob Kahn** — Invented TCP/IP, "fathers of the internet"
- **Tim Berners-Lee** — Invented the World Wide Web, HTML, HTTP, URLs
- **Ray Tomlinson** — Invented email, chose the @ symbol
- **Paul Baran & Donald Davies** — Independently invented packet switching
- **Leonard Kleinrock** — Early packet switching theorist
- **Lawrence Roberts** — ARPANET program manager
- **Jon Postel** — RFC editor, managed DNS root, internet governance pioneer
- **Paul Mockapetris** — Invented DNS

### Modern Internet Figures

- **Mitchell Baker** — Mozilla Foundation, open web advocacy
- **Larry Page & Sergey Brin** — PageRank and Google search
- **Marc Andreessen** — Mosaic browser, Netscape, early web popularizer
- **Tim Wu** — Coined "net neutrality", internet law scholar
- **Aaron Swartz** — RSS, Reddit, internet activism
- **Edward Snowden** — NSA surveillance revelations, internet privacy
- **Brewster Kahle** — Internet Archive founder, digital preservation

### Current Researchers & Advocates

- **Cory Doctorow** — Digital rights, internet regulation

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Cross-Discipline Links

- **Internet geography** — Physical infrastructure is not evenly distributed. Most cables land in wealthy nations. Internet has a geography.
  - Resource: https://global-internet-geography.com/

- **Internet and urbanism** — Data centers reshape cities. Fiber routes follow railroads. Internet geography mirrors colonialism.
  - Book: "The Undersea Network" by Nicole Starosielski

- **Memetics and internet culture** — How ideas spread on networks. Network topology affects information flow.
  - Resource: https://knowyourmeme.com/

- **Internet energy consumption** — Data centers use 1-2% of global electricity. Network efficiency matters for climate.
  - Resource: https://www.iea.org/reports/data-centres-and-data-transmission-networks

- **Cybernetics and internet theory** — Norbert Wiener's feedback loops, Vannevar Bush's Memex, Ted Nelson's hypertext. Internet has deep roots.
  - Original: Bush's "As We May Think" (1945)

- **Internet and democracy** — Does internet empower or concentrate power? Arab Spring vs Cambridge Analytica.
  - Resource: https://cyber.harvard.edu/

### Wild Cards (for engagement)

- **April Fools RFCs** — IETF publishes joke RFCs every April 1. RFC 2324 (HTCPCP - coffee pot protocol) is a classic.
  - https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2324.html

- **Internet protocols for unexpected things** — RFC 1149 (IP over Avian Carriers - carrier pigeons)
  - https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1149.html

- **The story of @** — Why Ray Tomlinson chose @ for email. It was available on his keyboard and meant "at"
  - https://www.internethalloffame.org/inductees/ray-tomlinson

- **The first webcam watched a coffee pot** — Cambridge University, 1991. Avoided walking to an empty pot.
  - https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-20439301

- **The Morris Worm** — First internet worm (1988) crashed 10% of the internet. Showed vulnerability at scale.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_worm

- **Project Xanadu** — Ted Nelson's hypertext system predated the web by decades. Still not finished. Beautiful failure.
  - http://xanadu.com/
