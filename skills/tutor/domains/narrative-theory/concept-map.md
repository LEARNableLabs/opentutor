# Narrative Theory — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Story vs. Discourse** — The fundamental distinction between the content (what happened) and the expression (how it's told)
2. **Fabula vs. Syuzhet** — Russian Formalist terms: fabula = chronological events, syuzhet = arranged plot
3. **Narrative Mediation** — All stories are told *through* something; there is no unmediated narrative
4. **Causality** — How narratives create cause-effect relationships from chronological sequences
5. **Emplotment** — The act of shaping events into a meaningful plot structure
6. **Order (Temporal)** — The relationship between story chronology and discourse presentation. Depends on: Story vs. Discourse
7. **Analepsis** — Flashback, retroversion, narrating earlier events later. Depends on: Order
8. **Prolepsis** — Flashforward, anticipation, narrating later events earlier. Depends on: Order
9. **Duration** — The relationship between story time and discourse time (how long vs. how much space). Depends on: Story vs. Discourse
10. **Summary, Scene, Pause, Ellipsis** — Four tempo variations in narrative duration. Depends on: Duration
11. **Frequency** — How many times something is narrated vs. how many times it happened. Depends on: Story vs. Discourse
12. **Singulative, Repeating, Iterative** — Three frequency modes. Depends on: Frequency
13. **Focalization** — Who perceives/sees the events (separate from who narrates them). Depends on: Narrative Mediation
14. **Internal vs. External Focalization** — Whether the perspective is inside or outside a character. Depends on: Focalization
15. **Narrative Levels** — The hierarchy of stories (primary diegesis, embedded stories, metadiegesis). Depends on: Narrative Mediation
16. **Homodiegetic vs. Heterodiegetic** — Narrator inside vs. outside the story world. Depends on: Narrative Levels
17. **Metalepsis** — Transgression of narrative levels (characters addressing readers, narrators entering story). Depends on: Narrative Levels
18. **Unreliable Narration** — When the narrator's account is questionable or deliberately misleading. Depends on: Focalization, Narrative Voice
19. **Free Indirect Discourse** — Blending of narrator's and character's voice/perspective. Depends on: Focalization, Narrative Voice
20. **Character Functions** — Characters as narrative roles rather than psychological entities. Depends on: Causality
21. **Actantial Model** — Greimas's six-role structure (subject, object, sender, receiver, helper, opponent). Depends on: Character Functions
22. **Propp's Functions** — Vladimir Propp's 31 narrative functions in folktales. Depends on: Character Functions
23. **Plot vs. Story** — E.M. Forster's distinction: story is chronology, plot is causation. Depends on: Causality, Emplotment
24. **Narrative Arc** — The shape of rising/falling action, tension, and resolution. Depends on: Plot, Causality
25. **Genre Conventions** — How genre creates expectations and narrative schemas. Depends on: Emplotment, Reader Response
26. **Seriality** — Narrative spread across episodes with deferred closure. Depends on: Narrative Arc, Duration
27. **Narrative Gaps** — Deliberate omissions that readers must infer or fill. Depends on: Reader Response, Focalization
28. **Reader Response** — How audiences actively construct meaning from narrative cues. Depends on: Narrative Mediation, Gaps
29. **Transmedia Narratology** — How narrative operates across different media platforms. Depends on: Medium Specificity, Story vs. Discourse
30. **Unnatural Narratology** — Narratives that violate natural story logic or physical/logical impossibilities. Depends on: All foundations

## Dependencies

### Temporal Concepts Chain
- **Order, Duration, and Frequency** all depend on the foundational **Story vs. Discourse** distinction — you can only analyze how time is manipulated once you can separate what happened from how it's presented
- **Analepsis and Prolepsis** require understanding **Order** — they're specific techniques for reordering chronology
- **Summary/Scene/Pause/Ellipsis** build on **Duration** — they're the specific tempo techniques

### Perspective Chain
- **Focalization** requires grasping **Narrative Mediation** — perspective is one form of mediation
- **Internal/External Focalization** are subcategories that depend on the general concept
- **Unreliable Narration** depends on both **Focalization** and **Narrative Voice** — you need to distinguish who sees from who speaks to identify when the speaker is unreliable
- **Free Indirect Discourse** requires understanding both **Focalization** and **Narrative Voice** — it's the deliberate blurring of both

### Structural Chain
- **Narrative Levels** build on **Narrative Mediation** — all stories are framed, some are multiply framed
- **Homodiegetic/Heterodiegetic** distinctions require understanding **Narrative Levels** — the narrator's position relative to the story world
- **Metalepsis** can only be understood once **Narrative Levels** are clear — it's the violation of level boundaries
- **Frame Narratives** depend on **Narrative Levels** and **Metalepsis**

### Character and Action Chain
- **Character Functions** depend on **Causality** — viewing characters as agents in causal chains rather than people
- **Actantial Model** and **Propp's Functions** are specific systematizations of **Character Functions**
- **Plot vs. Story** requires both **Causality** and **Emplotment** — moving from "and then" to "because"

### Reader-Oriented Chain
- **Narrative Gaps** depend on **Reader Response** and **Focalization** — restricted perspective creates gaps
- **Genre Conventions** link **Emplotment** with **Reader Response** — genres train readers in specific interpretive strategies

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are foundational — many others depend on them:

1. **Story vs. Discourse** — Nearly everything builds on this. Without it, you can't analyze narrative structure systematically
2. **Narrative Mediation** — Understanding that all narratives are told *through* some medium/perspective/voice
3. **Focalization** — Central to contemporary narratology; once mastered, unlocks much of narrative analysis
4. **Causality** — The engine of plot; essential for understanding how narratives create meaning
5. **Narrative Levels** — Key to analyzing complex, embedded, or metafictional narratives

## Mind-Blowing Moments

Points where concepts click and transform how students see stories:

1. **Realizing focalization ≠ narration** — The "aha!" that who *sees* is different from who *speaks*. Changes how you read everything.
2. **Grasping metalepsis** — When you recognize a character addressing you directly or a narrator entering the story, the fourth wall shatters in a new way
3. **Understanding frequency** — Especially iterative narration ("Sundays, my father would...") — recognizing this as a distinct narrative mode, not just convenience
4. **Free indirect discourse** — The moment you see how Austen or Flaubert blend narrator and character voice without quotation marks
5. **Narrative levels in nested structures** — Analyzing *The Arabian Nights* or *Cloud Atlas* and mapping the recursive embedding
6. **Recognizing how duration shapes attention** — Noticing that a novel spends 50 pages on 10 minutes (scene) then skips 10 years in one sentence (ellipsis)

## Common Misconceptions

1. **Focalization = Point of View** — Not quite. Traditional "POV" conflates who sees with who speaks; focalization separates them
2. **Story Order = Chronological** — Students assume "story" means the text's order; actually it's the reconstructed chronology
3. **Unreliable Narrator = Liar** — Unreliability can be from limited knowledge, bias, or values, not just deception
4. **Character = Person** — Narratology treats characters as functions, not psychological beings (though later reader-response theory reintroduces psychology)
5. **Genre is Just Marketing** — Genre creates deep narrative structures and reader expectations, not just surface categorization
6. **Narrator = Author** — The narrator is always a construct, never identical to the author (implied author vs. actual author)
7. **Flashback is Just Memory** — Analepsis is a structural feature of discourse, not necessarily a character's memory
8. **Film POV Works Like Literary POV** — Visual media has different focalization mechanics (camera ≠ character eyes)

## Prerequisite Topics

### Essential
- **Close Reading** — Needed for all narrative analysis; ability to notice textual details
- **Basic Literary/Film Analysis** — Familiarity with discussing stories, themes, characters

### Helpful but Not Required
- **Semiotics** — Genette emerges from structuralism; knowing Saussure helps but isn't essential
- **Linguistics** — Especially tense/aspect for understanding narrative time
- **Film Theory** — For applying narratology to visual media
- **Cognitive Science** — For postclassical/cognitive narratology approaches

## Synergies with Other Topics

- **Film Studies** — Direct application of narrative theory to visual storytelling
- **Game Studies** — Ludology vs. narratology debates; interactive narrative
- **Digital Humanities** — Computational analysis of narrative structures
- **Rhetoric** — Persuasive dimensions of narrative arrangement
- **Philosophy of Time** — Deeper exploration of temporal manipulation
- **Cognitive Science** — How minds process and construct stories
- **Anthropology** — Myth, ritual, and cultural narrative structures
- **Poetics** — Formalist approaches to literary structure
