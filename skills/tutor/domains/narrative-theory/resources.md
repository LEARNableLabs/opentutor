# Narrative Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (Foundational Texts)

### Essential Introductions
- **H. Porter Abbott**: *The Cambridge Introduction to Narrative* (2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2008)
  - Most accessible entry point for intermediate students
  - Clear explanations, excellent examples, covers both classical and postclassical approaches
  - Available via Cambridge Core: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/narrative-theory/9A543E7B4F659EE9796B34E14DB10D3E

- **Mieke Bal**: *Narratology: Introduction to the Theory of Narrative* (3rd ed., University of Toronto Press, 2009)
  - Systematic, comprehensive, slightly more technical than Abbott
  - Strong on focalization and narrative levels
  - Used in most graduate programs

- **Seymour Chatman**: *Story and Discourse: Narrative Structure in Fiction and Film* (Cornell University Press, 1978)
  - Classic text bridging literary and cinematic narrative
  - Essential for transmedia applications

### Gerard Genette's Foundational Work
- **Gerard Genette**: *Narrative Discourse: An Essay in Method* (Cornell University Press, 1983)
  - The origin of modern narratological vocabulary (focalization, narrative levels, time analysis)
  - Can be dense; use alongside secondary sources
  - Full text archive: https://dn790007.ca.archive.org/0/items/NarrativeDiscourseAnEssayInMethod/NarrativeDiscourse-AnEssayInMethod.pdf

- **ResearchGate**: "Time Structure in the Story: Gerard Genette, 'Narrative Discourse'"
  - Academic paper explicating Genette's temporal framework
  - https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318041820_Time_Structure_in_the_Story_Gerard_Genette_'Narrative_Discourse'_Narrative_Theory_3

### Companions and Handbooks
- **Routledge Companion to Narrative Theory** (Brian Richardson, ed.)
  - Comprehensive collection of essays on all major topics and debates
  - Good reference for deep dives
  - https://campusstore.miamioh.edu/routledge-companion-narrative-theory/bk/9781003100157

## Cross-Disciplinary Connections

### Film Studies
- **David Bordwell** and **Kristin Thompson**: *Film Art: An Introduction* — applies narrative theory to cinema
- **Edward Branigan**: *Narrative Comprehension and Film* — focalization in visual media

### Game Studies
- **Jesper Juul**: *Half-Real: Video Games between Real Rules and Fictional Worlds*
- **Ian Bogost**: Procedural rhetoric, how games make arguments through systems

### Cognitive Science
- **Mark Turner**: *The Literary Mind* — cognitive basis of narrative
- **Lisa Zunshine**: *Why We Read Fiction: Theory of Mind and the Novel*

### Philosophy
- **Paul Ricoeur**: *Time and Narrative* — phenomenology of time and storytelling
- **Hayden White**: *Metahistory* — narrative in historical writing

### Anthropology and Folklore
- **Claude Lévi-Strauss**: Structural analysis of myth
- **William Labov**: Oral narrative structure

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

- **Music Theory**: Sonata form as narrative arc; leitmotifs as character markers (Wagner)
- **Architecture**: Spatial narratives, how buildings "tell stories" through sequence of spaces
- **Data Visualization**: Edward Tufte on narrative flow in infographics
- **Law**: Legal storytelling, narrative persuasion in courtrooms
- **Medicine**: Narrative medicine, patient stories in clinical practice (Rita Charon)
- **Economics**: Narrative economics, how stories drive markets (Robert Shiller)
- **Neuroscience**: Neural basis of story comprehension, default mode network activation

## Suggested Reading Order for Intermediate Students

1. **Start**: H. Porter Abbott, *The Cambridge Introduction to Narrative* (Chapters 1-3)
2. **Deepen**: Genette's key concepts via Signo website (interactive)
3. **Apply**: Choose one MIT OCW course and work through materials
4. **Extend**: Mieke Bal, *Narratology* (selected chapters on focalization, levels)
5. **Contemporary**: One transmedia/digital narrative essay (Marie-Laure Ryan, Henry Jenkins)
6. **Primary Source**: Genette, *Narrative Discourse* (challenging but rewarding)

## Topics for Further Exploration

- **Unreliable narration**: Wayne Booth, *The Rhetoric of Fiction*; James Phelan on types of unreliability
- **Narrative ethics**: How stories shape moral understanding (Martha Nussbaum, Adam Zachary Newton)
- **Postcolonial narratology**: How Western narrative theory applies (or doesn't) to non-Western traditions
- **Graphic narratology**: How comics/manga tell stories (Thierry Groensteen, Scott McCloud's *Understanding Comics*)
- **Algorithms and narrative**: Computational generation, AI storytelling, procedural narrative
