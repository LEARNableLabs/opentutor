# Privacy Law and Data Protection — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 10 lessons (36%)
- **Socratic questions** — 6 lessons (21%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 6 lessons (21%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 3 lessons (11%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (7%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 1 lessons (4%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 50% accessible (1-2), 36% standard (3), 14% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 22: "What did the Schrems cases change about EU-US data flows?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 24: "Can anonymization actually protect privacy or is it a myth?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 25: "What privacy-enhancing technologies should every practitioner know?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 27: "How should privacy law address AI and automated decision-making?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers privacy law and data protection, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: privacy as a human right, informational self-determination, historical context, FIPPs, notice and choice, data minimization, accountability, comprehensive vs sectoral regulation, cultural differences, regulatory philosophy, personal data definition, identifiability, pseudonymization vs anonymization, controller vs processor, joint controllers (and 86 more).
