# Privacy Law and Data Protection — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Historical Foundations and Evolution
- Warren and Brandeis "Right to Privacy" (1890)
- Fair Information Practice Principles (FIPPs)
- Evolution from sectoral to comprehensive frameworks
- Cultural differences (EU vs US approaches)

### 2. GDPR and EU Framework
- Scope and territorial reach
- Legal bases for processing
- Data subject rights (access, erasure, portability, etc.)
- Controller vs processor obligations
- Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)
- Supervisory authorities and enforcement

### 3. US Privacy Law Landscape
- Sectoral approach (HIPAA, FERPA, GLBA, COPPA, etc.)
- State comprehensive laws (CCPA/CPRA, Virginia, Colorado, etc.)
- Federal initiatives and proposed legislation
- FTC enforcement under Section 5

### 4. Cross-Border Data Transfers
- Adequacy decisions
- Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)
- Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)
- Schrems I and II implications
- Data localization requirements

### 5. Privacy by Design and Technical Measures
- Privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs)
- Differential privacy, homomorphic encryption
- Pseudonymization and anonymization
- Security and privacy relationship

### 6. Emerging Issues
- AI and automated decision-making
- Biometric data governance
- Privacy in the workplace
- Children's privacy (COPPA, Age-Appropriate Design Code)
- Dark patterns and consent manipulation

## Content Organization Notes

For intermediate learners:
- Assume basic understanding of legal concepts but not deep privacy law knowledge
- Balance between EU (GDPR) and US frameworks — both are essential
- Emphasize practical application (compliance scenarios, case studies)
- Include technical foundations (anonymization, encryption) but not deep cryptography
- Use real-world cases to illustrate abstract principles
- Focus on "why" questions: why do laws differ, why do certain protections exist, why do enforcement actions happen

## Resource Gaps Identified
- Video lectures are scattered (YouTube channels like Computerphile cover technical aspects; IAPP has webinars)
- Interactive compliance simulations are mostly proprietary (OneTrust, TrustArc demos)
- Case study collections exist but are often paywalled (IAPP case reports)
- Best free video series: IAPP YouTube channel, EFF talks, specific university lectures on YouTube
