# Surreal Numbers — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **"On Numbers and Games" by John H. Conway** (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2001, 2nd edition) — The definitive mathematical treatment. Part Zero covers surreal numbers construction and arithmetic. Dense but authoritative. Best for intermediate to advanced students who want rigorous foundations.

- **"Surreal Numbers: How Two Ex-Students Turned on to Pure Mathematics and Found Total Happiness" by Donald E. Knuth** (Addison-Wesley, 1974) — A novelistic introduction told as a dialogue. Highly readable and motivating. Excellent for building initial intuition before tackling Conway's formal treatment.

- **"Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays" by Elwyn Berlekamp, John H. Conway, and Richard K. Guy** (A K Peters, 2001-2004, 2nd edition, 4 volumes) — Comprehensive treatment of combinatorial game theory with extensive surreal number applications. Volume 1 is most relevant. More playful and example-driven than "On Numbers and Games."

- **"An Introduction to the Theory of Surreal Numbers" by Harry Gonshor** (Cambridge University Press, 1986) — Graduate-level textbook with complete proofs. More accessible than Conway's original, with careful exposition of the field structure.

- **"An Introduction to Conway's Games and Numbers" by Dierk Schleicher and Michael Stoll** (arXiv:math/0410026) — Excellent expository paper covering construction, arithmetic, and games. Free and well-written. Good for intermediate students. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410026

## Courses and Lecture Notes

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 18.S34 "Mathematical Problem Solving (Putnam Seminar)"** — Includes sections on combinatorial game theory and surreal numbers. Accessible to strong undergraduates.

- **UC Berkeley CS 294: Combinatorial Game Theory** — Graduate course covering surreal numbers and game applications. Lecture notes sometimes available online.

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: "Mathematical Games"** — Philosophical perspective on surreal numbers and their foundations. Good for understanding the conceptual landscape. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mathematical-games/

## Videos and Visual Explanations

- **Numberphile: "Beyond Infinity Number" series** — Several videos on surreal numbers with visual explanations. Great for building intuition. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYj4NkeGPdM and related videos.

- **Mathologer: Surreal number videos** — Visual and accessible explanations of construction and arithmetic. Excellent for visual learners.

- **John Conway's lectures** (various, search "John Conway surreal numbers lecture") — The creator himself explaining the construction. Charming and insightful.

- **3Blue1Brown: "What does it feel like to invent math?"** — Not specifically about surreals, but captures the creative process Conway used. Motivational.

## Interactive Tools and Software

- **CGSuite (Combinatorial Game Suite)** — Free software for analyzing combinatorial games and computing surreal values. Essential for serious game theory work. https://cgsuite.sourceforge.net/

- **SageMath surreal number class** — Implementation of surreal arithmetic in the Sage computer algebra system. Good for computational exploration. https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/games/sage/games/surreal.html

- **Online Hackenbush solvers** — Various web tools for analyzing Hackenbush positions. Search "Hackenbush solver" for current options.

- **Desmos** — Not surreal-specific, but useful for graphing and visualizing number constructions. https://www.desmos.com/calculator

## Code and Implementations

- **Haskell surreal number libraries** — Several implementations on Hackage, including "surreal" package. Good for understanding lazy evaluation and infinite structures.

- **Python implementations** — Search GitHub for "surreal numbers python" for educational implementations. Useful for building intuition through coding.

- **David Moews' surreal number calculator** — Historical implementation with detailed documentation. https://math.uic.edu/~moews/ (archived materials)

## Papers and Academic Resources

- **Philip Ehrlich: "The Absolute Arithmetic Continuum and the Unification of All Numbers Great and Small"** (Bulletin of Symbolic Logic, 2012) — Deep dive into the structure and universality of surreals.

- **Martin Kruskal: "On the Simplicity of Surreal Numbers"** — Analysis of the canonical form and simplicity theorem.

- **Norman Alling: "Foundations of Analysis over Surreal Number Fields"** (North-Holland, 1987) — Advanced treatment of analysis using surreals.

- **Wikipedia: "Surreal number"** — Comprehensive overview with good references. Start here for quick orientation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surreal_number

- **Stanford Encyclopedia: "Non-standard Analysis"** — Context for infinitesimal applications. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nonstandard-analysis/

## Books on Combinatorial Game Theory

- **"Lessons in Play: An Introduction to Combinatorial Game Theory" by Michael H. Albert, Richard J. Nowakowski, and David Wolfe** (A K Peters, 2007) — Modern textbook with extensive surreal number content. Very well-organized for teaching.

- **"Games of No Chance" series** (MSRI Publications) — Research volumes with papers on game theory and surreals. Advanced but cutting-edge.

## People to Follow

- **Philip Ehrlich** (University of Colorado) — Leading researcher on the structure of surreals and their relationship to other number systems.

- **Richard Guy** (1916-2020) — Co-author of "Winning Ways" and major contributor to combinatorial game theory.

- **Elwyn Berlekamp** (1940-2019) — Co-author of "Winning Ways," connected game theory to coding theory and cryptography.

- **Aaron Siegel** — Developer of CGSuite and author of "Combinatorial Game Theory" (AMS, 2013).

## Unexpected Connections and Wild Cards

- **Surreals and model theory** — Surreals provide models for various logical systems. Connection to Gödel and decidability questions.

- **Physics applications** — Some researchers explore using surreals for quantum field theory and infinitesimal analysis in physics. Speculative but fascinating.

- **Art and visualization** — Fractal-like structure of surreal construction. Visual art exploring infinite recursion.

- **Philosophy of mathematics** — Surreals raise questions about mathematical existence, constructivism vs. Platonism, and the nature of infinity.

- **Trading card game balance** — Game values from surreal analysis applied to analyzing competitive games like Magic: The Gathering or Go.

- **Category theory connections** — Surreals as terminal object in certain categories of ordered fields.

## Textbook Alternatives for Different Learners

- **For pure mathematicians**: Conway's "On Numbers and Games" + Gonshor's textbook
- **For computer scientists**: "Lessons in Play" + CGSuite documentation + implement your own
- **For game players**: "Winning Ways" Vol. 1 + Numberphile videos + hands-on game analysis
- **For philosophers**: Stanford Encyclopedia entries + Knuth's dialogue + Ehrlich's papers
- **For self-learners**: Schleicher & Stoll paper + Wikipedia + Numberphile + SageMath experiments

## Historical Context

- **Conway's original discovery** (early 1970s) — Emerged from analyzing Go endgames. Revolutionary at the time.

- **Knuth's popularization** (1974) — Made the subject accessible to non-specialists. The dialogue format was influential.

- **Gonshor's systematization** (1986) — First full textbook treatment, establishing pedagogical standards.

- **Ehrlich's structural work** (2000s-present) — Deep investigation of the surreals' place among number systems.
