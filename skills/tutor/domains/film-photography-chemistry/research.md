# Film Photography Chemistry — Research Summary

**Note**: Web search budget exhausted. This curriculum draws from established educational sources and professional darkroom chemistry resources in the field.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Silver Halide Fundamentals
- Crystal structure and light sensitivity
- Latent image formation
- Spectral sensitization
- Grain structure and emulsion types

### 2. Film Development Chemistry
- Developer chemistry (reducing agents, accelerators, restrainers, preservatives)
- Stop bath and fixing chemistry
- Development processes (push/pull, compensating, stand)
- Temperature and time relationships
- Contrast control through development

### 3. Printing Chemistry
- Paper emulsions and contrast grades
- Print developer formulations
- Toning chemistry (selenium, sepia, gold, etc.)
- Split toning and creative effects

### 4. Alternative Processes
- Wet plate collodion
- Cyanotype
- Platinum/palladium printing
- Van Dyke brown printing

### 5. Archival Chemistry
- Washing and hypo clearing
- Permanence testing
- Archival processing standards
- Environmental considerations

## Key Sources

### Educational Resources
- **Kodak Professional Photographic Chemicals** — comprehensive technical documentation
- **The Film Developing Cookbook** by Bill Troop & Steve Anchell — formulations and theory
- **Way Beyond Monochrome** by Lambrecht & Woodhouse — advanced chemistry and zone system
- **Alternative Photographic Processes** by Christopherson — historical processes
- **Journal of Photographic Science** — peer-reviewed research

### Online Resources
- George Eastman Museum educational materials
- Photo.net darkroom chemistry forums
- APUG (Analog Photography Users Group) technical discussions
- RIT School of Photographic Arts darkroom curriculum

### Interactive/Visual
- YouTube channels: The Art of Photography (Ted Forbes), Ilford Photo
- Flickr darkroom chemistry groups
- The Massive Dev Chart (online development database)

## Learning Challenges

- Abstract chemistry concepts without lab access
- Safety considerations (working with toxic chemicals)
- Balancing theory with practical application
- Understanding interdependencies between variables
- Translating historical formulas to modern materials
