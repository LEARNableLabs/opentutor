# Film Photography Chemistry — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Photosensitivity** — why silver compounds react to light; fundamental principle underlying all silver-based photography
2. **Silver halide crystals** — the light-sensitive compounds (AgBr, AgCl, AgI) that form photographic emulsions
3. **Reduction reactions** — the chemical process that converts silver ions to metallic silver. Depends on: photosensitivity
4. **Latent image** — the invisible but developable pattern of reduced silver formed by exposure. Depends on: silver halide crystals, reduction reactions
5. **Sensitivity specks** — microscopic sites on crystal surfaces where latent image forms. Depends on: silver halide crystals
6. **Gurney-Mott theory** — explains latent image formation at the molecular level. Depends on: latent image, sensitivity specks
7. **Spectral sensitization** — extending film sensitivity to longer wavelengths using dyes. Depends on: silver halide crystals
8. **Grain structure** — physical arrangement and size of silver crystals affecting speed and sharpness. Depends on: silver halide crystals
9. **Emulsion layers** — how crystals are suspended in gelatin matrix. Depends on: grain structure
10. **Selective reduction** — how developers preferentially reduce exposed crystals. Depends on: latent image, reduction reactions
11. **Reducing agents** — chemicals (phenidone, metol, hydroquinone) that do the actual reduction. Depends on: selective reduction
12. **Oxidation** — the degradation process that limits developer shelf life. Depends on: reducing agents
13. **Preservatives** — chemicals (sulfite) that slow oxidation. Depends on: oxidation
14. **Compensating developers** — formulations that self-limit to control contrast. Depends on: reducing agents, selective reduction
15. **Development contrast control** — using time, temperature, and chemistry to control image gamma. Depends on: selective reduction
16. **Push/pull processing** — deliberate over/underdevelopment for contrast control. Depends on: development contrast control
17. **Sodium thiosulfate (fixer)** — the compound that removes undeveloped silver halides. Depends on: silver halide crystals
18. **Silver complexes** — soluble compounds formed during fixing. Depends on: sodium thiosulfate
19. **Paper emulsion** — how printing materials differ from film emulsions. Depends on: emulsion layers
20. **Variable contrast emulsions** — two-layer systems sensitive to different wavelengths. Depends on: paper emulsion, spectral sensitization
21. **Print developer formulation** — how print developers differ from film developers. Depends on: reducing agents
22. **Toning chemistry** — replacing metallic silver with other compounds for color/permanence. Depends on: reduction reactions
23. **Selenium toning** — protective toning that forms silver selenide. Depends on: toning chemistry
24. **Sepia toning** — converting to silver sulfide for warm tones. Depends on: toning chemistry
25. **Split toning** — selective toning of highlights vs shadows. Depends on: toning chemistry
26. **Iron-based processes** — non-silver processes like cyanotype. Independent foundation
27. **Platinum/palladium printing** — noble metal alternative to silver. Depends on: reduction reactions (parallel to silver)
28. **UV sensitization** — making emulsions respond to UV for contact printing. Depends on: spectral sensitization
29. **Residual silver salts** — unfixed chemicals that cause degradation. Depends on: sodium thiosulfate
30. **Archival washing** — complete removal of processing chemistry. Depends on: residual silver salts, silver complexes
31. **Chemical forensics** — identifying processes from chemical signatures. Depends on: all toning and alternative processes

## Dependencies

### Critical Foundation
- **Photosensitivity → Silver halide crystals → Reduction reactions** forms the core triad. Everything builds from here.
- **Latent image** requires understanding all three foundation concepts.

### Development Chain
- **Selective reduction** is the gateway concept to understanding all development chemistry
- **Reducing agents** depend on selective reduction; all developer formulations build from this
- **Compensating developers** and **contrast control** are advanced applications requiring deep understanding of selective reduction

### Toning & Alternative Processes
- **Toning chemistry** branches from reduction reactions but is semi-independent
- **Iron-based processes** (cyanotype) are conceptually separate from silver chemistry but share procedural similarities
- **Platinum/palladium** parallels silver reduction but uses different chemistry

### Archival Understanding
- **Residual silver salts** requires understanding both **silver halides** and **fixing chemistry**
- **Archival washing** requires understanding **silver complexes** and **residual salts**

## Bottlenecks

### Latent Image Formation (Lessons 2, 7)
Many students struggle with the invisible-but-developable concept. The Gurney-Mott theory is abstract without visualization. If this bottleneck isn't cleared, selective reduction won't make sense.

### Selective Reduction (Lesson 7)
This is the conceptual heart of development. Students need to understand WHY developers choose exposed crystals. Without this, all formulation discussions become memorization.

### Compensating vs Solvent Developers (Lesson 9)
Requires integrating multiple concepts: selective reduction, developer activity, local exhaustion, and edge effects. High cognitive load but essential for aesthetic control.

### Variable Contrast Papers (Lesson 14)
Requires understanding both spectral sensitization AND two-emulsion systems. Abstract without hands-on experience.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Developers make the image"** — No, exposure makes the latent image; developers merely amplify it. This confusion blocks understanding of selective reduction.

2. **"Grain is just random noise"** — Grain has specific structure and chemistry. This misconception prevents understanding speed/sharpness trade-offs.

3. **"All developers do the same thing"** — Missing the distinction between compensating and solvent developers, which is central to aesthetic control.

4. **"Fixer removes the image"** — Fixer removes unexposed silver halides, not the developed silver. Critical for archival understanding.

5. **"Toning is just adding color"** — Toning replaces silver atoms; it's a chemical transformation, not a coating. Essential for understanding split toning and permanence.

6. **"Alternative processes are low-tech versions of silver gelatin"** — Many (iron-based, platinum) use completely different chemistry. Respect the differences.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic chemistry** — needed for understanding reduction/oxidation, pH, temperature effects (lessons 1-11)
- **Redox reactions** — needed for understanding development and toning (lessons 1, 7-11, 16, 20-23)
- **Light and optics** — needed for understanding spectral sensitization and UV processes (lessons 3, 14, 19-22)
- **Film photography basics** — needed to contextualize all chemistry concepts (all lessons)

## Progression Strategy

- Start with the **visible** (silver turning black) and work toward the **invisible** (latent image)
- Introduce **film development** before **print development** — film is conceptually simpler
- Place **alternative processes** after silver mastery — they're easier to understand comparatively
- End with **archival chemistry** — requires understanding the full workflow first
