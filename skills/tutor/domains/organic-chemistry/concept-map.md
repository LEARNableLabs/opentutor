# Organic Chemistry: Reaction Mechanisms — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Electron density and polar bonds** — foundation for understanding electron movement
2. **Curved arrow notation** — formalism for showing electron pair movement. Depends on: 1
3. **Nucleophiles and electrophiles** — electron-rich vs electron-poor species. Depends on: 1, 2
4. **Lewis acid-base theory** — framework for understanding reactivity. Depends on: 3
5. **SN2 mechanism** — concerted nucleophilic substitution. Depends on: 2, 3
6. **Transition states** — energy barriers and reaction coordinates. Depends on: 5
7. **Stereochemistry of SN2** — inversion of configuration. Depends on: 5, 6
8. **Carbocations** — three-coordinate positive carbon intermediates. Depends on: 1, 3
9. **Carbocation stability** — hyperconjugation and resonance effects. Depends on: 8
10. **SN1 mechanism** — stepwise nucleophilic substitution via carbocation. Depends on: 8, 9
11. **Rate-determining steps** — slow step controls overall rate. Depends on: 6, 10
12. **Leaving groups** — factors affecting departure ability. Depends on: 4, 5, 10
13. **Nucleophile strength** — basicity vs nucleophilicity, solvent effects. Depends on: 3, 5
14. **Substrate effects** — steric and electronic factors. Depends on: 5, 10
15. **Solvent effects** — polar protic vs aprotic, solvation. Depends on: 5, 10, 13
16. **E2 mechanism** — concerted elimination. Depends on: 2, 3, 13
17. **Anti-periplanar geometry** — geometric requirement for E2. Depends on: 16
18. **Zaitsev's rule** — regioselectivity in elimination. Depends on: 16
19. **E1 mechanism** — elimination via carbocation. Depends on: 10, 16
20. **Substitution-elimination competition** — factors controlling pathway. Depends on: 5, 10, 16, 19
21. **Electrophilic addition to alkenes** — π bond as nucleophile. Depends on: 3, 8
22. **Markovnikov's rule** — regioselectivity via carbocation stability. Depends on: 9, 21
23. **Aromaticity** — special stability of benzene and derivatives. Depends on: resonance (prerequisite)
24. **Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS)** — mechanism via arenium ion. Depends on: 23, 21
25. **Directing effects** — ortho/meta/para selectivity in EAS. Depends on: 24, resonance
26. **Carbonyl group polarity** — electrophilic carbon in C=O. Depends on: 1, 3
27. **Nucleophilic addition to carbonyls** — tetrahedral intermediate formation. Depends on: 26, 3
28. **Nucleophilic acyl substitution** — addition-elimination at carboxylic acid derivatives. Depends on: 27, 12
29. **Enolates** — α-carbon deprotonation creates nucleophilic carbon. Depends on: 26, 3
30. **Aldol condensation** — enolate addition to carbonyl. Depends on: 29, 27
31. **Carbocation rearrangements** — hydride and alkyl shifts. Depends on: 9, 10
32. **Radical mechanisms** — single-electron transfer, chain reactions. Depends on: 2 (curved arrows for radicals)

## Key Dependencies

### Foundational (must be solid before proceeding)
- **Electron density → curved arrows → nucleophiles/electrophiles** — this sequence is the foundation for all mechanisms
- **Carbocations and their stability** — central to SN1, E1, addition reactions, and rearrangements
- **Transition states vs intermediates** — critical for understanding rate-determining steps and reaction energetics

### Parallel Tracks (can be learned somewhat independently)
- **Substitution (SN1/SN2) and Elimination (E1/E2)** — both involve alkyl halides but different outcomes
- **Aromatic chemistry** — somewhat independent module, requires aromaticity concept first
- **Carbonyl chemistry** — builds on nucleophile/electrophile but is a distinct mechanistic family

### Advanced Dependencies
- **Enolates** — require understanding of both carbonyl chemistry AND carbocation stability (for resonance)
- **Rearrangements** — require deep understanding of carbocation stability
- **Radical mechanisms** — different electron-counting rules, can be taught after ionic mechanisms

## Prerequisite Topics (external to this curriculum)

- **Lewis structures** — needed for: drawing mechanisms, identifying formal charges
- **Resonance theory** — needed for: carbocation stability, enolates, aromatic chemistry, directing effects
- **Stereochemistry** — needed for: SN2 inversion, E2 geometry, addition reactions
- **Acid-base chemistry** — needed for: understanding pKa, nucleophile strength, enolate formation
- **Thermodynamics basics** — needed for: understanding reaction energy diagrams, equilibria
- **Kinetics basics** — needed for: rate laws, rate-determining steps, reaction order

## Bottleneck Concepts (take extra time here)

1. **Carbocation stability** — students who don't master this struggle with SN1, E1, addition, and rearrangements
2. **Curved arrow notation** — poor arrow-pushing skills compound through every subsequent mechanism
3. **Transition state vs intermediate** — confusion here leads to incorrect mechanism proposals
4. **Nucleophile vs base** — distinguishing these roles is critical for predicting substitution vs elimination
5. **Directing effects in aromatic chemistry** — requires both resonance understanding AND mechanistic thinking

## Common Misconceptions

1. **Arrows point at atoms, not at bonds** — arrows show electron PAIR movement, origin and destination
2. **All carbocations are equally unstable** — stability order (tertiary > secondary > primary) is essential
3. **SN1 and E1 always give pure products** — they compete with each other and can give mixtures
4. **Markovnikov's rule is arbitrary** — it reflects thermodynamic preference for more stable carbocation
5. **Resonance structures are real, interconverting forms** — they're contributors to a single hybrid structure
6. **Aromatic substitution destroys aromaticity** — the arenium ion intermediate is not aromatic, but rearomatization drives the reaction
7. **Enolates attack with oxygen** — the α-carbon is the nucleophilic site (carbon nucleophile)
8. **Radical mechanisms follow the same rules as ionic** — different stability patterns and reactivity

## Learning Milestones

- **Milestone 1** (Lessons 1-5): Can draw curved arrows to show electron flow for simple acid-base reactions
- **Milestone 2** (Lessons 6-11): Can distinguish SN1 from SN2 based on substrate, nucleophile, solvent
- **Milestone 3** (Lessons 12-17): Can predict major products for substitution, elimination, and addition reactions
- **Milestone 4** (Lessons 18-24): Can propose mechanisms for aromatic substitution and carbonyl additions
- **Milestone 5** (Lessons 25-26): Can recognize and explain rearrangements and radical pathways

## Concept Connections to Other Domains

- **Synthesis** — mechanisms are the foundation for retrosynthetic analysis
- **Spectroscopy** — understanding intermediates helps interpret reactive species
- **Biochemistry** — enzyme mechanisms use the same principles (NAD+/NADH = hydride transfer, serine proteases = nucleophilic acyl substitution)
- **Materials science** — polymerization mechanisms connect to industrial chemistry
- **Medicinal chemistry** — mechanism-based drug design, covalent inhibitors, prodrugs
- **Physical organic chemistry** — Hammett plots, linear free energy relationships quantify mechanistic effects
