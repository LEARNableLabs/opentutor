# Organic Chemistry: Reaction Mechanisms — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Clayden, Greeves, Warren, Wothers — *Organic Chemistry* (2nd edition, Oxford University Press)**
  - The gold standard for mechanistic organic chemistry. Superb explanations, beautiful diagrams, problem sets organized by difficulty. Chapters 12-41 cover all major mechanism types. Perfect for intermediate students who want depth.
  - Emphasis on understanding WHY reactions happen, not just memorizing outcomes.

- **Klein, David — *Organic Chemistry as a Second Language* (Wiley)**
  - Workbook series focused on skill-building. Volume I covers electron-pushing and acid-base. Volume II covers mechanisms (substitution, elimination, addition).
  - Excellent for deliberate practice of arrow-pushing. Each chapter has hundreds of practice problems with worked solutions.

- **Smith, Janice — *Organic Chemistry* (McGraw-Hill)**
  - Clear, student-friendly textbook. Mechanism boxes highlight key reactions. Good balance of theory and application.
  - Chapters 7-11 (substitution, elimination, addition), 18-22 (carbonyl chemistry), 16 (aromatic chemistry).

- **Anslyn & Dougherty — *Modern Physical Organic Chemistry* (University Science Books)**
  - Advanced reference for students who want to go deeper into kinetics, thermodynamics, and linear free energy relationships.
  - Chapter 7 (energy surfaces, transition states), Chapter 8 (experiments related to structure), Chapter 10 (substitution and elimination).

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Biochemistry

- **Enzyme mechanisms as organic chemistry** — Serine proteases, NAD+/NADH redox, lysozyme carbocation-like intermediates
  - Resource: *Biochemistry* by Berg, Tymoczko, Stryer — Chapter 9 (Catalytic Strategies)
  - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK22586/ (NCBI Bookshelf)

- **Drug mechanisms** — How do antibiotics work? Beta-lactams (penicillin) inhibit cell wall synthesis via acyl-enzyme intermediate (nucleophilic acyl substitution)
  - Resource: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PF36HoQ8Qfw (mechanism of aspirin)

### Materials Science

- **Polymerization mechanisms** — Chain-growth polymers (addition polymerization), step-growth polymers (condensation)
  - Resource: https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Organic_Chemistry/Supplemental_Modules_(Organic_Chemistry)/Polymers

- **Battery chemistry** — Lithium-ion batteries involve organic carbonate solvents and SEI layer formation (radical mechanisms)

### Environmental Chemistry

- **Atmospheric chemistry** — Ozone depletion by CFCs (radical chain mechanisms), smog formation (radical reactions)
  - Resource: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rj2c58Pgxxw (radical polymerization and atmospheric radicals)

### History of Science

- **The Ingold-Robinson-Hughes nomenclature debate** — How SN1, SN2, E1, E2 got their names (1930s kinetic studies)
  - Resource: *The Foundations of Chemical Kinetics* by Laidler

- **Woodward and Hoffmann's orbital symmetry rules** — Nobel Prize-winning work on pericyclic reactions (1960s-70s)
  - Resource: *The Conservation of Orbital Symmetry* by Woodward and Hoffmann

### Philosophy of Chemistry

- **What is a chemical mechanism?** — Epistemology of mechanistic explanation. How do we know a mechanism is correct?
  - Resource: "Mechanisms in Chemistry" (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)
  - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chemistry/

## Tools for Different Learning Styles

- **Visual learners** — ChemTube3D animations, YouTube channels, 3D molecular viewers
- **Kinesthetic learners** — Molecular model kits, drawing mechanisms by hand repeatedly
- **Auditory learners** — Video lectures, explaining mechanisms out loud, study groups
- **Reading/writing learners** — Textbooks, problem sets, writing out mechanisms step-by-step
- **Logical/analytical learners** — Physical organic chemistry (Anslyn & Dougherty), computational modeling, kinetics

## Advanced Topics (Beyond This Curriculum)

- **Pericyclic reactions** — Diels-Alder, sigmatropic rearrangements, electrocyclic reactions (Woodward-Hoffmann rules)
- **Photochemistry** — Excited state mechanisms, photoisomerization, photocyclization
- **Organometallic chemistry** — Transition metal catalysis, oxidative addition, reductive elimination
- **Computational chemistry** — DFT calculations of reaction pathways, transition state optimization
- **Linear free energy relationships** — Hammett plots, Taft parameters, quantifying electronic effects
- **Isotope effects** — Kinetic isotope effects (KIE) as mechanistic probes
- **Non-classical carbocations** — Norbornyl cation controversy, bridged intermediates
- **Reactive intermediates** — Carbenes, nitrenes, arynes, benzyne chemistry

For students who master this curriculum and want more, start with pericyclic reactions (read *Pericyclic Reactions* by Ian Fleming) or organometallic chemistry (read *The Organometallic Chemistry of the Transition Metals* by Crabtree).
