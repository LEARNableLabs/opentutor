# Volcanology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Volcanoes: Global Perspectives** by John P. Lockwood and Richard W. Hazlett
  - Comprehensive, accessible, well-illustrated
  - Excellent for intermediate students—balances description with process explanation
  - Strong on hazards and monitoring applications
  - Available through university libraries and publishers

- **Encyclopedia of Volcanoes** (2nd Edition) edited by Haraldur Sigurdsson et al.
  - Reference-level depth across all subdisciplines
  - Individual chapters work well as targeted deep-dives
  - More advanced than curriculum needs, but excellent for rabbit holes

- **Volcanism** by Hans-Ulrich Schmincke
  - Process-focused approach aligned with intermediate curriculum goals
  - Strong on magma genesis and eruption dynamics
  - European perspective complements US-focused resources

- **Volcanic Processes** by Robin Gill (Oxford University Press)
  - Concise primer on mechanisms
  - Good for quick conceptual reviews

### Open Educational Resources

- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Introduction to Geology (12.001)**
  - Includes volcanism modules with lecture notes and problem sets
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/earth-atmospheric-and-planetary-sciences/

- **Open University: Volcanoes, Earthquakes and Tsunamis**
  - Free online course covering fundamentals
  - https://www.open.edu/openlearn/science-maths-technology/volcanoes-earthquakes-and-tsunamis

- **Oregon State University Volcano World**
  - Educational resource with teaching materials
  - http://volcano.oregonstate.edu

## Supplementary Resources

### Video Channels and Series

- **GeologyHub** (YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/@GeologyHub)
  - Detailed analyses of current eruptions
  - Excellent for case studies (Cumbre Vieja, Hunga Tonga, etc.)
  - High production quality, intermediate-appropriate depth

- **Nick Zentner** (Central Washington University)
  - Engaging geology lectures including Pacific Northwest volcanism
  - YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/@GeologyNick
  - Good for general engagement and Pacific Northwest context

- **IRIS Education** (https://www.iris.edu/hq/inclass/)
  - Seismology and volcano monitoring animations
  - "How Earthquakes Happen" and volcano-related content
  - Strong on geophysical monitoring techniques

- **VolFilm** (https://www.volfilm.com/)
  - Curated video database of volcanic phenomena
  - High-quality footage of eruptions, flows, and hazards
  - Subscription required but worth it for teaching

- **USGS Video Library**
  - https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos
  - Filter for volcano content—extensive archive from Hawaii, Cascades, Alaska

### Interactive Tools and Simulations

- **USGS Real-Time Volcano Monitoring**
  - https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/volcanoes
  - Live seismograms, webcams, deformation data for US volcanoes
  - Essential for connecting concepts to real-world observations

- **Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program**
  - https://volcano.si.edu
  - Comprehensive database of all Holocene volcanoes
  - Weekly Volcanic Activity Reports
  - Search by volcano name, location, or eruption characteristics

- **MIROVA** (Middle InfraRed Observation of Volcanic Activity)
  - https://www.mirovaweb.it/
  - Near-real-time thermal monitoring from satellites
  - Excellent for tracking lava effusion rates

- **InSAR Map of Ground Deformation**
  - https://volcanoes.usgs.gov/vsc/file_mngr/file-151/INSAR.pdf
  - Visualize ground deformation from satellite radar
  - NASA's ARIA project provides public InSAR products

- **Eruption Column Height Calculator**
  - Basic physics calculators available through educational sites
  - Good for quantitative exercises on eruption energetics

- **Google Earth Volcano Tours**
  - Pre-made tours of volcanic features
  - 3D visualization of calderas, lava flows, volcano morphology

### Data and Monitoring Networks

- **USGS Volcano Hazards Program** — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP
  - US volcano observatories (HVO, CVO, AVO, YVO, CalVO)
  - Real-time data, hazard assessments, eruption updates

- **INGV** (Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia, Italy) — https://www.ingv.it/en/
  - Monitoring for Etna, Stromboli, Vesuvius, Campi Flegrei
  - Excellent webcams and real-time seismic data

- **GNS Science** (New Zealand) — https://www.gns.cri.nz/Home/Our-Science/Natural-Hazards/Volcanic-Hazards
  - Monitoring for Ruapehu, Tongariro, White Island
  - Strong on volcanic hazard mapping

- **JMA** (Japan Meteorological Agency) — https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/en/Activities/volcano.html
  - Extensive monitoring network across Japan
  - Volcanic alert levels and eruption warnings

- **SERNAGEOMIN** (Chile) — https://www.sernageomin.cl/
  - Monitoring for the Chilean volcanic arc
  - Spanish language but good visual data

### Case Study Archives

- **1980 Mount St. Helens Eruption** — https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/cvo/science/1980-cataclysmic-eruption
  - Comprehensive archive: precursors, eruption, aftermath
  - Ideal for teaching lateral blasts, lahars, monitoring

- **2010 Eyjafjallajökull Eruption** — https://www.vedur.is/media/jar/Eyjafjallajokull_2010.pdf
  - Aviation impacts, phreatomagmatic eruptions
  - IMO (Icelandic Met Office) archives

- **2018 Kilauea Lower East Rift Zone Eruption** — https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/2018-lower-east-rift-zone-eruption
  - Modern basaltic fissure eruption with excellent documentation
  - Lava flows, fountains, summit collapse

- **2021 Cumbre Vieja Eruption (La Palma)** — https://www.ign.es/web/resources/volcanologia/html/CA_vulca_la_palma.html
  - Recent Strombolian eruption with massive lava flows
  - Excellent media coverage and real-time monitoring documentation

- **2022 Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha'apai Eruption** — https://www.gns.cri.nz/news/hunga-tonga-hunga-haapai-eruption/
  - Exceptional submarine eruption
  - Tsunami generation, atmospheric shock waves, satellite observations

## Tools and Software

- **GeoMapApp** — http://www.geomapapp.org/
  - Free mapping tool with bathymetry and volcano locations
  - Good for visualizing volcanic distribution and plate boundaries

- **QGIS with Volcano Plugins**
  - Free GIS software, can load USGS and Smithsonian volcano datasets
  - https://qgis.org/

- **Seismic Sound Visualization**
  - Convert seismograms to audio to "hear" volcanic tremor
  - https://www.iris.edu/hq/inclass/animation/seismicsound_spectrogramsonification

- **Google Earth Engine** — https://earthengine.google.com/
  - Access satellite imagery archives
  - Can track volcanic plumes, thermal anomalies, landscape changes over time
  - Requires some programming (JavaScript/Python) but has tutorials

- **PyroFlow** (GitHub: https://github.com/pyroflow/)

## Unexpected Connections

- **Volcanoes and Wine**: Volcanic soils (tuff, basalt) in Santorini, Etna, Vesuvius produce distinctive terroirs. Geochemistry → agriculture → culture.

- **Lunar and Martian Volcanism**: Comparative planetology reveals universal volcanic processes. NASA's planetary missions provide educational imagery.

- **Icelandic Sagas and Eruptions**: Historical accounts of medieval eruptions (Laki 1783-84) preserved in written records. Literature meets geology.

- **Volcanic Glass in Archaeology**: Obsidian sourcing traces ancient trade networks. Chemistry fingerprinting connects distant cultures.

- **Volcanic Pigments in Art**: Ultramarine (lapis lazuli), ochres from volcanic minerals. Art history connection.

- **Deep-Sea Hydrothermal Vents**: Submarine volcanism supports unique ecosystems. Astrobiology implications for life's origins.

- **Carbon Cycle and Long-Term Climate**: Volcanic outgassing vs. silicate weathering regulates atmospheric CO₂ over millions of years. Deep time climate connection.

- **Volcanic Hazards in Urban Planning**: Naples, Tokyo, Seattle face volcanic risk. How do societies balance risk and development? Urban planning + volcanology.

## Note on Resource Quality

All URLs listed are institutional (.gov, .edu) or established scientific organizations. Avoid general Wikipedia for primary lesson content (use for quick orientation only). Always cross-reference claims with peer-reviewed sources. For breaking eruption news, use official observatory websites (USGS, INGV, GNS, etc.) rather than media reports—reporters often misunderstand volcanic processes.

When using YouTube videos, preview for accuracy. GeologyHub and IRIS are consistently reliable; random channels vary widely in quality.
