# Volcanology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Encyclopedia of Volcanoes** (Sigurdsson et al., eds., Academic Press, 2nd ed. 2015; 1st ed. 2000) — the standard reference. Use chapter by chapter for magma properties, eruption styles, monitoring and hazards. Dense, so use as a backing source and not as reading for the student. Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780123859389/the-encyclopedia-of-volcanoes
- **Giordano, Russell & Dingwell (2008), "Viscosity of magmatic liquids: A model"**, Earth and Planetary Science Letters 271 — the authoritative reference behind the viscosity thread of the course. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2008.03.038
- **USGS Volcano Hazards Program** — official US monitoring, hazard and educational pages. Good for case studies (Mount St. Helens, Kīlauea, Pinatubo). https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP
- **USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory** — current Kīlauea activity updates and archived eruption reports. https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo
- **Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program** — eruption database, weekly activity reports, VEI values, volcano profiles. https://volcano.si.edu/
- **Cnudde & Boone (2013), "High-resolution X-ray computed tomography in geosciences"**, Earth-Science Reviews 123 — optional reading on how vesicles and crystals in volcanic rocks are imaged. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2013.04.003
- **Open University / OpenLearn** — free introductory earth science and volcano material. https://www.open.edu/openlearn/

## Supplementary (for engagement)

- **Videos**
  - IRIS (Incorporated Research Institutions for Seismology) educational animations on earthquakes, tectonics and volcano seismology. https://www.iris.edu/hq/
  - USGS YouTube channel — eruption footage, Kīlauea lava lake and Mount St. Helens retrospectives. https://www.youtube.com/@USGS
  - Volcano World (Oregon State University) — volcano profiles, photos and teaching material. https://volcano.oregonstate.edu/
- **Interactive tools**
  - Smithsonian GVP Volcanoes of the World map and eruption search. https://volcano.si.edu/
  - USGS earthquake map (to see seismicity at volcanic arcs). https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/map/
  - Giordano et al. viscosity calculator, published by the authors' group (search the paper title for the current host).
- **Code**
  - MSNoise — Python package for monitoring seismic velocity changes with ambient noise, an example of real volcano-monitoring software. https://github.com/ROBelgium/MSNoise
  - QuakeMigrate — Python package for automatic earthquake detection and location. https://github.com/QuakeMigrate/QuakeMigrate
  - REDPy — repeating earthquake detector, useful for earthquake swarms before eruptions. https://github.com/ahotovec/REDPy
  - TITAN2D — modelling of geophysical mass flows (pyroclastic flows, avalanches). https://github.com/TITAN2D/titan2d
- **Institutions and people to look up**
  - INGV (Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia) — Italian monitoring of Etna, Stromboli and Vesuvius. https://www.ingv.it/
  - Donald Dingwell and Daniele Giordano — magma rheology research.
  - Chris Newhall and Richard Hoblitt — Pinatubo-era forecasting and the VEI (Newhall & Self 1982).
  - Maurice and Katia Krafft — volcanologists and filmmakers who died at Unzen in 1991.
- **Unexpected connections**
  - Tambora 1815 and the 'year without a summer' — links to climate history and the origins of Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein*.
  - Pinatubo 1991 cooling — a natural analogue for debates on stratospheric aerosol injection.
  - Nevado del Ruiz 1985 — a case study in risk communication and institutional failure.
  - Io, Venus and Mars volcanism — volcanology beyond Earth.
  - Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (Monaghan 2005, https://doi.org/10.1088/0034-4885/68/8/r01) — how lava and flow models are simulated, as an optional rabbit hole.

## Notes on Source Use

- Rely on the USGS and Smithsonian GVP for case-study facts, and cite them in lessons.
- Check numerical values (viscosities, temperatures, VEI assignments) against the Encyclopedia of Volcanoes or the GVP database before teaching them.
- The Zhou et al. (2006) South China granitoid paper in the research set is regional petrology, out of scope, and should not be used.

## Lesson by lesson

Found by web search; every link was checked.

### Lesson 13: What makes liquid magma shatter into ash?
- ▶ [Rock ID With Willsey, Ep #5: Volcanic Rocks (Tuff, Obsidian, Pumice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xX6YGpk3DWk) — A geology professor holds up the actual rocks, so you see that obsidian and pumice can be the same melt, where one kept its gas and the other frothed into foam.
- ▶ [Volcanic eruption explained – Steven Anderson (TED-Ed)](https://ed.ted.com/lessons/what-makes-volcanoes-erupt-steven-anderson) — A short animation that makes the soda-bottle idea concrete: drop the pressure on gas-rich magma and bubbles decide whether it oozes or blows apart.
- 📑 [Magma Fragmentation – Helge Gonnermann (Annual Review, open PDF)](https://gonnermann.rice.edu/publications/gonnermann-2015-magma-fragmentation.pdf) — The field's own review of how magma breaks apart. Skim the intro and figures to see why runny magma gets torn into sheets and threads while sticky magma cracks like glass.

### Lesson 14: From Hawaiian fountains to Plinian columns: how do you measure how big an eruption was?
- ▶ [BRITANNICA FILE: The 6 types of volcanic eruptions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iic5VEpIdgE) — Runs the eruption styles side by side, from gentle Hawaiian lava to Plinian columns, so the spectrum sticks as pictures, not a list of names.
- 🕹 [Significant Global Volcanic Eruptions Map: Explore 887 Events by VEI, Deaths, and Tsunamis](https://mapscaping.com/significant-global-volcanic-eruptions-map/) — Filter real eruptions by VEI and you can see the log scale at work: small eruptions everywhere, giant ones very rare.

### Lesson 15: How far and how fast can lava travel, and why can you usually walk away from it?
- ▶ [Lava: The Hazard (VolFilm)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RhXdDA15SR4) — Made by volcanologists and filmmakers, it shows the odd thing about lava: it almost never kills anyone, yet it destroys everything in its path.
- ▶ [The Types of Lava Flows; ʻAʻā, Pāhoehoe, and More](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=vkaGvP8IKJM) — Walks through ropy pāhoehoe, jagged ʻaʻā, block lava and pillow lava, so you can name a flow just by looking at its surface.
- ▶ [Time Series of Kīlauea Volcano's Lower East Rift Zone Lava-Flow Maps](https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/time-series-kilauea-volcanos-lower-east-rift-zone-lava-flow-maps) — Near-daily USGS maps from 2018 play like a time-lapse, showing lava slowly spreading over Leilani Estates and out to the ocean.

### Lesson 16: What goes up an eruption column, and why did Iceland's ash cloud shut down Europe's airspace?
- ▶ [Iceland Eyjafjallajökull Volcano Eruption, 2010 Ash Cloud Dispersal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pkyNoJupihA) — Shows how a mid-sized eruption in Iceland ended up over European flight paths and caused the biggest air-travel shutdown since WWII.
- 📄 [Video analysis of Iceland 2010 eruption could improve volcanic ash forecasts for aviation safety](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/video-analysis-of-iceland-2010-eruption-could-improve-volcanic-ash-forecasts-for-aviation-safety) — Cambridge researchers measured the plume's behaviour from ordinary video footage, which shows that the jet and buoyant-plume physics are things you can actually watch.
- 📄 [Airborne Volcanic Ash—A Global Threat to Aviation (USGS Fact Sheet)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2010/3116/fs2010-3116.pdf) — A short primary source on how ash columns can reach jet cruising height in minutes and how glassy ash makes engines fail.
- 📄 [A history of ash clouds and aviation](https://all-geo.org/volcan01010/2014/02/a-history-of-ash-clouds-and-aviation/) — A volcanologist tells the earlier near-disasters with ash, which explain why Europe chose to ground everything in 2010.

### Lesson 17: Why are pyroclastic density currents the deadliest thing a volcano does?
- ▶ [Pyroclastic Flows: The Hazard (VolFilm)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N4-5kLbHY2Y) — Volcanologists explain on real footage why you cannot outrun or shelter from a hot avalanche of gas and rock.
- ▶ [The 1902 Eruption of Mount Pelee — USGS (1980s)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IeskQETCT8Q) — An old-school USGS and Michigan Tech documentary on how one glowing cloud wiped out the city of Saint-Pierre, about 28,000–30,000 people, in minutes.
- 📄 [1980 Cataclysmic Eruption (USGS)](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mount-st.-helens/science/1980-cataclysmic-eruption) — Follows the chain at St. Helens step by step: the landslide uncorked a pressurized dome of magma and set off a sideways blast that flattened about 600 km² of forest.
- ▶ [May 18, 1980 Mount St. Helens Eruption: Stories from USGS Scientists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p5RohaQ3wfk) — The geologists who were there describe what it was like to watch a sideways blast no scientist had ever recorded before.

### Lesson 18: Review: given a plume height and an erupted volume, what happened and who was in danger?
- 📄 [How Big Was That Eruption? Check the Volcanic Explosivity Index! (UCAR)](https://scied.ucar.edu/image/how-big-was-eruption-check-volcanic-explosivity-index) — One graphic that links plume height and erupted volume to a VEI number. Keep it next to you while you work through the review questions.
- 📄 [Amount of magma fragmentation and resulting dispersal of tephra (USGS figure)](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/amount-magma-fragmentation-and-resulting-dispersal-tephra) — A single USGS diagram that ties lesson 13 to lesson 16: the more finely the magma shatters, the farther its tephra travels.
- ▶ [Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jlPB0MWyA5o) — Works out the VEI for one real eruption, Taal in 2020, which is exactly the kind of reasoning this review asks you to do.
- 🕹 [Global Volcanism Program (Smithsonian) – eruptions by year](https://volcano.si.edu/faq/index.cfm?question=eruptionsbyyear) — The official record behind every VEI number. Look up any eruption from the course and check your own classification against it.

### Lesson 31: Why does a moon of Jupiter have more active volcanoes than Earth?
- ▶ [The Bizarre Characteristics of Io (Astrum)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=98iCzrNRWmQ) — Astrum's footage makes Io feel like a real place: a moon that Jupiter squeezes so hard it is turning itself inside out, with lava fountains taller than any on Earth.
- 📄 [NASA's Juno Gets a Close-Up Look at Lava Lakes on Jupiter's Moon Io](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/juno/nasas-juno-gets-a-close-up-look-at-lava-lakes-on-jupiters-moon-io/) — This is the primary source: infrared views of lava lakes up to 200 km across, each with a crusted center and a glowing ring at the edge, which tells you right away that Io works on a different scale from Earth.
- 📄 [This Is Why The Solar System's Largest Volcano Will Always Be Martian](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robinandrews/2018/08/31/this-is-why-mars-will-always-have-the-solar-systems-largest-volcano/) — Volcanologist Robin Andrews explains why low gravity alone doesn't account for Olympus Mons, and why the bigger factor is that Mars never moved its crust off the hotspot.
- 📄 [Cryovolcanism's Song of Ice and Fire (Eos)](https://eos.org/features/cryovolcanisms-song-of-ice-and-fire) — Here the volcano's 'magma' is salty water and the 'rock' is ice, so you can check which Earth rules about buoyancy and pressure still hold on Enceladus and Europa.

### Lesson 32: Capstone: you run the volcano observatory and the mayor is on the phone. What do you tell them?
- ▶ [Told to Stay At Home: The Armero Disaster 1985 (Plainly Difficult)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NpD16effFiQ) — Scientists had a hazard map that put Armero in the lahar path, yet people were told to stay home, and this video shows in a few minutes what the 'mayor phone call' looks like when it goes wrong.
- ▶ [How to predict a volcanic eruption (The Royal Society)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=um3APcwT8FQ) — Scientists explain how they read the warning signs, so you understand what the data can and can't promise before you give the mayor a number.
- 📑 [Volcano alert level systems: managing the challenges of effective volcanic crisis communication](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6560653/) — This open-access paper looks at how scientists turn uncertain data into a single alert level while dealing with politics and nervous officials, which is exactly the job in this capstone.
- 📄 [How the Armero Tragedy Changed Volcanology in Colombia (Eos)](https://eos.org/articles/how-the-armero-tragedy-changed-volcanology-in-colombia) — It shows the fix after the failure: Colombia went from one ignored map to 600 monitoring stations and 14 hazard maps, which gives you a model for a warning system people actually trust.
