# Screenwriting and Dramatic Structure — Research Summary

## Topic Overview

Screenwriting is the craft of writing scripts for film and television, with dramatic structure as its foundation. Intermediate study focuses on understanding the architectural frameworks that make stories work (three-act structure, beat sheets, character arcs) and applying them to create compelling narratives.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Foundational Dramatic Structure
- Three-act paradigm (setup, confrontation, resolution)
- Turning points and plot points
- Inciting incident, midpoint, climax
- Character transformation arcs

### 2. Modern Beat Frameworks
- Save the Cat (Blake Snyder's 15-beat structure)
- Hero's Journey (Campbell/Vogler)
- Sequence method (8-sequence structure)
- Genre-specific structures

### 3. Character Development
- Protagonist vs. antagonist construction
- Character wants vs. needs
- Internal and external conflict
- Character biographies and backstory

### 4. Scene-Level Craft
- Scene structure (goal, conflict, disaster/setback)
- Dialogue principles
- Visual storytelling and description
- Format and formatting conventions

### 5. Story Development Process
- Loglines and pitches
- Treatments and outlines
- Beat sheets
- Revision and rewriting strategies

### Foundational Textbooks
- **Screenplay** by Syd Field (1979) — codified three-act structure for a generation
- **Save the Cat!** by Blake Snyder — modern 15-beat framework
- **Story** by Robert McKee — deep exploration of why structure matters
- **The Hero with a Thousand Faces** by Joseph Campbell — mythic structure foundation

## Research Gaps

None significant for intermediate curriculum. Ample academic and professional resources available across all major subtopics. Strong mixture of theoretical frameworks (three-act, hero's journey) and practical beat-by-beat methods (Save the Cat). Good balance of feature film and TV writing resources.

## Recommended Approach

For intermediate students, start with foundational three-act structure, introduce Save the Cat as a practical framework, emphasize character wants/needs distinction, and build toward complete feature outline development. Use real screenplay examples and scene analysis throughout.
