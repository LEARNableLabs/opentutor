# Screenwriting and Dramatic Structure — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 7 lessons (27%)
- **Socratic questions** — 7 lessons (27%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (15%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 3 lessons (12%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (4%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 35% accessible (1-2), 46% standard (3), 19% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 10: "What happens when internal and external conflict align at the climax?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 14: "How do you keep Act Two from sagging in the middle?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 17: "How does Act Three deliver on every promise you made in Act One?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "Can you break your feature into eight 10-15 page sequences?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 22: "How do genre conventions shape structure in thriller vs. romance vs. comedy?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers screenwriting and dramatic structure, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: three-act structure, setup-confrontation-resolution, narrative arc, inciting incident, catalyst, status quo disruption, plot points, turning points, point of no return, midpoint, false victory, false defeat, stakes escalation, climax, resolution (and 81 more).
