# Urban Planning and City Science — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 8 lessons (29%)
- **Socratic questions** — 5 lessons (18%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (18%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 3 lessons (11%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 3 lessons (11%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 32% accessible (1-2), 39% standard (3), 29% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 8: "Why do highway expansions often fail to reduce traffic?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 10: "How do we measure transportation equity?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 14: "How does single-family zoning shape segregation and affordability?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 18: "What can street network analysis reveal about urban form?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "How do agent-based models help us simulate urban dynamics?" (difficulty 5)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers urban planning and city science, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: urban morphology, street networks, historical layering, pedestrian experience, human scale, Jane Jacobs principles, population density, built density, agglomeration effects, connectivity analysis, observational methods, spatial patterns, public space design, social infrastructure, placemaking (and 67 more).
