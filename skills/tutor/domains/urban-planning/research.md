# Urban Planning and City Science — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Urban Form and Morphology
- Street networks and connectivity patterns
- Density gradients and spatial distribution
- Block structure and parcel geometry
- Historical evolution of urban form

### 2. Transportation and Mobility
- Multi-modal transport systems
- Transit-oriented development (TOD)
- Network analysis and accessibility metrics
- Traffic flow dynamics and modeling

### 3. Land Use and Zoning
- Zoning regulations and their effects
- Mixed-use development
- Form-based codes vs. Euclidean zoning
- Impact of regulations on housing affordability

### 4. Computational Urban Science
- Spatial data analysis and GIS
- Agent-based modeling of cities
- Network science applications
- Urban data platforms and APIs

### 5. Sustainability and Resilience
- Green infrastructure and urban ecology
- Climate adaptation strategies
- Energy efficiency in urban systems
- Circular economy principles

### 6. Social Dimensions
- Environmental justice and equity
- Gentrification and displacement
- Participatory planning methods
- Community engagement strategies

### 7. Urban Economics
- Agglomeration effects
- Land value capture
- Housing markets and affordability
- Economic development strategies

## Key Educational Sources

### Academic Programs
- **MIT Department of Urban Studies and Planning** — pioneering computational approaches, emphasis on data-driven methods
- **Harvard Graduate School of Design** — focus on design and physical planning
- **UC Berkeley Department of City and Regional Planning** — strong environmental planning emphasis
- **LSE Cities** — research center focused on urban age challenges

### Textbooks and Core References
- "The Death and Life of Great American Cities" by Jane Jacobs — foundational critique of modernist planning
- "Cities for People" by Jan Gehl — human-centered design approach
- "The Triumph of the City" by Edward Glaeser — economic perspective on urbanization
- "Urban Design: Street and Square" by Cliff Moughtin — design fundamentals
- "Introduction to Urban Science" edited by Luis M.A. Bettencourt and others — computational approaches

### Online Courses

## Research Gaps and Challenges

- Rapidly evolving computational methods require current sources
- Interdisciplinary nature means drawing from geography, economics, sociology, design, computer science
- Need to balance theoretical frameworks with practical application
- Important to include diverse global perspectives beyond North American/European contexts
- Critical to address equity and justice issues throughout, not as separate topic

## Notes for Curriculum Design

For an **intermediate level** student, assume:
- Basic familiarity with cities and urban issues
- Some exposure to social science or design thinking
- Comfort with data and visualizations (though not necessarily programming)
- Interest in both physical form and social systems

Curriculum should:
- Build from observable patterns to analytical frameworks
- Mix physical/spatial topics with social/economic ones
- Include hands-on engagement with tools and data
- Connect historical context to contemporary challenges
- Emphasize systems thinking and interconnections
- Use real-world case studies throughout
