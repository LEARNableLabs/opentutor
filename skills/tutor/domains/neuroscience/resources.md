# Neuroscience — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Bear, Connors & Paradiso — "Neuroscience: Exploring the Brain" (3rd ed.)**
  - Comprehensive intro text used by MIT 9.01
  - Clear explanations, excellent figures, clinical boxes
  - Perfect for intermediate students — neither too simple nor too advanced
  - Covers cellular through cognitive neuroscience

- **Purves et al. — "Neuroscience" (6th ed.)**
  - More systems/clinical focus than Bear
  - Strong on sensory and motor systems
  - Good for students interested in medical applications

- **Kandel, Schwartz & Jessell — "Principles of Neural Science" (5th ed.)**
  - The definitive reference — encyclopedic
  - Use for deep dives on specific topics, not cover-to-cover reading
  - Advanced but authoritative

### University Courses

- **MIT OCW 9.01 — Introduction to Neuroscience**
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-01-introduction-to-neuroscience-fall-2007/
  - Complete lecture notes, problem sets, exams
  - Aligns closely with this curriculum
  - Uses Bear textbook

- **MIT OCW HST.131 — Introduction to Neuroscience**
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-131-introduction-to-neuroscience-fall-2005/
  - Medical/clinical perspective
  - Good for real-world applications and disease examples

- **MIT OCW 9.29J — Introduction to Computational Neuroscience**
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-29j-introduction-to-computational-neuroscience-spring-2004/
  - Mathematical foundations: Hodgkin-Huxley, cable theory, coding
  - For students comfortable with differential equations

- **MIT OCW 9.10 — Cognitive Neuroscience**
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-10-cognitive-neuroscience-spring-2006/
  - Higher-level functions: attention, memory, language
  - Complements cellular/systems material

- **MIT OCW 9.40 — Introduction to Neural Computation**
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-40-introduction-to-neural-computation-spring-2018/
  - Quantitative approaches to brain function
  - Data analysis, statistics, computational modeling

### Interactive Tools

- **Neuronify** — Build and explore neural circuits
  - https://www.eneuro.org/content/4/2/ENEURO.0022-17.2017
  - Available on Android, iOS, Windows, Mac, Linux
  - Plug-and-play circuit construction (no programming required)
  - **Best for**: Lessons 16-17 (neural circuits), hands-on exploration
  - Lets students build circuits and immediately see behavior

- **TensorFlow Neural Network Playground**
  - https://playground.tensorflow.org/
  - Interactive visualization of artificial neural networks
  - **Best for**: Connecting biological and artificial networks
  - Adjust parameters and see effects in real-time

- **NEST Desktop** — Web-based neuronal network modeling
  - https://ebrains.eu/data-tools-services/modelling-simulation/network-level-simulation
  - More advanced than Neuronify
  - Visual modeling language with automatic script generation
  - **Best for**: Students who want to go deeper on computational modeling

- **Neural Circuit Visualizer (NCV)**
  - https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-44588-0
  - Full-scale circuit simulation (hippocampus focus)
  - Public online resource
  - **Best for**: Lesson 28 (hippocampus and memory)

- **The Virtual Brain (TVB) on EBRAINS**
  - https://ebrains.eu/data-tools-services/modelling-simulation
  - Personalized brain network models
  - Advanced platform with prepackaged modules
  - **Best for**: Students interested in whole-brain modeling

- **nnplayground.com** — Neural network visualization
  - https://nnplayground.com/
  - Simple educational visualizer
  - Implements backpropagation
  - Good comparison point for biological vs. artificial networks

### People to Follow

- **Eve Marder** — Neural circuits and neuromodulation, pioneering work on small networks
- **Terrence Sejnowski** — Computational neuroscience, co-discoverer of Boltzmann machines
- **Karl Deisseroth** — Inventor of optogenetics, revolutionized experimental neuroscience
- **Mriganka Sur** — Cortical plasticity and sensory processing
- **May-Britt and Edvard Moser** — Nobel laureates for discovery of grid cells (spatial navigation)
- **Nancy Kanwisher** — Cognitive neuroscience, face and scene perception
- **Michael Graziano** — Motor control and attention, consciousness theories

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Neuroscience ↔ Machine Learning
- Backpropagation vs. biological learning rules — AI borrowed from neuroscience but took a very different path
- Attention mechanisms in transformers inspired by neural attention
- Spiking neural networks as bridge between biology and AI
- Deep learning's "unreasonable effectiveness" raises questions about what brains actually do

### Neuroscience ↔ Philosophy
- The hard problem of consciousness — why do neural processes feel like something?
- Free will and determinism — if neurons follow physical laws, what is choice?
- Split-brain studies reveal puzzles about unified self
- Blindsight and other dissociations challenge our intuitions about awareness

### Neuroscience ↔ Economics
- Neuroeconomics studies decision-making at the neural level
- Dopamine and reward prediction error = reinforcement learning
- Risk and uncertainty encoded in prefrontal and parietal cortex
- Neural basis of irrationality (framing effects, loss aversion)

### Neuroscience ↔ Music
- How does the brain parse rhythm, melody, harmony?
- Music training changes brain structure (plasticity in action)
- Why do we get chills from music? (Prediction and violation)
- Perfect pitch as example of critical periods

### Neuroscience ↔ Art & Design
- Visual illusions exploit neural processing shortcuts
- Color perception reveals how brain constructs reality
- Neuroaesthetics studies neural response to beauty
- Virtual reality design requires understanding sensory integration

### Neuroscience ↔ Social Sciences
- Mirror neurons and theory of mind — neural basis of empathy
- Social neuroscience studies group dynamics at neural level
- Cultural differences in brain function (language, perception)
- Adolescent brain development informs education and policy

## Recommended Learning Path

1. **Start with**: Khan Academy videos (neuron basics) + Bear textbook chapters
2. **Build foundation**: MIT OCW 9.01 lectures + problem sets
3. **Get hands-on**: Neuronify app (circuit building)
4. **Go computational**: MIT OCW 9.29J (if math-comfortable)
5. **Integrate higher functions**: MIT OCW 9.10 (cognitive neuroscience)
6. **Explore research**: Simons Foundation resources, recent papers

## When to Use Each Resource

- **Struggling with concepts?** → Khan Academy videos, Bear textbook
- **Want more depth?** → MIT OCW 9.01 full course, Kandel reference
- **Hands-on learner?** → Neuronify, interactive simulators
- **Math-inclined?** → Computational neuroscience courses (9.29J, 9.40)
- **Interested in medicine?** → HST.131, clinical case studies
- **Curious about cutting-edge?** → Simons Foundation, recent papers, research portals
