# Neuroscience — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Neuronal structure** — Neurons have specialized compartments (dendrites, soma, axon) for information processing
2. **Neuronal diversity** — Different neuron types have distinct morphologies suited to their functions. Depends on: neuronal structure
3. **Glial support** — Glial cells support, protect, and modulate neuronal function
4. **Resting membrane potential** — Ion gradients and selective permeability create electrical potential across membranes. Depends on: neuronal structure
5. **Electrochemical equilibrium** — Nernst and Goldman equations quantify ion-driven potentials. Depends on: resting membrane potential
6. **Action potential generation** — Voltage-gated channels create regenerative, all-or-nothing electrical signals. Depends on: resting membrane potential
7. **Positive feedback dynamics** — Channel opening begets more opening, creating threshold behavior. Depends on: action potential generation
8. **Action potential propagation** — Signals travel along axons via local current flow and channel activation. Depends on: action potential generation
9. **Myelination** — Insulating sheaths enable fast saltatory conduction. Depends on: action potential propagation, glial support
10. **Hodgkin-Huxley model** — Differential equations capture ion channel dynamics mathematically. Depends on: action potential generation, electrochemical equilibrium
11. **Chemical synaptic transmission** — Neurons communicate via neurotransmitter release at specialized junctions. Depends on: action potential propagation
12. **Excitation vs inhibition** — Different neurotransmitters and receptors either depolarize or hyperpolarize target neurons. Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission
13. **Synaptic pharmacology** — Drugs and toxins modify synaptic function by targeting specific molecular components. Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission
14. **Synaptic plasticity** — Synapses strengthen or weaken based on activity patterns (LTP, LTD). Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission
15. **Neural circuit architecture** — Neurons interconnect in patterns (feedforward, recurrent, lateral) that enable computation. Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission
16. **Neural coding** — Information is represented through firing rates, timing, and population activity. Depends on: neural circuit architecture
17. **Visual transduction** — Photoreceptors convert light into neural signals via biochemical cascades. Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission
18. **Hierarchical visual processing** — Visual cortex builds complex features from simple ones through receptive field organization. Depends on: neural circuit architecture, visual transduction
19. **Auditory frequency coding** — The cochlea spatially separates frequencies; hair cells transduce sound. Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission
20. **Cross-modal processing** — Sensory systems interact and integrate information. Depends on: neural circuit architecture
21. **Motor control hierarchy** — Motor cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebellum coordinate movement. Depends on: neural circuit architecture
22. **Motor learning** — Cerebellum enables error correction and skill acquisition. Depends on: synaptic plasticity, motor control hierarchy
23. **Basal ganglia function** — Dopamine-modulated circuits select and sequence actions. Depends on: chemical synaptic transmission, motor control hierarchy
24. **Hippocampal memory formation** — The hippocampus binds distributed representations into episodic memories. Depends on: synaptic plasticity, neural circuit architecture
25. **Procedural memory** — Repeated practice drives structural changes in motor and cognitive circuits. Depends on: synaptic plasticity, motor learning
26. **Memory consolidation** — Sleep and time-dependent processes stabilize memories. Depends on: hippocampal memory formation
27. **Attention and executive control** — Prefrontal cortex modulates processing in sensory and motor areas. Depends on: neural circuit architecture
28. **Language networks** — Specialized cortical areas (Broca's, Wernicke's) support language comprehension and production. Depends on: neural circuit architecture, attention and executive control
29. **Structure-function integration** — Cognitive phenomena emerge from coordinated activity across brain systems. Depends on: all previous concepts

## Critical Dependencies

- **Action potential generation requires understanding resting potential** — You can't understand how voltage-gated channels generate spikes without first knowing why membranes have resting potentials and what depolarization means.

- **Synaptic plasticity builds on synaptic transmission** — LTP and LTD mechanisms only make sense after understanding basic neurotransmitter release and receptor activation.

- **Neural coding requires circuit architecture** — Before understanding how information is encoded, students need to grasp how neurons connect and interact.

- **Sensory processing depends on synaptic transmission AND circuits** — Both the cellular mechanism (transduction, neurotransmitter release) and systems-level organization (receptive fields, hierarchical processing) are essential.

- **Memory requires plasticity** — Hebbian learning, LTP/LTD, and structural changes are the cellular substrate of learning and memory.

- **Higher cognition integrates all systems** — Language, attention, and executive function draw on sensory processing, motor control, memory, and plasticity.

## Bottleneck Concepts

**Action potential** — The all-or-nothing electrical signal is THE fundamental unit of neural computation. Everything downstream depends on understanding this.

**Synaptic transmission** — This is how neurons communicate. Without this, circuits, systems, and cognition make no sense.

**Synaptic plasticity** — This is the cellular mechanism of learning. It bridges single-cell physiology to behavior and cognition.

**Neural coding** — Understanding how populations of neurons represent information unlocks systems neuroscience and cognitive neuroscience.

## Mind-Blowing Moments

- **Hodgkin & Huxley nailed the action potential with equations in 1952** — Before modern computing, they predicted channel dynamics with stunning accuracy

- **Hebbian plasticity: "Cells that fire together wire together"** — A simple rule that explains learning, memory, and even development

- **The hippocampus creates spatial maps** — Place cells literally encode locations, revealing how abstract concepts get neural representations

- **Split-brain patients reveal two minds in one skull** — Severing the corpus callosum shows how much we don't know about unified consciousness

- **Optogenetics lets us control neurons with light** — Modern neuroscience can turn specific neurons on/off with millisecond precision

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"The brain is like a computer"** — While useful, this metaphor breaks down. Neurons are analog, probabilistic, and plastic; computation is distributed and noisy.

2. **"We only use 10% of our brain"** — Completely false. All brain areas are active and essential; damage anywhere causes deficits.

3. **"Neurons don't regenerate"** — Mostly true but not absolute. Neurogenesis occurs in specific areas (hippocampus, olfactory bulb) throughout life.

4. **"The action potential travels by electrons flowing down the axon"** — No! It's a wave of ion channel opening, not electron flow like in a wire.

5. **"Each neuron does one thing"** — Neurons are multifunctional; the same neuron can participate in different computations depending on network state.

6. **"Memory is stored in specific neurons"** — Memories are distributed patterns across populations; there's no "grandmother cell."

7. **"More neurons = more intelligence"** — Quality of connections (synapses) and network organization matter far more than neuron count.

8. **"The left brain is logical, the right brain is creative"** — Vastly oversimplified. Both hemispheres contribute to most functions.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Cell biology** — Needed for: neuronal structure, membrane properties, protein synthesis, cellular compartments
- **Basic chemistry** — Needed for: electrochemical gradients, ion behavior, neurotransmitter structure
- **Electricity fundamentals** — Needed for: current, voltage, resistance, capacitance concepts in neural signaling
- **Differential equations (basic exposure)** — Needed for: Hodgkin-Huxley model, cable theory, computational models
- **Graph/network theory (helpful but not required)** — Helpful for: understanding circuit architecture and connectivity patterns
