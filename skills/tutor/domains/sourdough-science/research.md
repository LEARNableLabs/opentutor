# Research Summary — Sourdough Science

## Major Subtopics

1. **Microbial Ecology** — Lactobacillus species diversity, yeast taxonomy (Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Candida milleri, Kazachstania exigua), symbiotic relationships, succession dynamics
2. **Fermentation Biochemistry** — Lactic and acetic acid production, CO2 generation, enzymatic degradation of starch and protein, metabolite production
3. **Environmental Factors** — Temperature effects on microbial populations, hydration ratios, flour chemistry (whole grain vs. refined), salt inhibition, feeding schedules
4. **Flavor Chemistry** — Volatile organic compounds, alcohol production, ester formation, Maillard reactions during baking
5. **Gluten Network Modification** — Protease activity, pH effects on gluten structure, dough rheology
6. **Applied Microbiology** — Starter maintenance, troubleshooting (over-acidification, poor rise), seasonal variation, scaling from home to bakery

## Key Sources Found

### Academic Resources
- **"The Sourdough Microflome" (Minervini et al., 2014)** — comprehensive review of microbial diversity in sourdough ecosystems
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Food Fermentation Microbiology** — covers LAB (lactic acid bacteria) biochemistry and fermentation kinetics
- **UC Davis Food Science lectures** — applications of sourdough in artisan baking, includes microbiology fundamentals
- **PubMed/Google Scholar** — extensive literature on LAB species, yeast metabolism, and starter ecology

### Textbooks
- **"Handbook on Sourdough Biotechnology"** (Gobbetti & Gänzle, 2013) — definitive academic text covering microbiology, biochemistry, and technology
- **"The Art and Science of Sourdough"** (Suas, 2021) — bridges scientific understanding with practical application
- **"Bread Science"** (Corriher, 1997) — includes chapters on fermentation chemistry

### Video Resources
- **YouTube: "The Sourdough Journey" series by The Bread Code** — visual demonstrations with scientific explanations
- **"Sourdough Microbiology Explained" by ChainBaker** — accessible breakdown of LAB and yeast interactions
- **MIT OCW: Fermentation lecture series** — academic-level biochemistry

## Coverage Strategy

For intermediate level, the curriculum will:
- Assume basic biology (cells, metabolism) but not microbiology coursework
- Build from observable phenomena (bubbles, smell, rise) to underlying mechanisms
- Integrate chemistry gradually (start with pH, build to organic acids and flavor compounds)
- Balance theoretical understanding with practical application
- Use case studies and troubleshooting scenarios to cement concepts
- Include microscopy images, fermentation curves, and metabolic pathway diagrams as resources
