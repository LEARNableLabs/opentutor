# Sourdough Science — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Wild fermentation** — microbial colonization from environmental sources (flour, air, hands) without commercial inoculant
2. **Microbial consortium** — stable community of lactic acid bacteria (LAB) and wild yeast working together
3. **Lactic acid bacteria** — primary acid producers (Lactobacillus species); create sour flavor and control pH
4. **Wild yeast** — non-commercial Saccharomyces and other genera; provide leavening and flavor compounds
5. **Acid production** — LAB metabolize sugars into lactic acid (mild) and acetic acid (sharp); drives pH down
6. **pH dynamics** — acidity controls microbial populations, inhibits pathogens, affects gluten structure
7. **Ecological succession** — predictable sequence of microbial colonization and dominance over starter development
8. **Lactobacillus species diversity** — L. sanfranciscensis, L. plantarum, L. brevis each have different temperature optima and acid profiles. Depends on: wild fermentation, microbial consortium
9. **Yeast diversity** — Candida milleri and Kazachstania exigua are acid-tolerant specialists; S. cerevisiae less common. Depends on: pH dynamics, wild yeast
10. **Syntrophy** — LAB and yeast cross-feed metabolites; LAB consume maltose and produce amino acids for yeast. Depends on: microbial consortium, LAB, wild yeast
11. **Amylase activity** — enzymes (from flour and microbes) break starch into maltose and glucose. Depends on: wild fermentation
12. **Maltose metabolism** — preferred sugar for LAB; yeast species differ in maltose utilization. Depends on: amylase activity, syntrophy
13. **Heterofermentation vs homofermentation** — LAB pathways producing different acid ratios and CO2. Depends on: lactic acid bacteria, acid production
14. **Acid ratio** — lactic:acetic ratio determines flavor (mild vs sharp); controlled by LAB species and environment. Depends on: heterofermentation, acid production
15. **Competitive exclusion** — low pH and acid production prevent pathogen growth. Depends on: pH dynamics, acid production
16. **Proteolysis** — LAB proteases break down gluten; increases extensibility, releases amino acids. Depends on: lactic acid bacteria, pH dynamics
17. **Gluten modification** — proteolysis and acidification change dough rheology and texture. Depends on: proteolysis, pH dynamics
18. **CO2 production** — yeast (primary) and heterofermentative LAB (secondary) produce gas for leavening. Depends on: wild yeast, heterofermentation
19. **Temperature effects** — warm temps favor LAB, cool temps favor yeast; shifts acid ratio and rise speed. Depends on: LAB species diversity, yeast diversity
20. **Hydration ratio** — stiff starters have less oxygen (more LAB), liquid starters have more oxygen (more yeast). Depends on: microbial consortium, temperature effects
21. **Flour composition** — whole grain adds bran microbiota, minerals, and enzymes; affects LAB diversity. Depends on: wild fermentation, amylase activity
22. **Osmotic stress** — salt inhibits microbial growth but enhances flavor and controls fermentation rate. Depends on: microbial consortium
23. **Feeding schedule** — frequency and ratio of fresh flour affects starter maturity and flavor intensity. Depends on: acid production, maltose metabolism
24. **Volatile organic compounds** — alcohols, esters, aldehydes produced by LAB and yeast create aroma. Depends on: syntrophy, heterofermentation
25. **Exopolysaccharides** — some LAB produce dextran and other sticky polymers; improve moisture retention. Depends on: LAB species diversity
26. **FODMAP reduction** — long fermentation breaks down fermentable oligo/di/monosaccharides; improves digestibility. Depends on: amylase activity, proteolysis
27. **Phytate degradation** — acidification activates phytase; increases mineral bioavailability. Depends on: pH dynamics
28. **Troubleshooting** — using microbial ecology principles to diagnose starter problems. Depends on: all prior concepts
29. **Scaling and consistency** — commercial approaches to maintaining stable starter populations. Depends on: feeding schedule, temperature effects, pH dynamics

## Dependencies

- **Syntrophy requires both LAB and wild yeast** — neither thrives alone in sourdough; LAB need yeast-produced vitamins, yeast need LAB-liberated amino acids
- **Acid ratio depends on heterofermentation pathway** — homofermentative LAB produce mainly lactic acid; heterofermentative produce lactic + acetic + CO2
- **Temperature effects build on species diversity** — different Lactobacillus and yeast species have different optimal temperatures; changing temp shifts which species dominate
- **Gluten modification requires both proteolysis and pH** — proteases work faster in acidic conditions; pH also directly affects gluten bonds
- **Flavor compounds emerge from syntrophy and fermentation pathways** — VOCs are byproducts of LAB heterofermentation and yeast alcohol metabolism
- **Troubleshooting synthesizes all prior knowledge** — requires understanding how temperature, hydration, feeding, and flour choice affect microbial populations and metabolite production

## Bottleneck Concepts

1. **Syntrophy** — students often think LAB and yeast compete; understanding their mutualism is key to everything else
2. **Heterofermentation vs homofermentation** — distinguishing these pathways is essential for predicting acid ratios and flavor
3. **pH dynamics** — pH isn't just "sour"; it controls microbial populations, enzymatic activity, gluten structure, and safety

## Mind-Blowing Moments

- **LAB produce CO2 too** — most students assume only yeast make gas; heterofermentative LAB contribute meaningfully to rise
- **Whole grain flour brings its own microbes** — bran is colonized before milling; you're inoculating with every feeding
- **Sourdough can reduce gluten content** — proteolysis actually breaks down gluten proteins; long fermentation may improve tolerance (though not safe for celiac)
- **The "hooch" layer isn't alcohol** — it's waste metabolites and water separation; sign of overfermentation, not drunken yeast

## Common Misconceptions

- **"All Lactobacillus are the same"** — species differ dramatically in temperature optima, acid ratios, and EPS production
- **"Acetic acid is bad"** — it's sharper than lactic, but contributes complexity; balance is key, not elimination
- **"Commercial yeast and wild yeast are interchangeable"** — S. cerevisiae (commercial) is acid-sensitive and maltose-negative; wild species are adapted to sourdough's harsh conditions
- **"Warmer is always faster and better"** — warm temps speed LAB but can kill yeast; slow cold fermentation develops better flavor
- **"You need to add sugar to feed the starter"** — LAB and wild yeast prefer maltose from starch; adding sucrose can favor unwanted microbes

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic cell biology** — needed for understanding microbial metabolism and growth
- **Introductory chemistry** — needed for pH, acids/bases, enzymatic reactions
- **Carbohydrate structure** — needed for starch vs maltose vs glucose distinctions
- **Familiarity with sourdough baking** — needed to connect theory to observable phenomena (rise, smell, texture)
