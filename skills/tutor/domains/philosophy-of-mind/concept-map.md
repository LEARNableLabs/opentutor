# Philosophy of Mind — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Mind-body problem** — The fundamental puzzle of how mental states relate to physical states
2. **Cartesian dualism** — Mind and body are distinct substances that somehow interact
3. **Substance dualism** — The view that mental and physical are fundamentally different kinds of things
4. **Interaction problem** — How can immaterial mind causally affect material body?
5. **Logical behaviorism** — Mental states are behavioral dispositions, not inner states. Depends on: rejection of dualism
6. **Type identity theory** — Mental states are identical to brain states (pain = C-fibers firing). Depends on: rejection of dualism
7. **Multiple realizability** — Same mental state can be implemented in different physical substrates. Depends on: type identity theory (as critique)
8. **Token identity** — Each individual mental event is a physical event, but types aren't identical. Depends on: multiple realizability
9. **Functionalism** — Mental states defined by causal roles, not physical makeup. Depends on: multiple realizability
10. **Computational functionalism** — Mind is to brain as software is to hardware. Depends on: functionalism
11. **Substrate independence** — Same computation = same mental state, regardless of implementation. Depends on: computational functionalism
12. **Chinese Room argument** — Computation alone doesn't guarantee understanding/meaning. Depends on: computational functionalism (as critique)
13. **Syntax vs semantics** — Symbol manipulation vs genuine meaning. Depends on: Chinese Room argument
14. **Subjective experience** — What it's like to be in a mental state (first-person perspective). Depends on: functionalism (as limitation)
15. **Phenomenal consciousness** — Subjective, qualitative feel of experience
16. **Access consciousness** — Information available for reasoning and report. Depends on: phenomenal consciousness (as contrast)
17. **Hard problem of consciousness** — Why physical processes give rise to subjective experience. Depends on: phenomenal consciousness, explanatory gap
18. **Easy problems of consciousness** — Explaining cognitive functions (attention, memory, etc.). Depends on: hard problem (as contrast)
19. **Qualia** — Intrinsic, subjective qualities of experience (redness of red, painfulness of pain). Depends on: phenomenal consciousness
20. **Knowledge argument (Mary's Room)** — No amount of physical knowledge captures phenomenal knowledge. Depends on: qualia
21. **Philosophical zombies** — Beings physically identical to us but lacking consciousness. Depends on: hard problem, conceivability arguments
22. **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)** — Consciousness as global broadcasting of information. Depends on: access consciousness
23. **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** — Consciousness measured by integrated information (phi). Depends on: phenomenal consciousness
24. **Higher-Order Thought (HOT) theory** — Consciousness requires thinking about one's own mental states. Depends on: access vs phenomenal distinction
25. **Heterophenomenology** — Third-person study of first-person reports (Dennett's method). Depends on: rejection of qualia
26. **Illusionism about consciousness** — Phenomenal consciousness is an illusion (Dennett). Depends on: heterophenomenology
27. **Strong AI** — Appropriately programmed computers have genuine minds. Depends on: computational functionalism
28. **Turing Test** — Behavioral test for machine intelligence. Depends on: strong AI
29. **Artificial consciousness** — Whether machines can have phenomenal experience. Depends on: strong AI, hard problem
30. **Property dualism** — Physical world plus fundamental consciousness properties. Depends on: hard problem, rejection of physicalism

## Dependencies

- **Functionalism requires multiple realizability** because it's the key argument against type identity theory and motivates defining mental states by causal role rather than physical composition
- **Computational functionalism builds on functionalism** by specifying the causal roles in computational/algorithmic terms
- **The Chinese Room attacks computational functionalism** by trying to show that computation (syntax) without semantic understanding isn't sufficient for genuine mentality
- **The hard problem depends on distinguishing phenomenal from access consciousness** because the puzzle is specifically about subjective experience, not cognitive function
- **Qualia arguments (Mary's Room, zombies) presuppose the hard problem** because they're designed to show why physical/functional explanations can't capture phenomenal properties
- **Scientific theories (GWT, IIT, HOT) attempt to solve or dissolve the hard problem** by providing either reductive explanations or explaining away the intuition
- **Artificial consciousness debate combines computational functionalism with the hard problem** — can substrate-independent computation generate phenomenal experience?
- **Property dualism emerges from the hard problem** as a position that accepts physicalism about most things but adds consciousness as fundamental

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are critical checkpoints — if a student doesn't grasp them, later material won't make sense:

1. **Multiple realizability** — The gateway from identity theory to functionalism. Without understanding this, functionalism seems unmotivated.
2. **Phenomenal vs access consciousness** — Essential for understanding why the hard problem is "hard" and why functional explanations might miss something.
3. **Computational functionalism** — The foundation for all AI consciousness debates and the Chinese Room argument.
4. **The hard problem** — Central organizing concept for the second half of the curriculum. Students must see why it's distinct from cognitive/functional questions.
5. **Syntax vs semantics** — Key to evaluating both the Chinese Room and broader questions about AI understanding.

## Mind-Blowing Moments

Points where students often have "aha!" experiences:

- **Multiple realizability insight** — "Wait, so pain can't just BE C-fibers because aliens/octopuses could feel pain differently!"
- **Chinese Room punch** — "The person in the room clearly doesn't understand Chinese, but the system passes the Turing Test..."
- **Mary's Room** — "She knows everything physical about color vision but still learns something when she sees red for the first time!"
- **Zombie conceivability** — "I can imagine a being physically identical to me but with no inner experience... what does that mean?"
- **IIT's counterintuitive implications** — "A grid of logic gates could have more consciousness than a human brain?!"
- **Dennett's radical move** — "What if qualia don't exist at all and we're just confused about our own experience?"

## Common Misconceptions

1. **Dualism = religion** — Students conflate Cartesian dualism with religious soul-talk. Need to present dualism as a philosophical position with secular arguments.
2. **Functionalism = computers** — Functionalism is about causal roles in general, not specifically computation. Computational functionalism is one version.
3. **Behaviorism = lying** — Students think behaviorism means hiding mental states. Actually, it's an ontological claim that mental states ARE dispositions to behave.
4. **Chinese Room proves AI can't think** — Searle's target is narrower: syntax alone doesn't guarantee semantics. Doesn't rule out all AI approaches.
5. **Hard problem = we don't know yet** — Students often think it's just a current knowledge gap. The claim is stronger: there's an explanatory gap in principle.
6. **Zombies are about actual beings** — The zombie argument is modal/conceivability reasoning, not claiming zombies exist or could exist physically.
7. **IIT measures consciousness quality** — IIT measures integrated information (phi), which Tononi identifies with consciousness, but this is controversial.
8. **Turing Test = consciousness test** — Turing Test was originally about intelligence/thinking, not phenomenal consciousness.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic symbolic logic** — Needed for evaluating philosophical arguments (especially zombie conceivability, knowledge argument)
- **Scientific method basics** — Understanding empirical vs conceptual questions helps frame consciousness science debates
- **Basic neuroscience** — Knowing what neurons, brain regions, neural activity are makes concrete examples work better
- **Philosophy of science** — Understanding reduction, explanation, emergence helps with physicalism debates
- **Modal logic basics** — Helpful for possibility/necessity reasoning in zombie arguments (can be taught on the fly)
- **Computability theory** — Basic Turing machine concept needed for computational functionalism (can be taught on the fly)

## Cross-Domain Connections

- **Cognitive science** — Empirical work on attention, memory, perception informs consciousness theories
- **Neuroscience** — Neural correlates of consciousness (NCC), brain lesion studies, global workspace evidence
- **Computer science** — AI, machine learning, neural networks provide test cases for consciousness theories
- **Physics** — Quantum consciousness theories (controversial), thermodynamics and information theory (IIT)
- **Ethics** — Moral status of AI, animal consciousness, implications of consciousness theories for personhood
- **Linguistics** — Searle's background, understanding vs processing, compositionality
- **Mathematics** — Information theory (IIT), formal theories of computation, complexity theory
