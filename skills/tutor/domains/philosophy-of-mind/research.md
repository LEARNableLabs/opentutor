# Philosophy of Mind — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Mind-Body Problem** — Classical debates (Descartes' dualism, materialism, property dualism)
2. **Behaviorism and Identity Theory** — Logical behaviorism, type/token identity theories
3. **Functionalism** — Multiple realizability, computational functionalism, Chinese Room argument
4. **Consciousness Studies** — Hard problem vs. easy problems, phenomenal consciousness, qualia
5. **Computational Theories of Mind** — Turing Test, strong AI, substrate independence
6. **Scientific Theories of Consciousness** — Global Workspace Theory, Integrated Information Theory, Higher-Order Thought theories
7. **Artificial Consciousness** — Machine consciousness debates, philosophical zombies, what-it's-like-ness

## Key Sources Found

### University Courses
- **MIT OCW - Philosophical Issues in Brain Science** (24.08J): Video lectures on consciousness theories that neuroscientists take seriously
- **MIT Open Learning Library - Minds and Machines** (24.09x): Covers Chinese Room, Turing Test, computational theories
- **UC San Diego - Philosophy of Mind**: Syllabus covering Descartes to functionalism, minds in computers
- **Birkbeck - Philosophy of Mind and Cognitive Science**: Dualism, behaviorism, identity theory, functionalism, computationalism
- **Minds and Machines courses** (Carlotta Pavese, Texas A&M): Turing's computational proposal, Searle's objections

### Primary Textbooks
- **David Chalmers - "The Conscious Mind: In Search of a Fundamental Theory"** (1996): Distinguishes easy vs. hard problem of consciousness, argues for property dualism, substrate independence
- **Andy Clark - "Mindware: An Introduction to the Philosophy of Cognitive Science"**: Used in multiple Minds & Machines courses
- **John Haugeland - "Mind Design II: Philosophy, Psychology, Artificial Intelligence"**: Classic anthology

### Key Philosophers
- **David Chalmers**: Hard problem of consciousness, property dualism, computational substrate independence
- **Daniel Dennett**: Materialist approach to consciousness, heterophenomenology
- **John Searle**: Chinese Room argument against strong AI
- **Ned Block**: Access vs. phenomenal consciousness distinction
- **Thomas Nagel**: "What is it like to be a bat?" subjective experience
