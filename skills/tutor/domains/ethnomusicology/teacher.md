# Ethnomusicology — music across cultures — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 7 lessons (23%)
- **Socratic questions** — 6 lessons (19%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (16%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 5 lessons (16%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 4 lessons (13%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 4 lessons (13%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 29% accessible (1-2), 45% standard (3), 26% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 9: "What role does music play in political resistance?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 10: "How do we analyze music on its own cultural terms?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 14: "How does the maqam system organize pitch differently than Western scales?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 15: "What makes African polyrhythm more than just 'complex rhythm'?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 20: "How does gamelan music embody Javanese cosmology?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers ethnomusicology — music across cultures, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: comparative musicology, anthropology of music, field history, music as social behavior, cultural context, functionalism, emic perspective, etic perspective, insider vs outsider, transcription limitations, microtones, timbral nuance, performance practice, cultural ownership, appropriation (and 96 more).
