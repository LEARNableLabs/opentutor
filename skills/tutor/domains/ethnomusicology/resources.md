# Ethnomusicology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Textbooks

**Bruno Nettl, "The Study of Ethnomusicology: Thirty-Three Discussions" (3rd ed., 2015)**
- The definitive theoretical introduction to the field
- Known affectionately as "The Red Book"—hailed for its disarming, witty style
- Covers 33 core issues/concepts from Nettl's 60-year career
- Best for: Theoretical frameworks, history of the field, methodological debates
- Level: Intermediate to advanced—perfect for this curriculum
- Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Study-Ethnomusicology-Thirty-Three-Discussions/dp/0252039289
- Project MUSE: https://muse.jhu.edu/book/18594/

**Jeff Todd Titon et al., "Worlds of Music: An Introduction to the Music of the World's Peoples" (6th ed., 2017)**
- Bestselling ethnomusicology textbook
- Case-study approach with chapters by field ethnomusicologists working in their areas of expertise
- All discussions based on authors' own fieldwork
- Assumes no formal musical background—accessible and rich
- Best for: Regional case studies, cultural context, listening examples
- Level: Beginner to intermediate
- Cengage: https://www.cengage.com/c/worlds-of-music-an-introduction-to-the-music-of-the-world-s-peoples-6e-titon/9781133953906/
- Google Books: https://books.google.com/books/about/Worlds_of_Music_An_Introduction_to_the_M.html?id=KzWmpqPTCwYC

**Bruno Nettl, "Theory and Method in Ethnomusicology" (1964)**
- Classic text on research methods and theoretical frameworks
- Examines the purpose of the field and the relationship between music and culture
- Foundational for understanding fieldwork methods
- Best for: Methodology lessons (lessons 3-5, 25)
- Level: Advanced undergraduate to graduate
- Internet Archive (free): https://archive.org/details/theorymethodinet0000nett

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Online Courses

**University of Pennsylvania (Coursera), "Listening to World Music"**
- Free course examining world music traditions (Tuvan throat singing, African drumming, etc.)
- Recorded video lectures, auto-graded assignments, discussion forums
- Best for: Structured introduction with guided listening
- Level: Beginner
- Class Central: https://www.classcentral.com/course/worldmusic-355

**SoundCollective Online, "Ethnomusicology and Modern Music"**
- Comprehensive course by Jae Franklin
- Examines music in cultural contexts, role of ethnomusicologists, technology's impact
- Practical applications through real-world examples
- Best for: Contemporary issues, technology, world music industry
- Level: Intermediate
- Course page: https://learn.soundcollective.com/courses/ethnomusicology-and-modern-music/

**Class Central, Ethnomusicology Courses**
- 30+ courses on YouTube, XuetangX, edX
- Topics: traditional instruments, microtonal systems, ethnographic approaches
- Best for: Diverse perspectives, free resources
- Search page: https://www.classcentral.com/subject/ethnomusicology

### Video Lectures & Documentaries

**UCLA Ethnomusicology Archive**
- Established 1961, over 200,000 audio, video, print, and photographic items
- Recordings of concerts and lectures by legendary scholars and performers (Mantle Hood, Ravi Shankar)
- Best for: Primary source recordings, historical lectures, authentic performances
- Research guide: https://guides.library.ucla.edu/ethno/av

## Supplementary Reading

### Key Ethnomusicologists & Works

**Foundational Figures**
- **Alan Merriam** — "The Anthropology of Music" (1964) — three-part model of music (concept, behavior, sound)
- **John Blacking** — "How Musical Is Man?" (1973) — humanistic approach, musical universals
- **Mantle Hood** — bi-musicality concept, learning to perform music you study
- **Steven Feld** — "Sound and Sentiment" (1982) — sound as culture, Kaluli of Papua New Guinea
- **Anthony Seeger** — "Why Suyá Sing" (1987) — music and social structure

**Contemporary Voices**
- **Kofi Agawu** — "Representing African Music" (2003) — critique of Western analytical bias
- **Timothy Rice** — "May It Fill Your Soul" (1994) — Bulgarian music, ethnography
- **Thomas Turino** — "Music as Social Life" (2008) — participatory vs. presentational performance
- **Deborah Wong** — "Speak It Louder: Asian Americans Making Music" (2004) — identity politics, contemporary ethnomusicology
- **Alejandro Madrid** — "Music in Mexico" (2013) — decolonizing methodologies

### Topics for Deep Dives

**Music and Globalization**
- Timothy Taylor, "Global Pop: World Music, World Markets" (1997)
- Martin Stokes, "Globalization and the Politics of World Music" (2004)

**Fieldwork Ethics**
- Michelle Kisliuk, "Seize the Dance!" (1998) — reflexivity in fieldwork
- Jeff Todd Titon, "Worlds of Music" — ongoing discussions of ethics and representation

**Applied Ethnomusicology**
- Svanibor Pettan and Jeff Todd Titon, eds., "The Oxford Handbook of Applied Ethnomusicology" (2015)
- Community-engaged scholarship, music and social justice

## Unexpected Connections

### Music and Cognitive Science
- **Daniel Levitin**, "This Is Your Brain on Music" (2006) — neuroscience of music perception
- **Aniruddh Patel**, "Music, Language, and the Brain" (2008) — evolutionary musicology
- **Cross-cultural hearing studies** — Do all humans perceive consonance/dissonance the same way? (Spoiler: no)

### Music and Environmentalism (Ecomusicology)
- **Jeff Todd Titon** — Music and sustainability, eco-critical approaches
- **Aaron Allen** — Environmental ethnomusicology
- **How climate change affects music traditions** — loss of materials (specific woods, animal skins), ecosystem changes

### Music and Technology
- **Paul Théberge**, "Any Sound You Can Imagine" (1997) — technology and musical creativity
- **Jonathan Sterne**, "The Audible Past" (2003) — sound reproduction technologies
- **Algorithmic bias in music streaming** — How Spotify/Apple Music categorize world music

### Music and Social Movements
- **Ron Eyerman and Andrew Jamison**, "Music and Social Movements" (1998)
- **Protest music across cultures** — Civil rights, anti-apartheid, Arab Spring
- **Sound as political resistance** — Noise protests, sonic warfare

### Music and Disability Studies
- **Joseph Straus**, "Extraordinary Measures: Disability in Music" (2011)
- **Neurodiversity and music perception** — Autism, synesthesia, aphasia
- **Accessible music-making** — Adaptive instruments, inclusive pedagogy

### Music and Artificial Intelligence
- **Ethnomusicological approaches to AI-generated music** — Bias in training data (mostly Western music)
- **Can AI learn non-Western musical systems?** — Maqam, raga, gamelan
- **Ethical questions** — Who owns AI-generated music in a cultural tradition?

## For Intermediate Students Specifically

**Best Starting Points**
1. Bruno Nettl, "The Study of Ethnomusicology" (theory)
2. Jeff Todd Titon, "Worlds of Music" (case studies)
3. UCLA Archive + Smithsonian Folkways (listening)
4. University of Pennsylvania Coursera (structured learning)

**After This Curriculum**
- Take the SoundCollective "Ethnomusicology and Modern Music" course
- Conduct a mini-ethnography (interview + transcription + analysis)
- Attend live world music performances in your area
- Join Society for Ethnomusicology (student membership available)
- Read a full monograph on one tradition that fascinates you (see key works above)

**For Further Study**
- Advanced textbooks: Merriam's "Anthropology of Music," Nettl's "Theory and Method"
- Specialized topics: fieldwork ethics, music and globalization, applied ethnomusicology
- Regional deep-dives: African music (Agawu), Indian music (Neuman), Latin American (Madrid)
- Methodological debates: emic/etic, universalism/relativism, insider/outsider authority
