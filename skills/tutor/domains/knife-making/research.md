# Knife Making — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Steel Metallurgy
- Carbon and alloy content
- Crystal structure (austenite, martensite, pearlite)
- Steel types (carbon, stainless, tool steels, damascus)
- Steel selection for different knife applications

### 2. Heat Treatment
- Critical temperatures and phase transformations
- Austenitizing (hardening)
- Quenching (media, rates, stress)
- Tempering (hardness vs. toughness trade-offs)
- Annealing and normalizing
- Cryo treatment
- Differential hardening

### 3. Blade Design and Geometry
- Blade profiles (drop point, clip point, tanto, etc.)
- Edge geometry (angles, grinds)
- Grind types (hollow, flat, convex, saber, scandi)
- Blade thickness and taper
- Distal taper and balance

### 4. Practical Construction
- Forging vs. stock removal
- Grinding techniques
- Heat treatment in practice
- Handle materials and attachment
- Finishing and polishing

## Learning Approach for Intermediate Level

Students at intermediate level are assumed to have:
- Basic metalworking experience
- Shop safety knowledge
- Access to basic tools (grinder, drill press, heat source)
- Some exposure to materials science concepts

The curriculum emphasizes:
1. **Theory-practice integration** — metallurgy theory immediately applied to heat treatment decisions
2. **Decision-making frameworks** — when to use which steel, grind, or heat treatment protocol
3. **Troubleshooting** — recognizing and fixing common problems (warping, soft spots, brittleness)
4. **Design iteration** — balancing performance, aesthetics, and manufacturability
