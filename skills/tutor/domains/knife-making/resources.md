# Knife Making — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books

- **"Knife Engineering: Steel, Heat Treating, and Geometry"** by Dr. Larrin Thomas
  - Comprehensive metallurgy reference for intermediate to advanced makers
  - Covers steel selection, heat treatment protocols, edge geometry optimization
  - Data-driven approach with testing results
  - Best single resource for understanding the science

- **"The Complete Bladesmith"** by Jim Hrisoulas
  - Classic text on forging and bladesmithing
  - Strong on traditional techniques and fundamentals
  - Good for students interested in forging approach

- **"The Wonder of Knifemaking"** by Wayne Goddard
  - Accessible for intermediate level
  - Covers design, construction, and finishing
  - Practical focus with clear instructions

- **"The $50 Knife Shop"** by Wayne Goddard
  - Budget tool setup guide
  - Excellent for students setting up their first shop
  - Shows that quality knives don't require expensive equipment

### Online Technical Resources

- **Knife Steel Nerds** — https://knifesteelnerds.com
  - Dr. Larrin Thomas's blog and research
  - In-depth metallurgy, steel rankings, heat treatment guides
  - ESSENTIAL resource — cite heavily
  - Articles on specific steels, heat treatment protocols, edge geometry
  - Regular updates with new research

- **Crucible Industries** — https://crucible.com
  - Manufacturer of CPM (Crucible Particle Metallurgy) steels
  - Technical data sheets with heat treatment specs
  - Steel selection guides
  - Download PDFs for S30V, S35VN, 154CM, CPM-3V, etc.

- **Carpenter Technology** — https://cartech.com
  - Steel manufacturer technical resources
  - Heat treatment guidelines
  - Metallurgical data

- **American Bladesmith Society (ABS)** — https://americanbladesmith.com
  - Educational resources and standards
  - Journeyman and Mastersmith requirements
  - Community and event information
  - Video library (members)

## Unexpected Connections (rabbit holes)

### Historical Metallurgy

- **Damascus steel mystery** — Historical wootz steel from India
  - Modern research on carbide banding and nanotubes
  - Peter Paufler's research (Dresden)
  - Good hook for materials science discussion

- **Japanese sword metallurgy** — Tamahagane and differential hardening
  - William Coffman's research
  - Cultural and technical analysis
  - Excellent case study in pre-industrial optimization

### Cross-Discipline

- **Surgical blade design** — Ultra-sharp, single-use edges
  - Different constraints than kitchen/outdoor knives
  - Obsidian vs. steel research

- **Industrial cutting tools** — Machining and manufacturing
  - Carbide tooling vs. HSS
  - Parallels to knife edge retention

- **Samurai sword testing myths** — Deadliest Warrior, etc.
  - Good for critical analysis of claims
  - Separating performance from marketing

### Materials Science

- **Grain structure visualization** — Macro-etching
  - Flow lines in forged blades
  - Decarburization detection
  - Beautiful and informative

- **Scanning electron microscopy** — Edge apex structure
  - Sub-micron view of sharpness
  - Carbide distribution in steel
  - Mind-blowing visuals for students

- **CATRA testing** — Standardized edge retention testing
  - British standard for blade testing
  - Shows geometry effects clearly
  - Data-driven performance measurement

## Quick Reference

### Heat Treatment Cheat Sheet
- **1084**: Harden 1475°F, oil quench, temper 375°F (58-60 HRC)
- **1095**: Harden 1475°F, oil quench, temper 350°F (60-62 HRC)
- **O1**: Harden 1475°F, oil quench, temper 400°F (58-60 HRC)
- **440C**: Harden 1875°F, plate quench, cryo, temper 400°F (57-59 HRC)
- **S30V**: Harden 1900-2050°F, plate quench, cryo, temper 400°F (58-60 HRC)

### Common Edge Angles
- **Slicing/fillet**: 12-15° per side
- **Chef's knife**: 15-18° per side
- **Outdoor/bushcraft**: 18-22° per side
- **Chopping/heavy use**: 20-25° per side

### Rockwell Hardness Targets
- **Kitchen knives**: 58-62 HRC
- **Hunting/outdoor**: 56-60 HRC
- **Tactical/hard use**: 57-59 HRC (favor toughness)
- **Straight razors**: 61-64 HRC
