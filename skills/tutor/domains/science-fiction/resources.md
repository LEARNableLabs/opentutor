# Science Fiction as a Genre of Ideas — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Essential Critical Texts
- **Darko Suvin, *Metamorphoses of Science Fiction*** — Foundational text on cognitive estrangement and the novum; defines SF as "literature of cognitive estrangement"
- **Carl Freedman, *Critical Theory and Science Fiction*** (https://www.weslpress.org/9780819574541/critical-theory-and-science-fiction/) — Traces relationships between SF and Frankfurt School critical theory; Choice Outstanding Academic Book
- **Rob Latham (ed.), *Science Fiction Criticism: An Anthology of Essential Writings*** (https://www.amazon.com/Science-Fiction-Criticism-Anthology-Essential/dp/1474248616) — Comprehensive anthology of critical essays spanning multiple frameworks and eras
- **Fredric Jameson, *Archaeologies of the Future*** — Marxist analysis of utopian and anti-utopian SF
- **Istvan Csicsery-Ronay Jr., *The Seven Beauties of Science Fiction*** — Contemporary SF theory covering key concepts and modes

### Accessible Introductions
- **Introduction to Science Fiction** (https://literariness.org/2018/07/26/introduction-to-science-fiction/) — Clear overview of SF as genre, history, and critical object
- **Edward James & Farah Mendlesohn, *The Cambridge Companion to Science Fiction*** — Scholarly but accessible essays on SF history, themes, and theory
- **Gary K. Wolfe, *Evaporating Genres*** — Essays on SF's boundaries, evolution, and critical status

### Anthologies (for teaching examples)
- **The Wesleyan Anthology of Science Fiction** (Evans, Csicsery-Ronay Jr., et al.) — Used in MIT's SF courses; excellent range of canonical and diverse texts
- **The Science Fiction Hall of Fame, Volume One 1929-1964** — Classic Golden Age stories demonstrating ideas-as-puzzles approach
- **The Big Book of Science Fiction** (VanderMeer & VanderMeer) — Global SF spanning a century; excellent for showing range and diversity

## University Courses (free, full syllabi)

### MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Writing Science Fiction** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21w-759-writing-science-fiction-spring-2016/) — Craft-focused but includes excellent readings list and analysis of contemporary SF
- **The Science Fiction of Freedom** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21l-434-the-science-fiction-of-freedom-fall-2024/pages/syllabus/) — Philosophy meets SF; explores freedom through speculative texts
- **Anthropology Through Speculative Fiction** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21a-270-anthropology-through-speculative-fiction-fall-2009/pages/syllabus/) — Uses SF to explore culture, society, technology, morality
- **Transmedia Storytelling: Modern Science Fiction** (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21w-763j-transmedia-storytelling-modern-science-fiction-spring-2014/) — Contemporary SF across platforms; worldbuilding emphasis

All include complete reading lists, assignments, and often lecture notes.

## Essential SF Texts for Analysis

(Examples frequently referenced in criticism; useful for illustrating concepts)

### Foundational Classics
- **Ursula K. Le Guin, *The Left Hand of Darkness*** — Gender as thought experiment; cognitive estrangement
- **Ursula K. Le Guin, *The Dispossessed*** — Anarchism vs. capitalism; utopia/dystopia dialectic
- **Philip K. Dick, *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*** — Identity, consciousness, what makes us human
- **Samuel R. Delany, *Babel-17*** — Language and thought; linguistic relativity in SF
- **Stanisław Lem, *Solaris*** — Alien as truly unknowable Other; limits of human understanding
- **Isaac Asimov, Robot stories** — Three Laws as thought experiment; AI ethics
- **Octavia Butler, *Parable of the Sower*** — Climate dystopia, social collapse, new religion
- **Octavia Butler, *Lilith's Brood*** — Posthumanism, consent, alien encounter as transformation

### Contemporary Essential Reading
- **N.K. Jemisin, *The Broken Earth* trilogy** — Afrofuturism, oppression, power, climate
- **Ted Chiang, *Stories of Your Life and Others*** — Philosophy through SF; time, language, consciousness
- **Ann Leckie, *Ancillary Justice*** — Gender, identity, empire, AI consciousness
- **Kim Stanley Robinson, *2312* or *The Ministry for the Future*** — Climate futures, political imagination
- **Becky Chambers, *The Long Way to a Small, Angry Planet*** — Hopepunk; found family in space; gentle SF
- **Cixin Liu, *The Three-Body Problem* trilogy** — Chinese SF; first contact, Fermi paradox, cosmic scale

### Shorter Works (for quick analysis)
- **Ted Chiang, "Story of Your Life"** — Time, language, determinism
- **Ken Liu, "The Paper Menagerie"** — Identity, immigration, magical realism/SF boundary
- **Ursula K. Le Guin, "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas"** — Utilitarianism as SF thought experiment
- **Octavia Butler, "Bloodchild"** — Reproduction, power, symbiosis, body horror
- **James Tiptree Jr., "The Women Men Don't See"** — Feminist SF; alienation and escape

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Philosophy
- **Philosophy of Time** — A-theory vs B-theory; eternalism; time travel SF as metaphysical argument
- **Philosophy of Mind** — Consciousness, qualia, Chinese Room; SF dramatizes thought experiments
- **Political Philosophy** — Utopian tradition from Plato to More to SF; anarchist theory (Le Guin and Peter Kropotkin)
- **Ethics** — Trolley problems in SF; AI ethics; terraforming and environmental ethics

### Anthropology
- **Cultural Relativism** — SF alien cultures as thought experiments in anthropology
- **Kinship Systems** — Le Guin's Gethenian sexuality; Butler's Oankali genetics
- **Ritual and Religion** — Dune's Bene Gesserit; Butler's Earthseed; invented religions as cultural analysis

### Sociology
- **Social Construction** — Gender (Le Guin), race (Butler), disability (Elgin); SF reveals contingency
- **Technology and Society** — Actor-Network Theory; SF as laboratory for STS (Science & Technology Studies)
- **Deviance and Normativity** — Cyberpunk subcultures; queer SF challenging heteronormativity

### Cognitive Science
- **Linguistic Relativity** — Sapir-Whorf hypothesis in *Babel-17* and "Story of Your Life"
- **Consciousness Studies** — Hard problem of consciousness; SF's phenomenological experiments
- **Extended Mind Thesis** — Cyborgs and augmentation; where does mind end and world begin?

### History of Science
- **Scientific Revolutions** — Kuhnian paradigm shifts; SF imagining new paradigms
- **Atomic Age Anxieties** — Cold War SF; nuclear fear and utopian technology
- **Space Age** — SF and actual space exploration mutually influencing

### Environmental Humanities
- **Anthropocene** — Climate fiction engaging with Earth system science
- **Extinction Studies** — SF's apocalyptic imagination; what comes after humans?
- **Multispecies Ethnography** — SF aliens and animals; Donna Haraway's cyborgs and companion species

### Literature & Cultural Studies
- **Postmodernism** — New Wave SF shares techniques with Pynchon, DeLillo, Barth
- **Postcolonial Theory** — SF's imperial fantasies and their critiques; Afrofuturism and Africanfuturism
- **Queer Theory** — Gender/sexuality fluidity in SF; temporal queerness; reproductive futurism critique

### Design & Futures Studies
- **Speculative Design** — "Design fiction" using SF worldbuilding for technology critique
- **Scenario Planning** — Corporate and policy futurism borrowing SF methods
- **Critical Making** — Building prototypes from SF concepts to interrogate technological futures
