# Research Summary — Science Fiction as a Genre of Ideas

## Major Subtopics

1. **Foundations & Genre Theory** — What distinguishes SF from other genres, cognitive estrangement (Darko Suvin), the role of the novum, SF vs fantasy boundaries
2. **Core Mechanisms** — Extrapolation, thought experiments, worldbuilding as philosophy, the scientific method in narrative form
3. **Major Thematic Clusters** — Time and temporality, identity and consciousness, technology and society, alien encounters and the Other, utopia/dystopia as political thought
4. **Historical Movements** — Golden Age (ideas as puzzles), New Wave (inner space), cyberpunk (technology critique), contemporary trends (climate fiction, Afrofuturism, hopepunk)
5. **Critical Approaches** — Marxist readings, feminist SF theory, postcolonial perspectives, SF as literature of estrangement

## Notes for Curriculum Design

This is an intermediate-level topic, so students likely:
- Have read some classic and contemporary SF
- Understand basic genre conventions
- Are ready to engage with critical theory and thematic analysis
- Want to understand SF as a mode of thought, not just entertainment

The curriculum should balance:
- Theoretical frameworks (cognitive estrangement, the novum, extrapolation)
- Concrete analysis of how SF explores specific ideas (time, identity, society, etc.)
- Historical context (how SF evolved as a laboratory for ideas)
- Critical approaches (feminist, postcolonial, Marxist readings)
- Practical application (recognizing and analyzing idea-driven SF)
