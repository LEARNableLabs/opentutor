# History of Photography — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Pre-photographic imaging** — camera obscura, early experiments with light-sensitive materials
2. **Invention era (1820s-1850s)** — daguerreotype, calotype, wet plate collodion
3. **Photographic expansion (1850s-1900)** — dry plates, roll film, democratization, documentary photography
4. **Pictorialism and art photography (1890s-1930s)** — photography as fine art, Photo-Secession, manipulation techniques
5. **Modernism (1920s-1940s)** — straight photography, New Vision, Bauhaus, f/64 Group
6. **Documentary and social photography (1930s-1970s)** — FSA, photojournalism, street photography
7. **Color photography** — early experiments, Kodachrome, acceptance in fine art
8. **Postmodern photography (1970s-1990s)** — constructed realities, appropriation, conceptual photography
9. **Digital revolution (1990s-present)** — digital sensors, manipulation, distribution, photography's changing role

## Key Sources

### Museum Collections & Exhibitions
- **George Eastman Museum** — world's oldest photography museum, extensive historical collection
- **Metropolitan Museum of Art** — Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History photography section
- **MoMA Photography Collection** — comprehensive modernist and contemporary holdings
- **Getty Museum** — strong 19th century collection, online exhibitions
- **Victoria & Albert Museum** — historical processes and equipment

### Educational Resources
- **Beaumont Newhall's "The History of Photography"** — foundational academic text
- **Naomi Rosenblum's "A World History of Photography"** — comprehensive survey
- **Yale University Press "Photography: A Cultural History"** — Mary Warner Marien
- **Coursera/Khan Academy** — limited but accessible introductory materials
- **YouTube documentaries** — BBC, PBS series on photography history

### Primary Sources
- **Camera Work magazine archives** — Alfred Stieglitz's influential publication
- **Aperture Foundation** — contemporary and historical photography journal
- **Photographers' writings** — manifestos, technical writings, artist statements
