# History of Photography — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Camera obscura** — optical projection device predating photography
2. **Light-sensitive materials** — chemicals that react to light exposure
3. **Daguerreotype process** — first practical photographic method producing unique images on metal plates
4. **Calotype/negative-positive process** — reproducible photography using paper negatives. Depends on: light-sensitive materials
5. **Exposure times** — duration needed to capture an image, constrained early practice. Depends on: light-sensitive materials
6. **Wet plate collodion** — improved process enabling sharper images and field photography. Depends on: negative-positive process
7. **Documentary photography** — photography as evidence and social record. Depends on: wet plate collodion
8. **Roll film and democratization** — George Eastman's innovations making photography accessible. Depends on: dry plate technology
9. **Pictorialism** — photography as fine art through manipulation and soft focus. Depends on: amateur photography movement
10. **Photo-Secession** — movement legitimizing photography as gallery art. Depends on: Pictorialism
11. **Straight photography** — modernist rejection of manipulation for pure optical vision. Depends on: Pictorialism (reaction against)
12. **f/64 Group** — formalist approach emphasizing sharp focus and tonal range. Depends on: straight photography
13. **New Vision/Bauhaus** — experimental modernist photography exploring new perspectives. Depends on: straight photography
14. **FSA photography** — government-sponsored documentary during Depression. Depends on: documentary photography
15. **Decisive moment** — Cartier-Bresson's concept of capturing fleeting perfect compositions. Depends on: handheld camera technology, documentary photography
16. **Photojournalism** — news photography as narrative medium. Depends on: documentary photography, mass media
17. **Objectivity vs. subjectivity** — photography's contested truth claims. Depends on: documentary photography
18. **Color photography acceptance** — color's slow legitimization in fine art. Depends on: color film technology, art world hierarchies
19. **New Color Photography** — Eggleston and peers elevating vernacular color. Depends on: color photography acceptance
20. **Constructed photography** — staged images challenging documentary tradition. Depends on: postmodern theory, objectivity vs. subjectivity
21. **Appropriation** — reusing existing images to question authorship. Depends on: postmodern theory, constructed photography
22. **Conceptual photography** — photography as vehicle for ideas over aesthetics. Depends on: conceptual art movement
23. **Digital imaging** — electronic sensors replacing film. Depends on: computer technology
24. **Digital manipulation** — expanded possibilities for altering images. Depends on: digital imaging
25. **Computational photography** — AI-driven image creation and enhancement. Depends on: digital imaging, machine learning
26. **Social media circulation** — contemporary distribution transforming photography's social role. Depends on: digital imaging, internet

## Dependencies

### Technical Foundations
- **Negative-positive process** enables reproducibility, which daguerreotype lacked — this fundamentally changes photography's potential applications from one-off portraits to mass distribution
- **Wet plate collodion** improves on calotype by using glass negatives (sharper, more durable than paper), enabling field photography and stereography
- **Roll film** depends on dry plate gelatin technology but adds portability and ease of use, creating amateur photography market
- **Digital imaging** replaces chemical light-sensitivity with electronic sensors, eliminating development and enabling instant review

### Aesthetic Movements
- **Pictorialism** emerges as reaction to photography's mechanical nature, attempting to prove it can be art through manipulation
- **Straight photography** reacts against Pictorialism, arguing photography's unique value lies in unmanipulated optical truth
- **New Color Photography** depends on acceptance of color materials but also shifts in conceptual frameworks about what constitutes serious subject matter
- **Constructed photography** builds on conceptual art and postmodern skepticism about documentary truth claims

### Documentary Evolution
- **Documentary photography** gains authority from belief in camera's objectivity
- **Photojournalism** extends documentary tradition into news media, depending on mass circulation
- **Objectivity vs. subjectivity debate** emerges from tension between photography's indexical nature and photographer's choices
- **Appropriation** challenges documentary authority by revealing how context shapes meaning

### Contemporary Synthesis
- **Computational photography** transforms what "taking a photograph" means, from capturing to constructing
- **Social media circulation** democratizes both creation and distribution, but raises questions about attention, authenticity, ephemerality

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Technical Bottlenecks
1. **Light-sensitive materials** — until chemistry advances, photography impossible
2. **Exposure times** — long exposures limit subjects (landscapes, still portraits) until faster materials developed
3. **Portability** — wet plate requires traveling darkroom; dry plates and roll film remove this barrier
4. **Color stability** — early color processes fade quickly; Kodachrome (1935) solves this

### Conceptual Bottlenecks
1. **Art vs. craft debate** — photography struggles for legitimacy as art form from 1839 through mid-20th century
2. **Truth claims** — photography's indexical nature creates assumptions about objectivity that both enable and constrain its uses
3. **Color prejudice** — black-and-white seen as artistic, color as commercial, creating barrier to color acceptance in fine art (1960s-70s shift)
4. **Postmodern skepticism** — challenges to photographic truth enable constructed/appropriated photography but also create epistemological crisis

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Photography killed painting"** — oversimplifies complex relationship; photography influenced painting but also learned from it
2. **"Documentary photography is objective"** — ignores framing, selection, context, caption power
3. **"Digital photography is fundamentally different"** — continuities matter as much as ruptures; compositional and conceptual concerns persist
4. **"Pictorialism was anti-modern"** — actually engaged with modernity through mass reproduction and new art institutions
5. **"Early photographers just pointed and shot"** — ignores immense technical skill and chemical knowledge required
6. **"Photojournalism shows what happened"** — neglects how editorial choices, cropping, sequencing shape narrative
7. **"Modernist photography was purely formalist"** — overlooks political dimensions of f/64, FSA, New Vision work

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic art history concepts** — needed for understanding movements (Modernism, Postmodernism), institutional contexts
- **Visual literacy** — needed for analyzing compositions, understanding formal elements
- **Camera basics** — needed for appreciating technical constraints and innovations (aperture, shutter speed, focal length)
- **19th century industrialization** — context for photography's invention and spread
- **Mass media history** — context for photojournalism and image circulation
