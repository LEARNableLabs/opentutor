# History of Photography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks & Comprehensive Surveys
- **Beaumont Newhall, "The History of Photography"** (1937, updated editions through 1982) — foundational scholarly text, modernist perspective. Excellent for technical history and formalist analysis. https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2350_300062630.pdf
- **Naomi Rosenblum, "A World History of Photography"** (5th ed., 2015) — comprehensive, includes non-Western photography, strong on social contexts. Standard intermediate textbook.
- **Mary Warner Marien, "Photography: A Cultural History"** (4th ed., 2014) — excellent for cultural studies approach, emphasizes photography's social roles beyond art.
- **Michel Frizot (ed.), "A New History of Photography"** (1998) — European perspective, multiple authors, thematic organization.

### Museum Collections (for images and context)
- **George Eastman Museum** — world's foremost photography museum. https://www.eastman.org/
  - Process demonstrations: https://www.eastman.org/photography-techniques
  - Timeline: https://www.eastman.org/photography-timeline
- **Metropolitan Museum of Art — Heilbrunn Timeline** — excellent thematic essays and high-res images. https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/
  - Photography overview: https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/phot/hd_phot.htm
- **MoMA Photography Collection** — strong modernist and contemporary holdings. https://www.moma.org/collection/
  - Glossary of terms: https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/
- **Getty Museum** — particularly strong in 19th century. https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/
- **Victoria & Albert Museum** — excellent on early processes and British photography. https://www.vam.ac.uk/collections/photographs
- **Library of Congress** — massive public domain archive, especially FSA collection. https://www.loc.gov/collections/
  - FSA/OWI collection: https://www.loc.gov/collections/fsa-owi-black-and-white-negatives/

### Key Periodicals & Online Resources
- **Aperture** — leading photography magazine since 1952. https://aperture.org/
  - Editorial/essays: https://aperture.org/editorial/
- **Magnum Photos** — documentary photography cooperative, excellent photographer profiles. https://www.magnumphotos.com/
- **International Center of Photography** — exhibitions and educational resources. https://www.icp.org/
- **British Journal of Photography** — industry and art photography coverage. https://www.bjp-online.com/

## People (key figures by era)

### Invention Era (1820s-1850s)
- **Joseph Nicéphore Niépce** (1765-1833) — earliest surviving photograph (1826/27)
- **Louis Daguerre** (1787-1851) — daguerreotype process (1839)
- **William Henry Fox Talbot** (1800-1877) — calotype/negative-positive process (1841)
- **Anna Atkins** (1799-1871) — first photobook, cyanotypes of algae (1843)
- **Hippolyte Bayard** (1801-1887) — early direct positive process, first staged photo

### Expansion Era (1850s-1890s)
- **Mathew Brady** (1822-1896) — Civil War photography studio
- **Julia Margaret Cameron** (1815-1879) — artistic portraiture, soft focus
- **Eadweard Muybridge** (1830-1904) — motion studies, zoopraxiscope
- **George Eastman** (1854-1932) — Kodak, roll film, democratization

### Pictorialism (1890s-1910s)
- **Alfred Stieglitz** (1864-1946) — Photo-Secession, Gallery 291, advocate for photography as art
- **Edward Steichen** (1879-1973) — pictorialist turned modernist, later MoMA curator
- **Gertrude Käsebier** (1852-1934) — pictorialist portraits
- **Alvin Langdon Coburn** (1882-1966) — vortographs, abstract photography

### Modernism (1920s-1940s)
- **Paul Strand** (1890-1976) — straight photography pioneer
- **Edward Weston** (1886-1958) — f/64 Group, formalist studies
- **Ansel Adams** (1902-1984) — f/64 Group, Zone System, landscapes
- **László Moholy-Nagy** (1895-1946) — Bauhaus, New Vision, photograms
- **Alexander Rodchenko** (1891-1956) — Russian constructivism, radical perspectives
- **Man Ray** (1890-1976) — Surrealist, rayographs

### Documentary & Photojournalism (1930s-1970s)
- **Dorothea Lange** (1895-1965) — FSA, "Migrant Mother"
- **Walker Evans** (1903-1975) — FSA, American vernacular
- **Henri Cartier-Bresson** (1908-2004) — "decisive moment," Magnum co-founder
- **Robert Capa** (1913-1954) — war photography, Magnum co-founder
- **W. Eugene Smith** (1918-1978) — photojournalism, photo essays
- **Diane Arbus** (1923-1971) — psychological portraits of marginalized subjects
- **Garry Winogrand** (1928-1984) — street photography, American social landscape

### Color Photography (1960s-1980s)
- **William Eggleston** (b. 1939) — legitimized color in fine art, vernacular subjects
- **Stephen Shore** (b. 1947) — New Color Photography, American surfaces
- **Joel Meyerowitz** (b. 1938) — street photography in color
- **Helen Levitt** (1913-2009) — street photography, transition to color

### Postmodern & Contemporary (1970s-present)
- **Cindy Sherman** (b. 1954) — constructed photography, performance, appropriation
- **Richard Prince** (b. 1949) — appropriation, rephotography
- **Sherrie Levine** (b. 1947) — appropriation, questioning authorship
- **Jeff Wall** (b. 1946) — constructed photography, cinematic tableaux
- **Andreas Gursky** (b. 1955) — large-scale digital composites
- **Nan Goldin** (b. 1953) — intimate documentary, The Ballad of Sexual Dependency
- **Sally Mann** (b. 1951) — intimate family photography, Southern landscapes
- **Carrie Mae Weems** (b. 1953) — conceptual photography, race and identity

## Unexpected Connections (wild cards)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links
- **Photography and astronomy** — how astronomical photography advanced both fields; Hubble images as scientific and aesthetic objects
- **Photography and forensics** — Alphonse Bertillon's mugshot system, crime scene photography, surveillance
- **Photography and psychiatry** — Hugh Diamond photographing asylum patients (1850s); phototherapy
- **Photography and physics** — how photographic plates led to discovery of X-rays (Röntgen, 1895) and radioactivity (Becquerel, 1896)
- **Photography and spiritualism** — spirit photography, double exposures as "proof" of afterlife (1860s-1920s)

### Philosophical Connections
- **Roland Barthes, "Camera Lucida"** (1980) — phenomenology of photography, punctum vs. studium. https://www.upress.umn.edu/book-division/books/camera-lucida
- **Susan Sontag, "On Photography"** (1977) — photography and violence, consumption of images. https://www.sontag.foundation/
- **Walter Benjamin, "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction"** (1935) — aura, authenticity, politics
- **Vilém Flusser, "Towards a Philosophy of Photography"** (1983) — apparatus, program, technical images

### Contemporary Art Extensions
- **Photography-based installation** — how photographs function in 3D space (Zoe Leonard, Thomas Demand)
- **Photography and sculpture** — photosculpture, lenticular printing, dimensionality
- **Photography and painting's resurgence** — Gerhard Richter, Luc Tuymans painting from photographs
- **Post-photography** — what comes after photography? (Joan Fontcuberta, Trevor Paglen)

### Social/Political Dimensions
- **Photography and Civil Rights movement** — Gordon Parks, Danny Lyon, Charles Moore
- **Photography and AIDS crisis** — Nan Goldin, David Wojnarowicz, Nicholas Nixon
- **Photography and environmental activism** — Ansel Adams (conservation), Edward Burtynsky (extraction)
- **Photography and feminist art** — challenging male gaze (Martha Rosler, Barbara Kruger)
