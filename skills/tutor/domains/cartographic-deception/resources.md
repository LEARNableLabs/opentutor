# Cartographic Deception — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books
- **Mark Monmonier — *How to Lie with Maps* (3rd ed., 2018)**
  - The foundational text on cartographic manipulation. Accessible, example-rich, perfect for intermediate students. Covers projection bias, color tricks, scale manipulation, and statistical distortion. Start here.
  - University of Chicago Press

- **Denis Wood & John Fels — *The Power of Maps* (1992)**
  - Critical cartography classic. More theoretical than Monmonier but essential for understanding maps as rhetorical instruments, not neutral records. Good for lessons on visual argument and political choices.
  - Guilford Press

- **Jeremy Black — *Maps and Politics* (1997)**
  - Historical focus on maps as instruments of state power. Strong on colonial and imperial cartography, territorial claims, and propaganda. Use for Module 4 (20th century propaganda).
  - University of Chicago Press

- **J.B. Harley — *The New Nature of Maps: Essays in the History of Cartography* (2001)**
  - Collected essays from the founder of critical cartography. "Deconstructing the Map" (1989) is essential reading. Advanced theory but excerpts work for intermediate students.
  - Johns Hopkins University Press

### Articles & Essays
- **Harley, J.B. (1989). "Deconstructing the Map." *Cartographica* 26(2): 1-20.**
  - The essay that launched critical cartography. Argues maps are "social constructions" that encode power relations. Dense but foundational.

- **Wood, D. (1992). "How Maps Work." *Cartographica* 29(3): 66-74.**
  - Accessible introduction to semiotic analysis of maps. Good for understanding symbol systems and visual rhetoric.

## Online Collections & Archives

### Map Collections
- **Library of Congress — Persuasive Cartography Collection**
  - https://www.loc.gov/collections/persuasive-cartography/
  - Extensive archive of propaganda maps from multiple eras (WWI, WWII, Cold War). Searchable by theme, country, time period. **Essential resource** for Modules 3-4. High-resolution images, detailed metadata.

- **David Rumsey Map Collection**
  - https://www.davidrumsey.com/
  - 150,000+ historical maps, many with propaganda/colonial themes. Excellent for finding specific examples (Nazi expansion maps, imperial atlases, Soviet cartography). Georeferencing tools let you overlay historical maps on modern geography.

- **British Library — Maps Collection**
  - https://www.bl.uk/collection-guides/maps
  - Strong on British imperial cartography. Good for lessons on colonial mapping, Greenwich meridian, and territorial claims in Africa/Asia.

- **Cornell University — Maps as Weapons of Imperialism (exhibit)**
  - https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2012/06/exhibit-focuses-maps-weapons-imperialism
  - Focused exhibit on maps as tools of conquest. Good curated introduction to the topic.

### Educational Resources
- **National Geographic — MapMaker Interactive**
  - https://mapmaker.nationalgeographic.org/
  - Tool for creating custom maps. Useful for demonstrating how choices (projection, color, data classification) shape interpretation.

- **Wikipedia — Critical Cartography**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_cartography
  - Solid overview article with bibliography. Good starting point for counter-mapping and decolonial practice.

- **Wikipedia — Propaganda Map**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_map
  - Entry point with historical examples. Useful for quick reference but not deep analysis.

## Interactive Tools

### Projection Exploration
- **The True Size**
  - https://thetruesize.com/
  - Drag countries around on a Mercator map to see how their size changes with latitude. **Must-use tool** for Lessons 1-2. Makes area distortion immediately, viscerally clear.

- **XKCD — What Your Favorite Map Projection Says About You**
  - https://xkcd.com/977/
  - Humorous guide to projection biases and the politics of projection choice. Accessible entry point; use in Lessons 4-5.

- **Projection Wizard**
  - https://projectionwizard.org/
  - Tool for selecting appropriate projections based on map extent and purpose. Good for showing that projection choice should be deliberate, not default.

### Map Design & Analysis
- **Axis Maps — Cartography Guide**
  - https://www.axismaps.com/guide
  - Professional cartography principles (color, symbols, typography, interaction). Use for Lessons 12, 14, 17 to understand *good* design vs. *manipulative* design.

- **ColorBrewer**
  - https://colorbrewer2.org/
  - Tool for selecting color schemes for maps. Demonstrates how sequential/diverging/qualitative palettes shape interpretation. Use in Lesson 12 (color psychology).

## Code & Reproducible Examples

### Mapping Libraries
- **Leaflet.js**
  - https://leafletjs.com/
  - Open-source JavaScript library for interactive maps. Good for students who want to experiment with creating their own maps and making design choices explicit.

- **D3.js — Map Examples**
  - https://observablehq.com/@d3/gallery#maps
  - Observable notebooks with code for various projections and map visualizations. Good for advanced students interested in technical implementation.

### GIS Platforms
- **QGIS**
  - https://qgis.org/
  - Free, open-source GIS software. More complex but useful for students who want hands-on experience with spatial data and projection transformations.

- **Mapbox**
  - https://www.mapbox.com/
  - Platform for custom map design. Good for exploring how commercial mapping makes design choices (what to emphasize, how to style).

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Philosophy & Epistemology
- **Alfred Korzybski — "The map is not the territory"**
  - Semantics and epistemology. Maps are models; all models simplify. Connect to Lesson 4 (trade-offs).

- **Jorge Luis Borges — "On Exactitude in Science"**
  - One-paragraph parable about a 1:1 scale map. Absurdist thought experiment on utility vs. accuracy. Use in Lesson 6 review.

### Literature & Fiction
- **Lewis Carroll — *Sylvie and Bruno Concluded* (1893)**
  - Contains a joke about a 1:1 scale map that was never unfolded because it would cover the country. Precursor to Borges.

- **Neal Stephenson — *Anathem***
  - Science fiction exploring parallel worlds and different geometric projections of reality. For students interested in speculative takes on cartography.

### Art & Visual Culture
- **Buckminster Fuller — Dymaxion Map**
  - Unfolded icosahedron projection. Alternative to standard projections. Use when discussing projection alternatives.

- **Ai Weiwei — map installations**
  - Contemporary artist working with maps, migration, and borders. Good for counter-mapping discussion.

### History & Politics
- **Sykes-Picot Agreement (1916)**
  - Colonial map-making that created modern Middle East borders. Good case study for Lesson 16 (erasure) or Lesson 18 (territorial claims).

- **Gerrymandering**
  - Domestic analog to propaganda maps — manipulating boundaries to shape political outcomes. Connect to Lesson 15 (election maps).

### Technology & Surveillance
- **Strava heatmap controversy (2018)**
  - Fitness tracker data revealed secret military bases. Modern cartographic surveillance. Use in Lesson 26 (algorithmic bias).

- **Edward Tufte — *Envisioning Information***
  - Classic text on information design. Not map-specific but essential for understanding visual rhetoric.

## Using These Resources

### For Lesson Prep
- **Always start with Monmonier** — his examples are clear and well-explained
- **Use Library of Congress for specific historical maps** — high quality, well-documented
- **Show interactive tools early** (The True Size, XKCD) — engagement matters

### For Student Exploration
- **Point advanced students to Harley and Wood** — theory for those ready
- **Encourage tool experimentation** (Projection Wizard, ColorBrewer) — learning by doing
- **Share Strange Maps and Pudding** — fun, accessible, regularly updated

### For Depth
- **Historical: David Rumsey, British Library** — primary sources
- **Contemporary: The Guardian, Pudding, Big Think** — ongoing examples
- **Technical: D3 Observable, QGIS tutorials** — implementation
