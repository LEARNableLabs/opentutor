# Research Summary — Cartographic Deception & Propaganda Maps

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Power of Projection
The mathematical impossibility of projecting a sphere onto a plane creates unavoidable distortions. These technical constraints become political choices: which regions to preserve, which to distort. Mercator's projection, for instance, inflates high-latitude regions, making colonial powers appear larger and more dominant.

### 2. Historical Propaganda Maps
From medieval mappae mundi to Cold War imagery, maps have served as instruments of persuasion. Nazi maps justified territorial claims; Soviet maps depicted encirclement; American maps visualized the domino theory. Each projection of space encoded a worldview.

### 3. Symbolic & Color Manipulation
Cartographers use color, scale, symbol density, and selective labeling to guide interpretation. Red = danger. Larger symbols = importance. Omission = erasure. These visual rhetorics shape perception before conscious analysis begins.

### 4. Colonial & Imperial Mapping
Maps legitimized conquest by depicting "empty" lands, erasing indigenous presence, and imposing European spatial logic. The Greenwich meridian, arbitrary yet universal, centers European time and space.

### 5. Counter-Mapping & Critical Cartography
Indigenous communities, activists, and artists create alternative maps that challenge official narratives. These "counter-maps" reveal what dominant cartography hides: displacement, environmental destruction, contested borders.

### 6. Digital Manipulation & Modern Deception
GIS, satellite imagery, and interactive web maps introduce new forms of selective truth. Algorithmic bias in map platforms, deepfake satellite imagery, and data visualization choices continue cartographic deception in digital form.

## Key Sources

- **"How to Lie with Maps" by Mark Monmonier** — seminal text on cartographic distortion and manipulation
- **Library of Congress Persuasive Cartography collection** — extensive archive of propaganda maps from multiple eras
- **Cornell's "Maps as Weapons" exhibit** — focus on imperialism and territorial claims
- **Denis Wood's "The Power of Maps"** — critical cartography theory
- **Jeremy Crampton's work on critical GIS** — digital-era analysis
- **David Rumsey Map Collection** — historical maps with propaganda examples
- **British Library map collection** — imperial and colonial cartography
