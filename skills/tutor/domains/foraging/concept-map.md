# Foraging — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Plant families** — groups of related species sharing consistent morphological features
2. **Pattern recognition** — identifying family-level traits rather than memorizing individual species
3. **Morphological consistency** — predictable structural features within families (flower parts, leaf arrangement, fruit types)
4. **Rosaceae characteristics** — 5-petaled flowers, alternate leaves, aggregate fruits (strawberries, blackberries)
5. **Apiaceae identification** — umbel flower structure, hollow stems, compound leaves. Depends on: plant families
6. **Toxic lookalikes** — species that superficially resemble edible plants but contain harmful compounds. Depends on: morphological consistency
7. **Brassicaceae features** — 4-petaled cruciform flowers, pungent taste, silique fruits. Depends on: plant families
8. **Diagnostic features** — critical characteristics that distinguish between similar species. Depends on: pattern recognition
9. **Ecological succession** — predictable changes in plant communities over time after disturbance
10. **Disturbance ecology** — understanding how soil disruption creates foraging opportunities
11. **Ruderal species** — plants adapted to disturbed habitats (many wild edibles). Depends on: ecological succession
12. **Indicator species** — plants whose presence signals specific soil or environmental conditions. Depends on: disturbance ecology
13. **Plant communities** — assemblages of species that co-occur predictably. Depends on: indicator species
14. **Phenology** — the study of seasonal biological events (flowering, leafing, fruiting). Depends on: ecological succession
15. **Growing degree days** — cumulative temperature metric for predicting plant development. Depends on: phenology
16. **Environmental plasticity** — variation in plant characteristics based on growing conditions. Depends on: plant communities
17. **Chemical variation** — differences in secondary compounds (flavor, toxins) due to environment. Depends on: environmental plasticity
18. **Microhabitat effects** — how small-scale environmental differences affect plant growth. Depends on: environmental plasticity
19. **Habitat assessment** — evaluating a location's foraging potential. Depends on: plant communities, indicator species
20. **Toxin types** — categories of plant toxins (alkaloids, glycosides, oxalates, etc.)
21. **Dose-response** — relationship between toxin amount and harmful effects. Depends on: toxin types
22. **Preparation effects** — how cooking, drying, or leaching can reduce toxicity. Depends on: dose-response
23. **Negative identification** — determining what a plant is NOT by ruling out dangerous lookalikes. Depends on: toxic lookalikes, diagnostic features
24. **Multi-sense verification** — using sight, smell, touch, and taste (carefully) to confirm identity. Depends on: negative identification
25. **Risk calibration** — assessing probability of misidentification and consequences. Depends on: negative identification
26. **Bioaccumulation** — concentration of toxins (heavy metals, pesticides) in plant tissues over time
27. **Contamination risk** — environmental sources of toxins (roads, industry, treated lawns). Depends on: bioaccumulation
28. **Knowledge boundaries** — recognizing limits of one's identification skills. Depends on: risk calibration
29. **Life cycle stages** — different phases of plant growth (seedling, vegetative, flowering, fruiting). Depends on: phenology
30. **Chemical changes** — shifts in plant chemistry as it matures. Depends on: life cycle stages, chemical variation
31. **Palatability shifts** — changes in edibility/flavor through the growing season. Depends on: chemical changes
32. **Winter foraging** — identifying dormant or evergreen edibles in cold months. Depends on: life cycle stages
33. **Root identification** — identifying plants by underground structures when above-ground parts are absent. Depends on: winter foraging
34. **Phenology calendar** — seasonal timeline of plant availability. Depends on: phenology, growing degree days
35. **Population dynamics** — how harvest affects plant abundance and reproduction
36. **Sustainable harvest** — taking amounts that don't harm population viability. Depends on: population dynamics
37. **Impact assessment** — evaluating whether foraging pressure is sustainable. Depends on: sustainable harvest
38. **Invasive species** — non-native plants that harm ecosystems, often good foraging targets
39. **Ecological restoration** — using foraging to reduce invasive populations. Depends on: invasive species, impact assessment
40. **Ethical framework** — personal principles guiding foraging decisions. Depends on: sustainable harvest, knowledge boundaries, risk calibration

## Dependencies

### Foundation Dependencies
- **Apiaceae identification** requires understanding **plant families** because recognition starts at the family level before species-level differentiation
- **Diagnostic features** build on **pattern recognition** because you need to know what varies vs. what's consistent within a family
- **Toxic lookalikes** require **morphological consistency** knowledge because distinguishing similar species depends on recognizing subtle structural differences

### Ecological Dependencies
- **Ruderal species** understanding requires **ecological succession** because these plants are adapted to early successional (disturbed) habitats
- **Indicator species** build on **disturbance ecology** because plants indicate specific soil conditions often created by disturbance
- **Plant communities** depend on **indicator species** because communities are assemblages where indicator relationships scale up

### Safety Dependencies
- **Negative identification** requires both **toxic lookalikes** awareness and **diagnostic features** knowledge because you must know what to rule out AND how to rule it out
- **Risk calibration** depends on **negative identification** because assessing risk requires understanding both probability of error (ID skill) and consequences (toxin severity)
- **Knowledge boundaries** depend on **risk calibration** because recognizing your limits requires understanding stakes

### Seasonal Dependencies
- **Phenology calendar** requires both **phenology** and **growing degree days** because predicting seasonal availability combines observational patterns with temperature-based models
- **Chemical changes** depend on **life cycle stages** and **chemical variation** because plants' chemistry shifts predictably as they mature, and environment modulates these shifts
- **Winter foraging** depends on **life cycle stages** because identification without typical vegetative features requires understanding dormancy strategies

### Ethical Dependencies
- **Sustainable harvest** depends on **population dynamics** because taking sustainable amounts requires understanding reproduction and abundance
- **Ecological restoration** depends on **invasive species** knowledge and **impact assessment** because targeting invasives for heavy harvest requires distinguishing them and gauging population effects
- **Ethical framework** integrates **sustainable harvest**, **knowledge boundaries**, and **risk calibration** because personal foraging philosophy balances ecological impact, skill limitations, and safety

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Plant Family Recognition (Lessons 1-5)
**Why it's critical**: Everything else builds on pattern-based identification. Students who skip to species-level memorization struggle with novel plants and miss the "why" behind features.

**Gatekeeper for**: Diagnostic features, negative identification, habitat assessment

### Negative Identification (Lessons 13-15)
**Why it's critical**: Safety depends on knowing what something is NOT, not just what you think it is. This is the conceptual shift from beginner to intermediate foraging.

**Gatekeeper for**: Risk calibration, knowledge boundaries, ethical framework

### Phenology (Lessons 9, 20-23)
**Why it's critical**: Understanding when plants are available and how they change seasonally is essential for practical foraging. Without this, students just follow guides without comprehension.

**Gatekeeper for**: Seasonal strategies, phenology calendar, life cycle awareness

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic botany** — needed for plant families, morphological consistency, diagnostic features
- **Ecological literacy** — needed for disturbance ecology, succession, plant communities
- **Outdoor skills** — needed for habitat assessment, field practice, navigation
- **Risk assessment basics** — needed for risk calibration, knowledge boundaries

## Common Confusion Points

### Plant Families vs. Species
Students often want to jump directly to "what can I eat" without learning families. This creates fragile knowledge that doesn't transfer.

**Resolution**: Emphasize that learning 10 family patterns > memorizing 50 species pictures

### Positive vs. Negative ID
Beginners think "it looks like X" is sufficient. Intermediate foragers must ask "what else could this be, and how do I rule those out?"

**Resolution**: Make negative identification explicit and practiced, especially for high-risk groups

### Ecological Context vs. Picture Matching
Students may not understand why habitat matters—"can't I just use an app?" Apps fail when plants look different than photos, or when similar species overlap.

**Resolution**: Teach indicator species and plant communities to show how context narrows possibilities

### Seasonality
Assuming plants look the same year-round. Wild edibles change dramatically through growth stages.

**Resolution**: Show same species across seasons; emphasize life cycle stages and chemical changes
