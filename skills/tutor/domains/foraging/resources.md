# Foraging — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Textbooks

- **Botany in a Day: The Patterns Method of Plant Identification** by Thomas J. Elpel
  - [https://www.hopspress.com/botany-in-a-day.htm](https://www.hopspress.com/botany-in-a-day.htm)
  - Pattern-based family identification. Essential for intermediate learners moving beyond picture matching.
  - Best for: Plant family recognition, morphological thinking

- **Edible Wild Plants: Wild Foods From Dirt To Plate** by John Kallas
  - Scientific rigor, detailed species monographs, safety-first approach
  - Best for: Accurate information, processing methods, toxin awareness

- **The Forager's Harvest** and **Nature's Garden** by Samuel Thayer
  - [https://www.foragersharvest.com/](https://www.foragersharvest.com/)
  - Deeply detailed species accounts with ecology, harvesting, and preparation
  - Best for: Intermediate to advanced learners, species deep-dives

- **Stalking the Wild Asparagus** by Euell Gibbons
  - Classic foundational text (1962), now somewhat dated but culturally important
  - Best for: Historical perspective, enthusiasm, accessible entry point

### Regional Field Guides

- **Peterson Field Guides to Edible Wild Plants** (Eastern/Central North America)
  - [https://www.hmhco.com/peterson-field-guides](https://www.hmhco.com/peterson-field-guides)
  - Portable, well-illustrated, region-specific

- **Newcomb's Wildflower Guide** by Lawrence Newcomb
  - [https://www.amazon.com/Newcombs-Wildflower-Guide-Lawrence-Newcomb/dp/0316604429](https://www.amazon.com/Newcombs-Wildflower-Guide-Lawrence-Newcomb/dp/0316604429)
  - Dichotomous key system—teaches systematic identification
  - Best for: Building ID skills beyond pictures

- **Audubon Society Field Guides** (regional variations)
  - [https://www.audubon.org/field-guides](https://www.audubon.org/field-guides)
  - Photo-based, comprehensive, regionally specific

### Academic and Scientific Resources

- **Plants For A Future Database**
  - [https://pfaf.org/](https://pfaf.org/)
  - Comprehensive database of edible, medicinal, and useful plants with detailed information
  - Best for: Research, unusual species, global perspective

- **USDA PLANTS Database**
  - [https://plants.usda.gov/](https://plants.usda.gov/)
  - Official taxonomy, range maps, characteristics
  - Best for: Verifying scientific names, checking native range

- **Missouri Botanical Garden's Tropicos**
  - [https://www.tropicos.org/](https://www.tropicos.org/)
  - Botanical nomenclature authority
  - Best for: Resolving taxonomic confusion

### Interactive Tools

- **iNaturalist**
  - [https://www.inaturalist.org/](https://www.inaturalist.org/)
  - Community-powered species identification, range data, observation logging
  - Best for: Hypothesis generation, citizen science, local flora discovery
  - **Critical note**: Use as research tool, NOT final authority for edible ID

- **Go Botany (Native Plant Trust)**
  - [https://gobotany.nativeplanttrust.org/](https://gobotany.nativeplanttrust.org/)
  - Simple and full key modes for New England plants
  - Best for: Beginner-friendly dichotomous key practice

- **PlantNet**
  - [https://plantnet.org/](https://plantnet.org/)
  - AI-powered plant ID app (alternative to iNaturalist)
  - Best for: Quick field checks, mobile use

- **ForageCast**
  - [https://www.foragecast.com/](https://www.foragecast.com/)
  - Phenology forecasting for foragers (US-based)
  - Best for: Seasonal planning, timing harvests

- **Falling Fruit**
  - [https://fallingfruit.org/](https://fallingfruit.org/)
  - Crowdsourced map of edibles (urban fruit trees, wild patches)
  - Best for: Urban foraging, finding local spots

- **USA National Phenology Network**
  - [https://www.usanpn.org/](https://www.usanpn.org/)
  - Phenology maps and forecasts
  - Best for: Understanding seasonal timing, climate impacts

### Code and Data

- **iNaturalist API**
  - [https://api.inaturalist.org/](https://api.inaturalist.org/)
  - Access observation data programmatically
  - Best for: Data analysis, building custom tools

- **GBIF (Global Biodiversity Information Facility)**
  - [https://www.gbif.org/](https://www.gbif.org/)
  - Massive biodiversity database including plant observations
  - Best for: Research, range verification, occurrence data

  - [https://www.poison.org/](https://www.poison.org/)

  - Emergency resource for suspected poisoning

- **USDA Forest Service Poisonous Plants Database**
  - [https://www.fs.usda.gov/wildflowers/ethnobotany/food/index.shtml](https://www.fs.usda.gov/wildflowers/ethnobotany/food/index.shtml)

- **Cornell University Poisonous Plants Database**
  - [https://poisonousplants.ansci.cornell.edu/](https://poisonousplants.ansci.cornell.edu/)

- **Eat The Weeds (Green Deane)**

  - Best for: Detailed comparisons, regional variation

## Tools and Gear

### Essential

- **Hand lens (10x or 20x)** — For examining flower structures, leaf details
- **Field notebook** — Waterproof preferred
- **Camera/phone** — For documentation (never for ID alone)
- **Harvesting tools** — Scissors, digging stick, bags/baskets
- **Regional field guide** — Physical book, not just apps

### Advanced

- **Microscope or jeweler's loupe** — For spores, trichomes (advanced ID)
- **Plant press** — For creating reference specimens
- **GPS/mapping app** — For relocating productive patches
- **Foraging vest/pack** — Organization for multi-species harvests

## Legal and Access Resources

### Foraging Regulations

- **Local parks department** — Regulations vary by municipality
- **National Park Service foraging rules** — Generally prohibited or highly restricted
- **State DNR/Fish & Wildlife** — Some states allow foraging on public land with permits

### Land Access

- **Public Lands Database**
  - [https://www.publiclands.org/](https://www.publiclands.org/)
  - Find accessible public lands for foraging
  - Best for: Locating legal foraging areas

- **Private land** — Always ask permission; build relationships with landowners

## Mobile Apps Summary

- **iNaturalist** — Community ID, observation logging
- **PlantNet** — AI-powered ID (use cautiously)
- **Seek (by iNaturalist)** — Gamified, beginner-friendly
- **Picture This** — Commercial app, subscription-based
- **ForageCast** — Phenology tracking

**CRITICAL REMINDER**: No app is 100% reliable for edible/toxic distinction. Always verify with multiple sources and morphological confirmation before consuming.
