# Propaganda and Information Warfare — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Historical Development
- WWI and WWII propaganda (posters, radio, newsreels)
- Cold War information campaigns
- Digital age disinformation (2000s-present)

### Psychological Foundations
- Cognitive biases and heuristics
- Persuasion techniques (Cialdini's principles)
- Social identity theory and in-group/out-group dynamics
- Motivated reasoning and confirmation bias

### Technical Mechanisms
- Classical propaganda techniques (bandwagon, card stacking, glittering generalities)
- Modern computational propaganda (bots, deepfakes, algorithmic amplification)
- Network effects and viral spread
- Microtargeting and psychographic profiling

### Information Warfare Tactics
- Disinformation vs. misinformation vs. malinformation
- Narrative warfare and strategic framing
- Censorship and information control
- Cyber operations and hack-and-leak campaigns

### Defense and Detection
- Media literacy frameworks
- Fact-checking methodologies
- Detection algorithms (bot detection, deepfake detection)
- Inoculation theory and prebunking
- Cognitive security measures

### Institutional Dimensions
- State actors (Russia's IRA, China's 50 Cent Army)
- Non-state actors (activist groups, corporations)
- Platform governance and content moderation
- Regulatory approaches (EU Digital Services Act)

## Research Gaps and Challenges

- Rapidly evolving tactics outpace academic research
- Platform data access restrictions limit external analysis
- Cross-cultural differences in propaganda susceptibility understudied
- Long-term effectiveness of media literacy interventions unclear
- Tension between content moderation and free speech
