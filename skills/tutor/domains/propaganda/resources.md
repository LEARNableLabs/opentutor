# Propaganda and Information Warfare — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Books

- **"Propaganda" by Edward Bernays (1928)** — The founding text of modern propaganda theory, written by the "father of public relations." Shows how propaganda techniques were systematized for corporate and political use. Short, readable, revelatory. Good for intermediate students to see the intellectual origins.

- **"Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media" by Edward S. Herman and Noam Chomsky (1988)** — Classic analysis of media propaganda in democracies. Introduces the propaganda model (five filters). Dense but essential. Use selected chapters rather than full book for intermediate level.

- **"LikeWar: The Weaponization of Social Media" by P.W. Singer and Emerson T. Brooking (2018)** — Accessible overview of digital propaganda and information warfare. Covers bots, trolls, viral spread, state actors. Excellent case studies. Perfect for intermediate students transitioning from theory to practice.
  - https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/555678/likewar-by-pw-singer-and-emerson-t-brooking/

- **"Network Propaganda: Manipulation, Disinformation, and Radicalization in American Politics" by Yochai Benkler, Robert Faris, and Hal Roberts (2018)** — Data-driven analysis of propaganda ecosystems. Shows asymmetric polarization. More academic but crucial for understanding network effects.
  - https://global.oup.com/academic/product/network-propaganda-9780190923631

- **"The Age of Surveillance Capitalism" by Shoshana Zuboff (2019)** — Explores how data extraction enables microtargeting and behavioral manipulation. Connects propaganda to business models. Long but influential.
  - https://www.publicaffairsbooks.com/titles/shoshana-zuboff/the-age-of-surveillance-capitalism/9781610395694/

- **"This Is Not Propaganda: Adventures in the War Against Reality" by Peter Pomerantsev (2019)** — Journalist's account of modern information warfare. Engaging narratives, less theoretical. Great for maintaining student interest.
  - https://www.publicaffairsbooks.com/titles/peter-pomerantsev/this-is-not-propaganda/9781541762114/

### Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Advertising and propaganda** — Bernays applied WWI propaganda techniques to corporate marketing. Study vintage ads to see propaganda in commercial context.
  - Prelinger Archives: https://archive.org/details/prelinger

- **Magic and misdirection** — Propaganda uses magician's techniques (attention manipulation, false causality, priming). Study Penn & Teller's "Fool Us" for pattern recognition.

- **Cults and persuasion** — Same psychological mechanisms. Steven Hassan's BITE model (Behavior, Information, Thought, Emotional control) maps to information warfare.
  - https://freedomofmind.com/cult-mind-control/bite-model/

- **Game design and engagement** — How games hook players mirrors how propaganda hooks audiences (reward loops, identity, community). Connect to Nir Eyal's "Hooked" model.

- **Memetics and cultural evolution** — Richard Dawkins' concept of memes as replicators. Connect to Susan Blackmore's work on memetic drive.

- **Rhetoric and classical persuasion** — Aristotle's ethos/pathos/logos predates modern propaganda but explains it. Study ancient Greek rhetoric.

- **Poker and deception** — Bluffing, tells, information asymmetry. Propaganda as strategic deception game. Maria Konnikova's "The Biggest Bluff."

- **Hypnosis and suggestion** — Milton Erickson's conversational hypnosis techniques show how language bypasses critical thinking.

## Primary News Sources and Databases

### Fact-Checking Organizations

- **Snopes** — Oldest fact-checking site. Covers urban legends to political claims.
  - https://www.snopes.com/

- **PolitiFact** — Political fact-checking with Truth-O-Meter ratings. Good for tracking claim evolution.
  - https://www.politifact.com/

- **FactCheck.org (Annenberg Public Policy Center)** — Nonpartisan political fact-checking. Detailed methodology.
  - https://www.factcheck.org/

- **Full Fact (UK)** — Independent fact-checker. Transparent about sources and reasoning.
  - https://fullfact.org/

- **Africa Check** — Fact-checking in African context. Shows cultural variation in misinformation.
  - https://africacheck.org/

### Platform Transparency Resources

- **Facebook Ad Library** — Searchable archive of political ads on Facebook/Instagram. Shows targeting, spend, reach.
  - https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/

- **Twitter Transparency Center** — Datasets of information operations, bot networks removed by Twitter.
  - https://transparency.twitter.com/

- **Google Transparency Report** — Government requests, content removal, traffic patterns.
  - https://transparencyreport.google.com/

- **TikTok Transparency Center** — Content moderation data, government requests (limited).
  - https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/

### Media Monitoring and Analysis

- **GDELT Project** — Global Database of Events, Language, and Tone. Tracks worldwide news and information flows.
  - https://www.gdeltproject.org/

- **Media Cloud (MIT/Harvard)** — Analyze media ecosystems, track story evolution across outlets.
  - https://mediacloud.org/

- **NewsGuard** — Browser extension rating news site credibility. Controversial but useful for discussion.
  - https://www.newsguardtech.com/

- **AllSides Media Bias Rating** — Rates news sources on political bias spectrum. Includes community feedback.
  - https://www.allsides.com/media-bias
