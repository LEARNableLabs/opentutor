# Propaganda and Information Warfare — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Propaganda definition** — information designed to influence attitudes and behavior, often through emotional appeals
2. **Historical context** — evolution from wartime posters to digital manipulation
3. **Information as weapon** — strategic use of information to achieve political/military objectives
4. **Ethical boundaries** — line between persuasion and manipulation
5. **Motivated reasoning** — tendency to accept information that confirms existing beliefs. Depends on: propaganda definition
6. **Confirmation bias** — seeking and interpreting information to support preconceptions. Depends on: motivated reasoning
7. **Social proof** — tendency to follow what others do or believe. Depends on: propaganda definition
8. **In-group bias** — favoring information and people from one's own group. Depends on: social proof
9. **Cialdini's principles** — six principles of persuasion (reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity). Depends on: ethical boundaries
10. **Classical propaganda techniques** — card stacking, glittering generalities, name calling, bandwagon, etc. Depends on: historical context, Cialdini's principles
11. **Emotional manipulation** — using fear, anger, or joy to bypass rational analysis. Depends on: classical propaganda techniques
12. **Framing** — presenting information to emphasize certain interpretations. Depends on: classical propaganda techniques
13. **Repetition** — reinforcing messages through repeated exposure. Depends on: cognitive fluency
14. **Algorithmic amplification** — automated systems that spread content based on engagement. Depends on: information as weapon
15. **Viral spread** — rapid information dissemination through networks. Depends on: social proof, algorithmic amplification
16. **Social bots** — automated accounts that mimic human behavior. Depends on: algorithmic amplification
17. **Coordinated inauthentic behavior** — networks of accounts working together to deceive. Depends on: social bots
18. **Deepfakes** — AI-generated synthetic media. Depends on: information as weapon
19. **Disinformation** — false information spread with intent to deceive. Depends on: ethical boundaries
20. **Misinformation** — false information spread without intent to deceive. Depends on: disinformation
21. **Malinformation** — true information used to cause harm. Depends on: disinformation
22. **Firehose of falsehood** — overwhelming volume of contradictory narratives. Depends on: repetition, disinformation
23. **Hack-and-leak operations** — stealing information to release strategically. Depends on: information as weapon
24. **Censorship** — suppressing information to control narratives. Depends on: information as weapon
25. **Inoculation theory** — building resistance to persuasion through exposure. Depends on: classical propaganda techniques
26. **Prebunking** — preemptively refuting false claims. Depends on: inoculation theory
27. **Lateral reading** — verifying information by checking multiple sources. Depends on: source evaluation
28. **Source evaluation** — assessing credibility and bias. Depends on: ethical boundaries
29. **Platform governance** — rules and enforcement for online content. Depends on: coordinated inauthentic behavior, censorship
30. **Content moderation** — removing or labeling problematic content. Depends on: platform governance
31. **Automated detection** — using AI to identify propaganda. Depends on: social bots, deepfakes
32. **Multi-stakeholder approach** — coordinated action across platforms, governments, civil society. Depends on: platform governance

## Dependencies

### Psychological Foundations Enable Technique Analysis
- Understanding **confirmation bias** and **motivated reasoning** is essential before analyzing why propaganda works. You can't recognize manipulation without understanding how your mind processes information.
- **Social proof** and **in-group bias** explain why people spread propaganda even when they know better — these are prerequisite to understanding viral spread and network effects.

### Classical Techniques Are Building Blocks for Digital Tactics
- **Classical propaganda techniques** must be mastered before studying digital propaganda. Modern tactics are often classical techniques at scale.
- **Emotional manipulation** and **framing** apply to both posters and tweets — understanding them in simple contexts makes complex applications clearer.

### Technical Mechanisms Build on Each Other
- **Algorithmic amplification** depends on understanding how platforms reward engagement, which builds on **social proof**.
- **Coordinated inauthentic behavior** only makes sense after understanding **social bots** — you need to know what a single bot does before understanding coordinated networks.
- **Viral spread** requires understanding both **social proof** (why people share) and **algorithmic amplification** (how systems boost content).

### Detection Requires Understanding Production
- **Automated detection** can't be understood without first studying **social bots** and **deepfakes** — you must know what you're detecting.
- **Lateral reading** and **source evaluation** are foundational skills that make advanced techniques like **prebunking** possible.

### Policy and Governance Are Applied Concepts
- **Platform governance** and **content moderation** only make sense after understanding the threats (**coordinated inauthentic behavior**, **censorship**).
- **Multi-stakeholder approach** is a synthesis concept that requires understanding all previous dimensions.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic psychology** — needed for understanding cognitive biases, motivated reasoning, persuasion principles
- **Media literacy fundamentals** — needed for source evaluation, lateral reading, understanding media formats
- **Critical thinking** — needed for distinguishing correlation from causation, evaluating arguments, recognizing fallacies
- **Social media platforms** — needed for understanding algorithmic amplification, viral spread, platform-specific features

## Concept Bottlenecks

### Motivated Reasoning (Lesson 3)
The single most important concept. If students don't grasp that their brains actively resist unwelcome information, nothing else makes sense. This explains why smart people fall for propaganda and why fact-checking often fails.

**Signals of confusion:**
- Thinking propaganda only works on "other people"
- Believing facts alone change minds
- Missing the emotional dimension of belief formation

**How to reinforce:** Use personal examples. Ask students to recall times they dismissed evidence that challenged their views. Make it safe to admit bias.

### Disinformation vs. Misinformation (Lesson 15)
Students often conflate these terms. Understanding intent is crucial for:
- Choosing appropriate responses (education vs enforcement)
- Analyzing threat actors
- Evaluating policy solutions

**Signals of confusion:**
- Using terms interchangeably
- Focusing only on content accuracy, ignoring intent
- Treating all false information as equally dangerous

**How to reinforce:** Use case studies with clear intent differences. Compare honest mistakes to deliberate campaigns.

### Algorithmic Amplification (Lesson 11)
The bridge between individual psychology and mass persuasion. Students must understand:
- Algorithms aren't neutral
- Engagement ≠ truth
- Scale changes everything

**Signals of confusion:**
- Blaming individuals for systemic problems
- Thinking "going viral" is random
- Missing the feedback loop between human behavior and algorithmic optimization

**How to reinforce:** Show the math. Demonstrate how engagement metrics drive amplification regardless of veracity.

## Common Misconception Chains

1. **"Propaganda doesn't work on educated people"** → fails to understand motivated reasoning → misses how in-group bias operates → can't explain why experts fall for domain-specific propaganda
2. **"Fact-checking solves misinformation"** → misses emotional dimension → doesn't understand backfire effect → fails to see why repetition beats correction
3. **"Technology is the problem"** → focuses on tools not incentives → misses that propaganda predates social media → fails to see how platforms amplify human psychology
4. **"Censorship is the solution"** → doesn't understand information control dynamics → misses free speech tensions → fails to consider adversarial adaptation
