# Environmental Law and Regulation — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Foundational Framework**
   - National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) and environmental impact assessment
   - Administrative law foundations (rulemaking, judicial review, standing)
   - Constitutional dimensions (Commerce Clause, federalism, takings)

2. **Core Regulatory Statutes**
   - Clean Air Act (CAA) — criteria pollutants, NAAQS, state implementation plans, mobile sources
   - Clean Water Act (CWA) — NPDES permits, wetlands jurisdiction, water quality standards
   - Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) — hazardous waste management
   - Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA/Superfund) — site cleanup, liability

3. **Species and Land Protection**
   - Endangered Species Act (ESA) — listing, critical habitat, Section 7 consultation, Section 9 take prohibition
   - National Forest Management Act, Federal Land Policy and Management Act
   - Wetlands regulation (Section 404, Rapanos/WOTUS debate)

4. **Emerging and Cross-Cutting Issues**
   - Climate change law (EPA's authority under CAA, state initiatives, Paris Agreement)
   - Environmental justice (Executive Order 12898, NEPA integration, disparate impact)
   - Toxic substances regulation (TSCA reform)
   - International environmental law (treaties, trade and environment)

## Research Quality Assessment

- **Coverage**: Strong institutional support from law schools, EPA, ELI; well-established casebooks; active litigation and rulemaking provide real-world data
- **Currency**: Climate law and environmental justice are rapidly evolving (2020s focus); traditional pollution control law is mature but still dynamic (e.g., WOTUS rule revisions)
- **Interdisciplinarity**: Environmental law uniquely integrates science, economics, policy, and ethics — resources reflect this (e.g., joint law/policy courses)
- **Accessibility**: Intermediate learners can access case law, regulations, and secondary sources; primary statutes are readable but complex without guidance
- **Gaps**: Less emphasis on state/local environmental law in national curricula; limited comparative/international coverage in intro courses

## Recommended Learning Sequence

1. Start with NEPA (procedural backbone, easier conceptually)
2. Introduce administrative law mechanics alongside first substantive statutes
3. Build pollution control law (CAA/CWA) as core — illustrative of cooperative federalism, permits, standards
4. Layer in CERCLA (retroactive liability, more complex causation)
5. Add ESA (species protection, distinct regulatory logic)
6. Integrate cross-cutting themes (environmental justice, climate) throughout, culminating in capstone
7. Include real cases, current rulemakings, and enforcement actions to ground abstract doctrine
