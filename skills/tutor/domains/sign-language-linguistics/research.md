# Sign Language Linguistics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Phonology
- Sign language phonemes (handshape, location, movement, palm orientation, non-manual markers)
- Minimal pairs and phonological contrast
- Syllable structure in sign languages
- Phonological processes (assimilation, metathesis, deletion)

### 2. Morphology
- Classifier constructions
- Verb agreement and spatial modification
- Derivational and inflectional processes
- Iconicity vs. arbitrariness in sign formation
- Productive morphology (aspect, plurality, intensification)

### 3. Syntax
- Word order variation across sign languages
- Role shift and constructed action/dialogue
- Topicalization and topic-comment structure
- Spatial grammar and reference tracking
- Null arguments and pro-drop properties

### 4. Semantics & Pragmatics
- Iconicity and its linguistic role
- Depicting signs (formerly "classifiers")
- Constructed action vs. quotation
- Discourse markers and turn-taking

### 5. Sociolinguistics
- Language variation (regional, social, ethnic)
- Language contact and code-blending
- Deaf communities and language attitudes
- Language planning and standardization debates
- Bilingualism and multilingualism

### 6. Acquisition & Processing
- First language acquisition in deaf children
- Critical period effects
- Bilingual acquisition (sign + spoken language)
- Neural processing of sign languages

### 7. Typology & History
- Cross-linguistic variation in sign languages
- Sign language families and historical relationships
- Emergence of new sign languages (NSL, ABSL, ISN)
- Contact-induced change

## Key Sources

### Foundational Textbooks
- **Linguistics of Sign Languages: An Introduction** (Baker, van den Bogaerde, Pfau, Schermer, 2016)
- **The Signs of Language** (Klima & Bellugi, 1979) — classic, foundational work
- **Sign Language: An International Handbook** (Pfau, Steinbach, Woll, 2012) — comprehensive reference

### Academic Journals
- **Sign Language & Linguistics** (John Benjamins)
- **Sign Language Studies** (Gallaudet University Press)
- **Journal of Deaf Studies and Deaf Education**

### Pedagogical Gaps
- Most resources assume familiarity with at least one sign language
- Few intermediate-level resources bridge elementary ASL and linguistic theory
- Need for cross-linguistic examples beyond ASL

## Assessment for Intermediate Level

**Prerequisites needed:**
- Basic familiarity with general linguistics (phonology, morphology, syntax concepts)
- Exposure to at least one sign language (ASL, BSL, etc.) — not fluency, but familiarity
- Understanding of language as a cognitive system

**Appropriate depth:**
- Formal analysis of sign language structure
- Cross-linguistic comparison
- Reading primary literature (research papers)
- Critical engagement with debates (iconicity, modality effects, etc.)
