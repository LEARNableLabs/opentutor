# Sign Language Linguistics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Linguistic universals** — properties shared by all human languages, regardless of modality
2. **Signed vs. spoken modality** — visual-gestural vs. auditory-vocal channel differences
3. **Modality effects** — how the visual-gestural channel shapes language structure. Depends on: 2
4. **Simultaneity** — expressing multiple morphemes at once (unlike spoken languages' linearity). Depends on: 3
5. **Spatial grammar** — using physical space to encode grammatical relationships. Depends on: 3
6. **Iconicity** — form-meaning resemblance (more prevalent in sign languages than spoken). Depends on: 2
7. **Sign language typology** — cross-linguistic variation among sign languages. Depends on: 1
8. **Language emergence** — how new sign languages arise in deaf communities. Depends on: 1, 7
9. **Phonological parameters** — handshape, location, movement, orientation, non-manuals. Depends on: 2
10. **Minimal pairs** — signs differing in one phonological parameter. Depends on: 9
11. **Phonological processes** — assimilation, deletion, epenthesis in sign production. Depends on: 9, 10
12. **Non-manual signals** — facial expressions and head/body movements with linguistic function. Depends on: 9
13. **Syllable structure** — prosodic organization of signs. Depends on: 9, 11
14. **Compounding** — combining signs to create new lexical items. Depends on: 9
15. **Simultaneous morphology** — expressing multiple morphemes on different articulators at once. Depends on: 4, 9
16. **Sequential morphology** — linear concatenation of morphemes. Depends on: 9
17. **Classifier constructions/depicting signs** — productive use of handshapes to represent entities. Depends on: 6, 15
18. **Verb agreement** — directional modification to encode arguments. Depends on: 5, 15
19. **Aspectual inflection** — modifying movement to express temporal properties. Depends on: 15
20. **Grammaticalization** — shift from iconic/lexical to arbitrary/grammatical. Depends on: 6, 17
21. **Basic word order** — default constituent order in sign languages. Depends on: 1, 7
22. **Topicalization** — information structure and syntactic positioning. Depends on: 21
23. **Constructed action/dialogue** — shifting perspective to represent others' actions/speech. Depends on: 5, 12
24. **Null arguments** — argument omission patterns. Depends on: 5, 18
25. **Clause structure** — syntactic organization, embedding, subordination. Depends on: 21, 22
26. **Syntactic non-manuals** — non-manual markers signaling syntactic boundaries. Depends on: 12, 25
27. **Regional variation** — dialectal differences within sign languages. Depends on: 7
28. **Code-blending** — mixing sign and spoken language elements in bilingual contexts. Depends on: 7, 27
29. **Neurolinguistics of sign** — brain processing of visual-gestural language. Depends on: 1, 2

## Dependencies

### Critical Pathways

**Modality foundation** → **Phonology** → **Morphology** → **Syntax**
- Understanding modality effects (2, 3) is essential before diving into phonological parameters (9)
- Phonological parameters (9) must precede morphological analysis (14-19) because morphemes are composed of phonological units
- Morphological knowledge (especially 18, 19) is needed for understanding syntactic structure (21-26)

**Iconicity thread** (6 → 17 → 20)
- Basic iconicity concept (6) must come early but gets revisited throughout
- Classifier constructions (17) are a prime example of productive iconicity
- Grammaticalization (20) explains how iconic forms become arbitrary over time

**Spatial grammar thread** (5 → 18 → 23 → 24)
- Spatial grammar (5) is foundational for understanding verb agreement (18)
- Both feed into constructed action (23) and null argument licensing (24)

**Non-manual thread** (12 → 26)
- Non-manual signals are introduced in phonology (12)
- Then reappear as syntactic markers (26)
- This shows the dual function of the same articulatory features

### Bottleneck Concepts

**Phonological parameters (9)** — all subsequent linguistic analysis depends on recognizing the compositional structure of signs

**Simultaneity (4)** — crucial for understanding why sign language morphology differs from spoken language morphology

**Spatial grammar (5)** — unlocks verb agreement, reference tracking, and much of sign language syntax

**Iconicity (6)** — recurs throughout the curriculum; must be introduced early but revisited often to avoid oversimplification

## Prerequisite Topics

### Essential Background

- **General linguistics fundamentals** — needed for: concepts 1, 9-11, 14-16, 21-26
  - Understanding of phoneme, morpheme, syntax, grammaticality
  - Familiarity with linguistic terminology and argumentation
  
- **Phonology basics** — needed for: concepts 9-13
  - Distinctive features, minimal pairs, phonological rules
  - Prosody and suprasegmental structure

- **Morphology basics** — needed for: concepts 14-20
  - Derivation vs. inflection
  - Productivity and compositionality
  - Lexicalization

- **Syntax basics** — needed for: concepts 21-26
  - Constituent structure, phrase structure
  - Agreement, case, word order typology
  
### Helpful but Not Essential

- **Familiarity with a sign language** — helps with: all concepts
  - Not fluency, but exposure to signing
  - Recognition of basic grammatical patterns
  
- **Cognitive linguistics** — helps with: concepts 6, 17, 20, 23
  - Embodiment, conceptual metaphor
  - Usage-based theories

- **Sociolinguistics** — needed for: concepts 27-28
  - Language variation and change
  - Bilingualism and language contact

## Common Misconception Nodes

1. **Iconicity ≠ lack of linguistic structure** (concept 6)
   - Students often think iconic = not fully linguistic
   - Must emphasize conventionalization and grammatical constraints

2. **Simultaneity ≠ chaotic** (concept 4)
   - Students may think simultaneous morphology lacks structure
   - Must show clear morphological slots and constraints

3. **Spatial grammar ≠ just pointing** (concept 5)
   - Students may reduce spatial grammar to simple deixis
   - Must demonstrate abstract grammatical use of space

4. **Classifiers as morphemes, not gestures** (concept 17)
   - Most controversial area
   - Must present evidence for linguistic status while acknowledging iconicity

5. **Role shift as grammatical, not theatrical** (concept 23)
   - Students may confuse constructed action with acting
   - Must emphasize grammatical functions and constraints

## Pedagogical Flow

### Phase 1: Foundations (Lessons 1-5)
Establish that sign languages are full linguistic systems, shaped by but not limited by modality.

### Phase 2: Phonology (Lessons 6-11)
Build the sublexical analysis toolkit. Show how abstract units compose signs.

### Phase 3: Morphology (Lessons 12-17)
Explore word-building, with special attention to simultaneity and iconicity debates.

### Phase 4: Syntax & Discourse (Lessons 18-23)
Analyze sentence structure, with focus on spatial and non-manual grammar.

### Phase 5: Variation & Applications (Lessons 24-26)
Connect to sociolinguistics and emerging research frontiers.

### Review Points
- After Phonology (Lesson 11): consolidate parameter analysis and phonological processes
- After Morphology (Lesson 17): consolidate morpheme types and iconicity debates
- After Syntax (Lesson 23): consolidate clause structure and information structure
