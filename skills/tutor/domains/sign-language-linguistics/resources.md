# Sign Language Linguistics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Linguistics of Sign Languages: An Introduction** (Baker, van den Bogaerde, Pfau, Schermer, 2016)
  - Comprehensive undergraduate-level introduction
  - Covers phonology, morphology, syntax, acquisition, sociolinguistics
  - Good for intermediate learners
  - https://doi.org/10.1075/z.199

- **The Signs of Language** (Klima & Bellugi, 1979)
  - Classic foundational work on ASL linguistics
  - Dated but historically important
  - Introduced phonological analysis of signs
  - Available through university libraries

- **Sign Language: An International Handbook** (Pfau, Steinbach, Woll, 2012)
  - Advanced reference work
  - Comprehensive chapters on all aspects of sign linguistics
  - For diving deep on specific topics
  - https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110261325

- **Sign Languages** (Brentari, ed., 2010) — Cambridge Language Surveys
  - Cross-linguistic typological perspective
  - Good for comparative work
  - Intermediate to advanced
  - https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511712203

- **Syntax of American Sign Language** (Neidle et al., 2000)
  - Generative formal approach to ASL syntax
  - Advanced, assumes formal syntax background
  - https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262640671/

### Academic Journals

- **Sign Language & Linguistics** (John Benjamins)
  - Top journal in the field
  - Peer-reviewed research articles
  - https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/15691586

- **Sign Language Studies** (Gallaudet University Press)
  - Linguistics and deaf studies
  - More interdisciplinary than SL&L
  - https://www.signlanguagestudies.org

- **Journal of Deaf Studies and Deaf Education** (Oxford)
  - Includes linguistic, educational, and social research
  - https://academic.oup.com/jdsde

### Online Course Materials

  - Video examples with glossing

### Interactive Tools & Databases

- **SignBank** (various sign languages)
  - https://www.signbank.org
  - Searchable lexical databases for ASL, BSL, Auslan, NGT, etc.
  - Annotated video examples
  - Phonological parameter search

- **Spreadthesign.com**
  - https://www.spreadthesign.com
  - Multilingual sign dictionary (33+ sign languages)
  - Great for cross-linguistic comparison

- **SignWriting**
  - https://www.signwriting.org
  - Notation system for writing sign languages
  - SignPuddle online tool: https://www.signbank.org/signpuddle2.0/

- **ELAN (Annotation Software)**
  - https://archive.mpi.nl/tla/elan
  - Professional tool for annotating sign language video
  - Free download, used in research
  - Steep learning curve but powerful

- **SignStream**
  - https://www.bu.edu/asllrp/SignStream/
  - Boston University's annotation software
  - Used for ASL corpus research

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

### 1. Music and Rhythm in Sign Languages
- **Connection:** Prosody in sign languages shares properties with musical rhythm
- **Resources:** Ronnie Wilbur's work on prosodic structures
- **Why interesting:** Challenges auditory-centric views of musicality

### 2. Dance and Sign Language Choreography
- **Connection:** Sign languages and dance both use spatial-kinetic expression
- **Resources:** Deaf dancers, sign language poetry performance
- **Why interesting:** Artistic expression in visual-gestural modality

### 3. Visual Vernacular and Cinematic Techniques
- **Connection:** Visual Vernacular (artistic signing) uses camera-like perspective shifts
- **Resources:** Deaf storytelling and performance art
- **Why interesting:** Parallels to film grammar and visual narratives

### 4. Animal Communication Systems
- **Connection:** Great apes learning sign languages (Washoe, Koko, etc.)
- **Resources:** Francine Patterson, Sue Savage-Rumbaugh research
- **Why interesting:** Debates about language vs. learned communication, though controversial

### 5. Constructed Languages (Conlangs)
- **Connection:** Creating signed conlangs for fiction (e.g., Star Trek)
- **Resources:** Fan communities, linguistic consulting for media
- **Why interesting:** Designing sign languages makes linguistic universals explicit

### 6. Architecture and Deaf Space
- **Connection:** "DeafSpace" design principles for Deaf-centric environments
- **Resources:** Gallaudet University DeafSpace project
- **Why interesting:** How visual-spatial language use shapes built environments

### 7. Tactile Sign Languages
- **Connection:** Sign languages adapted for deafblind users (protactile)
- **Resources:** Jelica Nuccio, research on tactile ASL
- **Why interesting:** Modality shift within the visual-gestural family

### 8. Notation Systems Across Modalities
- **Connection:** SignWriting, HamNoSys (Hamburg Notation System), Stokoe notation
- **Resources:** Compare to IPA for spoken languages, Labanotation for dance
- **Why interesting:** What makes a good writing system for non-linear language?

### 9. Virtual Reality and Sign Language
- **Connection:** VR avatars for signing, spatial grammar in virtual spaces
- **Resources:** Emerging research on VR for Deaf users
- **Why interesting:** Technology designed around visual-spatial language

### 10. Legal and Medical Interpreting
- **Connection:** Register variation, specialized lexicons, interpreting ethics
- **Resources:** Registry of Interpreters for the Deaf (RID)
- **Why interesting:** Applied sociolinguistics, power and access issues

### 11. Signed Poetry and Wordplay
- **Connection:** Linguistic creativity, puns, rhyme, meter in sign languages
- **Resources:** Flying Words Project, Deaf poetry slam
- **Why interesting:** What counts as rhyme in a visual language?

### 12. Comics and Sequential Art
- **Connection:** Visual narrative structures parallel sign language discourse
- **Resources:** Matt Daigle (Deaf cartoonist), analysis of visual narrative
- **Why interesting:** Both use space and sequence to encode meaning

## Accessibility & Equity Note

When using resources, prioritize:
- **Deaf-created content** — center Deaf scholars and community members
- **Open access** when possible — not everyone has institutional library access
- **Captioned/transcribed videos** — ironic but important: many sign language resources lack captions
- **Multiple sign languages** — don't assume ASL is universal; seek BSL, Auslan, DGS, JSL, etc.

## Further Exploration Paths

**If the student gets excited about phonology:** Dive into Brentari's Prosodic Model, autosegmental representations, hand-internal features.

**If the student gets excited about morphology:** Explore classifier debates, read Emmorey vs. Liddell arguments, analyze corpus data.

**If the student gets excited about syntax:** Study formal approaches (Neidle et al.), role shift as logophoricity, null arguments and agreement.

**If the student gets excited about emergence:** Deep dive on NSL, ABSL, ISN; read Senghas, Meir, Sandler.

**If the student gets excited about sociolinguistics:** Explore variation (Black ASL, regional dialects), language attitudes, Deaf community studies.

**If the student gets excited about neurolinguistics:** Read Emmorey's "Language, Cognition, and the Brain", explore fMRI studies of sign processing.

**If the student gets excited about typology:** Use SignTyp database, compare sign languages cross-linguistically, read Zeshan's work.
