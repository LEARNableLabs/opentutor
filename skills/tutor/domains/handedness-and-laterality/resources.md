# Handedness and Laterality — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Comprehensive Reviews
- **Ocklenburg, S., et al. (2016)** — "Epigenetic regulation of lateralized fetal spinal gene expression underlies hemispheric asymmetries" — *eLife*. Genetics and epigenetics of handedness. [https://elifesciences.org/articles/22784](https://elifesciences.org/articles/22784)
- **Corballis, M.C. (2014)** — "Left Brain, Right Brain: Facts and Fantasies" — *PLOS Biology*. Debunks myths, covers real asymmetries. [https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001767](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001767)
- **Willems, R.M., et al. (2014)** — "On the other hand: including left-handers in cognitive neuroscience and neurogenetics" — *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*. [https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn3679](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn3679)
- **Annett, M. (2002)** — *Handedness and Brain Asymmetry: The Right Shift Theory* — Psychology Press. Classic theoretical framework.

### Textbooks with Strong Laterality Coverage
- **Kolb, B. & Whishaw, I.Q. (2015)** — *Fundamentals of Human Neuropsychology* (7th ed.). Chapter 11 covers asymmetry, handedness, split-brain patients. Intermediate-level neuropsychology text.
- **Hugdahl, K. & Davidson, R.J. (Eds.) (2003)** — *The Asymmetrical Brain*. MIT Press. Edited volume with chapters on development, genetics, evolution, clinical correlates.
- **Springer, S.P. & Deutsch, G. (1998)** — *Left Brain, Right Brain* (5th ed.). Classic accessible text on hemispheric specialization.
- **McManus, I.C. (2002)** — *Right Hand, Left Hand: The Origins of Asymmetry in Brains, Bodies, Atoms and Cultures*. Weidenfeld & Nicolson. Engaging popular science treatment with depth.

### Key Empirical Papers
- **Wiberg, A., et al. (2019)** — "Handedness, language areas and neuropsychiatric diseases: insights from brain imaging and genetics" — *Brain*. Large-scale GWAS of handedness. [https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/142/10/2938/5556709](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/142/10/2938/5556709)
- **Knecht, S., et al. (2000)** — "Handedness and hemispheric language dominance in healthy humans" — *Brain*. fMRI study of handedness-language correlation. [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11090305/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11090305/)
- **Hepper, P.G., et al. (1991)** — "Handedness in the human fetus" — *Neuropsychologia*. Ultrasound evidence of prenatal hand preference. [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1944872/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1944872/)
- **Geschwind, N. & Galaburda, A.M. (1985)** — "Cerebral lateralization: Biological mechanisms, associations, and pathology" — *Archives of Neurology*. Influential (though controversial) theory linking testosterone to laterality.

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Discipline)

### Art and Design
- **Left-Handed Calligraphy** — challenges and adaptations for left-handed writers. [https://www.leftyslefthanded.com/](https://www.leftyslefthanded.com/)
- **Leonardo da Vinci's Mirror Writing** — historical analysis of whether he was left-handed.
- **Design Museum: Left-Handed Design Collection** — scissors, can openers, tools adapted for left-handers.

### Sports and Performance
- **Left-Hander Advantage in Baseball** — statistical analysis of batting/pitching asymmetries. [https://www.baseballprospectus.com/](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/)
- **Fencing and Tennis Left-Handers** — overrepresentation at elite levels (Nadal, McEnroe).
- **Musical Instruments** — piano (non-adaptable), guitar (reversible strings), violin (less common left-handed versions).

### Cultural Studies
- **Language and Laterality** — Hebrew/Arabic (right-to-left writing) vs. Latin scripts. Biomechanics of writing direction.
- **Historical Stigma** — "sinister" (Latin for left), forcing right-hand use in schools until mid-20th century.
- **International Left-Handers Day** — August 13th, raising awareness of design and social issues.

### Philosophy and Consciousness
- **Split-Brain and Personal Identity** — Do split-brain patients have unified consciousness? Derek Parfit's philosophical analysis.
- **Dual-Process Theories** — System 1/System 2 (Kahneman) sometimes incorrectly mapped to left/right brain.

### Medical and Clinical
- **Stroke Rehabilitation** — hemispheric plasticity and recovery, constraint-induced movement therapy.
- **Dyslexia and Laterality** — weak or atypical lateralization in some dyslexic individuals.
- **Autism Spectrum** — higher rates of mixed-handedness, potential link to brain organization differences.
- **Schizophrenia** — reduced brain asymmetry (Crow's theory of schizophrenia as "price of language").

## Recommended Learning Path

For an intermediate student working through the curriculum:

1. **Start with McManus's *Right Hand, Left Hand*** — engaging popular science, sets the stage.
2. **Take the Edinburgh Handedness Inventory** — direct experience with measurement (lesson 2).
3. **Watch Gazzaniga's split-brain videos** — foundational demonstrations (lesson 11).
4. **Read Corballis's 2014 PLOS Biology review** — myth-busting and real asymmetries (throughout).
5. **Explore Human Connectome Project data** — see real brain asymmetries (lessons 7-9).
6. **Read Willems et al. (2014) Nature Reviews** — comprehensive overview of neuroimaging and genetics (lessons 9-14).
7. **Deep dive into Wiberg et al. (2019) GWAS paper** — state-of-the-art genetics (lesson 14).
8. **Follow Ocklenburg on Twitter** — current research updates.
9. **Reflect on daily experiences** — notice design bias, observe your own hand use, ask friends about their handedness.

## Final Note

This topic has **extensive literature** spanning neuroscience, psychology, genetics, evolution, and clinical medicine. The resources above emphasize **quality over quantity** — better to deeply engage with McManus, Corballis, Gazzaniga, and key reviews (Willems, Ocklenburg) than to skim dozens of papers. Use PubMed and Google Scholar to follow citation trails from these core sources when you want to go deeper on a specific aspect (e.g., genetics, imaging methods, clinical correlates).
