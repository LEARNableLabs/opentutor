## Domain Hooks
This field covers ancient roman engineering — aqueducts, roads, and concrete, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Romans just copied Greek engineering"** — While Romans learned from Greeks (especially arch construction), they innovated extensively in concrete chemistry, hydraulic engineering scale, and standardized road networks. The Pantheon's dome and opus caementicium are distinctly Roman.

2. **"Roman concrete was better because of a secret ingredient"** — Not a secret, just volcanic ash (pozzolana). The misconception is that it's mysterious rather than chemically understood. Modern engineers can replicate it; we choose not to because Portland cement is cheaper and faster-curing for most applications.

3. **"Aqueducts are just sloped pipes"** — Misses the precision (1:300 gradient over dozens of miles), settling tanks, distribution systems, siphons, and maintenance design. Students often underestimate the computational and surveying difficulty.

4. **"Romans built everything to last forever"** — Selection bias: we see what survived. Plenty of Roman structures failed or were poorly built.
