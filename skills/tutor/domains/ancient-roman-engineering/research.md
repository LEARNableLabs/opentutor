# Research Summary — Ancient Roman Engineering

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Roman Roads (Via Romana)
- Construction techniques: layering system (statumen, rudus, nucleus, summum dorsum)
- Surveying and engineering: groma, chorobates, straight-line precision
- Strategic network: cursus publicus, milestones, road stations
- Durability: why Roman roads outlasted modern alternatives
- Notable examples: Via Appia, Via Flaminia, Via Aurelia

### 2. Aqueducts (Aquaeductus)
- Hydraulic engineering: gravity flow, gradient calculation (1:300 typical slope)
- Structural design: arches, siphons, settling tanks
- Water distribution: castellum divisorium, public fountains, baths
- Maintenance systems: inspection shafts, cleaning protocols
- Case studies: Aqua Claudia, Pont du Gard, Segovia aqueduct

### 3. Roman Concrete (Opus Caementicium)
- Material science: volcanic ash (pozzolana), lime, aggregate
- Chemical properties: hydration, durability, seawater resistance
- Construction techniques: formwork, layering, curing
- Applications: Pantheon dome, harbor structures, massive foundations
- Modern rediscovery: why Roman concrete outperforms modern Portland cement in marine environments

### 4. Cross-Cutting Themes
- Standardization and modularity
- Labor organization: slaves, soldiers, skilled craftsmen
- Political and military strategy
- Environmental adaptation
- Economic impact: trade, urbanization, empire cohesion

## Key Historical Sources

- **Vitruvius, De Architectura (c. 15 BCE)** — primary source on Roman building techniques
- **Frontinus, De aquaeductu (c. 98 CE)** — detailed account of Rome's water system
- **Archaeological evidence** — excavations at Pompeii, Herculaneum, Ostia Antica

## Pedagogical Approach

For **intermediate** level:
- Start with tangible outcomes (why roads/aqueducts mattered)
- Build to engineering principles (gradients, materials, statics)
- Include hands-on calculations (aqueduct slope, concrete mixing ratios)
- Connect to modern engineering (what we've learned/lost)
- Emphasize interdisciplinary nature: physics, chemistry, politics, economics

Balance between:
- Historical narrative (stories, people, places)
- Technical depth (engineering principles, calculations)
- Hands-on exploration (thought experiments, back-of-envelope calculations)
- Modern relevance (lessons for contemporary infrastructure)
