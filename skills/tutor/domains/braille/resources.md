# Braille — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Organizations

- **American Foundation for the Blind (AFB)** — https://www.afb.org
  - Comprehensive history, literacy research, assistive technology database
  - "AccessWorld" magazine for in-depth tech reviews
  - Good for: all lessons, especially history (1-4), technology (16-20), design (21-24)

- **Perkins School for the Blind** — https://www.perkins.org
  - Teaching resources, professional development, deafblind programs
  - Excellent Braille learning center and curriculum materials
  - Good for: history (2), literacy pedagogy (12-15), assistive tech (16, 20)

- **National Braille Press** — https://www.nbp.org
  - Braille book production, children's literacy programs
  - "Braille Bug" educational site for kids (adaptable for intermediate learners)
  - Good for: Braille structure (5-6), early literacy (12)

- **Braille Institute** — https://www.brailleinstitute.org
  - Adult education, instructional materials, vision rehabilitation
  - Good for: literacy debates (11), teaching methods (15)

### Standards and Technical

- **Braille Authority of North America (BANA)** — http://www.brailleauthority.org
  - Official code standards: UEB, Nemeth, music Braille
  - Technical specifications for transcription
  - Good for: code lessons (7-10), specialized notation (8-9)

- **Unified English Braille (UEB)** — http://www.iceb.org/ueb.html
  - International Council on English Braille resources
  - Good for: lesson 4 (international variants and unification)

- **Library of Congress, National Library Service (NLS)** — https://www.loc.gov/nls
  - Braille history, standards documentation, talking books and Braille
  - Good for: lesson 3 (adoption timeline), lesson 4 (standardization)

### Academic and Research

- **Journal of Visual Impairment & Blindness** — https://journals.sagepub.com/home/jvb
  - Peer-reviewed research on literacy, pedagogy, assistive technology
  - Good for: literacy debates (11), teaching methods (15), technology trends (16-20)

- **American Printing House for the Blind (APH)** — https://www.aph.org
  - Educational products, research, ConnectCenter database
  - "Braille Formats: Principles of Print-to-Braille Transcription" (comprehensive guide)
  - Good for: all technical lessons (5-10), teaching (12-15)

- **Paths to Literacy** — https://www.pathstoliteracy.org
  - Evidence-based teaching practices, pre-Braille skills, tactile graphics
  - Good for: lesson 12 (pre-Braille skills), lesson 21 (tactile graphics)

### Interactive Tools and Apps

- **Braille Tutor** (iOS/Android) — Interactive Braille learning app
  - Good for: practicing Braille recognition (lessons 5-7)

- **Braille Skate** (iOS) — Skateboarding game teaching Braille
  - Good for: gamified learning, engagement

- **Liblouis** — https://liblouis.io
  - Open-source Braille translator, supports multiple languages and codes
  - Good for: lesson 18 (translation software)

- **Duxbury Braille Translator (DBT)** — https://www.duxburysystems.com
  - Industry-standard commercial translation software
  - Good for: lesson 18 (professional-grade translation)

- **TactileView** — https://www.tactileview.com
  - Software for creating tactile graphics from digital images
  - Good for: lesson 21 (tactile graphics production)

- **NVDA Screen Reader** — https://www.nvaccess.org
  - Free, open-source screen reader with Braille display support
  - Good for: lesson 19 (screen reader + Braille integration)

- **JAWS Screen Reader** — https://www.freedomscientific.com/products/software/jaws
  - Commercial screen reader, industry standard
  - Good for: lesson 19 (professional assistive tech)

### Code and Development

- **Unicode Braille Patterns** — https://www.unicode.org/charts/PDF/U2800.pdf
  - U+2800 to U+28FF, visual rendering of Braille in digital text
  - Good for: lesson 10 (eight-dot Braille and Unicode)

- **Liblouis GitHub** — https://github.com/liblouis/liblouis
  - Source code for open-source Braille translation
  - Good for: lesson 18 (translation algorithms)

- **Braille ASCII** — http://brailleauthority.org/bana/banabrascii.html
  - Text representation of Braille for email and digital communication
  - Good for: lesson 10 (computer Braille)

### People and Advocates

- **Louis Braille (1809-1852)** — inventor of Braille system
  - Good for: lessons 1-4 (historical context)

- **Helen Keller (1880-1968)** — deafblind author, activist, lecturer
  - Good for: lesson 14 (deafblindness), broader disability rights context

- **Sabriye Tenberken** — founder of Braille Without Borders, created Tibetan Braille
  - Good for: lesson 4 (international variants), rabbit hole on accessibility in developing countries

- **Haben Girma** — deafblind lawyer, disability rights advocate
  - Good for: lesson 14 (deafblindness), lesson 25-26 (policy and advocacy)

- **Lawrence Scadden** — blind physicist and NASA accessibility consultant
  - Good for: STEM accessibility, Nemeth Code applications (lesson 8)

- **Chancey Fleet** — assistive technology coordinator, New York Public Library
  - Good for: modern library accessibility, technology trends (lessons 16-20)

### Museums and Exhibits

- **Art Beyond Sight** — https://artbeyondsight.org
  - Museum accessibility resources, multisensory exhibit design
  - Good for: lesson 24 (universal design in museums)

- **Lighthouse Guild** — https://www.lighthouseguild.org
  - Environmental design, wayfinding, rehabilitation services
  - Good for: lesson 22 (wayfinding and signage)

- **Perkins Museum** (Watertown, MA) — https://www.perkins.org/museum

### Unexpected Connections

- **Cryptography and Braille** — Braille as a substitution cipher, code-breaking exercises
  - Good for: lesson 1 (night writing as military code), engagement hook

- **Braille in Speculative Fiction** — "The Day of the Triffids" (John Wyndham), "Blindsight" (Peter Watts)
  - Good for: cultural representations, sci-fi tactile interfaces (lesson 26)

- **Tactile Music Notation History** — before Braille, blind musicians memorized everything
  - Good for: lesson 9 (music Braille context)

- **Braille on Elevator Buttons** — why numbers are in Grade 1, not Grade 2?
  - Good for: lesson 7 (contractions) — functional design decisions

- **Braille Graffiti and Guerrilla Accessibility** — activist art adding tactile labels to unmarked spaces
  - Good for: lesson 22 (wayfinding), lesson 25 (functional vs symbolic accessibility)

- **Quantified Self and Braille** — fitness trackers, smart home devices with Braille labels
  - Good for: lesson 25 (product labeling), lesson 26 (future of tactile tech)

- **Braille on Currency** — India's ₹500 note has tactile marks; US bills are identical size and texture
  - Good for: lesson 25 (functional accessibility in everyday objects)

- **NASA's Tactile Universe** — 3D-printed models of galaxies, planets, nebulae
  - Good for: lesson 23 (3D printing), STEM accessibility

## Recommended Reading

### Books

- **"Louis Braille: A Touch of Genius"** by C. Michael Mellor
  - Biography, historical context, invention story
  - Good for: lessons 1-4 (history)

- **"The Lighthouse Handbook on Vision Impairment and Vision Rehabilitation"** edited by Barbara Silverstone et al.
  - Comprehensive clinical and educational reference
  - Good for: literacy pedagogy (lessons 11-15)

- **"Exceptional Teaching: A Comprehensive Guide for Including Students with Disabilities"** by Jim Ysseldyke et al.
  - Inclusive education strategies, Braille instruction chapter
  - Good for: teaching methods (lesson 15)

- **"The Tactile Graphics Guidebook"** by Elizabeth Aldrich
  - Practical guide to designing raised-line images
  - Good for: lesson 21 (tactile graphics)

- **"What Do Blind People See?"** by Judy Greenfeld
  - Accessible introduction to blindness, perception, Braille
  - Good for: conceptual foundations

### Articles and White Papers

- **"The Braille Literacy Crisis for Children"** by the National Federation of the Blind
  - Statistics, advocacy, policy recommendations
  - Good for: lesson 11 (literacy rates)

- **"Refreshable Braille Displays: Technology Overview"** by AFB AccessWorld
  - Technical deep dive into display mechanisms
  - Good for: lesson 16 (how displays work)

- **"Universal Design for Learning: Theory and Practice"** by Anne Meyer and David Rose
  - UDL framework, relevance to accessibility
  - Good for: lesson 24 (universal design principles)
