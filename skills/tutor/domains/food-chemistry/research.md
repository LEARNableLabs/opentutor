# Food Chemistry Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Water and Solutions** — pH, ionic strength, water activity, solubility
2. **Proteins** — denaturation, coagulation, enzymatic browning, Maillard reaction
3. **Carbohydrates** — starch gelatinization, caramelization, sugar crystallization
4. **Lipids** — emulsification, oxidation, fat crystallization, smoke points
5. **Flavor Chemistry** — volatile compounds, taste receptors, aroma extraction
6. **Fermentation** — microbial action, enzymatic reactions, preservation
7. **Texture and Structure** — gels, foams, emulsions, colloids

## Key Sources Found

### Textbooks
- **Harold McGee, "On Food and Cooking: The Science and Lore of the Kitchen"** (2004) — The definitive text for food chemistry, widely used in university courses. Combines chemistry with culinary history and practical application.
- **Tom Coultate, "Food: The Chemistry of its Components"** (7th edition) — Comprehensive food chemistry textbook used in academic settings
- **"Fennema's Food Chemistry"** edited by Damodaran, Parkin, and Fennema (CRC Press) — Reference used at Rutgers and other universities
- **"The Science of Cooking"** by Provost, Colabroy, Kelly, and Wallert — Used in UT Tyler's Culinary Chemistry course

### University Courses
- **MIT OCW: Kitchen Chemistry (ES-287)** — Experimental/hands-on course examining chemical, biochemical, biological, and microbiological principles through edible experiments
- **Rutgers University: Food Chemistry (11:400:411)** — Standard university food chemistry course
- **University of Toronto: NFS386H1F Food Chemistry** — Upper-level food chemistry course
- **UT Tyler: Culinary Chemistry (CHEM 1330)** — Chemistry course focused on cooking applications
- **Coast Community College: Biology of Food and Cooking (BIOL G101)** — Uses McGee as primary text

### Video Lectures and Online Courses
- **MIT OCW Kitchen Chemistry** — Full course materials with video lectures available at https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/experimental-study-group/es-287-kitchen-chemistry-spring-2009/
- **MIT's "Ten Free Courses for Food Lovers"** collection includes Chinese Cooking and other food science material
- **EdX: "Science & Cooking: From Haute Cuisine to Soft Matter Science"** (SPU27x) — Emphasizes material science aspects

### Interactive Tools and Resources
- **MoleculeKitchen.com** — Molecular gastronomy resource with AI recipe generators, spherification calculators, hydrocolloid ratio optimization tools
- **Chemistry LibreTexts** — Interactive web-based chemistry tutorials and simulations
- **Educational Designer: Molecular Gastronomy Manual** (PDF) — Teaching materials for molecular gastronomy
- **LUMAT Journal: "Introduction to Molecular Gastronomy"** — Academic resource on molecular gastronomy pedagogy
- **Georgetown ITEL: Online Modules in Molecular Gastronomy** — OER modules for teaching food chemistry

### Key Researchers and Practitioners
- **Harold McGee** — Food science writer, author of "On Food and Cooking"
- **Hervé This** — Physical chemist, father of molecular gastronomy
- **Fennema, Damodaran, Parkin** — Food chemistry textbook authors
- **Nathan Myhrvold** — Modernist Cuisine author (not found in search but relevant)
