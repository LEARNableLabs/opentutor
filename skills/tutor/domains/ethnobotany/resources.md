# Ethnobotany — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Plants, People, and Culture: The Science of Ethnobotany** by Michael J. Balick and Paul Alan Cox (2nd ed.)
  - Comprehensive undergraduate text, accessible for intermediate learners
  - Covers history, methods, applications, and ethics
  - https://www.garlandscience.com/product/isbn/9780815365914

- **Ethnobotany: A Methods Manual** by Gary J. Martin
  - The standard guide for ethnobotanical field research
  - Practical, detailed, culturally sensitive
  - https://www.routledge.com/Ethnobotany-A-Methods-Manual/Martin/p/book/9781844073900

- **Economic Botany: Plants in Our World** by Beryl Simpson and Molly Conner-Ogorzaly (4th ed.)
  - Broader coverage of useful plants
  - Strong on chemistry and pharmacology
  - https://www.mheducation.com/

- **Ethnobotany: Principles and Applications** by C.M. Cotton
  - More academic, good for deeper dives
  - https://www.wiley.com/

### University Courses (open syllabi and materials)

- **UC Berkeley — Environmental Sciences, Policy and Management: Ethnobotany**
  - https://ourenvironment.berkeley.edu/

- **University of Hawaii — Botany Department Ethnobotany Courses**

  - https://www.botany.hawaii.edu/

- **University of Kent — Centre for Biocultural Diversity**
  - Leading program in Europe
  - https://www.kent.ac.uk/sac/research/biocultural-diversity

- **Yale — Indigenous Knowledge Systems and Environmental Governance**

## Databases and Digital Resources

### Plant Use Databases

- **Plants For A Future (PFAF)**
  - Searchable database of 7,000+ useful plants
  - https://www.pfaf.org/

- **Native American Ethnobotany Database** (University of Michigan)
  - 44,000+ records of plant uses by Indigenous peoples of North America
  - http://naeb.brit.org/

- **PROTA4U** (Plant Resources of Tropical Africa)
  - Comprehensive, region-specific
  - https://www.prota4u.org/

- **Ecocrop** (FAO)
  - Crop ecology and distribution
  - http://www.fao.org/land-water/databases-and-software/crop-information/ecocrop/en/

- **NAPRALERT** (Natural Products Alert)
  - Chemical and biological information on traditional medicines
  - https://www.napralert.org/ (subscription required, but many universities have access)

### Herbarium Collections (online)

- **JSTOR Global Plants**
  - Digitized herbarium specimens with ethnobotanical notes
  - https://plants.jstor.org/

- **SEINet** (Southwest Environmental Information Network)
  - Regional collections with Indigenous use data
  - http://swbiodiversity.org/seinet/

## Unexpected Connections

### Ethnobotany and Literature

- **"The Signature of All Things" by Elizabeth Gilbert** — historical fiction about botanical exploration
- **"The Overstory" by Richard Powers** — Pulitzer winner about trees and human lives
- **"Braiding Sweetgrass" by Robin Wall Kimmerer** — Indigenous botanical knowledge as poetry
- **"Lab Girl" by Hope Jahren** — memoir of a plant scientist

### Ethnobotany and Art

- **Botanical illustration traditions** across cultures (Chinese, Islamic, European natural history)
- **Herbarium as art** — contemporary artists using herbarium techniques
- **Land art and ecological restoration** (Andy Goldsworthy, eco-artists)

### Ethnobotany and Food Studies

- **"The Omnivore's Dilemma" by Michael Pollan**
- **Slow Food movement** — preservation of traditional food plants and preparations
  - https://www.slowfood.com/
- **Ark of Taste** — catalog of endangered heritage foods
  - https://www.fondazioneslowfood.com/en/what-we-do/the-ark-of-taste/

### Ethnobotany and Climate Justice

- Indigenous-led climate movements emphasize traditional land management
- Traditional varieties as climate adaptation resources
- **Climate Change and Indigenous Peoples** (UN resources)
  - https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/climate-change.html

## Ethics and Protocols

### Required Reading on Ethics

- **"Tuhoe Claims Settlement Act 2014"** (New Zealand) — legal personhood for forests
- **UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP)**
  - https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples.html
- **Nagoya Protocol** — full text and explanatory materials
  - https://www.cbd.int/abs/doc/protocol/nagoya-protocol-en.pdf
- **International Society of Ethnobiology Code of Ethics**
  - https://www.ethnobiology.net/what-we-do/core-programs/ise-ethics-program/code-of-ethics/

These resources provide a comprehensive foundation for intermediate ethnobotany study. Always prioritize resources created by or in collaboration with Indigenous and local communities when available.
