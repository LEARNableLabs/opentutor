# Amateur Radio — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Manuals

- **ARRL Handbook for Radio Communications (2026 edition)** — https://www.arrl.org/arrl-handbook-2026
  - Comprehensive reference covering propagation, antennas, modes, and operating. Updated annually. The gold standard for amateur radio technical information. Chapters 19-21 cover propagation in depth; chapters 22-24 cover antennas. Excellent for intermediate learners because it balances theory with practical application.

- **ARRL Antenna Book (25th Edition)** — https://www.arrl.org/shop/ARRL-Antenna-Book-25th-Edition/
  - Definitive guide to antenna design, theory, and construction. Includes measured antenna patterns, modeling examples, and construction projects. Chapters 2-4 provide intermediate-level theory; chapters 8-18 cover specific antenna types. Essential reference for the antenna portion of this curriculum.

- **ARRL Operating Manual (12th Edition)** — https://www.arrl.org/shop/The-ARRL-Operating-Manual-12th-Edition/
  - Comprehensive guide to operating procedures, modes, contests, and DXing. Covers CW, phone, digital modes, satellite, and emergency communications. Practical focus with real operating scenarios. Good companion for lessons 24-27.

- **ON4UN's Low-Band DXing (6th Edition)** — https://www.arrl.org/shop/ON4UN-s-Low-Band-DXing/
  - Advanced but accessible treatment of propagation and antennas for 160m-40m. Excellent for understanding how propagation and antenna design interact for specific operating goals. Best used as enrichment after completing main curriculum.

### Online Courses and Guides

- **ARRL Learning Center** — https://arrl.cloud/courses
  - Structured online courses covering license exam preparation and specialized topics (antennas, propagation, digital modes, contesting). Self-paced with quizzes. Useful as parallel study or review.

- **HamStudy.org** — https://hamstudy.org
  - Interactive practice exams with detailed explanations for each question. Covers Technician, General, and Extra class material. Good for filling knowledge gaps and testing understanding of specific concepts.

- **AC6V Amateur Radio Mega-Site** — https://www.ac6v.com
  - Comprehensive link directory to educational resources, organized by topic. Includes propagation tools, antenna calculators, mode guides, and operating resources. Overwhelming breadth but excellent for finding niche topics.

### Amateur Radio Organizations

- **American Radio Relay League (ARRL)** — https://www.arrl.org
  - Primary amateur radio organization in the US. Publishes QST magazine, maintains Learning Center, provides technical support, and advocates for amateur radio. Membership includes access to archived articles and online courses.

- **Radio Society of Great Britain (RSGB)** — https://rsgb.org
  - UK equivalent of ARRL. Publishes RadCom magazine with international perspective. Good for learning about amateur radio outside North America.

## Interactive Tools and Simulators

### Propagation Prediction

- **VOACAP Online** — https://www.voacap.com/hf/
  - HF propagation prediction based on ionospheric models and historical data. Input: locations, power, antennas, time, date, solar indices. Output: predicted signal strength, best frequency, best time. Essential tool for planning DX contacts. Use for lessons 6, 9, and summative assessment.

- **PSK Reporter** — https://pskreporter.info
  - Real-time propagation monitoring via reception reports from digital mode stations worldwide. Visual map showing who's hearing whom on which bands. Excellent for seeing actual propagation conditions vs. predictions. Use to validate propagation lessons.

- **Reverse Beacon Network (RBN)** — https://www.reversebeacon.net
  - Real-time CW and RTTY propagation monitoring. Automated stations report your signal strength and location. Shows actual band openings and propagation paths. Useful for testing antenna changes and propagation theories.

- **DX Atlas** — https://www.dxatlas.com
  - Propagation prediction software with beautiful visual mapping of MUF, coverage areas, and gray-line. Integrates solar data and custom antenna modeling. Paid software but offers demo version. More polished than VOACAP for visual learners.

- **Faros HF Propagation** (app) — iOS/Android propagation prediction tool with real-time solar data, MUF charts, and band condition forecasts. Mobile-friendly for quick checks.

### Antenna Modeling

- **4nec2** — https://www.qsl.net/4nec2/
  - Free antenna modeling software using NEC2 engine. Calculates radiation patterns, impedance, gain, and SWR. Includes geometry editor and optimizer. Windows only but runs well in Wine on macOS/Linux. Essential for lessons 12-23. Steeper learning curve but powerful.

- **EZNEC** — https://www.eznec.com
  - Commercial antenna modeling software with excellent visualization and easier interface than 4nec2. Demo version available with some limitations. Gold standard for serious antenna work. Highly recommended if student can afford it (~$140 for EZNEC+).

- **Antenna Modeling by G3TXQ** — http://www.karinya.net/g3txq/
  - Free web-based tools for specific antenna calculations: dipole design, impedance matching, feedline loss, SWR. Less comprehensive than NEC modeling but faster for simple calculations.

- **TLW (Transmission Line for Windows)** — https://www.qsl.net/ac6la/tlw.html
  - Free software for transmission line calculations: impedance transformation, loss, SWR, stub matching. Complements antenna modeling by handling feedline design.

### Operating and Logging

- **WSJT-X** — https://physics.princeton.edu/pulsar/k1jt/wsjtx.html
  - Free software for weak-signal digital modes (FT8, FT4, JT65, JT9, WSPR). Developed by K1JT (Joe Taylor, Nobel Prize physicist). Essential for lessons 26-27. Integrates with logging software and propagation monitoring.

- **Fldigi** — http://www.w1hkj.com/
  - Free software for digital modes (PSK31, RTTY, Olivia, MT63, and dozens more). Waterfall display, macros for common exchanges, logging integration. More modes than WSJT-X but less focused on weak-signal work.

- **Log4OM** — https://www.log4om.com
  - Free advanced logging software with propagation prediction, QSL management, contest support, and award tracking. Helps students see patterns in their operating (which bands/times/modes work).

## Code Repositories and Projects

- **Red Pitaya SDR Transceiver** — https://github.com/pavel-demin/red-pitaya-notes
  - Open-source SDR transceiver project using Red Pitaya hardware. Shows modern integration of software-defined radio with amateur radio. Advanced but fascinating for students interested in SDR.

- **QRadioLink** — https://github.com/qradiolink/qradiolink
  - Open-source SDR transceiver application for Linux. Supports many modes and can work with RTL-SDR, LimeSDR, PlutoSDR. Good example of modern software integration.

- **Hamlib** — https://github.com/Hamlib/Hamlib
  - Library for controlling amateur radio transceivers via computer. Used by logging software, digital mode applications, and contesting tools. Demonstrates software integration in amateur radio.

- **WSPRnet** — https://wsprnet.org
  - WSPR (Weak Signal Propagation Reporter) network for propagation monitoring using ultra-weak beacons. See how signals propagate worldwide. Free to use; shows real-time propagation for all bands.

## Cross-Discipline Connections

### Unexpected Applications and Analogies

1. **Fiber Optic Communication and Ionospheric Propagation**
   - Both use refractive index gradients to guide waves. Understanding total internal reflection in fiber optics helps visualize ionospheric refraction. The critical angle concept is identical.

2. **Meteorology and Tropospheric Propagation**
   - Temperature inversions in weather forecasting are the same phenomenon that creates VHF/UHF ducting. Weather maps become propagation prediction tools for VHF/UHF operators.

3. **Acoustic Wave Propagation and Antenna Theory**
   - Radiation patterns, directivity, and beamforming in antennas directly parallel speaker and microphone array design. The math is identical. Noise-canceling headphones use the same phasing principles as phased antenna arrays.

4. **Electrical Power Distribution and Transmission Lines**
   - Impedance matching in power systems (to minimize loss) uses the same theory as RF feedlines. Power engineers use Smith charts too. Understanding one helps with the other.

5. **Solar Physics and Amateur Radio**
   - Solar astronomers study the same phenomena (solar cycle, flares, coronal mass ejections) that affect amateur radio propagation. Solar data from NASA/NOAA drives propagation predictions.

6. **Signal Processing and Digital Modes**
   - DSP techniques in amateur radio digital modes (error correction, modulation, FFT analysis) are identical to those used in cell phones, WiFi, and modern communications. FT8 is essentially modern OFDM applied to HF.

7. **Cognitive Radio and Dynamic Spectrum Access**
   - Amateur radio operators adapt band/mode selection based on real-time conditions—essentially manual cognitive radio. Research in dynamic spectrum access for commercial systems learns from amateur practices.

8. **Emergency Management and Ham Radio**
   - ARES/RACES protocols align with ICS (Incident Command System) used by FEMA and emergency responders. Amateur radio emergency communications training transfers directly to professional emergency management.

## Supplementary Publications

### Magazines (Archives Useful for Research)

- **QST** (ARRL monthly) — Technical articles, product reviews, operating tips. Archives back to 1915 available to ARRL members. Search for specific topics (e.g., "NVIS antennas," "FT8 operating").

- **CQ Magazine** — Contest and DX focus with operating techniques and station building. Less technical theory than QST but strong on practical operating.

- **The DX Magazine** — Focused on DXing and propagation. Detailed propagation forecasts and DXpedition planning articles.

- **RadCom** (RSGB monthly) — UK perspective with European propagation focus and international operating techniques.

### Newsletters and Blogs

- **The Daily DX** — https://www.dailydx.com — DX news and propagation forecasts. Good for staying current on band conditions.

- **The ARRL Letter** — Weekly email newsletter with news, technical tips, and upcoming events. Free subscription.

- **eHam.net** — https://www.eham.net — Articles, antenna and equipment reviews, and forums. User-contributed content with varying quality but high volume.

## Historical and Context Resources

- **"200 Meters and Down" by Clinton B. DeSoto** — Classic history of early amateur radio and the development of shortwave communication. Provides context for why amateur radio practices evolved the way they did.

- **"The ARRL Handbook Centennial Edition"** — Historical perspective on amateur radio technology development over 100 years. Shows evolution of propagation understanding and antenna design.

- **Smithsonian National Museum of American History — Amateur Radio Collection** — https://americanhistory.si.edu — Exhibits on amateur radio history, equipment evolution, and cultural impact.

## Communities and Forums

- **Reddit r/amateurradio** — https://www.reddit.com/r/amateurradio/ — Active community for questions, photos of stations, and discussions. Good for getting diverse opinions and real-world operator perspectives.

- **QRZ Forums** — https://forums.qrz.com — Long-running forums with subforums for antennas, propagation, operating, and specific modes. Searchable archive of past discussions.

- **eHam.net Forums** — https://www.eham.net/ehamforum/ — Forums for contesting, DXing, antennas, and equipment. Good for getting feedback on antenna designs and station setups.

- **Groups.io Amateur Radio Groups** — https://groups.io/search?search=amateur+radio — Email discussion groups on specific topics (EFHW antennas, QRP operation, SDR, etc.). More focused than general forums.

## Local Resources

- **Local Amateur Radio Clubs** — Find via ARRL club search: https://www.arrl.org/find-a-club — In-person mentoring, equipment access (club stations), group projects (Field Day, antenna builds), and social connections. Often have experienced operators willing to mentor (Elmers).

- **Hamfests and Conventions** — Regional gatherings with forums, flea markets, and social events. Good for meeting operators, finding used equipment, and attending educational presentations. Calendar at: https://www.arrl.org/hamfests-and-conventions-calendar

- **University Amateur Radio Clubs** — Many universities have active clubs (MIT W1MX, Stanford W6YX, etc.). Often open to community members and host educational events. Good for accessing advanced equipment (HF stations, antenna ranges).

## Equipment Recommendations for Hands-On Learning

While not strictly "resources," these tools enable practical application of curriculum concepts:

- **NanoVNA** (~$60) — Vector Network Analyzer for measuring antenna impedance, SWR, and feedline characteristics. Makes antenna theory tangible.

- **RTL-SDR dongle** (~$30) — Software-defined radio receiver covering 24 MHz - 1.7 GHz. Enables reception experimentation, propagation monitoring, and digital mode decoding.

- **Antenna analyzer** (e.g., RigExpert, MFJ) — Measures antenna resonance, impedance, and SWR across frequency ranges. Essential for antenna building projects.

These resources collectively support all 27 lessons in the curriculum and provide pathways for continued learning beyond the structured curriculum.
