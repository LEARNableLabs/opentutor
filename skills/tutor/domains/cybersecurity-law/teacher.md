# Cybersecurity Law and Policy — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 8 lessons (28%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 6 lessons (21%)
- **Socratic questions** — 5 lessons (17%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 5 lessons (17%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (3%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 41% accessible (1-2), 38% standard (3), 21% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 8: "Why do most data breach lawsuits fail at the standing stage?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 11: "When does logging into a website become a federal crime?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 15: "Does the Fourth Amendment protect your emails stored in the cloud?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 16: "How does FISA allow warrantless surveillance of Americans?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 22: "How can the EU fine an American company for violating European law?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers cybersecurity law and policy, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: privacy as a right, market failure in data protection, regulatory rationale, data subject rights, lawful basis for processing, controller vs processor, territorial scope, GDPR enforcement, supervisory authorities, GDPR fines, CCPA/CPRA, state privacy fragmentation, opt-out vs opt-in, private right of action, HIPAA (and 97 more).
