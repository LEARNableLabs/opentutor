# Literary Criticism — Theory and Practice: Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Classical & Early Foundations
- **Close Reading** — systematic textual analysis, attention to language, form, and structure
- **New Criticism/Formalism** — text-centered approach, intentional fallacy, affective fallacy, organic unity

### Structural & Semiotic Approaches
- **Structuralism** — language as system, binary oppositions, narrative structures
- **Semiotics** — signs, signifiers/signified, codes and conventions
- **Narratology** — story vs. discourse, focalization, narrative levels

### Reader-Oriented Approaches
- **Reader-Response Criticism** — reader's role in meaning-making, interpretive communities
- **Reception Theory** — horizon of expectations, historical reading contexts

### Poststructuralist Approaches
- **Deconstruction** — différance, aporia, binary oppositions, undecidability
- **Poststructuralism** — death of the author, textuality, intertextuality

### Political & Ideological Criticism
- **Marxist Criticism** — base/superstructure, ideology, class consciousness, cultural materialism
- **New Historicism** — cultural poetics, power relations, circulation of social energy
- **Postcolonial Criticism** — orientalism, subaltern, hybridity, imperial discourse
- **Critical Race Theory** — race and representation, whiteness, intersectionality

### Psychological Approaches
- **Psychoanalytic Criticism** — unconscious, Oedipal reading, Lacanian mirror stage, desire and lack
- **Trauma Theory** — representing the unrepresentable, testimony, witnessing

### Gender & Identity Criticism
- **Feminist Criticism** — patriarchy, women's writing, écriture féminine, gynocriticism
- **Gender Studies** — performativity, social construction of gender
- **Queer Theory** — heteronormativity, queerness, sexuality and textuality

### Contemporary & Emerging Approaches
- **Ecocriticism** — nature writing, environmental justice, Anthropocene
- **Affect Theory** — feeling vs. emotion, embodied reading, affective turn
- **Digital Humanities** — distant reading, computational analysis, text mining

- **Yale University**: Introduction to Theory of Literature (ENGL 300) — comprehensive survey from formalism to queer theory

### Major Textbooks
- **Charles E. Bressler**: *Literary Criticism: An Introduction to Theory and Practice* (5th ed.) — covers thirteen major schools with philosophical contexts and practical methodology
- **Anne H. Stevens**: *Literary Theory and Criticism: An Introduction* (2nd ed.) — historical overview from antiquity to 21st century, includes disability studies, environmental studies, critical race theory, affect theory
- **Andrew Bennett & Nicholas Royle**: *An Introduction to Literature, Criticism and Theory* (6th ed.) — engaging approach through concepts like character, narrative, secrets, pleasure, ghosts

### Course Structure Patterns
Most intermediate courses follow this arc:
1. **Foundations** (weeks 1-2): What is literary criticism? Close reading practice
2. **Formalist approaches** (weeks 3-4): New Criticism, Russian Formalism
3. **Structuralist approaches** (weeks 5-6): Structuralism, semiotics, narratology
4. **Reader-oriented** (weeks 7-8): Reader-response, reception theory
5. **Poststructuralist** (weeks 9-10): Deconstruction, death of the author
6. **Political** (weeks 11-13): Marxist, New Historicist, Postcolonial
7. **Psychological** (weeks 14-15): Psychoanalytic, trauma
8. **Identity** (weeks 16-18): Feminist, gender, queer, race
9. **Contemporary** (weeks 19-20): Ecocriticism, affect, digital humanities

## Assessment Patterns
- **Short papers** (5-7 pages): Apply one theoretical lens to a literary text
- **Theory comparison essays**: Compare how two different approaches read the same text
- **Final project** (6-10 pages): Develop original critical analysis using theoretical framework
- **Reading responses**: Weekly reflections on theoretical readings
- **Presentations**: Teach a theory to peers
