# Literary Criticism — Resources

## Primary Textbooks

### Comprehensive Introductions

**Literary Criticism: An Introduction to Theory and Practice** by Charles E. Bressler (5th Edition)
- **What it covers**: Thirteen major schools of twentieth-century literary theory and criticism with historical and philosophical contexts
- **Why it's good for intermediate students**: Each school includes clear methodology for writing essays according to that school's beliefs. Accessible but rigorous.
- **URL**: https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/literary-criticism-an-introduction-to-theory-and-practice/P200000002374/9780205212149
- **Free access**: https://archive.org/details/literarycriticis0004bres (Internet Archive)

**Literary Theory and Criticism: An Introduction** by Anne H. Stevens (2nd Edition)
- **What it covers**: Overview from antiquity to 21st century, including disability studies, environmental studies, Critical Race Theory, and affect theory
- **Why it's good for intermediate students**: Historical progression helps contextualize movements; contemporary approaches show theory's relevance
- **URL**: https://broadviewpress.com/product/literary-theory-and-criticism-an-introduction-second-edition/

**An Introduction to Literature, Criticism and Theory** by Andrew Bennett and Nicholas Royle (6th Edition)
- **What it covers**: Topical approach (Character, Narrative, Author, Secrets, Pleasure, Ghosts) rather than school-by-school
- **Why it's good for intermediate students**: Highly readable, engages directly with literary works, illuminates complex ideas through accessible writing
- **URL**: https://www.routledge.com/An-Introduction-to-Literature-Criticism-and-Theory/Bennett-Royle/p/book/9781032158846

### Open Educational Resources

**Introduction to Literary Criticism** — American Literature I (TCC)
- **What it covers**: Free online textbook with approaches to literary criticism
- **Why it's good for intermediate students**: No cost, clear explanations, integrated with literary examples
- **URL**: https://open.ocolearnok.org/stuckeyamericanliterature1/chapter/approaches-to-literary-criticism/

## Unexpected Connections & Wild Cards

### Literary Theory and Law
- **The Law and Literature Movement** — how legal interpretation resembles/differs from literary interpretation
- Stanley Fish's work on interpretive communities has major legal theory applications

### Theory and Video Games
- **Critical Gaming Studies** — applying narratology, postcolonial theory, gender theory to games
- Ian Bogost's "procedural rhetoric" as heir to New Criticism

### Theory and Film
- **Film Theory** — parallel development using many of the same theoretical approaches
- Laura Mulvey's "male gaze" as visual equivalent of feminist literary criticism

### Theory and Science
- **The Science Wars** — 1990s debates about whether literary theory's relativism threatened scientific objectivity
- Sokal hoax (physicist publishing nonsense in cultural studies journal to prove theory was meaningless)

### Theory and Music
- **Musicology** — structural analysis, semiotics of music, queer musicology
- Susan McClary's feminist criticism of classical music

### Theory and Data Science
- **Distant Reading** — Franco Moretti's computational approach challenges close reading tradition
- Matthew Jockers, Ted Underwood — using machine learning on literary history

## Practice Texts for Applying Theory

Good texts for practicing multiple theoretical approaches (because they're rich, canonical, and already have critical literature):

- **Short stories**: Charlotte Perkins Gilman's "The Yellow Wallpaper", James Joyce's "The Dead", Edgar Allan Poe's "The Fall of the House of Usher"
- **Poetry**: William Blake, Emily Dickinson, T.S. Eliot's "The Waste Land"
- **Novels**: Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein*, Charlotte Brontë's *Jane Eyre*, Joseph Conrad's *Heart of Darkness* (excellent for postcolonial critique), Virginia Woolf's *To the Lighthouse*
- **Drama**: Shakespeare (any play — endless theoretical applications)

*These texts have extensive critical literature demonstrating different theoretical approaches, making them ideal for comparing how theories work in practice.*
