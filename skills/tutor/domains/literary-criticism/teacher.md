# Literary criticism — theory and practice — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 9 lessons (29%)
- **Socratic questions** — 7 lessons (23%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (16%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 5 lessons (16%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 4 lessons (13%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (3%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 29% accessible (1-2), 45% standard (3), 26% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 11: "Are all stories built from the same deep structure?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 16: "What if a text undermines its own argument?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 20: "Can we separate art from its historical power relations?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "Who gets to write history, and who gets written out?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 22: "How do texts construct racial identity?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers literary criticism — theory and practice, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

Key terms for this domain: interpretation, literary criticism vs. theory, critical thinking, close reading, textual evidence, analytical observation, interpretive communities, subjectivity, textual openness, theoretical frameworks, methodology, critical practice, intentional fallacy, authorial intent, New Criticism (and 74 more).
