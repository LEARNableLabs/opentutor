# Econometrics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Foundations** — probability review, statistical inference, sampling distributions
2. **Simple Linear Regression** — OLS estimation, interpretation, hypothesis testing
3. **Multiple Regression** — multivariate models, omitted variable bias, functional forms
4. **Model Specification** — goodness-of-fit, variable selection, transformation
5. **Classical Assumptions** — Gauss-Markov theorem, heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation
6. **Advanced Methods** — instrumental variables, panel data, limited dependent variables
7. **Time Series** — stationarity, ARIMA models, cointegration
8. **Applied Practice** — data cleaning, software tools, interpreting results

## Pedagogical Notes

Econometrics sits at the intersection of statistics, economics, and computation. At the intermediate level, students need:
- Solid intuition for regression mechanics (not just "push-button")
- Understanding of when methods break down (violations of assumptions)
- Practical experience with real data (messy, missing, non-normal)
- Connection to causal inference (policy evaluation, natural experiments)

The field has shifted toward:
- Causal methods (DAGs, potential outcomes, IV)
- Machine learning integration (lasso, cross-validation)
- Computational approaches (bootstrapping, simulation)
- Reproducible research (version control, literate programming)

Students often struggle with:
- Interpreting coefficients (especially logs, interactions)
- Diagnosing assumption violations
- Choosing between competing models
- Moving from correlation to causation
