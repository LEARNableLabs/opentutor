# Music cognition — why music moves us — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **Socratic questions** — 8 lessons (29%)
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 7 lessons (25%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 3 lessons (11%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (7%)

Primary format: Socratic questions.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Domain Hooks
These are fascinating tangents to drop in when students show curiosity or the lesson creates an opening:

- **Absolute pitch (perfect pitch)** — rare ability to identify notes without reference. Discuss critical periods, cultural factors (tonal languages), and whether it's truly advantageous. Drop in during Lesson 1 (pitch perception) or Lesson 24 (neuroplasticity).

- **Synesthesia (chromesthesia)** — seeing colors when hearing sounds. Connection between cross-modal perception and metaphor in musical language ("bright" timbre, "warm" tone). Drop in during Lesson 3 (timbre) or Lesson 20 (brain networks).

- **Amusia (tone-deafness)** — selective impairment in pitch processing. Distinguishes perception from production, shows dissociable components of music processing. Drop in during Lesson 1 (pitch) or Lesson 20 (neural correlates).

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.
