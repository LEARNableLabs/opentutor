# Music Cognition — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Sweet Anticipation: Music and the Psychology of Expectation** by David Huron (MIT Press, 2006)  
  https://mitpress.mit.edu/books/sweet-anticipation  
  Comprehensive theory of how expectation drives musical emotion. Accessible to intermediate learners, with experimental evidence and musical examples. Core reference for ITPRA theory (Lessons 9, 11).

- **Music, Language, and the Brain** by Aniruddh Patel (Oxford, 2008)  
  https://global.oup.com/academic/product/music-language-and-brain-9780199755301  
  Comparative approach examining shared and distinct processing for music and language. Excellent for Lessons 23, 20. Intermediate level, assumes basic neuroscience.

- **This Is Your Brain on Music** by Daniel Levitin (Plume, 2006)  
  https://www.daniellevitin.com/publicpage/books/this-is-your-brain-on-music/  
  Accessible neuroscience of music covering perception, emotion, memory, evolution. Good supplementary reading for Lessons 1-5, 12-13, 20-21. Written for general audience but scientifically solid.

- **The Psychology of Music** (3rd edition) edited by Diana Deutsch (Academic Press, 2013)  
  https://www.elsevier.com/books/the-psychology-of-music/deutsch/978-0-12-381460-9  
  Edited volume covering perception, cognition, performance, development. More technical than Levitin. Use specific chapters as needed for deep dives.

### Interactive Tools & Experiments

- **Chromatic Music Lab: MusicLab**  
  https://themusiclab.org/  
  Citizen science platform with interactive experiments on music universals, infant music perception. Participate or explore data. Excellent for Lesson 10, 18, 19.

- **Music Cognition Browser-Based Experiments**  
  https://musiccognition.org/experiments/  
  Collection of perception tests (rhythm, pitch, memory). Students can try experiments and experience phenomena firsthand.

- **Chrome Music Lab**  
  https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/  
  Interactive tools for exploring pitch (spectrogram), rhythm (rhythm builder), harmony (chords). Use for Lessons 1-3.

- **Deutsch Lab Auditory Illusions**  
  https://deutsch.ucsd.edu/psychology/pages.php?i=206  
  Collection of famous auditory illusions (octave illusion, tritone paradox, Shepard tones). Great for demonstrating constructive perception. Use for Lessons 1, 4, 5.

- **EarBeater: Ear Training Tool**  
  https://www.earbeater.com/online-ear-training/  
  Pitch and rhythm training. Students can test their own perception. Use for Lesson 1-2.

### Code & Computational Resources

- **Music21 (Python library for music analysis)**  
  https://web.mit.edu/music21/  
  Analyze musical structure computationally. For students interested in music informatics. Mention in Lesson 27 (future directions).

- **Essentia (audio analysis library)**  
  https://essentia.upf.edu/  
  Extract musical features (rhythm, pitch, timbre). For computationally inclined students.

- **OpenSMILE (audio feature extraction)**  
  https://www.audeering.com/opensmile/  
  Similar to Essentia. Mention alongside for Lesson 27.

- **Magenta (Google's music AI project)**  
  https://magenta.tensorflow.org/  
  AI-generated music. Rabbit hole for Lesson 7 (prediction) or Lesson 27 (future directions).

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Discipline Links)

- **Music and Mathematics**  
  Fourier analysis, group theory in music theory, fractal patterns in composition. See Dmitri Tymoczko's work on geometry of music.  
  https://dmitri.tymoczko.com/

- **Music and Linguistics**  
  Generative grammar approaches to music (Lerdahl & Jackendoff's *A Generative Theory of Tonal Music*). Phonology parallels in musical structure.  
  https://mitpress.mit.edu/books/generative-theory-tonal-music

- **Music and Physics**  
  Acoustics, psychoacoustics, room acoustics. The physics of musical instruments.  
  See Fletcher & Rossing, *The Physics of Musical Instruments*  
  https://www.springer.com/gp/book/9780387983745

- **Music and Anthropology**  
  Cross-cultural music practices, music in ritual, music and identity. See Ethnomusicology journal.  
  https://www.ethnomusicology.org/

- **Music and Machine Learning**  
  Music information retrieval (MIR), audio classification, generative models. See ISMIR conference proceedings.  
  https://www.ismir.net/

- **Music and Philosophy**  
  Aesthetics, ontology of musical works, expression and emotion. See Peter Kivy, Jerrold Levinson.  
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/music/

- **Music and Medicine**  
  Music therapy for Parkinson's, stroke rehabilitation, dementia care. See Concetta Tomaino's work at Institute for Music and Neurologic Function.  
  https://imnf.org/

- **Music and Economics**  
  The music industry, streaming economics, copyright and intellectual property. Behavioral economics of taste formation.  
  See Alan Krueger, *Rockonomics*  
  https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780525509813/rockonomics

## Curated Playlists for Teaching

Create Spotify/YouTube playlists demonstrating:

- **Expectation violation examples**: deceptive cadences, suspensions, metric modulation
- **Frisson-inducing moments**: Barber's Adagio, Rachmaninoff climaxes, Jeff Buckley's "Hallelujah"
- **Cross-cultural comparison**: pentatonic scales, rhythmic complexity, tonal systems
- **Timbre exploration**: solo instruments across families, extended techniques, synthesis
- **Rhythm and meter**: polymeter (3 against 4), syncopation, metric modulation
- **Musical universals**: lullabies from different cultures, dance music patterns

Students can listen while reading lessons and analyze their own responses.

## For the Curious Student

If a student wants to go deeper:

- **Join SMPC** (student membership available): attend conferences, network with researchers
- **Participate in MusicLab experiments**: contribute to citizen science, see how research is done
- **Audit online courses**: Coursera, edX have music cognition offerings
- **Follow the literature**: set up Google Scholar alerts for key researchers
- **Try computational analysis**: download Music21, analyze a corpus
- **Read popular books**: Oliver Sacks' *Musicophilia*, Gary Marcus' *Guitar Zero*, Elena Mannes' *The Power of Music*
- **Explore adjacent fields**: psycholinguistics, auditory neuroscience, evolutionary psychology
- **Consider graduate programs**: many universities have music cognition labs seeking interdisciplinary students

The field is highly interdisciplinary and welcoming to newcomers from music, psychology, neuroscience, computer science, and beyond.
