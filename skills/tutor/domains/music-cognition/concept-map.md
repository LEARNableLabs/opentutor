# Music Cognition — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Pitch perception** — how the auditory system encodes frequency information
2. **Rhythm perception** — temporal processing and beat induction
3. **Timbre** — spectral characteristics that distinguish sound sources
4. **Auditory scene analysis** — grouping and streaming principles that parse complex auditory environments. Depends on: pitch perception, timbre
5. **Gestalt principles in music** — perceptual organization and context effects. Depends on: auditory scene analysis
6. **Frisson (chills)** — physiological response to emotionally powerful music moments
7. **Expectation** — predictive processing and statistical learning in music. Depends on: gestalt principles
8. **Musical syntax** — structural rules and patterns in music. Depends on: expectation
9. **Prediction error** — mismatch between expected and actual musical events. Depends on: expectation, musical syntax
10. **ITPRA theory** — Imagination, Tension, Prediction, Reaction, Appraisal framework for musical emotion. Depends on: prediction error, frisson
11. **Enculturation** — cultural learning of musical norms and patterns. Depends on: musical syntax, statistical learning
12. **Musical universals** — features shared across musical cultures. Depends on: enculturation
13. **Musical semantics** — how music conveys meaning and emotion. Depends on: ITPRA theory, enculturation
14. **Earworms (involuntary musical imagery)** — spontaneous recall and repetition of music in memory
15. **Musical memory systems** — episodic, semantic, and procedural memory for music. Depends on: earworms
16. **Musical recognition** — identifying familiar music from partial cues. Depends on: musical memory systems
17. **Mozart effect** — claims about music enhancing cognition (largely debunked)
18. **Sexual selection theory** — music evolved to attract mates. Depends on: musical universals
19. **Social bonding theory** — music evolved to strengthen group cohesion. Depends on: musical universals
20. **Synchrony and entrainment** — temporal alignment of individuals through music. Depends on: rhythm perception, social bonding theory
21. **Infant-directed singing** — specialized vocal communication with infants. Depends on: social bonding theory
22. **Cultural evolution of music** — how musical features spread and change. Depends on: enculturation, musical universals
23. **Auditory cortex** — primary brain region for sound processing
24. **Distributed neural networks** — multiple brain regions coordinate for music processing. Depends on: auditory cortex
25. **Reward pathways** — dopaminergic circuits activated by pleasurable music. Depends on: frisson, distributed neural networks
26. **Music-language relationship** — shared and distinct neural substrates. Depends on: musical syntax, distributed neural networks
27. **Neuroplasticity in musicians** — structural brain changes from training. Depends on: distributed neural networks
28. **Music therapy** — clinical applications for neurological disorders. Depends on: neuroplasticity, reward pathways

## Dependencies

### Perceptual Foundation
- **Auditory scene analysis** requires understanding **pitch perception** and **timbre** because parsing complex sounds depends on discriminating frequency and spectral content
- **Gestalt principles** build on **auditory scene analysis** because perceptual grouping operates on already-segmented auditory streams

### Expectation and Emotion
- **Musical syntax** depends on **expectation** because structural rules emerge from patterns listeners learn to predict
- **Prediction error** requires **expectation** and **musical syntax** because violations are meaningful only relative to learned patterns
- **ITPRA theory** builds on **prediction error** and **frisson** because it explains how expectation violations generate emotional responses
- **Musical semantics** depends on **ITPRA theory** and **enculturation** because meaning arises from both biological responses to structure and learned cultural associations

### Memory
- **Musical recognition** depends on **musical memory systems** because identifying songs requires accessing stored representations
- **Earworms** relate to **musical memory systems** because they involve spontaneous retrieval from memory

### Social and Evolutionary
- **Sexual selection** and **social bonding** theories both build on **musical universals** because evolutionary explanations must account for cross-cultural patterns
- **Synchrony and entrainment** depend on **rhythm perception** (the mechanism) and **social bonding theory** (the function)
- **Cultural evolution** depends on **enculturation** and **musical universals** because it explains how universal constraints interact with cultural transmission

### Neural Systems
- **Distributed neural networks** build on **auditory cortex** because higher-level processing integrates primary auditory input
- **Reward pathways** depend on **frisson** (the phenomenon) and **distributed neural networks** (the substrate) because emotional responses emerge from coordinated activity across regions
- **Music-language relationship** depends on **musical syntax** (the cognitive parallel) and **distributed neural networks** (the neural overlap)
- **Neuroplasticity** depends on understanding **distributed neural networks** because training-induced changes affect multiple coordinated regions

### Applications
- **Music therapy** builds on **neuroplasticity** and **reward pathways** because clinical interventions leverage both structural brain changes and emotional engagement

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are critical prerequisites for many later ideas:

1. **Expectation** — foundational for emotion, syntax, prediction error, and meaning
2. **Musical syntax** — required for prediction error, semantics, and music-language comparisons
3. **Distributed neural networks** — required for all advanced neuroscience topics
4. **Enculturation** — critical for distinguishing universal vs. cultural phenomena

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Music perception is purely bottom-up"** — Actually, top-down expectation shapes what we hear. Understanding requires grasping the interaction.

2. **"Emotional responses are just associations"** — While associations matter, structural features (expectation violation, tension-resolution) drive emotion even in novel music. Both matter.

3. **"There's a single 'music center' in the brain"** — Music processing is distributed across many regions. Students must understand network thinking, not localization.

4. **"The Mozart effect means music makes you smarter"** — The original effect was small, temporary, and doesn't generalize. Critical thinking about research claims is essential.

5. **"Music is universal, so responses must be innate"** — Statistical universals can emerge from cultural evolution without being innate. The universal/cultural distinction is subtle.
