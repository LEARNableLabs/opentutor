# Aerospace Engineering — Flight and Propulsion — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Continuum assumption** — treating air as a continuous fluid rather than discrete molecules
2. **Conservation laws** — mass, momentum, and energy must be conserved in fluid flows
3. **Viscosity** — internal friction in fluids that creates shear stress. Depends on: continuum assumption
4. **Reynolds number** — dimensionless ratio of inertial to viscous forces. Depends on: viscosity
5. **Boundary layers** — thin regions near surfaces where viscous effects dominate. Depends on: viscosity, Reynolds number
6. **Flow separation** — detachment of flow from a surface due to adverse pressure gradients. Depends on: boundary layers
7. **Circulation** — line integral of velocity around a closed curve, fundamental to lift generation
8. **Vorticity** — local rotation of fluid elements. Depends on: circulation
9. **Kutta-Joukowski theorem** — relates circulation to lift force. Depends on: circulation
10. **Airfoil geometry** — shape parameters (camber, thickness) that determine aerodynamic properties
11. **Induced drag** — drag due to finite wing effects and wingtip vortices. Depends on: circulation, vorticity
12. **Aspect ratio** — wingspan squared divided by wing area, affects induced drag. Depends on: induced drag
13. **Pressure drag** — drag from pressure distribution around a body. Depends on: flow separation
14. **Skin friction drag** — drag from viscous shear at the surface. Depends on: boundary layers
15. **Laminar-turbulent transition** — change from smooth to chaotic flow in boundary layers. Depends on: Reynolds number, boundary layers
16. **Stall** — loss of lift due to flow separation at high angle of attack. Depends on: flow separation
17. **Mach number** — ratio of flow velocity to speed of sound
18. **Compressibility effects** — density changes in flow at high Mach numbers. Depends on: Mach number
19. **Shock waves** — discontinuous pressure jumps in supersonic flow. Depends on: Mach number, compressibility
20. **Converging-diverging nozzle** — geometry that accelerates flow to supersonic speeds. Depends on: Mach number, compressibility
21. **Wave drag** — drag from shock waves at transonic/supersonic speeds. Depends on: shock waves
22. **Thrust** — force generated by expelling mass (Newton's third law). Depends on: conservation laws
23. **Specific impulse** — efficiency metric for propulsion (thrust per unit weight flow rate). Depends on: thrust
24. **Brayton cycle** — thermodynamic cycle for gas turbines (compression-combustion-expansion). Depends on: conservation laws
25. **Bypass ratio** — ratio of bypass air to core airflow in turbofan engines. Depends on: Brayton cycle
26. **Compressor staging** — multiple compression stages to achieve high pressure ratios. Depends on: Brayton cycle
27. **Thrust-specific fuel consumption** — fuel efficiency metric for air-breathing engines. Depends on: thrust, Brayton cycle
28. **Ramjet** — engine with no moving parts that works at supersonic speeds. Depends on: Mach number, compressibility
29. **Rocket equation** — Tsiolkovsky equation relating velocity change to mass ratio. Depends on: thrust, conservation laws
30. **Nozzle expansion ratio** — area ratio that determines optimal altitude for rocket nozzles. Depends on: converging-diverging nozzle, shock waves
31. **Propulsion-airframe integration** — coupling between engine and aircraft aerodynamics. Depends on: thrust, boundary layers, drag components

## Dependencies

### Aerodynamics Chain
- **Boundary layers** require understanding **viscosity** and **Reynolds number** because viscous effects are only significant in the near-wall region where velocity gradients are steep
- **Flow separation** builds on **boundary layers** because separation occurs when the boundary layer can't overcome adverse pressure gradients
- **Stall** requires **flow separation** because massive separation over the wing upper surface causes lift loss
- **Induced drag** builds on **circulation** and **vorticity** because finite wings shed trailing vortices that induce downwash and change the effective angle of attack

### Compressible Flow Chain
- **Compressibility effects** require **Mach number** because density changes become significant only when flow speeds approach the speed of sound
- **Shock waves** build on **Mach number** and **compressibility** because shocks are supersonic flow discontinuities where compressibility is extreme
- **Wave drag** requires **shock waves** because it results from entropy generation across shock waves
- **Converging-diverging nozzles** build on **Mach number** and **compressibility** because the area-Mach relationship inverts at M=1, requiring divergence to continue acceleration

### Propulsion Chain
- **Thrust** fundamentally requires **conservation laws** (momentum and energy) because thrust is the rate of change of momentum of the exhaust
- **Specific impulse** builds on **thrust** because it normalizes thrust by propellant weight flow rate
- **Brayton cycle** requires **conservation laws** because the cycle analysis uses first law (energy) and second law (entropy)
- **Bypass ratio** builds on **Brayton cycle** because the fan and core both operate on Brayton principles but with different pressure ratios
- **Rocket equation** combines **thrust** and **conservation laws** because it integrates the momentum equation accounting for changing vehicle mass
- **Nozzle expansion ratio** requires understanding **converging-diverging nozzles** and **shock waves** because overexpanded nozzles form internal shocks

### Cross-Domain Dependencies
- **Propulsion-airframe integration** requires understanding both **thrust** generation and **drag** components because engine installation affects both
- **Ramjets** require **Mach number**, **compressibility**, and **Brayton cycle** because they use ram compression instead of mechanical compressors
- **Aeroacoustics** builds on **boundary layers**, **shock waves**, and **jet exhaust** because noise sources include turbulent mixing, shocks, and shear layers

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts unlock multiple downstream topics:

1. **Reynolds number** — gates understanding of boundary layers, transition, drag prediction, and scaling
2. **Mach number** — gates all compressible flow phenomena (shocks, wave drag, nozzles, high-speed propulsion)
3. **Circulation** — gates lift theory, induced drag, wing design, and vortex dynamics
4. **Conservation laws** — gate all propulsion analysis (thrust, efficiency, cycle analysis)
5. **Boundary layers** — gate drag prediction, separation, stall, and transition phenomena

## Mind-Blowing Moments

1. **Circulation creates lift** — the non-intuitive fact that lift comes from a mathematical circulation around the wing, not just flow deflection (Kutta-Joukowski theorem)
2. **Nozzles accelerate by diverging** — above Mach 1, you must expand the area to accelerate the flow (counterintuitive!)
3. **Bypass makes jets efficient** — turbofans are more efficient because they move more air slower, not because they're more powerful
4. **Rockets carry oxidizer** — the rocket equation shows that carrying your own oxidizer leads to exponential mass penalties
5. **Swept wings delay shock** — sweeping a wing doesn't eliminate compressibility, it tricks the flow into "seeing" a lower effective Mach number
6. **Vortices cost energy** — finite wings generate vortices that persist for miles behind the aircraft, representing wasted energy (induced drag)

## Common Stumbling Blocks

1. **Bernoulli oversimplification** — students try to explain all lift with Bernoulli, missing the role of circulation and the Kutta condition
2. **Mach vs. Reynolds confusion** — conflating compressibility effects (Mach) with viscous effects (Reynolds)
3. **Thrust vs. power** — confusing thrust (force) with power (force × velocity), especially in propeller vs. jet comparisons
4. **Specific impulse units** — the dual unit systems (seconds vs. m/s) for Isp cause confusion
5. **Stagnation vs. static** — mixing up stagnation (total) and static thermodynamic properties in compressible flows
6. **Rocket staging** — understanding why staging is exponentially beneficial requires deep engagement with the rocket equation

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Multivariable calculus** — needed for: vector fields (velocity, vorticity), surface and line integrals (circulation, flux), divergence and curl operators
- **Differential equations** — needed for: boundary layer equations, compressible flow equations, unsteady flow problems
- **Classical mechanics** — needed for: force balance, momentum conservation, Newton's laws applied to control volumes
- **Thermodynamics** — needed for: first and second laws, entropy, enthalpy, isentropic relations, cycle analysis
- **Basic fluid mechanics** — needed for: continuity equation, Bernoulli equation, control volume analysis, pressure and shear stress concepts

## Unexpected Connections

- **Music and shock waves** — sonic booms are shock waves; understanding supersonic aerodynamics illuminates why you can't hear a supersonic jet approaching
- **Weather and compressible flow** — atmospheric flow over mountains can go supersonic (relative to the sound speed in that air), creating lee waves and rotors
- **Formula 1 and wings** — racing car aerodynamics uses the same principles but inverted (downforce instead of lift)
- **Hummingbirds and helicopters** — both generate lift through unsteady vortex dynamics rather than steady circulation
- **Sailing and airfoils** — sails are vertical wings; America's Cup boats use rigid wings that are literally aircraft wings turned sideways
