# Archaeoastronomy — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Comprehensive Works

- **Aveni, Anthony F. (2001). _Skywatchers_. Yale University Press.**
  - Comprehensive global survey of archaeoastronomy from the leading North American scholar. Accessible intermediate-level writing. Covers Mesoamerica, North America, Europe, and beyond. Excellent for cultural context and case studies.
  - https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300090031/skywatchers/

- **Ruggles, Clive (2005). _Ancient Astronomy: An Encyclopedia of Cosmologies and Myth_. ABC-CLIO.**
  - Reference work with entries on sites, concepts, and cultures worldwide. Good for looking up specific topics. More Eurocentric than Aveni but strong on British Isles.
  - https://www.abc-clio.com/products/a2139c/

- **Krupp, E. C. (1983). _Echoes of the Ancient Skies: The Astronomy of Lost Civilizations_. Oxford University Press.**
  - Combines archaeology with myth and cosmology. Engaging narrative style. Slightly dated but still valuable for understanding cultural astronomy.
  - https://global.oup.com/academic/product/echoes-of-the-ancient-skies-9780195042597

- **Hoskin, Michael (2001). _Tombs, Temples and Their Orientations: A New Perspective on Mediterranean Prehistory_. Cambridge University Press.**
  - Rigorous quantitative approach to Mediterranean and Middle Eastern sites. Good model for statistical methods.
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/tombs-temples-and-their-orientations/9780521765541

- **Magli, Giulio (2016). _Archaeoastronomy: Introduction to the Science of Stars and Stones_. Springer.**
  - Modern undergraduate-level textbook with mathematical rigor. Covers methods, statistics, and global case studies. Best for students comfortable with equations.
  - https://www.springer.com/gp/book/9783319227573

### Interactive Tools and Software

- **Stellarium (FREE)** — essential open-source planetarium software. Can set location, date (including ancient dates with precession correction), and view horizon profiles.
  - https://stellarium.org/

- **JPL Horizons System** — NASA's tool for precise ephemerides of solar system bodies. Advanced users can calculate exact positions for ancient dates.
  - https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/

- **Cartes du Ciel / Skychart (FREE)** — alternative planetarium software with sky chart printing.
  - https://www.ap-i.net/skychart/

- **SunCalc** — simple web tool for visualizing Sun position and path for any location and date.
  - https://www.suncalc.org/

- **The Photographer's Ephemeris** — Sun, Moon, Milky Way positions with topographic maps. Great for planning site visits.
  - https://photoephemeris.com/

- **PeakFinder** — identifies mountains and hills on the horizon; useful for horizon profile analysis.
  - https://www.peakfinder.org/

- **TimeandDate.com** — Sun/Moon calculators, eclipse tables, calendar converters.
  - https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/

- **In-The-Sky.org** — planetary visibility charts, ephemerides, sky simulations.
  - https://in-the-sky.org/

## People (Scholars and Practitioners)

### Foundational Figures

- **Gerald Hawkins** — astronomer who proposed Stonehenge as Neolithic computer (controversial but influential).
- **Alexander Thom** — engineer who surveyed hundreds of stone circles; proposed "megalithic yard."
- **Anthony Aveni** — pioneered Mesoamerican archaeoastronomy; key figure in establishing field's academic credibility.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Urban light pollution and loss of dark skies** — modern people have lost connection to the starry sky that ancient peoples lived under daily. Visit a dark sky preserve (International Dark-Sky Association) to regain perspective.
  - https://www.darksky.org/

- **Biorhythms and circadian biology** — connections between lunar cycles and biological rhythms (menstruation, tides, animal behavior). Emerging research on "lunaception" and circadian photobiology.

- **Astroarchaeology and SETI** — if we detect alien civilizations, how would we interpret their astronomical monuments? Carl Sagan's thoughts on this.

- **Disaster archaeology** — ancient records of comets, novae, supernovae (Crab Nebula 1054 CE, SN 1006) preserved in petroglyphs and texts. Links to catastrophe narratives.

- **Cognitive archaeology** — what does astronomical knowledge tell us about ancient cognition, numeracy, and symbolic thought? Connections to Paleolithic "notation" theories (Marshack's bone tallies).

- **Music and astronomy** — Pythagorean "music of the spheres," Maya conch shell trumpets at equinox, Stonehenge acoustics. Sensory dimensions of ritual astronomy.

- **Astrology vs astronomy** — historical relationship. Ancient astronomy often served astrological ends (Babylonian omen texts, Hellenistic horoscopes). When/how did they diverge?

- **Climate change and astronomical traditions** — will Arctic communities lose traditional sky knowledge as daylight/darkness patterns shift? Preservation of intangible heritage.

## Visiting Sites (if possible)

- **Stonehenge** — Wiltshire, England. Managed access; book solstice visits months ahead.
  - https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/stonehenge/

- **Newgrange** — County Meath, Ireland. Winter solstice lottery for roofbox viewing.
  - https://www.worldheritageireland.ie/bru-na-boinne/

- **Chichen Itza** — Yucatán, Mexico. Equinox serpent shadow on El Castillo.
  - https://www.inah.gob.mx/

- **Chaco Culture National Historical Park** — New Mexico, USA. Remote; plan logistics.
  - https://www.nps.gov/chcu/

- **Maeshowe** — Orkney, Scotland. Winter solstice illumination chamber.
  - https://www.historicenvironment.scot/visit-a-place/places/maeshowe/

- **Angkor Wat** — Cambodia. Equinox sunrise over central tower.
  - https://www.tourismcambodia.com/

- **Jantar Mantar observatories** — Jaipur, Delhi, India. 18th-century but showcase observational techniques.
  - https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1338/

- **Kokino megalithic observatory** — North Macedonia. Claimed Bronze Age observatory.
  - https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5347/
