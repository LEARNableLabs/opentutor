# Archaeoastronomy — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Celestial Mechanics for Ancient Cultures
- Solar cycles (solstices, equinoxes, annual motion)
- Lunar cycles (phases, standstills, eclipse prediction)
- Stellar motion (precession, heliacal risings, circumpolar stars)
- Planetary visibility and cycles
- Horizon astronomy and local topography

### 2. Detection and Analysis Methods
- Alignment surveys and statistical significance
- Theodolite and GPS surveying techniques
- Digital elevation models and horizon profiles
- Statistical tests (Schaefer, Ruggles methodologies)
- Software tools (Stellarium, Horizons, archaeoastronomical calculators)

### 3. Major Archaeological Sites
- **European megalithic sites**: Stonehenge, Newgrange, Callanish, Carnac
- **Mesoamerican observatories**: Chichen Itza (El Caracol), Uaxactun, Chaco Canyon
- **Egyptian monuments**: Pyramids of Giza, Abu Simbel, Karnak
- **Asian sites**: Angkor Wat, Jantar Mantar observatories
- **Native American sites**: Medicine wheels, Cahokia, Fajada Butte

### 4. Cultural Context and Interpretation
- Cosmology and worldview in ancient cultures
- Calendar systems (Maya, Egyptian, Babylonian)
- Sacred geography and landscape astronomy
- The role of astronomy in agriculture, religion, and power
- Avoiding overinterpretation and confirmation bias

### 5. Modern Research Methods
- Remote sensing and LiDAR
- 3D modeling and virtual reconstructions
- Interdisciplinary approaches (archaeology + astronomy + ethnography)
- Ethical considerations and indigenous knowledge

## Key Sources

### Textbooks and Comprehensive Works
- **Aveni, A. (2001). _Skywatchers_.** Yale University Press. Comprehensive survey of archaeoastronomy across cultures.
- **Ruggles, C. (2005). _Ancient Astronomy: An Encyclopedia of Cosmologies and Myth_.** ABC-CLIO. Reference work covering global practices.
- **Krupp, E. C. (1983). _Echoes of the Ancient Skies_.** Oxford University Press. Cultural astronomy and mythology.
- **Hoskin, M. (2001). _Tombs, Temples and Their Orientations_.** Cambridge University Press. Mediterranean and Middle Eastern focus.

### Academic Resources
- Journal of Skyscape Archaeology (https://journals.equinoxpub.com/JSA)
- Archaeoastronomy: The Journal of Astronomy in Culture (University of Texas Press)
- Oxford IX International Conference proceedings (ongoing series)

### Online Courses and Syllabi
- Universidad Politécnica de Madrid: Archaeoastronomy courses

### Software and Tools
- **Stellarium** (https://stellarium.org) — open-source planetarium for sky reconstruction
- **Horizons** (JPL) — precise ephemerides for celestial bodies
- **Cartes du Ciel / Skychart** — astronomical charts
- **PhotoScan / Metashape** — 3D reconstruction from photographs

### Interactive Resources

## Research Gaps Identified
- Limited resources on Southern Hemisphere archaeoastronomy (Australia, Polynesia, South America)
- Need more accessible introductions to statistical methods
- Cultural sensitivity in interpretation varies widely across sources
- Software tutorials specific to archaeoastronomy are scattered

## Recommended Learning Path for Intermediate Students
1. Start with foundational astronomy (sky mechanics, coordinate systems)
2. Learn alignment detection methods before diving into specific sites
3. Study 2-3 well-documented sites in depth (Stonehenge, Newgrange, Chichen Itza)
4. Explore cultural context and interpretation challenges
5. Hands-on practice with Stellarium and alignment analysis
6. Survey global diversity of archaeoastronomical practices
