# Archaeoastronomy — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Solar declination** — the Sun's north-south position in the sky changes throughout the year, causing seasonal rising/setting point shifts
2. **Solstices and equinoxes** — extreme and midpoint positions of solar declination. Depends on: solar declination
3. **Ecliptic** — the Sun's apparent annual path through the stars. Depends on: solar declination
4. **Horizon astronomy** — observing celestial events at the local horizon, shaped by topography. Depends on: solar declination, solstices/equinoxes
5. **Lunar phases** — the Moon's appearance changes on a ~29.5-day cycle
6. **Lunar standstills** — the Moon's extreme declination positions on an 18.6-year cycle. Depends on: lunar phases, solar declination
7. **Synodic vs sidereal periods** — different ways to measure orbital cycles (relative to Sun vs stars)
8. **Heliacal rising** — a star's first pre-dawn appearance after being lost in solar glare. Depends on: ecliptic
9. **Stellar calendars** — using heliacal risings to mark seasons. Depends on: heliacal rising
10. **Axial precession** — Earth's spin axis wobbles over 26,000 years, changing which stars are "pole stars" and when heliacal risings occur. Depends on: heliacal rising, stellar calendars
11. **Horizon profile** — the local topographic horizon defines where celestial objects rise/set. Depends on: horizon astronomy
12. **Azimuth and altitude** — horizontal coordinate system for measuring celestial positions. Depends on: horizon profile
13. **Statistical significance** — distinguishing intentional alignments from random chance
14. **Null hypothesis** — the assumption that observed patterns are due to chance. Depends on: statistical significance
15. **Target richness** — the number of potential celestial targets increases the probability of chance alignments. Depends on: null hypothesis, statistical significance
16. **Confirmation bias** — the tendency to seek evidence supporting pre-existing beliefs. Depends on: target richness
17. **Theodolite surveying** — precise measurement of horizontal and vertical angles in the field. Depends on: azimuth and altitude
18. **Stellarium** — planetarium software for reconstructing ancient skies. Depends on: axial precession, horizon profile
19. **Schaefer test** — statistical method for evaluating alignment significance. Depends on: statistical significance, null hypothesis
20. **Ruggles declination distribution** — analyzing the spread of monument orientations. Depends on: Schaefer test
21. **Monte Carlo simulation** — random sampling to estimate probabilities of chance alignments. Depends on: Ruggles declination distribution
22. **Passage tomb astronomy** — Neolithic monuments with astronomical alignments (e.g., Newgrange). Depends on: solstices, horizon astronomy
23. **Maya calendar systems** — Tzolk'in, Haab', Long Count for astronomical timekeeping. Depends on: stellar calendars, synodic vs sidereal
24. **Venus tables** — Maya records of Venus synodic cycles. Depends on: Maya calendar systems
25. **E-Groups** — Maya architectural complexes for solar observation. Depends on: solstices/equinoxes, Maya calendar systems
26. **Egyptian decans** — 36 star groups used for timekeeping and calendar. Depends on: heliacal rising, stellar calendars
27. **Cosmology** — a culture's model of the universe's structure and origin
28. **Cosmography** — the mapping of cosmological concepts onto physical/architectural space. Depends on: cosmology
29. **Sacred directions** — culturally significant cardinal or intercardinal directions. Depends on: cosmography
30. **Overinterpretation** — claiming astronomical significance without adequate evidence. Depends on: confirmation bias, statistical significance
31. **LiDAR archaeology** — remote sensing technique revealing landscape features invisible on the ground. Depends on: horizon profile, digital elevation models

## Dependencies

### Foundational chains
- **Solar mechanics** → horizon observations → alignment detection → site-specific case studies
- **Lunar mechanics** → standstill detection → megalithic monuments (e.g., Stonehenge, Callanish)
- **Stellar mechanics** → precession → reconstructing ancient skies → interpreting ancient alignments

### Statistical reasoning
- Understanding **statistical significance** is essential before evaluating any claimed alignment
- **Target richness** and **confirmation bias** explain why many claimed alignments are spurious
- **Schaefer** and **Ruggles** methods provide rigorous frameworks but require understanding null hypothesis testing

### Case study dependencies
- **Stonehenge** requires: solstices, lunar standstills, statistical significance
- **Newgrange** requires: solstices, passage tomb concept, horizon profile
- **Maya observatories** require: Venus cycles, E-Groups, solar/lunar calendars
- **Egyptian pyramids** requires: stellar alignments, precession, decans
- **Chaco Canyon** requires: solstices, horizon calendars, cultural context
- **Medicine wheels** requires: solstices, stellar alignments, sacred directions

### Cultural interpretation layer
- **Cosmology** and **cosmography** are necessary for interpreting *why* ancient cultures built alignments
- **Sacred directions** vary by culture and cannot be assumed universal
- **Overinterpretation** awareness is critical — requires understanding of confirmation bias and statistical methods

## Bottlenecks (concepts that unlock many others)

1. **Solar declination** — understanding this unlocks solstices, equinoxes, and seasonal astronomy
2. **Statistical significance** — essential before evaluating any alignment claim; guards against overinterpretation
3. **Horizon astronomy** — most ancient observations were horizon-based, not zenithal
4. **Axial precession** — necessary for accurately reconstructing ancient skies >1000 years ago
5. **Cosmology vs observation** — distinguishing what ancient peoples *believed* from what they *observed*

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Ancient peoples had primitive astronomy"** — Many cultures had sophisticated observational records (Maya Venus tables, Babylonian lunar predictions)
2. **"All stone circles are observatories"** — Ritual, burial, and social functions often coexisted with or superseded astronomical use
3. **"Alignments prove advanced lost civilizations"** — Careful observation + oral tradition can achieve impressive results without writing or advanced math
4. **"Pyramids point to Orion/star maps"** — Many such claims fail statistical testing; correlation ≠ causation
5. **"Precession was unknown to ancients"** — Some cultures (Babylonians, possibly Maya) had observational records spanning centuries and may have detected it
6. **"Any site with a solstice alignment was an observatory"** — Solar extremes are easily observed; intentionality requires broader context
7. **"Archaeoastronomy is about alien contact"** — Archaeoastronomy is rigorous interdisciplinary science; pseudoarchaeology exploits public fascination
8. **"We can know ancient intentions from alignments alone"** — Ethnographic, textual, and archaeological context are essential for interpretation

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic astronomy** — needed for: celestial coordinates, solar system mechanics, apparent motion
- **Ancient civilizations** — needed for: cultural context, archaeological interpretation, chronology
- **Geometry and trigonometry** — needed for: azimuth/altitude calculations, surveying, spherical astronomy
- **Statistics** — needed for: significance testing, null hypothesis, p-values, Monte Carlo methods
- **Archaeological methods** — needed for: site interpretation, dating, material culture analysis
