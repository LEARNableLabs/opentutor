# Research Summary: Mathematics of Voting and Fair Division

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Voting Theory
- **Voting systems**: Plurality, runoff, Borda count, approval voting, ranked choice (IRV), Condorcet methods
- **Voting paradoxes**: Condorcet paradox, majority cycles, spoiler effects
- **Impossibility theorems**: Arrow's theorem, Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem
- **Strategic voting**: Manipulation, sincere vs. strategic voting
- **Voting power**: Shapley-Shubik index, Banzhaf power index

### 2. Fair Division
- **Discrete division**: Indivisible goods, envy-free allocation
- **Continuous division**: Cake-cutting protocols
- **Fairness criteria**: Proportionality, envy-freeness, equitability, efficiency (Pareto optimality)
- **Division algorithms**: Cut-and-choose, Steinhaus procedure, Selfridge-Conway, moving knife
- **Impossibility results**: Trade-offs between fairness and efficiency

### 3. Real-World Applications
- Elections and political systems
- Resource allocation in economics
- Estate division
- International disputes and border agreements
- Computational social choice

## Pedagogical Notes

The topic naturally splits into two complementary halves:
1. **Aggregating preferences** (voting) — combining individual rankings into a collective choice
2. **Dividing resources** (fair division) — distributing goods fairly among individuals

Both domains share mathematical tools (combinatorics, graph theory, optimization) and philosophical questions about fairness, but use different approaches. Voting focuses on impossibility results, while fair division emphasizes constructive algorithms.

For intermediate students, the focus should be on:
- Understanding multiple voting systems and their trade-offs
- Grasping the significance of Arrow's theorem
- Working through concrete fair division protocols
- Recognizing real-world applications
- Engaging with proofs at a semi-formal level
