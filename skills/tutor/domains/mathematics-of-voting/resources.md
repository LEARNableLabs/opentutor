# Mathematics of Voting and Fair Division — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"Mathematics and Democracy: Designing Better Voting and Fair-Division Procedures"** by Steven J. Brams (Princeton University Press, 2008) — Comprehensive coverage of both voting theory and fair division at an accessible level. Excellent for intermediate students. Strong emphasis on real-world applications and mechanism design.
  - https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691133218/mathematics-and-democracy

- **"Fair Division and Collective Welfare"** by Hervé Moulin (MIT Press, 2004) — More mathematically rigorous treatment of fair division. Good for deep dives into cake cutting and envy-freeness. Advanced sections can be skipped at intermediate level.
  - https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262134231/fair-division-and-collective-welfare/

- **"The Mathematics of Voting and Elections: A Hands-On Approach"** by Jonathan K. Hodge and Richard E. Klima (American Mathematical Society, 2018) — Very student-friendly, activity-based approach. Excellent for concrete examples and exercises.
  - https://bookstore.ams.org/text-30-2/

- **"Cake-Cutting Algorithms: Be Fair If You Can"** by Jack Robertson and William Webb (A K Peters, 1998) — Focused entirely on continuous fair division. Very readable, with detailed protocol descriptions.
  - https://www.routledge.com/Cake-Cutting-Algorithms-Be-Fair-If-You-Can/Robertson-Webb/p/book/9781568810768

- **"Social Choice and the Mathematics of Manipulation"** by Alan D. Taylor (Cambridge University Press, 2005) — Focused on strategic voting and manipulation. Covers Gibbard-Satterthwaite and related results.
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/social-choice-and-the-mathematics-of-manipulation/

### Academic Papers (Foundational)

- **Arrow, Kenneth J.** "A Difficulty in the Concept of Social Welfare" (1950) — The original Arrow's theorem paper. Surprisingly readable for a foundational result.
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/1828886

- **Gibbard, Allan.** "Manipulation of Voting Schemes: A General Result" (1973)
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914083

- **Brams, Steven J., and Alan D. Taylor.** "Fair Division: From Cake-Cutting to Dispute Resolution" (1996)
  - https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/fair-division/

### Open Educational Resources

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Voting Methods**
  - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/voting/
  - Excellent philosophical and mathematical overview

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Social Choice Theory**
  - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-choice/
  - Covers Arrow's theorem and related impossibility results

- **MIT OpenCourseWare — Topics in Mathematics: Voting Theory and Fair Division**
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-18-011-algebra-i-student-notes-fall-2021/
  - (Note: Specific voting theory courses may vary by semester; search MIT OCW for "voting theory")

## Supplementary Resources

### Interactive Tools & Simulators

- **Nicky Case's "To Build a Better Ballot"**
  - https://ncase.me/ballot/
  - ESSENTIAL interactive exploration of voting systems. Students should spend significant time here. Covers plurality, IRV, Borda, approval, score voting with live simulations.

- **Ka-Ping Yee's Voting Simulation Visualizations**
  - https://zesty.ca/voting/sim/

- **Spliddit — Provably Fair Solutions**
  - https://www.spliddit.org/

## Recent Research Directions (for rabbit holes)

### Approximate Fairness
- When exact fairness is impossible, how close can we get?
- "Fair Allocation of Indivisible Goods" by Procaccia & Wang (2014)
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0075

### Online Fair Division
- Fair division when items arrive sequentially
- "Online Fair Division" by Aleksandrov et al. (2015)
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.03903

### Fair Machine Learning
- Applying fairness criteria to algorithmic decision-making
- "Fairness and Abstraction in Sociotechnical Systems" by Selbst et al. (2019)
  - https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3287560.3287598

### Participatory Budgeting
- Fair division applied to municipal budget allocation
- "Participatory Budgeting: A Computational Perspective" by Aziz & Shah (2021)
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00606

### Liquid Democracy
- Delegation-based voting systems, hybrid representative/direct democracy
- "Liquid Democracy: An Analysis in Binary Aggregation and Diffusion" by Brill (2018)
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01548

## Unexpected Connections (wild cards)

- **Gerrymandering** — Cake cutting with adversarial goals (divide to maximize advantage)
  - https://mggg.org/ (Metric Geometry and Gerrymandering Group)

- **Kidney Exchange** — Fair allocation of transplant organs using graph matching
  - Nobel Prize work by Alvin Roth
  - https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2012/roth/facts/

- **Blockchain Governance** — DAOs use voting mechanisms for decentralized decision-making
  - Ethereum governance, proof-of-stake voting

- **Recipe for Success** — How would you fairly divide a recipe's credit among contributors?
  - Relates to Shapley value in cooperative game theory

- **Airport Game** — How to fairly split costs of shared infrastructure
  - Connects to cost-sharing and fair division

- **Divorce Settlement** — Real-world application of fair division with legal constraints
  - "Fair Division in Dispute Resolution" by Brams & Taylor

- **Sports Drafts** — Fair methods for talent allocation (snake drafts as fairness mechanism)

- **Open Source Contributions** — How to fairly credit contributors to collaborative projects
  - GitHub contribution graphs as crude fairness metrics
