# Game Theory — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"A Course in Game Theory"** by Martin J. Osborne and Ariel Rubinstein (MIT Press, 1994)
  - URL: https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/EM20/OsborneRubinsteinMasterpiece.pdf
  - Gold standard graduate textbook, comprehensive and rigorous
  - Four parts: strategic games, extensive games (perfect info), extensive games (imperfect info), coalitional games
  - Perfect for intermediate students who want depth
  - Solution manual: http://gelogica.weebly.com/uploads/2/6/6/1/26617550/a_course_in_game_theory_solution_manual_-_martin_j._osborne.pdf

- **"Algorithmic Game Theory" Lecture Notes** by Tim Roughgarden (Stanford CS364A)
  - URL: https://timroughgarden.org/f13/f13.pdf
  - Covers mechanism design, auctions, and computational aspects
  - Great for students interested in computer science applications
  - Accessible introduction to algorithmic game theory

## Code & Computational Tools

- **Gambit: Software Tools for Game Theory**
  - Homepage: http://www.gambit-project.org/ (linked from society page)
  - Open-source suite for constructing and analyzing finite games
  - Command-line tools and GUI
  - Nash equilibrium computation for normal and extensive form games

- **Game Theory Explorer (GTE)**
  - Web-based tool for creating and analyzing games
  - Extensive and strategic form
  - Equilibrium computation with visualization

- **Nashpy (Python library)**
  - Python library for computing Nash equilibria
  - Good for students with programming background
  - Can integrate with data science workflows

- **Stanford CS364A Code Examples**
  - Available through course materials
  - Mechanism design and auction algorithms

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Biology & Evolution
- **Maynard Smith & Price** — Evolutionarily stable strategies (ESS)
- **Richard Dawkins** — "The Selfish Gene" — evolutionary game theory for general audiences
- **Connection**: Animal behavior (hawk-dove games, sex ratios, altruism) is game-theoretic. ESS predicts stable population distributions.

### Computer Science & AI
- **Multi-agent systems** — Game theory models interactions between AI agents
- **Adversarial machine learning** — Attacker-defender games in security
- **Auction algorithms** — Mechanism design for online advertising (Google, Facebook ad auctions)
- **Connection**: Nash equilibria in AlphaGo, poker AI (Libratus, Pluribus). Reinforcement learning and game theory converge.

### Political Science
- **Voting theory** — Strategic voting, agenda control, gerrymandering as games
- **International relations** — Arms races, deterrence, alliance formation
- **Legislative bargaining** — Coalition formation, log-rolling
- **Connection**: Median voter theorem, Hotelling's location game (candidate positioning). Game theory explains political strategy.

### Philosophy
- **Rational choice theory** — Foundations of decision-making
- **Social contract theory** — Hobbes, Rousseau through game-theoretic lens
- **Ethics and cooperation** — Why cooperate if defection is rational?
- **Connection**: Prisoner's dilemma as philosophical puzzle. Kant's categorical imperative vs Nash equilibrium.

### Law & Regulation
- **Antitrust** — Detecting and preventing collusion (repeated games)
- **Contract design** — Incentive-compatible contracts under asymmetric information
- **Plea bargaining** — Defendant-prosecutor as a sequential game
- **Connection**: Mechanism design for regulation, auction design for spectrum licenses (FCC auctions).

### Psychology & Behavioral Economics
- **Bounded rationality** — Herbert Simon, Daniel Kahneman
- **Fairness and reciprocity** — People punish unfairness even at a cost (ultimatum game experiments)
- **Level-k reasoning** — Iterative models of strategic thinking
- **Connection**: Experimental game theory tests Nash predictions. Deviations inspire behavioral models (inequity aversion, social preferences).

### Linguistics
- **Signaling games** — How language evolves (Lewis signaling games)
- **Pragmatics** — Game-theoretic semantics, conversational implicature
- **Connection**: Cheap talk, costly signaling. Why do we say "please"? Coordination on meaning.

### Traffic & Urban Planning
- **Braess's paradox** — Adding roads can increase congestion (Nash equilibrium vs social optimum)
- **Routing games** — Selfish routing in networks
- **Connection**: Price of anarchy — how much do we lose from non-cooperative behavior?
