# History and Philosophy of Technology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Historical Dimensions
1. **Ancient and Medieval Technologies** — irrigation, metallurgy, construction, mechanization
2. **Industrial Revolutions** — steam power, mass production, electrification, automation
3. **Information Age** — computing, telecommunications, internet, AI
4. **Military and Medical Technology** — warfare innovations, public health, biotechnology

### Philosophical Dimensions
1. **Nature of Technology** — definitions, essence, technological artifacts vs natural objects
2. **Technology and Society** — social construction, determinism, co-evolution
3. **Ethics and Values** — responsibility, dual-use, embedded values, techno-politics
4. **Phenomenology of Technology** — Heidegger's "The Question Concerning Technology", lived experience
5. **Critical Theory** — Ellul, Marcuse, Feenberg on domination and liberation
6. **Postmodern and Contemporary Debates** — posthumanism, transhumanism, the Anthropocene

## Key Thinkers

**Classical**
- Lewis Mumford — technics and civilization
- Jacques Ellul — technological society and autonomy
- Martin Heidegger — technology as enframing
- Herbert Marcuse — one-dimensional technological rationality

**Contemporary**
- Andrew Feenberg — critical constructivism
- Langdon Winner — politics of artifacts
- Don Ihde — postphenomenology
- Bruno Latour — actor-network theory
- Donna Haraway — cyborgs and situated knowledge
- Peter-Paul Verbeek — mediation theory

## Pedagogical Approach for Intermediate Level

At intermediate level, students should:
- Engage with primary philosophical texts (excerpts from Heidegger, Ellul, Winner)
- Compare competing philosophical frameworks (determinism vs constructivism)
- Apply philosophical concepts to contemporary case studies (social media, AI, climate tech)
- Understand historical context without getting lost in excessive detail
- Develop critical thinking about technology's role in society

Balance historical narrative with philosophical analysis. Use case studies to ground abstract concepts.
