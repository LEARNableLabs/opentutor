# History and Philosophy of Technology — Resources

## Primary Textbooks and Anthologies

- **"Philosophy of Technology: The Technological Condition"** (Scharff & Dusek, eds.)  
  Comprehensive anthology with primary source excerpts from Heidegger, Ellul, Winner, Feenberg, Haraway, and more. Excellent for intermediate students. Each excerpt includes helpful introductions.  
  Available through university libraries and major academic publishers.

- **"Technology and the Good Life?"** (Eric Higgs, Andrew Light, David Strong, eds.)  
  Accessible collection focusing on practical and ethical dimensions. Less dense than Scharff & Dusek, good for students struggling with heavy theory.

- **"Readings in the Philosophy of Technology"** (David Kaplan, ed.)  
  Another strong anthology with broader coverage including recent work on digital technology, AI, and environmental issues.

## Individual Books (Primary and Secondary Sources)

### Historical
- **Lewis Mumford, "Technics and Civilization" (1934)**  
  https://archive.org/details/technicsciviliza00mumf  
  Classic work on the development of Western technology from medieval period through industrial age. Still influential, readable style.

- **David Edgerton, "The Shock of the Old: Technology and Global History Since 1900"**  
  Challenges innovation-centric narratives, focuses on use and maintenance rather than invention. Great corrective to Silicon Valley triumphalism.

- **Jared Diamond, "Guns, Germs, and Steel"**  
  https://archive.org/details/gunsgermssteelfa00diam  
  Controversial but influential work on geographical determinism in technological development. Good for Great Divergence discussions.

### Philosophical Foundations

- **Martin Heidegger, "The Question Concerning Technology" (1954)**  
  https://archive.org/details/questionconcerni00heid  
  Essential primary source. Dense but crucial. Use excerpts with guidance at intermediate level.

- **Jacques Ellul, "The Technological Society" (1964)**  
  https://archive.org/details/technologicalsoc00ellu  
  Concept of "technique" and autonomous technology. Influential on later critical theory.

- **Don Ihde, "Technology and the Lifeworld" (1990)**  
  Postphenomenological approach, more accessible than Heidegger, focuses on human-technology relations.

- **Andrew Feenberg, "Questioning Technology" (1999)**  
  Critical constructivism, democratic rationalization. Good synthesis of Frankfurt School and STS.

- **Langdon Winner, "The Whale and the Reactor" (1986)**  
  Includes famous "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" essay. Accessible, concrete examples.

### Contemporary

- **Donna Haraway, "A Cyborg Manifesto" (1985)**  
  https://www.jstor.org/stable/3178066  
  Foundational feminist philosophy of technology. Posthuman theory, situated knowledge.

- **Shoshana Zuboff, "The Age of Surveillance Capitalism" (2019)**  
  https://www.publicbooks.org/the-age-of-surveillance-capitalism/  
  Contemporary analysis of data extraction and platform power. Students find it highly relevant.

- **Kate Crawford, "Atlas of AI" (2021)**  
  Materialist analysis of AI infrastructure — labor, resources, politics. Excellent for contemporary debates.

- **Bruno Latour, "Reassembling the Social" (2005)**  
  Actor-Network Theory. Challenges human/technology divide. More advanced, use selectively.

## Open Educational Resources

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
- **Philosophy of Technology**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/technology/  
  Comprehensive, authoritative, regularly updated. Essential reference.

- **Phenomenological Approaches to Ethics and IT**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-it-phenomenology/  
  Covers Heidegger, Ihde, Verbeek applied to information technology.

- **Computing and Moral Responsibility**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/computing-responsibility/

## Scholarly Articles (Accessible to Intermediate Students)

- **Langdon Winner, "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"** (1980)  
  https://www.jstor.org/stable/20024652  
  Also: https://www.cc.gatech.edu/~beki/cs4001/Winner.pdf  
  Classic, readable, concrete examples. Essential reading.

- **Wiebe Bijker, "The Social Construction of Bakelite" (1987)**  
  https://www.jstor.org/stable/285743  
  Introduces SCOT framework with detailed case study.

- **Trevor Pinch and Wiebe Bijker, "The Social Construction of Facts and Artifacts" (1984)**  
  Foundational SCOT article. Compare technological and scientific construction.

- **W. Brian Arthur, "Competing Technologies, Increasing Returns, and Lock-In by Historical Events" (1989)**  
  https://www.jstor.org/stable/2234208  
  Path dependence, QWERTY problem, economic perspective on technology adoption.

## Unexpected Connections and Rabbit Holes

### Literature as Philosophy of Technology
- **Mary Shelley, "Frankenstein" (1818)**  
  Early critique of unconstrained technological creation, Romantic response to industrialization.

- **E.M. Forster, "The Machine Stops" (1909)**  
  Prescient dystopian story about technology dependence, written before modern computing.

- **Kurt Vonnegut, "Player Piano" (1952)**  
  Automation anxiety, dehumanization through technology.

### Film and Media
- **"Blade Runner" (1982)** and **"Blade Runner 2049" (2017)**  
  Posthumanism, what counts as human, technology and identity.

- **"Black Mirror" (Netflix series)**  
  Contemporary technology parables. Good discussion starters but often simplistic.

- **"Lo and Behold: Reveries of the Connected World" (Werner Herzog documentary, 2016)**  
  Philosophical documentary on internet and connectivity.

### Cross-Disciplinary Connections
- **Environmental humanities** — Anthropocene, planetary-scale technology
- **Disability studies** — technology as prosthesis, access, and accommodation
- **Postcolonial studies** — technology transfer, development, and dependency
- **Media theory** — Marshall McLuhan, "the medium is the message"
- **Architectural theory** — space and technology, Reyner Banham

## Note on Access

Many resources require institutional access (JSTOR, academic journals). If student doesn't have university affiliation:
- Check public library systems (many offer academic database access)
- Look for open-access versions on author websites or PhilPapers
- Use Internet Archive and Stanford/IEP encyclopedias (freely available)
- Many scholars post preprints on their institutional websites
