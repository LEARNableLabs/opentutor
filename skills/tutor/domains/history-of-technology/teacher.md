## Domain Hooks
- **Lewis Mumford's megamachine** — Drop in when discussing Ellul or industrial organization. Mumford argues that large-scale social organization (Egyptian pyramids, modern bureaucracy) is itself a technology. Blows students' minds about what counts as "technology."

- **Technological momentum** — Thomas Hughes' concept that technologies gain momentum over time, becoming harder to redirect. Bridges determinism and construction. Great for path dependence discussions.

- **Frankenstein and the Romantic critique** — When covering 19th-century industrialization, mention that Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) is an early philosophical critique of unconstrained technological creation. Literature as philosophy of technology.

- **Appropriate technology movement** — E.F. Schumacher's "Small is Beautiful" and the 1970s movement for human-scale, democratic technology. Historical alternative to both technophobia and techno-optimism. Relevant for lesson 26 on future directions.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Technology is neutral, only its uses are good or bad"**  
   Students often start with this instrumental view. Counter it early by introducing Winner's "politics of artifacts" and showing how design choices embed values. Use concrete examples: highway overpasses, building accessibility, social media algorithms.

2. **"Technological progress is linear and inevitable"**  
   Challenge this with the "Greeks and steam power" question and path dependence examples (QWERTY keyboard). Emphasize historical contingency — things could have gone differently.

3. **"Determinism and construction are opposing theories you must choose between"**  
   Many students want a clear "right answer." Resist this. Show how both offer insights. Technology constrains possibilities (determinism) while society shapes which possibilities are realized (construction). The dialectical view is most sophisticated.
