# Research Summary — Tilings and Tessellations

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Periodic Tilings
- Regular and semi-regular tilings
- Symmetry groups and wallpaper patterns
- Crystallographic constraints

### 2. Aperiodic Tilings
- Definition and fundamental properties
- Non-periodicity vs. aperiodicity
- Inflation and substitution systems

### 3. Penrose Tilings
- Kite-and-dart tiles
- Rhombus tilings
- Matching rules and forbidden patterns
- Five-fold rotational symmetry

### 4. Wang Tiles
- Edge-matching tile sets
- The domino problem (undecidability)
- Robinson's tiles and hierarchical structures
- Computational complexity

### 5. Quasicrystals
- Discovery by Shechtman (1982)
- Connection to aperiodic tilings
- Real-world materials science applications

### 6. Mathematical Foundations
- Local vs. global properties
- Finite local complexity
- Repetitivity and pattern frequencies
- Substitution matrices and scaling

## Key Sources

**Foundational texts:**
- Grünbaum & Shephard, "Tilings and Patterns" (1987) — comprehensive reference
- Senechal, "Quasicrystals and Geometry" (1995) — connects to crystallography
- Baake & Grimm, "Aperiodic Order" (2013) — modern mathematical treatment

**Seminal papers:**
- Penrose, R. (1974) "The role of aesthetics in pure and applied mathematical research"
- Berger, R. (1966) "The undecidability of the domino problem"
- Wang, H. (1961) "Proving theorems by pattern recognition"

**Educational resources:**
- MIT OpenCourseWare: Geometry course modules
- Wolfram Demonstrations: Interactive tiling generators
- YouTube: Numberphile, 3Blue1Brown on aperiodic tilings

## Prerequisites

Students need:
- Basic geometry (polygons, angles, symmetry)
- Set theory and proof techniques
- Some linear algebra (for substitution matrices)
- Computational thinking (for Wang tiles and decidability)
