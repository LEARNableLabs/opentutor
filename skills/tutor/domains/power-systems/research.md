# Power Systems Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Grid Structure and Components** — transmission, distribution, generation, loads; voltage levels; three-phase systems
2. **AC Power Analysis** — phasors, complex power (P, Q, S), power factor, single-line diagrams
3. **Transmission Lines** — line models (short, medium, long), impedance, ABCD parameters, voltage regulation
4. **Transformers** — principles, per-unit systems, three-phase configurations, tap changers
5. **Power Flow** — bus types, Gauss-Seidel and Newton-Raphson methods, optimal power flow
6. **Synchronous Generators** — construction, operation, excitation, frequency control, economic dispatch
7. **System Protection** — fault analysis (symmetrical and asymmetrical), circuit breakers, relays, grounding
8. **Stability** — transient, dynamic, voltage stability; swing curves, governors, exciters
9. **Renewable Integration** — solar, wind, storage, inverter-based resources, intermittency challenges
10. **Smart Grid and Modernization** — SCADA, PMUs, demand response, microgrids, distributed energy resources

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **Power System Analysis and Design** by Glover, Sarma, and Overbye — industry standard, strong on fundamentals and practical examples
- **Power System Analysis** by Grainger and Stevenson — classic reference, rigorous treatment
- **Power System Stability and Control** by Kundur — definitive stability reference
- **Electric Energy Systems** by Olle Elgerd — clear explanations, good for intermediate learners

- **MIT 6.061** (Introduction to Power Systems) — covers fundamentals through stability

## Research Notes

Power systems education at the intermediate level should balance:
1. **Physical intuition** — how power actually flows, why grids work the way they do
2. **Mathematical tools** — phasors, per-unit, basic optimization, but not overwhelming formalism
3. **Real-world context** — why does Texas have its own grid? What caused the 2003 blackout?
4. **Modern challenges** — renewables, storage, resilience, electrification

The curriculum should emphasize understanding over memorization, use real grid examples (ERCOT, PJM, CAISO), and connect to current events (grid modernization, climate adaptation, energy transition).
