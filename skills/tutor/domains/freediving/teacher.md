## Common Failure Modes
1. **"The urge to breathe means I'm running out of oxygen"**
   - Why: Intuitive but wrong. Breathlessness comes from CO2 buildup, not O2 depletion.
   - Correction: Explain chemoreceptor sensitivity hierarchy — CO2 drives breathing urge much more strongly than low O2. Graph PaCO2 vs PaO2 timecourse to show CO2 rises faster and triggers urge before O2 becomes dangerously low. Emphasize that this is a *safety margin* — the body warns you early via CO2, not late via O2.

2. **"Hyperventilating before a dive gives you more oxygen"**
   - Why: Misunderstanding of what hyperventilation does (blows off CO2, barely increases O2 due to saturation).
   - Correction: Show oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve — already ~98% saturated at rest. Hyperventilation only drops CO2, which delays the urge to breathe but *doesn't extend safe breath-hold time*. This creates shallow water blackout risk. Make the danger concrete: you can override the CO2 warning but still run out of O2.
