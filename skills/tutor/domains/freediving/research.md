# Freediving Physiology Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Mammalian Dive Reflex
- Evolutionary adaptation shared with marine mammals
- Trigeminal nerve activation and autonomic responses
- Bradycardia, peripheral vasoconstriction, blood shift
- Individual variation and training effects

### 2. Respiratory Physiology
- Gas exchange mechanics (alveolar-capillary interface)
- Role of CO2 vs O2 in breath-hold urge
- Hypoxia vs hypercapnia: distinct mechanisms and dangers
- Chemoreceptor function (peripheral vs central)
- Lung volumes and capacity adaptations

### 3. Cardiovascular Adaptations
- Heart rate response to apnea and immersion
- Peripheral vasoconstriction patterns
- Splenic contraction and hematocrit changes
- Blood pressure regulation during breath-hold
- Cerebral blood flow maintenance

### 4. Pressure and Depth Effects
- Boyle's law application to lung compression
- Residual volume and depth limits
- Blood shift mechanism (thoracic blood pooling)
- Barotrauma risks and prevention
- Equalization physiology

### 5. Training Adaptations
- Hypoxic tolerance improvements
- CO2 tolerance development
- Cardiovascular conditioning
- Psychological factors and relaxation
- Detraining effects

### 6. Safety and Pathophysiology
- Shallow water blackout mechanism
- Hypoxic blackout vs syncope
- Taravana (decompression sickness in freedivers)
- Contraindications and risk factors
- Recovery breathing and surface protocols
