# Freediving Physiology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks
- **Brubakk AO & Neuman TS. "Bennett and Elliott's Physiology and Medicine of Diving" (5th ed., 2003)** — The definitive reference on diving physiology. Chapters 2-4 cover respiratory and cardiovascular responses. Dense but authoritative. Good for intermediate students willing to work through technical material.
- **Lindholm P & Pollock N. "The Science of Breath-Hold Diving and Surfacing" (2016)** — Freediving-specific physiology text. More accessible than Bennett & Elliott. Covers mammalian dive reflex, blood shift, training adaptations, and safety. Best match for this curriculum's level.
- **West JB. "Respiratory Physiology: The Essentials" (10th ed., 2020)** — General respiratory background. Chapters on gas exchange, control of breathing, and blood gas transport provide foundations for lessons 7-11. Clear diagrams and accessible writing.

### Academic Papers (key references)
- **Ferretti G. "Extreme human breath-hold diving." European Journal of Applied Physiology (2001)** — Review of physiological limits in competitive freediving. Data on elite divers' adaptations.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11316498/

- **Schagatay E. "Predicting performance in competitive apnoea diving." Undersea & Hyperbaric Medicine (2009)** — Identifies physiological predictors of freediving performance (splenic volume, dive reflex strength).
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19092328/

- **Lindholm P & Lundgren C. "The physiology and pathophysiology of human breath-hold diving." Journal of Applied Physiology (2009)** — Comprehensive review covering all major topics in this curriculum.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19211945/

- **Foster GE & Sheel AW. "The human diving response, its function, and its control." Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports (2005)** — Detailed coverage of mammalian dive reflex mechanisms.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15798847/

### Course Materials
- **AIDA International Medical Resources** — Official education materials from the international freediving federation. Includes physiology modules for instructor training.
  https://www.aida-international.org/Education/Medical

- **Divers Alert Network (DAN) Health Resources** — Medical articles on dive reflex, blood shift, shallow water blackout, contraindications. Written for divers, not researchers — accessible and practical.
  https://www.dan.org/health-medicine/

- **Molchanovs Freediving Education** — Teaching methodology from one of the largest freediving education systems. Includes physiology modules and training guidance.
  https://www.molchanovs.com/blogs/education

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Cross-Discipline Links
- **Yoga and Pranayama** — Breath retention practices (kumbhaka) in yoga have physiological overlap with freediving. Some elite freedivers train with pranayama techniques. Research on yoga practitioners shows similar CO2 tolerance adaptations.
  - Jerath R et al. "Physiology of long pranayamic breathing." Medical Hypotheses (2006)
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16529878/

- **High-Altitude Physiology** — Chronic hypoxia adaptations (increased hematocrit, 2,3-DPG shifts) parallel some freediving training effects. Altitude training used by some freedivers.
  - West JB. "The physiologic basis of high-altitude diseases." Annals of Internal Medicine (2004)

- **Sleep Apnea Research** — Obstructive sleep apnea creates repetitive hypoxic episodes. Research on cardiovascular consequences and chemoreceptor dysfunction informs freediving safety.
  - Dempsey JA et al. "Pathophysiology of human ventilatory control." European Respiratory Journal (2014)

- **Antarctic Diving Seals** — Weddell seals dive to 600m and hold breath for 80+ minutes. Comparative physiology reveals extreme versions of human dive adaptations (massive spleens, high myoglobin).
  - Ponganis PJ. "Diving Physiology of Marine Mammals and Seabirds" (2015, Cambridge University Press)

- **Cold Water Immersion and Vagal Tone** — Cold exposure activates parasympathetic nervous system (same pathway as dive reflex). Wim Hof method and cold therapy research.
  - Buijze GA et al. "The effect of cold showering on health and work." PLoS One (2016)
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27532257/

- **Fighter Pilot G-LOC (G-induced Loss of Consciousness)** — Cerebral hypoxia from blood pooling during high-G maneuvers. Opposite mechanism from freediving but similar outcome (blackout). Interesting comparison for lesson 26.
  - Whinnery JE. "Observations on the neurophysiologic theory of acceleration (+Gz) induced loss of consciousness." Aviation, Space, and Environmental Medicine (1989)

### Historical/Cultural Context
- **Ama Divers of Japan** — Traditional female pearl divers, some active into their 80s. Studied for physiological adaptations to cold and breath-hold.
  - Hong SK et al. "Daily diving pattern of Korean diving women (ama)." Journal of Applied Physiology (1963)

- **Bajau "Sea Nomads" of Southeast Asia** — Genetic adaptations for diving (enlarged spleens) discovered in 2018. Evolution of freediving physiology in action.
  - Ilardo MA et al. "Physiological and genetic adaptations to diving in sea nomads." Cell (2018)
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29677510/

### Safety and Medical
- **NIH StatPearls: Breath-Holding Spells** — Pediatric breath-holding (cyanotic vs pallid types). Different mechanism from voluntary apnea but shows range of respiratory control pathology.
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470392/

- **DAN Annual Diving Report** — Accident statistics and analysis. Real-world safety data.
  https://dan.org/safety-prevention/diver-safety/reports-and-resources/

- **PubMed Clinical Queries: Freediving** — Filter for recent case reports and research.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=freediving
