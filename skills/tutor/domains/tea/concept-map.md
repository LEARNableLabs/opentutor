# Tea — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Camellia sinensis taxonomy** — two main varieties and their geographic/morphological differences
2. **Genetic diversity in cultivars** — how breeding and selection create flavor variation. Depends on: 1
3. **Terroir chemistry** — altitude, soil, climate effects on secondary metabolite expression. Depends on: 1, 2
4. **Varietal tasting** — sensory recognition of cultivar markers. Depends on: 2, 3
5. **Enzymatic oxidation** — polyphenol oxidase activity after leaf damage
6. **Withering** — controlled water loss and early enzymatic changes. Depends on: 5
7. **Kill-green (sha qing)** — enzyme deactivation to halt oxidation. Depends on: 5, 6
8. **Partial oxidation control** — bruising and timing to create oolong range. Depends on: 5, 6, 7
9. **Full oxidation** — complete enzymatic conversion to theaflavins/thearubigins. Depends on: 5, 6
10. **Microbial fermentation** — bacterial/fungal activity in dark tea aging. Depends on: 5, 9
11. **Flavor compound classes** — catechins, amino acids, volatiles, tannins
12. **L-theanine and umami** — amino acid contribution to taste. Depends on: 11
13. **Astringency mechanisms** — tannin-protein binding on palate. Depends on: 11
14. **Aromatic chemistry** — volatile compound classes and perception. Depends on: 11
15. **Gongfu extraction principles** — high leaf ratio, short infusions, control. Depends on: 5, 11
16. **Vessel heat retention** — material effects on temperature stability. Depends on: 15
17. **Water chemistry** — mineral content and pH effects on extraction. Depends on: 15
18. **Extraction timing** — compound release order across infusions. Depends on: 11, 15, 17
19. **Flavor evolution across infusions** — tasting progression through session. Depends on: 11, 18
20. **Quality grading systems** — visual, olfactory, gustatory assessment standards. Depends on: 4, 11, 13, 14
21. **Aging chemistry** — storage effects on flavor development. Depends on: 10, 11

## Dependencies

### Linear Foundations
- **Cultivar science (1-4)** forms the biological foundation. Understanding genetic diversity and terroir is essential before exploring how processing transforms these raw materials.
- **Processing chemistry (5-10)** builds directly on enzymatic activity. Each processing type (white, green, oolong, black, dark) represents different points on the oxidation/fermentation spectrum.
- **Sensory science (11-14)** requires processing knowledge because the compounds being tasted are products of processing decisions.
- **Gongfu technique (15-19)** requires sensory understanding because extraction control is about managing flavor compound release.
- **Quality assessment (20-21)** synthesizes all prior knowledge.

### Critical Bottlenecks
- **Enzymatic oxidation (5)** is the central concept. All processing types are defined by how they manage oxidation, and all flavor chemistry flows from oxidation products.
- **Flavor compound classes (11)** unlocks sensory evaluation. Without this, students can't connect chemistry to taste.
- **Gongfu extraction principles (15)** is the gateway to practical mastery. All technique lessons build from this foundation.

### Cross-Dependencies
- **Varietal tasting (4)** becomes deeper after **flavor chemistry (11)** — students can return to cultivar comparisons with chemical vocabulary.
- **Water chemistry (17)** affects **extraction timing (18)** and **flavor evolution (19)** — these form a tight interdependent cluster.
- **Aging chemistry (21)** integrates **microbial fermentation (10)** with **flavor compounds (11)** — understanding both is essential.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Organic chemistry** — needed for: oxidation-reduction, phenolic compounds, enzymatic reactions, volatile chemistry
- **Basic botany** — needed for: plant taxonomy, cultivar genetics, secondary metabolites, growth conditions
- **Taste physiology** — needed for: receptor mechanisms, umami, astringency, aroma perception
- **Heat transfer basics** — needed for: vessel material effects, temperature control, brewing physics

## Common Misconception Connections

- **"Fermentation vs. oxidation"** — students conflate enzymatic oxidation (all tea except green/white) with microbial fermentation (dark tea only). This confusion appears at concepts 5, 9, and 10.
- **"Black tea isn't oxidized because it's called red tea"** — translation confusion. Address when teaching concept 9.
- **"Gongfu is just a ritual"** — students miss the extraction science. Critical to emphasize at concept 15.
- **"Quality is subjective"** — students don't realize grading has objective chemical/visual markers. Address at concept 20.

## Difficulty Peaks

- **Lesson 10 (Oolong complexity)** — managing partial oxidation gradients is the hardest processing concept
- **Lesson 12 (Microbial fermentation)** — distinguishing microbial from enzymatic processes
- **Lesson 16 (Astringency mechanisms)** — molecular-level perception science
- **Lesson 25 (Aging chemistry)** — synthesizes fermentation, oxidation, and storage chemistry
- **Lesson 26 (Integrated analysis)** — capstone synthesis requiring application of all concepts from cultivar identification through brewing optimization
