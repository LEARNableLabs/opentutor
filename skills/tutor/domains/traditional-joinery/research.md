# Traditional Joinery Research

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Wood Fundamentals
- Wood grain behavior and movement
- Species selection for joinery
- Seasoning and moisture content
- Grain direction and strength

### 2. Western Joinery Tradition
- **Mortise and Tenon Family**: through mortise, blind mortise, haunched tenon, wedged tenon
- **Dovetail Family**: through dovetails, half-blind dovetails, sliding dovetails, secret mitered dovetails
- **Lap Joints**: half-lap, cross-lap, dovetail lap
- **Bridle Joints**: corner bridle, T-bridle
- **Finger Joints**: box joints, lock joints
- **Frame Joints**: cope and stick, miter joints with splines

### 3. Japanese Joinery Tradition (仕口/継手 - shiguchi/tsugite)
- **Core Philosophy**: without nails or glue, self-locking mechanical advantage
- **Foundation Joints**: hozo (mortise and tenon variants), kama-hozo (lipped mortise)
- **Timber Frame Joints**: watari-ago (jaw joint), kanawa-tsugi (metal ring splice)
- **Complex Interlocking**: shachi-sen (gooseneck wedge), ari-otoshi (sliding dovetail wedge)
- **Kumiko**: delicate lattice work, compound geometric patterns

### 4. Tool Traditions
- **Western**: chisels, backsaws, marking gauges, try squares, bench planes
- **Japanese**: nomi (chisels pushed toward body), dozuki/ryoba (pull saws), kanna (pull planes), kebiki (marking gauge)

### 5. Design Principles
- Load distribution and structural integrity
- Seasonal wood movement accommodation
- Aesthetic choices: visible vs. hidden joinery
- Reversibility and disassembly
- Cultural context and historical evolution

## Key Sources

### Books and Texts
- **"The Complete Manual of Wood Bending"** by Lon Schleining — includes joinery for curved work
- **"Japanese Woodworking Tools: Their Tradition, Spirit and Use"** by Toshio Odate — foundational philosophy
- **"The Art of Japanese Joinery"** by Kiyosi Seike — comprehensive joint catalog
- **"Taunton's Complete Illustrated Guide to Joinery"** by Gary Rogowski — Western methods reference

### Video Resources
- Paul Sellers YouTube channel — hand tool joinery demonstrations
- Japanese Carpenter channel — traditional temple carpentry
- The Woodwright's Shop (PBS) — historical techniques
- Fine Woodworking YouTube — project-based joinery

- Center for Furniture Craftsmanship (Maine) — intensive workshops
- Traditional Japanese carpentry apprenticeships (miyadaiku)
