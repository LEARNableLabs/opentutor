# Bioethics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Textbooks
- **Beauchamp & Childress, *Principles of Biomedical Ethics*** — The gold standard. Four principles framework (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice). Dense but authoritative. Best for intermediate students who want depth.
- **Helga Kuhse & Peter Singer, *A Companion to Bioethics*** — Anthology covering all major topics. Good for dipping into specific issues rather than linear reading.
- **Gregory Pence, *Classic Cases in Medical Ethics*** — Case-based approach. Excellent for students who learn from examples (Baby Fae, Karen Ann Quinlan, Jesse Gelsinger). Very readable.
- **Jonathan Glover, *Choosing Children*** — Focused on reproductive ethics and genetic selection. Philosophically sophisticated but accessible.

### Online Courses & Open Educational Resources
- **Harvard's Justice with Michael Sandel**: https://www.edx.org/course/justice — Includes bioethics modules on abortion, genetic enhancement. Sandel's teaching style is engaging and dialectical. Free audit option.
- **Georgetown Bioethics online programs**: https://bioethics.georgetown.edu/online-learning/ — Kennedy Institute runs certificate programs. More advanced than this curriculum but excellent for going deeper.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare — Brains, Minds and Machines**: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-9-003-brains-minds-and-machines-summer-course-summer-2015/ — For AI ethics, neuroscience, and BCIs (lessons 14-18, 32).
- **Coursera Medical Humanities**: https://www.coursera.org/learn/medical-humanities — Pairs ethics with literature and history. Broader than pure philosophy.

### Interactive Tools & Databases

#### Case Databases
- **AI Incident Database**: https://incidentdatabase.ai/ — Real-world AI failures. Search for healthcare incidents for lessons 14-18. Students can explore bias, transparency, liability cases.
- **NIH Bioethics Resources**: https://www.nih.gov/health-information/nih-clinical-research-trials-you/bioethics-resources — Case studies, policy documents, teaching materials.
- **Presidential Commission for Bioethical Issues (archive)**: https://bioethicsarchive.georgetown.edu/pcsbi/ — Historical policy reports on cloning, synthetic biology, neuroscience, pandemic ethics.

#### Genetics & Genomics
- **Genome.gov Policy Issues**: https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues — NHGRI resources on genetic discrimination (GINA), editing, privacy. For lessons 5-9.
- **Global Observatory for Genome Editing (WHO)**: https://www.who.int/groups/expert-advisory-committee-on-developing-global-standards-for-governance-and-oversight-of-human-genome-editing — Governance frameworks, international consensus documents.
- **Your Genome (Wellcome)**: https://www.yourgenome.org/ — UK-based genetics education. Good explainers on PGD, gene therapy, CRISPR.

#### Thought Experiment Generators
- **Trolley Problem variations**: http://moralmachine.mit.edu/ — MIT's Moral Machine for autonomous vehicle ethics. Generates trolley-like dilemmas. Useful for lesson 4 and lesson 18 (triage).
- **The Good Project (Harvard)**: https://www.thegoodproject.org/ — Case studies on ethical decision-making in professions. Medical ethics cases available.

#### Data & Simulations
- **Our World in Data — Life Expectancy**: https://ourworldindata.org/life-expectancy — For lesson 20 context on longevity trends.
- **Statista — IVF access by country**: https://www.statista.com/topics/6020/ivf/ — For lesson 10 on reproductive justice.

### Code & Open Datasets (for computationally-inclined students)

- **MIMIC-III Clinical Database**: https://mimic.mit.edu/ — De-identified health data for ML research. Shows the data behind AI diagnostics (lesson 14). Requires training to access.
- **Fairlearn (Microsoft)**: https://fairlearn.org/ — Python toolkit for assessing and improving fairness in ML. For lesson 15, 17.
- **AI Fairness 360 (IBM)**: https://aif360.mybluemix.net/ — Another fairness toolkit with metrics and algorithms. Interactive demos.
- **UCI Machine Learning Repository — Health datasets**: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/ml/datasets.php — Accessible datasets for students to experiment with bias/fairness.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards and rabbit holes)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

#### Bioethics ↔ Science Fiction
- **Gattaca** (film) — Genetic discrimination, PGD, enhancement inequality. For lessons 7, 11, 22.
- **Never Let Me Go** by Kazuo Ishiguro — Organ harvesting, personhood, human dignity. For lesson 28.
- **Brave New World** by Aldous Huxley — Genetic engineering, social control, happiness vs freedom. For lesson 20.
- **The Dispossessed** by Ursula K. Le Guin — Thought experiment on different social structures. For justice discussions.
- **Black Mirror** (series) — "Be Right Back" (grief tech), "Hated in the Nation" (surveillance), "Arkangel" (parental monitoring). Multiple episodes relevant.

#### Bioethics ↔ Climate Ethics
Both wrestle with:
- Future persons (non-identity problem)
- Intergenerational justice
- Collective action problems
- Precautionary principle vs innovation

Resource: **Stephen Gardiner, *A Perfect Moral Storm*** — Applies climate ethics framework to bioethics challenges. Advanced.

#### Bioethics ↔ Animal Ethics
Shared concepts:
- Moral status (what makes something count morally?)
- Speciesism vs genetic discrimination
- Enhancement (should we enhance animals? uplift?)
- Utilitarianism's scope (does suffering matter regardless of species?)

Resource: **Peter Singer, *Animal Liberation*** — Foundational text. Students who accept Singer on animals often resist him on disability.

#### Bioethics ↔ Philosophy of Mind
- Personal identity over time (lesson 20: am I still me if I live 200 years?)
- Consciousness and moral status (lesson 27: why does brain death matter more than cardiac?)
- Mental privacy (lesson 32: can BCIs read thoughts? Should they?)

Resource: **Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*** — Part III on personal identity. Challenging but mind-expanding.

#### Bioethics ↔ Economics
- Market failures in healthcare (adverse selection, moral hazard)
- Cost-effectiveness analysis (QALYs, DALYs)
- Global health priorities (effective altruism)
- Commodification and market design (organ markets, surrogacy)

Resource: **Alvin Roth, *Who Gets What — and Why*** — Market design for kidneys, school choice. Nobel Prize winner. Accessible.

#### Bioethics ↔ Law
- Constitutional rights (privacy, bodily autonomy, reproduction)
- Tort liability (medical malpractice, AI liability)
- Regulation (FDA, IRBs, data privacy laws)
- International law (human rights, biosecurity)

Resource: **George Annas, *American Bioethics*** — Legal perspective from leading health law scholar.

#### Bioethics ↔ History
- Eugenics movements (US, Nazi Germany, Scandinavia)
- Tuskegee syphilis study (research ethics)
- Nuremberg Code and Helsinki Declaration (informed consent origins)
- Buck v. Bell (forced sterilization, still good law)

Resource: **Rebecca Skloot, *The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks*** — Accessible, gripping, raises consent, race, commercialization issues.

#### Bioethics ↔ Anthropology
- Cultural variation in death definitions (Japan's rejection of brain death)
- Reproductive norms across cultures (kinship, surrogacy, abortion)
- Disability as cultural construct
- Ritual and meaning in medical contexts

Resource: **Paul Farmer, *Pathologies of Power*** — Medical anthropology meets human rights. Shows how structural violence shapes health.

### Wild Cards (to drop in for "did you know?" moments)

1. **CRISPR came from bacteria studying viruses** — lesson 5. The immune system we're using to edit humans evolved to fight phages. Nature repurposed; so did we.

2. **The trolley problem was invented to criticize consequentialism** — lesson 4. Philippa Foot wanted to show limits of "maximize good outcomes." Now it's used to teach intro ethics uncritically.

3. **Some bioethicists think we have a *duty* to enhance** — lesson 19. Julian Savulescu's "procreative beneficence" argues parents are obligated to select the best embryos. Very controversial.

4. **Brain death was partly invented to enable organ transplantation** — lesson 27. The Harvard Ad Hoc Committee (1968) wanted a new death definition when hearts were still beating. Genuine medical advance or interest-driven redefinition? Debate continues.

5. **Informed consent is only ~75 years old** — lesson 2. Nuremberg Code (1947) after Nazi medical experiments. Before that, paternalism was the norm. Still is in many places.

6. **He Jiankui is in prison, but the CRISPR babies are alive and growing up** — lesson 6. What happens to them? Are they research subjects for life? Who monitors their health? No one knows.

7. **Some AI fairness metrics are mathematically incompatible** — lesson 15. You literally cannot satisfy all fairness criteria at once. Impossibility theorem. Fairness requires *choosing* which unfairness to accept.

8. **Life expectancy at 40 has barely changed; what changed is infant mortality** — lesson 20. Misleading to say humans lived to 30 in the past. If you survived childhood, you often lived to 60-70. Relevance: what does life extension actually extend?

9. **Oregon's assisted dying law allows it, but doesn't require insurance to cover it** — lesson 30. Meanwhile, some insurers deny expensive treatments but cover assisted dying. Perverse incentives?

10. **Your genetic data is already being used by police** — lesson 8. GEDmatch, genetic genealogy, Golden State Killer case. You don't have to upload your own DNA; a third cousin can identify you.

11. **There's a market for "smart" eggs from Ivy League students** — lesson 11. Some egg donor agencies charge premiums for SAT scores, beauty, height. Commodification in action.

12. **Disability advocates protested *Million Dollar Baby*** — lesson 21. Film portrayed quadriplegia as fate worse than death. Disability community pushed back: "We're not all waiting to die."

13. **WHO declared a moratorium on germline editing… but it's advisory** — lesson 6. No enforcement mechanism. What happens when a country defects? Bioethics arms race?

14. **Peter Singer thinks some animals matter more than some humans** — throughout. Utilitarian consistency: if a chimp has more capacity than a severely disabled infant, treat the chimp better? Students find this shocking. Useful for challenging humanist assumptions.

15. **One of the He Jiankui CRISPR babies might not even be HIV-resistant** — lesson 6. The edits were incomplete. So: all the risk, consent violations, international condemnation… and it might not have worked.
