# Sleep Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

**The Science of Sleep** (Open Textbook Library)  
https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/the-science-of-sleep  
Open-access comprehensive textbook covering fundamentals, neurobiology, disorders, and health impacts. Excellent for intermediate learners. Peer-reviewed and freely available. Best for structured topic coverage.

**Sleep Science** by Hawley Montgomery-Downs (Oxford University Press, 2019)  
https://global.oup.com/academic/product/sleep-science-9780190923259  
Upper-division undergraduate textbook designed for students with prerequisites in behavioral science, physiology, and research methods. Strong on research methods and empirical evidence. More academic than Walker.

**The Science of Sleep: What It Is, How It Works, and Why It Matters** by Wallace B. Mendelson (University of Chicago Press, 2017)  
https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo27346926.html  
Accessible introduction balancing basic science and clinical applications. Covers mechanisms, states, and disorders. Good for mechanistic deep dives.

**Why We Sleep: Unlocking the Power of Sleep and Dreams** by Matthew Walker (2017)  
https://www.amazon.com/Why-We-Sleep-Unlocking-Dreams/dp/1501144316  
Popularization by UC Berkeley sleep researcher. Highly engaging, strong on functions and health consequences. Be aware: some claims have been critiqued as overstated, so use alongside peer-reviewed sources. Excellent for engagement and rabbit holes.

https://online.umich.edu/courses/sleep-neurobiology-medicine-and-society/

## Interactive Tools & Simulators

### Sleep Tracking & Analysis

**Sleep diaries and logs** — Available through National Sleep Foundation and sleep medicine organizations  
Can be used to track personal sleep patterns and apply circadian rhythm concepts

### Circadian Rhythm Simulators

**Circadian rhythm visualization tools** — Web-based demos showing two-process model dynamics  
Search for "two process model simulator" or "circadian phase response curve calculator"

### Polysomnography Practice

**Sleep stage scoring practice** — Some sleep societies offer practice hypnograms for learning to identify sleep stages from EEG  
Useful for understanding what sleep stages actually look like in data

### Mobile Apps

**Sleep Cycle, Sleep as Android** — Consumer sleep tracking apps using actigraphy  
Can experiment with tracking your own sleep and seeing circadian patterns  
Note: These are not as accurate as polysomnography but useful for pattern detection

## Research Papers (Key Foundational Work)

### Discovery Papers

**Aserinsky, E., & Kleitman, N. (1953). Regularly occurring periods of eye motility, and concomitant phenomena, during sleep. Science.**  
The discovery of REM sleep. Historical milestone.

**Borbély, A. A. (1982). A two process model of sleep regulation. Human Neurobiology.**  
Original two-process model paper. Still the dominant framework.

### Modern Reviews

**Cirelli, C., & Tononi, G. (2008). Is sleep essential? PLOS Biology.**  
Comprehensive review of sleep functions and theories.

**Xie, L., et al. (2013). Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain. Science.**  
Discovery of the glymphatic system. Major recent finding.

**Walker, M. P., & Stickgold, R. (2004). Sleep-dependent learning and memory consolidation. Neuron.**  
Authoritative review on memory consolidation.

**Scammell, T. E., et al. (2017). Neural circuitry of wakefulness and sleep. Neuron.**  
Modern review of flip-flop switch and arousal circuits.

### Recent Findings (2020+)

Search PubMed or Google Scholar for:
- "glymphatic system" (waste clearance)
- "sleep and Alzheimer's" (neurodegeneration)
- "adolescent sleep and school start times" (applied research)
- "orexin" or "hypocretin" (narcolepsy mechanisms)

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Sleep & Athletic Performance

**Connection:** Sleep enhances motor learning, reaction time, and injury recovery. NBA teams and Olympic athletes now prioritize sleep as performance enhancement.  
**Resources:** Cheri Mah's work on sleep extension in athletes, "The Sleep Solution" by Chris Winter (sports sleep consultant)  
**Why it's wild:** Challenges the "no pain no gain" mentality. Rest is training.

### Sleep & Climate/Light Pollution

**Connection:** Artificial light disrupts circadian rhythms at population scale. Light pollution may contribute to public health issues (cancer, metabolic disorders). Urban design and public policy implications.  
**Resources:** Horacio de la Iglesia's work on pre-industrial sleep in communities without electricity  
**Why it's wild:** Sleep science intersects with environmental policy and urban planning.

### Sleep & Criminal Justice

**Connection:** Interrogation and sleep deprivation can produce false confessions. Sleep deprivation impairs judgment and memory, contributing to wrongful convictions.  
**Resources:** Research on sleep deprivation and decision-making, false confession literature  
**Why it's wild:** Neuroscience evidence used in legal contexts.

### Sleep & Economics (Matthew Gibson's "Why Do We Sleep?" paper)

**Connection:** Daylight Saving Time shifts cause measurable economic costs (accidents, productivity losses, health impacts). Sleep deprivation costs the U.S. economy $411 billion annually (RAND study).  
**Resources:** RAND "Why Sleep Matters" report, DST impact studies  
**Why it's wild:** Sleep is an economic issue, not just a personal health issue.

### Sleep & Evolution (Unihemispheric Sleep)

**Connection:** Dolphins, seals, and some birds sleep with half their brain at a time to maintain vigilance or continue swimming. Shows that sleep is non-negotiable but flexible.  
**Resources:** Jerome Siegel's comparative sleep work  
**Why it's wild:** Evolution solved the "sleep vulnerability problem" in creative ways.

### Sleep & Creativity (Dalí's "Slumber with a Key" technique)

**Connection:** Salvador Dalí would nap holding a key; as he fell asleep, the key would drop and wake him, capturing hypnagogic imagery. Thomas Edison did similar. REM and N1 sleep boost creative insight.  
**Resources:** Cai et al. on REM and creativity, Wagner on sleep and insight  
**Why it's wild:** Artists and inventors intuited what science later confirmed.

### Sleep & Space Exploration

**Connection:** Astronauts on the ISS experience 16 sunrises/sunsets per day, disrupting circadian rhythms. NASA studies sleep in microgravity for long-duration missions to Mars.  
**Resources:** NASA sleep research, Charles Czeisler's work with astronauts  
**Why it's wild:** Sleep science is critical for space travel. Can't explore the cosmos without understanding sleep.

### Sleep & Military Performance

**Connection:** Sleep deprivation is a major factor in military errors and accidents. Modafinil (wakefulness-promoting drug) developed for military use. "Strategic napping" in special forces.  
**Resources:** Military sleep research (Walter Reed Army Institute of Research), modafinil studies  
**Why it's wild:** Sleep is a national security issue.

## Live Your Learning

The best way to learn sleep science is to apply it:

- **Track your own sleep** — use a sleep diary or app, notice circadian patterns
- **Experiment with sleep hygiene** — test whether blue light blocking, consistent schedule, or caffeine timing affects your sleep
- **Notice misalignment** — observe social jet lag on weekends, jet lag during travel
- **Read your dreams** — keep a dream journal, notice REM vs NREM differences
- **Observe others** — notice chronotype differences in friends/family, developmental changes

Sleep science is unique in that everyone has the laboratory (their own sleep) available 24/7. Use it!
