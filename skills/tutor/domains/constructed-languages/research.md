# Research Summary — Constructed Languages

## Major Subtopics

### 1. History and Motivation
- International auxiliary languages (17th-20th century)
- Philosophical languages (Leibniz, Wilkins)
- Modern revival and artistic conlangs
- Digital age conlanging communities

### 2. Classification
- **A posteriori** languages (based on natural languages): Esperanto, Interlingua, Latino sine flexione
- **A priori** languages (built from scratch): Toki Pona, Lojban, aUI
- **Artistic/fictional** languages: Quenya, Sindarin, Klingon, Dothraki, Na'vi
- **Engelangs** (engineered for specific purposes): Lojban, Ithkuil

### 3. Linguistic Design Principles
- Phonology and phonotactics
- Morphology (agglutination, isolation, fusion)
- Syntax (word order, case systems, verb agreement)
- Semantic systems (how meaning is encoded)
- Writing systems (Latin extensions, novel scripts)
- Regularity vs. naturalism trade-offs

### 4. Case Studies
- **Esperanto** — most successful auxlang, regular morphology, European roots
- **Toki Pona** — minimalist philosophy, 120-137 words, Taoist influences
- **Lojban** — logical language based on predicate logic
- **Quenya/Sindarin** — Tolkien's artistic languages, Finnish/Welsh influences
- **Klingon** — Marc Okrand's alien aesthetic, retroactive worldbuilding

### 5. Communities and Practice
- Language learning platforms (Duolingo, Memrise)
- Conlang communities (Conlang mailing list, r/conlangs, Language Creation Society)
- Constructed language documentation standards
- Usage in fiction, film, and culture

## Notes for Curriculum Design

- **Intermediate level** means students likely have some linguistics background (phonetics, basic syntax) but may not have studied conlangs specifically
- Balance theory (linguistic design principles) with practice (analyzing real conlangs)
- Progression: history → principles → case studies → creation
- Emphasize hands-on analysis of real conlang features
- Include both practical (Esperanto, Toki Pona) and artistic (Tolkien, modern media) examples
- Address common misconceptions (all conlangs are Esperanto clones, conlangs are "easier" than natural languages)
- Leverage interactive tools and community resources for engagement
