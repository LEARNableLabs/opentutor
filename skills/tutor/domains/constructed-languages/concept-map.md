# Constructed Languages — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Constructed language (conlang)** — any language deliberately created by a person or group, rather than evolving naturally
2. **Auxiliary language (auxlang)** — conlang designed for international communication (e.g., Esperanto, Interlingua)
3. **Artistic language (artlang)** — conlang created for aesthetic or fictional purposes (e.g., Quenya, Klingon)
4. **A posteriori language** — conlang based on existing natural languages. Depends on: constructed language
5. **A priori language** — conlang built from scratch without direct natural language influence. Depends on: constructed language
6. **Philosophical language** — historical attempts to create logical or taxonomic languages. Depends on: constructed language
7. **Phonology** — sound system of a language (inventory, phonotactics, stress)
8. **Phonotactics** — rules for how sounds can combine in a language. Depends on: phonology
9. **Morphology** — how words are formed and change shape (inflection, derivation, compounding)
10. **Agglutination** — morphological strategy where words are built from clearly separable morphemes. Depends on: morphology
11. **Isolating morphology** — words don't change form; grammar expressed through word order and particles. Depends on: morphology
12. **Fusional morphology** — grammatical information encoded in morpheme changes. Depends on: morphology
13. **Syntax** — rules for sentence structure and word order
14. **Case system** — grammatical marking of nouns to show their role in a sentence. Depends on: syntax, morphology
15. **Free word order** — syntax where word position doesn't determine grammatical role. Depends on: syntax, case system
16. **Semantics** — how meaning is organized and expressed in a language
17. **Semantic primes** — minimal set of universal meaning components. Depends on: semantics
18. **Esperanto** — most successful auxlang, created by L.L. Zamenhof (1887). Depends on: a posteriori language, agglutination
19. **Correlatives** — systematic table of related question/demonstrative/indefinite words in Esperanto. Depends on: Esperanto
20. **Interlingua** — naturalistic auxlang based on Romance/Germanic vocabulary. Depends on: a posteriori language, auxlang
21. **Ido** — reformed Esperanto variant emphasizing naturalism. Depends on: Esperanto, a posteriori language
22. **Toki Pona** — minimalist a priori language with ~120 words. Depends on: a priori language, isolating morphology, semantic primes
23. **Semantic composition** — expressing complex ideas by combining simple elements. Depends on: semantics, Toki Pona
24. **Lojban** — logical language based on predicate logic. Depends on: a priori language, philosophical language
25. **Predicate logic in language** — encoding logical relations directly in grammar. Depends on: Lojban
26. **Unambiguity** — design goal of eliminating syntactic or semantic ambiguity. Depends on: Lojban, syntax
27. **Engelang** — engineered language optimized for specific purposes. Depends on: a priori language
28. **Ithkuil** — extremely complex philosophical engelang. Depends on: engelang, fusional morphology
29. **Quenya** — Tolkien's Elvish language with Finnish influences. Depends on: artistic language, a posteriori language
30. **Sound symbolism** — using phonetics to evoke meaning or feeling. Depends on: phonology, artistic language
31. **Linguistic aesthetics** — designing for beauty and cultural feel. Depends on: artistic language, phonology
32. **Dothraki** — David Peterson's language for Game of Thrones. Depends on: artistic language, worldbuilding
33. **Cultural linguistics** — encoding cultural concepts in linguistic structure. Depends on: artistic language, worldbuilding
34. **Worldbuilding** — creating fictional worlds with cultural/historical depth. Depends on: artistic language
35. **Naturalism** — making a conlang behave like natural languages (irregularity, historical change). Depends on: linguistic aesthetics
36. **Language change** — simulating historical linguistic evolution in conlangs. Depends on: naturalism
37. **Typological patterns** — cross-linguistic tendencies that make languages feel "real". Depends on: naturalism, syntax, morphology
38. **Design trade-offs** — balancing regularity, learnability, expressiveness, and naturalness. Depends on: all major design concepts
39. **Purpose-driven design** — aligning linguistic features with the language's intended use. Depends on: design trade-offs

## Dependencies

### Foundation Dependencies
- **A posteriori and a priori languages** both depend on understanding what a constructed language is and why people create them
- **Philosophical languages** provide historical context for modern logical languages like Lojban

### Linguistic System Dependencies
- **Phonotactics** requires understanding phonology — you need an inventory before you can make combination rules
- **Case systems and free word order** are intimately connected — case marking enables flexible word order
- **All morphological types** (agglutination, isolation, fusion) depend on general morphology concepts
- **Semantic composition** builds on both semantics and specific examples (like Toki Pona)

### Conlang-Specific Dependencies
- **Esperanto-specific concepts** (correlatives) require understanding Esperanto's structure
- **Lojban's logical features** build on both a priori design and philosophical language traditions
- **Toki Pona's minimalism** combines isolating morphology with semantic prime theory
- **Artistic language concepts** (Quenya, Dothraki) depend on understanding both linguistic aesthetics and worldbuilding

### Advanced Synthesis
- **Naturalism** requires understanding multiple linguistic levels (phonology, morphology, syntax) to know what to make irregular
- **Design trade-offs** can only be understood after seeing multiple approaches (Esperanto's regularity vs Quenya's aesthetics vs Lojban's logic)
- **Purpose-driven design** synthesizes all previous concepts — you need to know the options to make informed choices

## Bottlenecks

### Critical Path Concepts
1. **Morphology** — students must grasp agglutination/isolation/fusion before analyzing any specific conlang deeply. This is the bottleneck for Module 3 onward.
2. **Syntax and case systems** — needed to understand why Esperanto, Lojban, and artistic languages make different word order choices
3. **Semantic primes and composition** — essential for understanding Toki Pona's minimalism and how meaning can be constructed
4. **Naturalism** — the trickiest concept; students need exposure to multiple conlangs before they can evaluate "natural feel"

### Common Sticking Points
- **Conflating a posteriori/a priori with auxlang/artlang** — these are orthogonal dimensions (you can have a posteriori artlangs like Quenya)
- **Assuming regularity = simplicity** — Lojban is extremely regular but very hard to learn
- **Missing the purpose-design connection** — a "good" language is one that serves its purpose well, not one that maximizes any single metric

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic linguistics** — needed for phonetics, morphology, syntax terminology. Without this, students will struggle with concepts 7-16.
- **Understanding of language variation** — helps grasp why conlangs make different design choices. Needed for Module 2 onward.
- **At least one Indo-European language** — helps when analyzing Esperanto, Interlingua, and recognizing Romance/Germanic roots. Not strictly necessary but very helpful.
- **Familiarity with linguistic typology** — useful for understanding typological patterns and naturalism (concepts 37, 35)

## Learning Sequence Rationale

The curriculum follows this arc:

1. **Motivation and Classification** (Lessons 1-4) — establish why conlangs exist and how to categorize them
2. **Linguistic Foundations** (Lessons 5-9) — build technical vocabulary and design concepts that apply to ALL conlangs
3. **A Posteriori Case Studies** (Lessons 10-13) — analyze European-based auxlangs, starting with most successful (Esperanto)
4. **A Priori Case Studies** (Lessons 14-18) — examine radical departures from natural language patterns
5. **Artistic Languages** (Lessons 19-22) — explore aesthetic and worldbuilding considerations
6. **Synthesis and Creation** (Lessons 23-25) — apply all concepts to evaluate trade-offs and design a basic conlang

This order works because:
- Students see concrete examples (Esperanto, Toki Pona) soon after learning theory
- Progression moves from familiar (European auxlangs) to exotic (Lojban, Ithkuil) to creative (Tolkien, Peterson)
- Review lessons space out difficult concepts
- Final module requires synthesis of all previous learning
