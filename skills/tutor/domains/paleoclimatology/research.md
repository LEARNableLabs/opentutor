# Paleoclimatology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Climate Proxies & Reconstruction Methods
- **Ice cores** — trapped air bubbles, isotope ratios (δ18O, δD), dust particles
- **Tree rings** — dendroclimatology, growth patterns, temperature/precipitation signals
- **Sediment cores** — ocean and lake sediments, foram assemblages, varve counting
- **Corals** — growth bands, isotope ratios, sea surface temperature
- **Speleothems** — cave formations, stalagmites/stalactites, isotope records
- **Pollen analysis** — vegetation changes, temperature/moisture inference
- **Loess deposits** — wind-blown sediments, glacial/interglacial indicators

### 2. Dating Techniques
- **Radiometric dating** — radiocarbon (14C), uranium-thorium, argon-argon
- **Relative dating** — stratigraphy, biostratigraphy, magnetic reversals
- **Varve counting** — annual sediment layers
- **Tephrochronology** — volcanic ash layers as time markers
- **Astronomical tuning** — Milankovitch cycle matching

### 3. Major Climate Events & Periods
- **Quaternary glacial/interglacial cycles** — last 2.6 Ma, ~100 ka cycles
- **Last Glacial Maximum (LGM)** — ~21 ka, peak ice extent
- **Holocene** — current interglacial, last 11.7 ka
- **Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM)** — ~56 Ma, rapid warming event
- **Snowball Earth** — ~720-635 Ma, global glaciation hypothesis
- **Cretaceous hothouse** — high CO2, warm poles, no ice sheets
- **Pliocene** — 5.3-2.6 Ma, warm period with ice sheets

### 4. Climate Forcing Mechanisms
- **Milankovitch cycles** — orbital variations (eccentricity, obliquity, precession)
- **Greenhouse gases** — CO2, CH4 variations through time
- **Solar variability** — sunspot cycles, solar irradiance changes
- **Volcanic activity** — aerosols, cooling effects
- **Plate tectonics** — continental positions, ocean circulation changes
- **Biological feedbacks** — carbon cycle, vegetation changes

### 5. Paleoclimate Modeling
- **General Circulation Models (GCMs)** — testing hypotheses
- **Energy Balance Models** — simpler climate representations
- **Data-model comparison** — validating reconstructions
- **Inverse problems** — inferring forcing from climate response

### 6. Modern Applications
- **Climate sensitivity** — learning from past warming events
- **Carbon cycle understanding** — ice age CO2 changes
- **Sea level history** — ice sheet dynamics, coastal planning
- **Abrupt climate change** — tipping points, Younger Dryas
- **Anthropocene context** — current changes vs. natural variability

## Teaching Approach

Paleoclimatology is inherently interdisciplinary, combining:
- **Geology** — sedimentology, stratigraphy
- **Chemistry** — isotope geochemistry
- **Physics** — orbital mechanics, radiative transfer
- **Biology** — ecological responses, biomarkers
- **Mathematics** — time series analysis, uncertainty quantification

At the intermediate level, students should develop:
1. **Proxy literacy** — understand what different archives tell us and their limitations
2. **Time scale awareness** — grasp vastly different temporal resolutions
3. **Uncertainty reasoning** — work with imperfect, noisy records
4. **Mechanistic thinking** — connect forcing to response
5. **Modern relevance** — apply past climate changes to current warming
