# Nuclear Physics and Nuclear Engineering — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Strong force** — fundamental force binding nucleons together
2. **Binding energy** — energy required to disassemble nucleus into constituent nucleons
3. **Mass defect** — difference between nucleus mass and sum of nucleon masses; source of binding energy
4. **Liquid drop model** — treats nucleus as charged liquid drop with volume, surface, Coulomb, asymmetry, and pairing terms
5. **Semi-empirical mass formula (SEMF)** — quantitative liquid drop prediction of binding energy. Depends on: binding energy, mass defect
6. **Magic numbers** — special nucleon numbers (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) corresponding to filled nuclear shells
7. **Shell model** — quantum mechanical model of nucleus with discrete energy levels. Depends on: strong force, magic numbers
8. **Chart of nuclides** — 2D map of all isotopes showing stability regions. Depends on: binding energy, liquid drop model
9. **Valley of stability** — region in chart of nuclides where stable nuclei exist. Depends on: chart of nuclides, neutron-proton ratio
10. **Alpha decay** — emission of helium-4 nucleus; occurs in heavy nuclei
11. **Beta decay** — neutron→proton+electron (β⁻) or proton→neutron+positron (β⁺); changes nuclear charge
12. **Gamma emission** — photon emission from excited nuclear state; no change in composition
13. **Half-life** — time for half of radioactive sample to decay. Depends on: decay modes
14. **Decay constant** — probability per unit time of nuclear decay. Depends on: half-life
15. **Activity** — number of decays per unit time. Depends on: decay constant, half-life
16. **Decay chains** — sequence of decays from parent to stable daughter. Depends on: decay modes, half-life
17. **Neutron interactions** — elastic/inelastic scattering, capture, fission
18. **Elastic scattering** — neutron bounces off nucleus without energy loss to internal excitation
19. **Inelastic scattering** — neutron excites nucleus, loses kinetic energy. Depends on: elastic scattering
20. **Neutron capture** — nucleus absorbs neutron, often followed by gamma emission
21. **Q-value** — energy released or required in nuclear reaction. Depends on: binding energy, mass defect
22. **Cross-section** — effective target area for nuclear reaction; measures reaction probability. Depends on: neutron interactions
23. **Resonances** — energy-specific peaks in cross-section caused by compound nucleus states. Depends on: cross-section
24. **Nuclear fission** — heavy nucleus splits into two fragments plus neutrons and energy. Depends on: Q-value, binding energy
25. **Fission fragments** — two medium-mass nuclei produced in fission; highly radioactive
26. **Prompt neutrons** — neutrons emitted immediately (<10⁻¹⁴ s) during fission. Depends on: nuclear fission
27. **Delayed neutrons** — neutrons emitted by fission fragments after beta decay (0.1-60s). Depends on: fission fragments, beta decay
28. **Nuclear fusion** — light nuclei combine to form heavier nucleus plus energy. Depends on: Q-value, binding energy
29. **Coulomb barrier** — electrostatic repulsion preventing fusion at low energies. Depends on: nuclear fusion
30. **Chain reaction** — self-sustaining fission sequence where neutrons from one fission cause additional fissions. Depends on: nuclear fission, prompt neutrons
31. **Multiplication factor (k)** — ratio of neutrons in successive generations. Depends on: chain reaction
32. **Criticality** — state where k=1 and chain reaction is self-sustaining. Depends on: multiplication factor
33. **Neutron moderation** — slowing down fast fission neutrons to thermal energies. Depends on: elastic scattering, cross-section
34. **Moderators** — light nuclei (H₂O, D₂O, graphite) that slow neutrons efficiently. Depends on: neutron moderation
35. **Thermal neutrons** — neutrons in thermal equilibrium with moderator (~0.025 eV). Depends on: neutron moderation
36. **Six-factor formula** — detailed criticality calculation including reproduction, absorption, leakage. Depends on: multiplication factor, criticality
37. **Reactivity** — fractional deviation from criticality (ρ = (k-1)/k). Depends on: multiplication factor
38. **Control rods** — neutron-absorbing materials (B, Cd, Hf) used to regulate reactivity. Depends on: reactivity, neutron capture
39. **Reactor kinetics** — time-dependent behavior of reactor power. Depends on: reactivity, delayed neutrons
40. **Prompt critical** — dangerous state where chain reaction sustains on prompt neutrons alone. Depends on: prompt neutrons, delayed neutrons, reactor kinetics
41. **Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR)** — most common reactor type using pressurized water as moderator and coolant
42. **Boiling Water Reactor (BWR)** — reactor where coolant boils in core to drive turbines directly
43. **CANDU** — Canadian heavy water reactor using natural uranium fuel. Depends on: moderators
44. **Fast reactors** — reactors operating without moderator, using fast neutrons. Depends on: neutron moderation
45. **Defense in depth** — layered safety approach with multiple independent barriers. Depends on: reactor safety
46. **Emergency core cooling** — systems to prevent core meltdown in accident scenarios. Depends on: defense in depth
47. **Containment** — final physical barrier preventing radioactive release. Depends on: defense in depth
48. **Dose** — energy deposited per unit mass by ionizing radiation
49. **Rem and sievert** — dose equivalent units accounting for biological effectiveness. Depends on: dose
50. **ALARA** — "As Low As Reasonably Achievable" radiation protection principle. Depends on: dose, rem and sievert
51. **Medical isotopes** — radioactive isotopes used for imaging and therapy (Tc-99m, I-131, etc.)
52. **PET imaging** — positron emission tomography using β⁺ emitters. Depends on: beta decay, medical isotopes
53. **Radiotherapy** — cancer treatment using ionizing radiation. Depends on: dose, medical isotopes
54. **Uranium enrichment** — increasing U-235 concentration from 0.7% natural to 3-5% reactor fuel
55. **Fuel fabrication** — manufacturing fuel pellets and assemblies. Depends on: uranium enrichment
56. **Reprocessing** — chemical separation of usable materials from spent fuel. Depends on: nuclear fuel cycle
57. **Nuclear waste disposal** — long-term storage of radioactive materials. Depends on: half-life, decay chains
58. **Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)** — factory-built reactors <300 MWe with enhanced safety features. Depends on: reactor design, passive safety

## Dependencies

### Foundation Layer (Lessons 1-4)
- **Shell model** requires understanding of strong force, binding energy, and magic numbers to explain why certain configurations are stable
- **Liquid drop model** builds on binding energy and mass defect to create a classical analog of nuclear behavior
- **Chart of nuclides** visualizes patterns predicted by both shell and liquid drop models

### Decay Layer (Lessons 5-7)
- **Decay modes** (alpha, beta, gamma) determine movement paths on chart of nuclides toward valley of stability
- **Half-life and activity** calculations depend on understanding exponential decay and the decay constant
- **Decay chains** require tracking multiple sequential decays, each with its own half-life

### Reaction Layer (Lessons 8-13)
- **Q-value calculations** use binding energy differences to predict energy release/absorption
- **Cross-sections** quantify reaction probabilities and depend on understanding neutron-nucleus interactions
- **Fission** requires understanding Q-values, neutron interactions, and prompt/delayed neutron emission
- **Fusion** requires overcoming Coulomb barrier and understanding Q-values for light nuclei

### Reactor Physics Layer (Lessons 14-19)
- **Criticality** depends on understanding chain reactions, multiplication factor, and neutron economy
- **Neutron moderation** uses elastic scattering physics to thermalize neutrons for higher fission cross-sections
- **Six-factor formula** combines fission, absorption, moderation, and leakage into single criticality criterion
- **Reactor kinetics** critically depends on delayed neutrons to provide controllable time constants
- **Prompt critical** state is dangerous precisely because delayed neutrons no longer govern reactor period

### Engineering Layer (Lessons 20-23)
- **Reactor types** (PWR, BWR, CANDU, fast) represent different optimization trade-offs of moderation, enrichment, and cooling
- **Safety systems** implement defense in depth using emergency cooling, containment, and passive features
- **Radiation protection** applies dose calculations and ALARA principles to worker and public safety

### Applications Layer (Lessons 24-27)
- **Medical applications** use specific isotopes and decay modes (β⁺ for PET, γ for imaging, β⁻ for therapy)
- **Fuel cycle** integrates enrichment, fabrication, reactor operation, and waste management
- **Advanced reactors** (SMRs) build on lessons from existing designs plus passive safety innovations

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Neutron Physics
Understanding neutron interactions (scattering, capture, fission) and energy-dependent cross-sections is essential for:
- Moderation and thermalization
- Criticality calculations
- Reactor control
- Shielding design

Without solid grasp of neutron physics, reactor concepts become a black box of empirical rules.

### Delayed Neutrons
This single concept explains why nuclear reactors are controllable. Students often:
- Underestimate their importance (only ~0.7% of neutrons)
- Don't connect them to fission fragment beta decay
- Miss that they set the reactor time constant (~seconds instead of ~microseconds)

### Binding Energy Curve
The shape of binding energy per nucleon vs. mass number explains:
- Why fission of heavy nuclei releases energy
- Why fusion of light nuclei releases energy
- Why iron-56 is the most stable nucleus
- Limits of stellar nucleosynthesis

This is the conceptual foundation for all nuclear energy applications.

### Reactivity vs. Multiplication Factor
Students confuse k and ρ, especially the non-linear relationship ρ = (k-1)/k. This matters because:
- Control rod worth is measured in reactivity
- Safety margins are expressed in dollars of reactivity
- Reactor kinetics equations use reactivity, not k

## Common Misconceptions

Addressed throughout curriculum in teaching-notes.md:
- "Radiation = radioactive contamination" (dose vs. material)
- "All reactors can explode like bombs" (criticality vs. weapon-grade geometry)
- "Nuclear waste glows green" (most activity is beta/gamma, not Cherenkov)
- "Spent fuel is 'waste'" (95% U-238, 1% U-235, 1% Pu, 3% fission products)
- "Neutrons slow down gradually" (logarithmic energy loss per collision)
- "Control rods stop the reaction" (they reduce k below 1, decay heat continues)
- "Passive safety means no active systems" (passive systems still need triggers/actuators)

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Calculus** — needed for decay equations (exponential), Q-value calculations, neutron transport
- **Classical mechanics** — needed for reaction kinematics, scattering, conservation laws
- **Electromagnetism** — needed for Coulomb barrier, beta decay, detector physics
- **Quantum mechanics (basic)** — helpful for shell model, tunneling (alpha decay), wave-particle duality
- **Statistical mechanics** — needed for neutron thermalization, Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
- **Chemistry** — needed for fuel cycle, reprocessing, chemical forms of isotopes
- **Probability** — needed for decay statistics, cross-sections, stochastic processes
