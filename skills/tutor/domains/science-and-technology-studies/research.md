# Science and Technology Studies (STS) — Research Summary

## Field Overview

Science and Technology Studies (STS) is an interdisciplinary field examining how social, political, and cultural values affect scientific research and technological innovation, and conversely how science and technology shape society. The field emerged in the 1970s-80s from sociology of science, history of technology, and philosophy of science.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Social Construction of Technology (SCOT)
- How social groups shape technological design and adoption
- Interpretive flexibility and closure
- Technological frames and relevant social groups
- Key scholars: Wiebe Bijker, Trevor Pinch

### 2. Actor-Network Theory (ANT)
- Treating humans and non-humans symmetrically
- Translation, enrollment, and black-boxing
- Laboratories as sites of knowledge production
- Key scholars: Bruno Latour, Michel Callon, John Law

### 3. Laboratory Studies & Ethnography
- Constructivist view of scientific knowledge
- Observation of scientific practice
- Credibility, inscription devices, and evidence-making
- Key work: Latour & Woolgar's "Laboratory Life"

### 4. Technological Determinism vs. Social Shaping
- Does technology drive social change or vice versa?
- Langdon Winner's "artifacts have politics"
- Co-production of technology and society

### 5. Feminist STS
- Gender and science
- Situated knowledge and standpoint theory
- Donna Haraway's cyborg theory
- Intersectionality in technoscience

### 6. Postcolonial STS
- Science and empire
- Indigenous knowledge systems
- Technology transfer and development
- Decolonizing technoscience

### 7. Environmental STS
- Controversy studies (climate change, GMOs)
- Risk assessment and expertise
- Citizen science and public participation
- Sheila Jasanoff's work on regulatory science

### 8. STS Methods
- Controversy mapping
- Ethnographic methods
- Historical case studies
- Quantitative approaches (citation analysis, co-word analysis)

## Research Notes

STS is highly interdisciplinary, drawing from sociology, history, philosophy, anthropology, political science, and communication studies. At the intermediate level, students benefit from:
1. Mix of theoretical frameworks and empirical case studies
2. Engagement with primary sources (scholarly articles)
3. Practice analyzing contemporary controversies
4. Understanding methodological approaches

The field is particularly relevant for understanding contemporary issues: AI ethics, climate change debates, pandemic response, platform governance, and biotechnology regulation.
