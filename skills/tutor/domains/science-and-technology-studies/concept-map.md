# Science and Technology Studies (STS) — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Social constructivism** — knowledge is shaped by social context, not just discovered
2. **Technological determinism** — the belief that technology drives social change
3. **Social shaping of technology** — society shapes how technologies develop and are used
4. **Co-production** — science/technology and society mutually constitute each other. Depends on: social constructivism, social shaping
5. **Relevant social groups** — groups that share interpretations of a technology (SCOT framework). Depends on: social shaping
6. **Interpretive flexibility** — technologies can be understood in multiple ways by different groups. Depends on: relevant social groups
7. **Closure and stabilization** — how technological debates settle and designs stabilize. Depends on: interpretive flexibility, relevant social groups
8. **Embedded values** — technologies embody political and social values in their design. Depends on: social shaping, co-production
9. **Actor-Network Theory (ANT)** — framework treating humans and non-humans as actants in networks
10. **Symmetry principle** — treating human and non-human actors with the same analytical approach. Depends on: ANT
11. **Translation** — how actors enroll others into their networks. Depends on: ANT
12. **Inscription devices** — instruments that transform matter into written documents. Depends on: ANT
13. **Black-boxing** — process by which complex assemblies become taken-for-granted. Depends on: stabilization, ANT
14. **Laboratory studies** — ethnographic study of scientific practice in labs. Depends on: social constructivism
15. **Credibility** — how scientists establish trustworthiness of claims. Depends on: laboratory studies, inscription devices
16. **Technological systems** — large-scale interconnected technical and social components. Depends on: co-production, black-boxing
17. **Path dependence** — early choices constrain future options in technological development. Depends on: technological systems
18. **Technological momentum** — large systems resist change over time. Depends on: path dependence, technological systems
19. **Situated knowledge** — knowledge is partial and located in specific contexts. Depends on: social constructivism
20. **Standpoint theory** — marginalized positions can produce better knowledge. Depends on: situated knowledge
21. **Cyborg theory** — humans and technology are inseparably hybrid. Depends on: co-production, ANT
22. **Postcolonial STS** — examining how colonialism shaped science and technology. Depends on: situated knowledge, embedded values
23. **Epistemic justice** — fairness in knowledge production and recognition. Depends on: postcolonial STS, standpoint theory
24. **Expertise** — specialized knowledge and its social authority. Depends on: credibility, social constructivism
25. **Lay expertise** — non-certified knowledge that can rival professional expertise. Depends on: expertise, situated knowledge
26. **Citizen science** — public participation in knowledge production. Depends on: lay expertise, epistemic justice
27. **Regulatory science** — science used for policy and governance. Depends on: expertise, co-production
28. **Controversy studies** — analysis of scientific and technological disputes. Depends on: SCOT, ANT, expertise
29. **Algorithmic bias** — embedded discrimination in computational systems. Depends on: embedded values, black-boxing
30. **Data politics** — power relations in data collection and use. Depends on: co-production, epistemic justice

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies
- **SCOT framework** requires understanding interpretive flexibility, relevant social groups, and closure mechanisms
- **ANT** builds on rejecting human/non-human distinctions and understanding translation processes
- Both SCOT and ANT depend on rejecting pure technological determinism

### Framework Integration
- **Black-boxing** connects ANT's translation process with technological systems' momentum
- **Co-production** unifies multiple frameworks by showing mutual shaping
- **Situated knowledge** provides epistemological foundation for feminist and postcolonial critiques

### Critical Perspectives
- **Feminist STS** builds on social constructivism but emphasizes gender and power
- **Postcolonial STS** extends situated knowledge to colonial contexts
- Both challenge who gets to be an "expert" and what counts as valid knowledge

### Contemporary Applications
- **Algorithmic bias** applies embedded values concept to AI/ML systems
- **Controversy mapping** operationalizes SCOT and ANT for contemporary debates
- **Regulatory science** shows co-production in policy contexts

## Bottleneck Concepts

These concepts are prerequisites for multiple downstream topics:

1. **Social constructivism** — unlocks all major frameworks (SCOT, ANT, feminist STS)
2. **Co-production** — essential for understanding contemporary issues and regulatory science
3. **ANT basics** — required for laboratory studies, black-boxing, and cyborg theory
4. **Expertise** — necessary for understanding public participation, controversy, and regulatory science

## Common Misconceptions

Students often struggle with:

1. **Constructivism ≠ relativism** — saying science is socially constructed doesn't mean it's "not real" or arbitrary
2. **ANT's symmetry** — non-human "actors" doesn't mean scallops have intentions; it's a methodological stance
3. **Determinism spectrum** — most STS scholars reject both pure technological and pure social determinism
4. **SCOT vs. ANT** — these are complementary frameworks with different emphases, not competing theories
5. **Expertise critique** — STS doesn't dismiss expert knowledge, but examines how expertise is constructed and deployed

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic sociology** — needed for understanding social groups, power, and institutions (all core concepts)
- **History of science** — needed for understanding laboratory studies, credibility, and historical cases
- **Critical thinking** — needed for analyzing arguments, identifying assumptions, and evaluating frameworks
- **Ethics** — helpful for embedded values, algorithmic bias, and epistemic justice

## Advanced Connections (rabbit holes)

- **Philosophy of science** — Kuhn's paradigms, Popper's falsification, Lakatos's research programs
- **Media studies** — platform governance, digital methods, data visualization
- **Political economy** — innovation systems, intellectual property, science policy
- **Anthropology** — material culture, multispecies ethnography, ontological politics
- **Environmental humanities** — Anthropocene, more-than-human, climate politics
