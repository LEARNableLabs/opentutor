# Architecture — Space, Form, and Meaning: Research Summary

## Topic Overview

This curriculum explores architecture as the deliberate creation of spatial experiences that carry meaning. At the intermediate level, students move beyond basic design principles to understand how architects manipulate space, develop formal strategies, and embed cultural and symbolic meaning into built environments.

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Spatial Concepts
- **Enclosure and openness** — how boundaries define space
- **Sequence and procession** — movement through architectural space
- **Scale and proportion** — relationship between human body and built form
- **Light and shadow** — temporal qualities of space
- **Threshold and transition** — boundaries between spaces

### 2. Formal Systems
- **Geometric order** — grids, axes, symmetry, and organizing principles
- **Massing and composition** — three-dimensional form-making
- **Structure and expression** — how buildings stand and what they communicate
- **Tectonics** — material assembly and its expressive potential
- **Typology** — architectural forms as cultural patterns

### 3. Meaning and Symbolism
- **Cultural context** — architecture as social and historical expression
- **Phenomenology** — embodied experience of space
- **Semiotics** — buildings as sign systems
- **Memory and narrative** — how architecture tells stories
- **Place-making** — architecture's relationship to site and community

### 4. Historical Examples and Case Studies
- Classical temples and sacred architecture
- Modernist experiments in space and form
- Contemporary explorations of materiality and experience
- Vernacular traditions and cultural meaning

## Learning Approach

At the intermediate level, students are expected to:
- Analyze existing buildings for spatial, formal, and symbolic strategies
- Develop visual literacy through drawing and diagramming
- Connect theoretical concepts to lived experience
- Understand architecture across multiple scales (detail to urban)
- Engage critically with architectural meaning and cultural context

The curriculum balances conceptual frameworks with specific examples, emphasizing both analytical thinking and experiential awareness.
