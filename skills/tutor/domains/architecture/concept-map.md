# Architecture — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Enclosure** — how boundaries define spatial experience. The most fundamental act of architecture.
2. **Natural light** — illumination as a design medium that shapes perception and mood. Depends on: Enclosure
3. **Threshold** — transitional zones between spaces; the boundary that separates and connects. Depends on: Enclosure
4. **Procession** — movement through a sequence of spaces over time. Depends on: Threshold
5. **Spatial sequence** — the ordered composition of multiple spaces experienced in time. Depends on: Procession
6. **Geometric order** — organizing systems (axis, grid, symmetry) that bring clarity to composition
7. **Massing** — three-dimensional formal composition of solid volumes. Depends on: Geometric order
8. **Scale** — the relationship between built form and human measure. Depends on: Massing
9. **Typology** — recurring formal patterns that carry cultural and functional associations. Depends on: Geometric order, Massing
10. **Structure** — the system that keeps a building standing
11. **Tectonics** — structure as expressive language; how materials are assembled. Depends on: Structure
12. **Cultural context** — the social, historical, and symbolic frameworks that give architecture meaning
13. **Place** — the specific qualities of a site and its cultural identity (genius loci). Depends on: Cultural context
14. **Semiotics** — architecture as a system of signs and symbols that communicate meaning. Depends on: Cultural context
15. **Sacred architecture** — buildings designed for ritual and spiritual experience. Depends on: Semiotics, Spatial sequence
16. **Memory** — how architecture evokes and preserves collective and individual narratives. Depends on: Cultural context, Place
17. **Vernacular** — local building traditions that respond to climate, materials, and culture. Depends on: Cultural context, Place, Tectonics
18. **Materiality** — the sensory and expressive qualities of building materials. Depends on: Tectonics
19. **Phenomenology** — the embodied, multi-sensory experience of architecture. Depends on: Materiality, Natural light, Spatial sequence
20. **Atmosphere** — the intangible mood or quality of a space. Depends on: Phenomenology, Natural light, Materiality

## Dependencies

### Foundational Layer: Space
- **Threshold** requires **Enclosure** because you can't have a transition without defined spaces to transition between.
- **Procession** requires **Threshold** because movement through architecture is about crossing thresholds in sequence.
- **Spatial sequence** builds on **Procession** by adding compositional intention to the journey.

### Formal Layer: Geometry and Composition
- **Massing** requires **Geometric order** because three-dimensional composition depends on organizing principles.
- **Scale** builds on **Massing** because proportion only has meaning in relation to a composed form.
- **Typology** synthesizes **Geometric order** and **Massing** because architectural types are formal patterns with cultural associations.
- **Tectonics** extends **Structure** by adding expressive and material dimensions to structural logic.

### Meaning Layer: Culture and Symbolism
- **Place** requires **Cultural context** because the meaning of a site is culturally constructed.
- **Semiotics** depends on **Cultural context** because signs only function within shared cultural frameworks.
- **Sacred architecture** combines **Semiotics** and **Spatial sequence** because ritual spaces use symbolic form and choreographed movement.
- **Memory** builds on **Cultural context** and **Place** because architectural narratives are tied to specific sites and communities.
- **Vernacular** synthesizes **Cultural context**, **Place**, and **Tectonics** as locally-specific building wisdom.

### Experiential Layer: Materials and Phenomenology
- **Materiality** extends **Tectonics** by emphasizing sensory and expressive qualities beyond structural assembly.
- **Phenomenology** integrates **Materiality**, **Natural light**, and **Spatial sequence** because embodied experience arises from the interaction of material, light, and movement.
- **Atmosphere** is the emergent quality that depends on **Phenomenology**, **Natural light**, and **Materiality** working together.

## Critical Bottlenecks

### 1. Threshold (Lesson 3)
Understanding threshold is essential for grasping spatial sequence, procession, and later concepts of place and meaning. Students who struggle with threshold often see buildings as collections of rooms rather than orchestrated experiences.

### 2. Geometric Order (Lesson 7)
Organizing principles are the foundation for formal analysis. Without this, students can't decode how architects compose in three dimensions or understand typological patterns.

### 3. Cultural Context (Lesson 14)
This unlocks the entire meaning layer. Students who don't grasp that architecture is culturally constructed struggle with semiotics, place, and memory.

### 4. Phenomenology (Lesson 22)
The synthesis concept that connects material, spatial, and experiential dimensions. This is where intermediate students integrate everything they've learned into a holistic understanding.

## Common Misconceptions

### "Architecture is just about aesthetics"
Students often focus on visual appearance without understanding that architecture is a spatial, experiential, and cultural medium. **Address by:** Emphasizing spatial experience and meaning from Lesson 1 onward.

### "Form follows function" is the only rule
Many students oversimplify modernist principles. **Address by:** Showing how architecture balances functional, formal, symbolic, and experiential concerns (especially in Lessons 14-20).

### "Materials are just structural"
Students may see materials as purely utilitarian. **Address by:** Introducing sensory and expressive dimensions of materiality (Lessons 21-23).

### "Sacred architecture is only religious"
Students may limit sacred architecture to churches/temples without seeing how architecture creates meaning more broadly. **Address by:** Connecting ritual, symbolism, and spatial sequence (Lesson 17).

### "Vernacular is primitive or unsophisticated"
Students may undervalue local building traditions. **Address by:** Emphasizing vernacular as sophisticated response to climate, culture, and materials (Lesson 19).

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic design principles** — needed for understanding composition, balance, hierarchy (Lessons 7-12)
- **Visual literacy (reading drawings)** — needed for analyzing plans, sections, diagrams throughout
- **Art history fundamentals** — needed for understanding cultural context and historical examples (Lessons 14-20)

## Integration Points

- **Lessons 6, 13, 20, 25** are review/synthesis moments that integrate concepts from the previous module
- **Teach-back lessons (5, 12, 24)** ask students to apply multiple concepts to real buildings
- **Real-world lessons (2, 9, 17)** connect theory to lived experience
- **Resource-drop lessons (10, 18, 22)** introduce more advanced frameworks that students can explore at their own depth
