# Architecture — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Textbooks
- **Francis D.K. Ching: *Architecture: Form, Space, and Order*** — visual encyclopedia of spatial and formal principles. Essential reference for intermediate students. Clear diagrams and examples across history. https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Architecture%3A+Form%2C+Space%2C+and+Order%2C+4th+Edition-p-9781118745083
- **Steen Eiler Rasmussen: *Experiencing Architecture*** — classic text on perceptual and experiential dimensions. Accessible, humane writing. Ideal for phenomenology lessons.
- **Bruno Zevi: *Architecture as Space*** — focuses on spatial analysis as the core of architectural thinking. Complements Ching's formal approach.
- **Christian Norberg-Schulz: *Genius Loci: Towards a Phenomenology of Architecture*** — essential for understanding place and meaning. More theoretical but foundational for Lessons 14-20.
- **Juhani Pallasmaa: *The Eyes of the Skin: Architecture and the Senses*** — phenomenology made accessible. Perfect for Lessons 21-23. https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Eyes+of+the+Skin%3A+Architecture+and+the+Senses%2C+3rd+Edition-p-9781119941286

### Academic Courses (Open Educational Resources)
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 4.401 Environmental Design** — foundational course on spatial design. Includes lectures, assignments, and student work. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-401-environmental-design-fall-2017/
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 4.205 Analysis of Contemporary Architecture** — theory and criticism. Good for cultural context and meaning modules. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-205-analysis-of-contemporary-architecture-fall-2009/
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: 4.663 Architecture Studios: Building in Landscapes** — case studies in site and place. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/4-663-architecture-studios-building-in-landscapes-spring-2016/
- **Khan Academy: Art History** — includes strong architectural coverage across cultures and periods. Accessible videos and readings. https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/art-history

### Online Lectures and Talks

### Key Buildings to Study (by concept)

#### Spatial Sequence and Procession
- **Acropolis, Athens** — choreographed approach and spatial revelation
- **Alhambra, Granada** — sequence through courtyards, light, water
- **Kimbell Art Museum (Louis Kahn)** — gallery sequence and natural light
- **Barcelona Pavilion (Mies van der Rohe)** — flowing space and threshold

#### Light and Atmosphere
- **Pantheon, Rome** — oculus and changing daylight
- **Ronchamp (Le Corbusier)** — colored light and sculptural form
- **Therme Vals (Peter Zumthor)** — stone, water, and controlled illumination
- **Teshima Art Museum (Ryue Nishizawa)** — minimal enclosure and sky

#### Form and Geometry
- **Villa Rotonda (Palladio)** — pure geometric order
- **Villa Savoye (Le Corbusier)** — five points of modern architecture
- **Guggenheim Bilbao (Frank Gehry)** — complex curvilinear form
- **British Museum Great Court (Foster + Partners)** — parametric geometry

#### Structure and Tectonics
- **Gothic Cathedrals** — stone vaulting and flying buttresses
- **Farnsworth House (Mies van der Rohe)** — steel frame as expression
- **Sydney Opera House (Jørn Utzon)** — structure as sculptural form
- **Pompidou Center (Piano + Rogers)** — exposed systems as language

#### Place and Cultural Meaning
- **Machu Picchu** — landscape integration and cosmic alignment
- **Fallingwater (Frank Lloyd Wright)** — building and site as unity
- **Salk Institute (Louis Kahn)** — courtyard open to Pacific Ocean
- **Jewish Museum Berlin (Daniel Libeskind)** — memory and trauma in form

#### Phenomenology and Material
- **Bruder Klaus Chapel (Peter Zumthor)** — rammed concrete and charred interior
- **Therme Vals (Peter Zumthor)** — layered stone and water
- **Church of Light (Tadao Ando)** — concrete, light, and contemplation
- **Castelvecchio Museum (Carlo Scarpa)** — material detail and layered history

## People (Architects and Theorists to Explore)

### Historical Figures
- **Vitruvius** — ancient Roman architect and theorist; foundational text *De architectura*
- **Leon Battista Alberti** — Renaissance theorist and practitioner
- **Andrea Palladio** — geometric order and proportion; *The Four Books of Architecture*
- **Le Corbusier** — modernist pioneer; wrote extensively on space, form, and urbanism

### Contemporary Practitioners (Phenomenological/Material Focus)
- **Peter Zumthor** — atmosphere, material, sensory experience
- **Tadao Ando** — concrete, light, minimalism, spiritual space
- **Glenn Murcutt** — sustainable design, site-specific architecture
- **Álvaro Siza** — subtle material and spatial poetry

### Theorists and Critics
- **Gaston Bachelard** — *The Poetics of Space*; phenomenology of intimate spaces
- **Martin Heidegger** — philosopher; "Building Dwelling Thinking" on the meaning of place
- **Juhani Pallasmaa** — sensory experience and embodiment
- **Christian Norberg-Schulz** — genius loci and phenomenology
- **Kenneth Frampton** — critical regionalism and tectonics
- **Beatriz Colomina** — architecture and media theory

### Radical/Visionary Architects
- **Archigram** — 1960s speculative megastructures and plug-in cities
- **Superstudio** — critical architecture and anti-design
- **Lebbeus Woods** — visionary drawings and war-torn architecture
- **Cedric Price** — conceptual architecture and indeterminacy

## Unexpected Connections (Wild Cards)

### Architecture and Literature
- **Italo Calvino: *Invisible Cities*** — poetic descriptions of imaginary urban spaces
- **Georges Perec: *Species of Spaces*** — essayistic exploration of spatial experience
- **Gaston Bachelard: *The Poetics of Space*** — phenomenology of intimate domestic spaces

### Architecture and Film
- **Sergei Eisenstein** — film montage theory influenced architectural sequence thinking
- **Alfred Hitchcock** — spatial suspense and control (e.g., *Rear Window*)
- **Andrei Tarkovsky** — atmospheric space and duration (e.g., *Stalker*, *The Sacrifice*)
- **Documentary: *My Architect*** — Louis Kahn's life and work. https://www.myarchitectfilm.com/

### Architecture and Philosophy
- **Michel Foucault: *Discipline and Punish*** — architecture as social control (panopticon)
- **Henri Lefebvre: *The Production of Space*** — social and political construction of space
- **Walter Benjamin: *The Arcades Project*** — urban experience and modernity

### Architecture and Music
- **Iannis Xenakis** — architect and composer; designed Philips Pavilion, composed spatial music
- **John Cage: *4'33"*** — silence and ambient sound; architectural parallels in negative space
- **Relationship:** Both arts unfold in time; rhythm, phrasing, crescendo apply to spatial sequence

### Cross-Cultural Perspectives
- **Japanese *Ma*** — concept of negative space and interval. Foundational to understanding void as designed element.
- **Islamic Geometry** — pattern, tiling, and symbolic mathematics in architectural ornament
- **Indigenous Architecture** — kivas, longhouses, vernacular wisdom embedded in climate-responsive design

## Further Learning Paths

After completing this curriculum, students interested in deeper engagement might explore:
- **Architectural History** — periodized study (ancient, medieval, modern, contemporary)
- **Urban Design** — scale shift from buildings to cities
- **Landscape Architecture** — outdoor space as designed medium
- **Historic Preservation** — conservation, adaptive reuse, cultural heritage
- **Sustainability and Climate** — building performance, passive design, regenerative architecture
- **Digital Fabrication** — CNC, 3D printing, robotic assembly
- **Architectural Theory** — primary texts from Vitruvius to contemporary critical discourse
