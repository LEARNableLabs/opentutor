# Number Theory and Cryptography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **"A Course in Number Theory and Cryptography" by Neal Koblitz** (Springer Graduate Texts in Mathematics #114)
  - Graduate-level standard covering elementary number theory, finite fields, quadratic residues, public key systems, primality and factoring, elliptic curves
  - PDF available: http://almuhammadi.com/sultan/crypto_books/Koblitz.2ndEd.pdf
  - Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Course-Number-Cryptography-Graduate-Mathematics/dp/0387942939
  - Best for: Theoretical foundations, rigorous proofs, elliptic curves

- **"Lecture notes Number Theory and Cryptography" by Matt Kerr** (Washington University in St. Louis)
  - Comprehensive lecture notes, intermediate-friendly
  - PDF: https://www.math.wustl.edu/~matkerr/NTCbook.pdf
  - Best for: Clear exposition, good examples, RSA coverage

- **"An Introduction to Number Theory with Cryptography" by James Kraft and Lawrence Washington** (2nd Edition)
  - Undergraduate textbook integrating cryptography with traditional number theory
  - Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Introduction-Number-Cryptography-Textbooks-Mathematics/dp/1138063479
  - Best for: Accessible proofs, historical context, computational exercises

### Online Courses

- **Coursera: "Number Theory and Cryptography"** (University of California San Diego)
  - Starts with basics of number theory, progresses to cryptographic protocols
  - Teaches RSA encryption/decryption, how to break RSA if applied carelessly
  - URL: https://www.coursera.org/learn/number-theory-cryptography
  - Best for: Structured learning path, autograded exercises

- **Coursera: "Cryptography I"** (Stanford Online, Dan Boneh)
  - Inner workings of cryptographic systems, programming projects
  - Covers symmetric and public key crypto, message integrity, authentication
  - URL: https://www.coursera.org/learn/crypto
  - Best for: Practical cryptography, security analysis, real-world systems

## People (researchers and practitioners to look up)

- **Neal Koblitz** — Co-inventor of elliptic curve cryptography, author of the standard textbook
- **Whitfield Diffie & Martin Hellman** — Invented public key cryptography and Diffie-Hellman key exchange (1976)
- **Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, Leonard Adleman** — Invented RSA algorithm (1978)
- **Dan Boneh** — Stanford professor, leading cryptographer, teaches "Cryptography I" on Coursera
- **Peter Shor** — Discovered quantum algorithm for factoring (Shor's algorithm, 1994)
- **Victor Shoup** — Author of "A Computational Introduction to Number Theory and Algebra" (free online book)
- **Avi Wigderson** — Complexity theory and cryptography, recent Turing Award winner

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards and rabbit holes)

- **Blockchain and cryptocurrency** — Bitcoin uses ECDSA (elliptic curve digital signature algorithm), not RSA, but same discrete log foundation
  - Explore: How does Bitcoin's cryptography differ from RSA?

- **Quantum computing** — Shor's algorithm threatens RSA and discrete log systems
  - Explore: How does a quantum computer factor numbers? (period finding via quantum Fourier transform)

- **Music and modular arithmetic** — 12-tone equal temperament uses mod 12 arithmetic
  - Explore: How do musicians use modular arithmetic without knowing it?

- **Art and number theory** — Prime spirals (Ulam spiral), Fibonacci and golden ratio
  - Explore: Visualizing primes as art

- **Cryptography in history** — Enigma machine (WWII), Navajo code talkers, Caesar cipher
  - Explore: How did pre-computer cryptography work?

- **Zero-knowledge proofs** — Prove you know a secret without revealing it
  - Explore: Interactive proof systems, applications in blockchain privacy (zk-SNARKs)

- **Homomorphic encryption** — Compute on encrypted data without decrypting
  - Explore: The holy grail of cloud security

- **Post-quantum lattice cryptography** — NIST's new standards for quantum-resistant crypto
  - Explore: Learning With Errors (LWE) problem, lattice-based encryption

## Additional Reading

- **"The Code Book" by Simon Singh** — Popular science history of cryptography from ancient Egypt to quantum cryptography
  - Engaging stories: Mary Queen of Scots, Enigma, RSA invention
  - Best for: Historical context, motivation, non-technical background

- **"Cryptography Engineering" by Ferguson, Schneier, Kohno** — Practical cryptographic engineering
  - Best for: Real-world implementation, security pitfalls

- **"Introduction to Modern Cryptography" by Katz and Lindell** — Rigorous theoretical treatment
  - Best for: Advanced students, provable security, formal definitions
