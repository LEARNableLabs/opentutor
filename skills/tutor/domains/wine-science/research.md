# Wine Science Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. **Viticulture (Grape Growing)**
- Vine biology and phenology (growth stages, photosynthesis, water relations)
- Terroir and site selection (soil, climate, topography)
- Vineyard establishment and management (variety selection, rootstocks, trellising)
- Canopy management and pruning (light exposure, crop load, vine balance)
- Pest and disease management (powdery mildew, phylloxera, integrated pest management)
- Harvest timing and maturity assessment (sugar, acid, phenolic ripeness)

### 2. **Enology (Winemaking)**
- Pre-fermentation processing (crushing, destemming, sulfur dioxide use)
- Fermentation science (yeast ecology, alcoholic fermentation kinetics, temperature control)
- Malolactic fermentation (bacteria, timing, flavor impact)
- Extraction techniques (cap management, maceration, color and tannin extraction)
- Wine styles and techniques (red, white, rosé, sparkling, fortified)
- Oak aging and alternatives (barrel types, toasting, oxygen transmission)
- Clarification and stabilization (fining, filtration, cold stabilization)
- Bottling and post-bottling evolution

### 3. **Wine Chemistry**
- Primary compounds (sugars, acids, alcohols, phenolics)
- Secondary metabolites and aroma compounds (thiols, esters, pyrazines)
- Oxidation and reduction reactions
- Wine faults and off-flavors (Brett, TCA, VA, hydrogen sulfide)
- Protein and tartrate stability

### 4. **Sensory Science**
- Sensory evaluation methodology (threshold testing, descriptive analysis)
- Aroma and flavor perception (orthonasal vs retronasal, trigeminal sensations)
- Wine faults identification
- Tasting protocol and vocabulary

### 5. **Sustainability and Industry Trends**
- Climate change impacts (heat stress, water scarcity, shifting growing regions)
- Sustainable and organic viticulture
- Precision viticulture (remote sensing, variable rate application)
- Wine microbiology and minimal intervention

## Key Sources

### Academic Programs
- **UC Davis Department of Viticulture & Enology** — leading research institution, comprehensive curriculum materials
  - https://wineserver.ucdavis.edu/
- **Cornell University Viticulture & Enology** — cool-climate focus, excellent extension resources
  - https://grapesandwine.cals.cornell.edu/
- **Washington State University Viticulture & Enology** — strong research program
  - https://wine.wsu.edu/

### Textbooks and References
- *Wine Science: Principles and Applications* (Ronald S. Jackson) — comprehensive, intermediate-advanced
- *Understanding Wine Chemistry* (Andrew L. Waterhouse et al.) — detailed chemistry focus
- *Viticulture* (Stephen Skelton) — practical viticulture reference
- OIV Standards and Technical Documents — https://www.oiv.int/

### Educational Platforms
- **Wine Folly** — visual learning resources, wine maps, infographics
  - https://winefolly.com/
- **WSET (Wine & Spirit Education Trust)** — structured certification program (Level 3-4 relevant for intermediate)
  - https://www.wsetglobal.com/
- **GuildSomm** — professional education for sommeliers, strong technical content
  - https://www.guildsomm.com/

### Video and Interactive Resources
- UC Davis Mondavi Center lectures and webinars
- Jancis Robinson's wine education content — https://www.jancisrobinson.com/learn
- Wine Scholar Guild courses — https://winescholarguild.org/
- Dr. Andrew Waterhouse's wine chemistry videos

### Research Journals
- *American Journal of Enology and Viticulture* (AJEV)
- *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry* (wine sections)
- *OENO One* — open access enology journal
