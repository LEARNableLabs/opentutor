# Wine Science — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Vine phenology** — growth stages from budbreak to harvest, seasonal carbohydrate cycling
2. **Photosynthesis and sugar accumulation** — how vines convert light energy into grape sugars
3. **Varietal characteristics** — genetic basis for flavor, aroma, and structural differences between grape varieties. Depends on: vine phenology
4. **Terroir** — soil, climate, and topography interaction shaping grape composition. Depends on: photosynthesis, varietal characteristics
5. **Climate classification** — heat accumulation metrics (GDD, Winkler regions) and their viticulture implications. Depends on: terroir
6. **Vine balance and crop load** — ratio of canopy to fruit, impact on ripening and quality. Depends on: photosynthesis, vine phenology
7. **Canopy management** — leaf removal, shoot positioning to optimize light exposure and microclimate. Depends on: vine balance, photosynthesis
8. **Rootstock selection** — phylloxera resistance, vigor control, and site adaptation. Depends on: terroir, vine balance
9. **Ripeness assessment** — sugar, acid, pH, phenolic ripeness as harvest decision criteria. Depends on: vine phenology, terroir, crop load
10. **Integrated pest management** — disease and pest control strategies balancing efficacy and sustainability. Depends on: climate classification, canopy management
11. **Yeast metabolism** — glycolysis and alcoholic fermentation biochemistry. Prerequisites complete
12. **Fermentation kinetics** — temperature, nutrition, oxygen impact on fermentation rate and completion. Depends on: yeast metabolism
13. **Yeast ecology** — native vs. inoculated fermentation, strain selection. Depends on: yeast metabolism, fermentation kinetics
14. **Volatile compound formation** — esters, higher alcohols, sulfur compounds from fermentation. Depends on: fermentation kinetics, yeast ecology
15. **Malolactic fermentation** — bacterial conversion of malic to lactic acid, flavor and stability impacts. Depends on: fermentation kinetics
16. **Stuck fermentation troubleshooting** — causes and remediation strategies. Depends on: fermentation kinetics, yeast metabolism
17. **Phenolic extraction** — color and tannin extraction from skins, seed contributions. Depends on: ripeness assessment
18. **Maceration techniques** — cap management, cold soak, extended maceration. Depends on: phenolic extraction
19. **Oak aging chemistry** — extraction of oak compounds, micro-oxygenation, flavor development. Depends on: phenolic extraction, volatile compounds
20. **Sparkling wine production** — méthode traditionnelle secondary fermentation and riddling. Depends on: fermentation kinetics, yeast metabolism
21. **Clarification methods** — fining agent chemistry, filtration mechanisms. Depends on: phenolic extraction
22. **Wine stabilization** — protein, tartrate, microbial stability approaches. Depends on: clarification methods, malolactic fermentation
23. **Oxidation-reduction reactions** — bottle aging chemistry, redox potential management. Depends on: phenolic extraction, oak aging
24. **Wine faults** — chemical and microbial origins of off-flavors (TCA, Brett, VA, H2S). Depends on: fermentation, oxidation-reduction, malolactic fermentation
25. **Aroma perception** — sensory mechanisms, threshold detection, orthonasal vs. retronasal. Depends on: volatile compounds, wine faults
26. **Climate change impacts** — viticulture adaptation strategies, phenological shifts, regional changes. Depends on: terroir, climate classification, ripeness assessment
27. **Sensory evaluation methodology** — structured tasting protocols, descriptive analysis. Depends on: aroma perception

## Dependencies and Bottlenecks

### Critical Foundation Concepts

These must be solid before progressing:

- **Vine phenology** → Everything in viticulture builds on understanding growth stages
- **Yeast metabolism** → All fermentation concepts require this foundation
- **Phenolic extraction** → Core to understanding red winemaking and aging

### Key Dependency Chains

**Viticulture chain:**
Vine phenology → Photosynthesis → Terroir → Climate classification → Canopy management → Ripeness assessment

**Fermentation chain:**
Yeast metabolism → Fermentation kinetics → (branches to) Volatile compounds + Malolactic fermentation + Stuck fermentation

**Winemaking chain:**
Ripeness → Phenolic extraction → Maceration techniques → Oak aging → Oxidation-reduction

**Sensory chain:**
Volatile compounds + Wine faults → Aroma perception → Sensory evaluation

### Common Bottlenecks

- **Fermentation kinetics (Lesson 12-13)** — Students often struggle with the interplay of temperature, nutrition, and yeast strain. Need concrete examples and practice interpreting fermentation curves.

- **Phenolic extraction (Lesson 17)** — The balance of color, tannin structure, and astringency is nuanced. Requires hands-on tasting or video demonstrations of different extraction levels.

- **Ripeness assessment (Lesson 9)** — Tension between sugar ripeness, acid balance, and phenolic ripeness confuses students. The "perfect harvest" is multidimensional and variety-dependent.

- **Terroir (Lesson 3)** — Concept is often romanticized without mechanistic understanding. Students need to connect soil chemistry/physics and climate to specific grape metabolic pathways.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Organic chemistry basics** — needed for understanding fermentation (lessons 12-16), volatile compounds (14, 19, 23), wine faults (24)
  - Specifically: functional groups (alcohols, acids, esters), redox reactions, esterification
  
- **Plant biology fundamentals** — needed for viticulture concepts (lessons 1-10)
  - Specifically: photosynthesis, plant water relations, phenology, nutrient uptake
  
- **Microbiology basics** — needed for fermentation (lessons 12-16, 24)
  - Specifically: prokaryotes vs. eukaryotes, microbial metabolism, growth curves
  
- **Basic statistics** — helpful for sensory evaluation (lesson 27)
  - Specifically: mean/variance, threshold detection, experimental design

## Concept Progression Notes

The curriculum is structured in three major arcs:

1. **Viticulture (Lessons 1-11)** — Builds from individual vine biology outward to vineyard scale and harvest decisions
2. **Fermentation & Winemaking (Lessons 12-22)** — Progresses through fermentation science, then winemaking techniques, then stabilization
3. **Chemistry & Sensory (Lessons 23-27)** — Integrates prior learning with chemical mechanisms and sensory perception

Review lessons (11, 22) consolidate before transitioning to the next arc. Final lessons (23-27) synthesize across arcs.
