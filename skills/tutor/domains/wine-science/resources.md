# Wine Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Wine Science: Principles and Applications (4th ed.)** by Ronald S. Jackson
  - Comprehensive, intermediate-to-advanced level. Covers viticulture, fermentation, winemaking techniques, chemistry, and sensory science. Dense but authoritative. Best for Lessons 12-16 (fermentation), 23-24 (chemistry and faults).
  
- **Understanding Wine Chemistry** by Andrew L. Waterhouse, Gavin L. Sacks, David W. Jeffery
  - Deep dive into wine chemistry: phenolics, aroma compounds, oxidation, aging. Assumes organic chemistry background. Excellent for Lessons 14, 17, 23, 24.
  
- **Viticulture** by Stephen Skelton MW
  - Practical viticulture reference, especially strong on cool-climate regions. Good for Lessons 1-10.
  
- **The Oxford Companion to Wine (4th ed.)** edited by Jancis Robinson and Julia Harding
  - Encyclopedic reference. Not structured for learning but excellent for deep dives on specific varieties, regions, techniques.

  - Accessible science writing for intermediate students. Covers viticulture, fermentation, chemistry, terroir.

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Discipline Links)

### **Wine and Climate Science**
- Climate change is reshaping viticulture: shifting regions, altered phenology, extreme weather. Links wine science to atmospheric science, carbon cycles, and adaptation strategies. See OIV climate reports.

### **Wine and Microbial Ecology**
- Fermentation is a complex microbial ecosystem (yeast, bacteria, fungi). Connect to microbiome science, ecological succession, and microbial competition. Relevant papers on *Saccharomyces* and *non-Saccharomyces* interactions.

### **Wine and Neuroscience**
- Sensory perception involves olfactory receptors, retronasal olfaction, cross-modal integration (smell + taste + touch). Connect to neuroscience of flavor, threshold detection, and psychophysics.

### **Wine and Economics**
- Wine pricing, terroir premiums, "cult wine" markets. Behavioral economics of taste perception (does price influence enjoyment?). See research by Robin Goldstein, Orley Ashenfelter (wine economics).

### **Wine and Data Science**
- Precision viticulture uses remote sensing, machine learning for site-specific management. Wine quality prediction from chemical analysis. Connect to computer vision, regression modeling, sensor networks.

### **Wine and Art/Culture**
- Wine's role in history (ancient Greece/Rome, medieval monasteries, phylloxera crisis). Literature (wine writing, sommelier memoirs). Philosophy (aesthetics of taste, natural vs. constructed). Cross into humanities.

## Practical Recommendations

For students at the intermediate level:

1. **Read**: Start with *Wine Science* (Jackson) as primary text. Supplement with *The Science of Wine* (Goode) for accessible explanations.
2. **Watch**: Wine Folly and UC Davis videos for process visualization.
3. **Taste**: Organize side-by-side tastings to experience concepts (e.g., oaked vs. unoaked Chardonnay, cool vs. warm-climate Syrah, wine with Brett vs. without).
4. **Explore**: Use Wine Folly maps and WSET study guides to understand regional differences.
5. **Stay Current**: Follow ASEV news, OIV reports, and wine science blogs for emerging research on climate, sustainability, and technology.
