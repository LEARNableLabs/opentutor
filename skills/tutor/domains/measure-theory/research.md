# Measure Theory and Integration — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Foundation
- **Sigma-algebras and measurable spaces** — the abstract structure underlying all of measure theory
- **Measures and outer measures** — assigning "size" to sets, Carathéodory's extension theorem
- **Lebesgue measure** — the canonical measure on the real line and Euclidean space

### Integration Theory
- **Measurable functions** — the functions we can integrate
- **Construction of the Lebesgue integral** — simple functions, positive functions, general functions
- **Convergence theorems** — Monotone Convergence, Fatou's Lemma, Dominated Convergence (the workhorses of integration)
- **Modes of convergence** — pointwise, uniform, almost everywhere, in measure, in Lp

### Advanced Structures
- **Product measures** — measures on product spaces, Fubini-Tonelli theorems for iterated integrals
- **Signed measures** — relaxing positivity, Hahn decomposition, Jordan decomposition
- **Radon-Nikodym theorem** — absolute continuity and densities
- **Lp spaces** — function spaces with integral norms, completeness, duality
- **Differentiation** — Lebesgue differentiation theorem, fundamental theorem of calculus for Lebesgue integrals

## Pedagogical Notes

Measure theory is a standard first-year graduate topic in pure mathematics. At intermediate level, students should have:
- Solid understanding of real analysis (epsilon-delta, sequences, series, continuity)
- Point-set topology (metric spaces at minimum)
- Some experience with rigorous proofs

The subject is highly abstract — the challenge is building intuition while maintaining rigor. Best approach: motivate with deficiencies of Riemann integral, show concrete examples (Lebesgue measure on R), then abstract to general measure spaces.
