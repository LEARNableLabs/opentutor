# Electrochemistry and Batteries — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Bard, Faulkner & White: "Electrochemical Methods: Fundamentals and Applications" (3rd edition, 2022)**
  - The gold standard comprehensive electrochemistry textbook
  - Covers fundamental principles, experimental techniques, and applications
  - Suitable for senior undergraduate to graduate level
  - Strong mathematical treatment with physical intuition
  - [Publisher link](https://www.wiley.com/en-ae/Electrochemical+Methods:+Fundamentals+and+Applications,+3rd+Edition-p-9781119334057)
  - [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Electrochemical-Methods-Fundamentals-Allen-Bard/dp/1119334063)

- **Newman & Thomas-Alyea: "Electrochemical Systems" (3rd edition)**
  - Essential for battery modeling and transport theory
  - More mathematically rigorous than Bard & Faulkner
  - Foundation for porous electrode theory
  - Industry standard for lithium-ion battery modeling

- **Huggins: "Advanced Batteries: Materials Science Aspects"**
  - Materials science perspective on battery technology
  - Covers intercalation, structure, and solid-state chemistry
  - Recommended in MIT electrochemical energy systems course

### University Courses

## Code Repositories and Computational Resources

### PyBaMM (Python Battery Mathematical Modelling)
- Open-source framework for physics-based battery models
- Implements Newman-type porous electrode models
- GitHub: actively maintained by community including academic and industry contributors

### LIONSIMBA
- MATLAB toolbox for lithium-ion battery simulation
- Implements Doyle-Fuller-Newman model
- Educational resource for understanding battery modeling

### Electrochemical Data Analysis
- Python electrochemistry packages (e.g., `electrochemistry`, `cycler` for battery cycling data)
- Impedance.py for EIS data analysis and equivalent circuit fitting

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Biology: Ion Channels and Bioelectricity
- Neurons use ion gradients to generate action potentials — biological analogue of batteries
- Nernst equation predicts resting membrane potential
- Voltage-gated ion channels function like molecular batteries and switches
- Electric eels generate 600V through stacked electrochemical cells (electrocytes)

### Geology: Natural Electrochemical Cells
- Corrosion is unwanted electrochemistry — oxidation of metals driven by galvanic cells
- Ore deposits form through electrochemical processes over geological timescales
- Microbial fuel cells in sediments generate electricity from organic matter decomposition

### Economics: Battery Cost Curves and Energy Transition
- Wright's Law: battery costs drop ~20% for every doubling of cumulative production
- Lithium-ion costs fell from $1000/kWh (2010) to ~$100/kWh (2023)
- Grid storage and electric vehicles become economically viable at different price points
- Geopolitics of lithium, cobalt, and nickel supply chains

### Materials Science: Phase Diagrams and Intercalation
- Lithium intercalation creates non-equilibrium phases
- Spinodal decomposition in NMC cathodes during cycling
- Fracture mechanics of electrode particles (mechanical degradation)

### Computer Science: Machine Learning for Battery Management
- State-of-charge estimation from voltage and current measurements
- Remaining useful life prediction using degradation data
- Optimal charging algorithms to minimize degradation
- High-throughput computational screening of battery materials

### Climate Science: Energy Storage and Grid Decarbonization
- Intermittent renewables (solar, wind) require energy storage
- Diurnal vs. seasonal storage requirements
- Battery manufacturing carbon footprint vs. lifetime emissions avoided
- Circular economy and battery recycling challenges

## Historical Context

### Key Milestones
- **1800** — Volta's pile (first battery)
- **1859** — Gaston Planté invents lead-acid battery (still used today!)
- **1887** — Carl Gassner invents zinc-carbon dry cell
- **1970s** — Whittingham develops lithium-titanium disulfide intercalation electrode
- **1980** — Goodenough discovers lithium cobalt oxide cathode
- **1985** — Yoshino creates first prototype lithium-ion battery
- **1991** — Sony commercializes lithium-ion batteries for camcorders
- **2019** — Nobel Prize awarded to Goodenough, Whittingham, and Yoshino
- **2020s** — Solid-state and sodium-ion batteries emerge as next generation
