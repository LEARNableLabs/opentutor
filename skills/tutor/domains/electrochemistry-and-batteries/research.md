# Electrochemistry and Batteries — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Fundamental Electrochemistry
- Galvanic and electrolytic cells
- Electrode potentials and the Nernst equation
- Thermodynamics of electrochemical systems
- Electrochemical kinetics (Butler-Volmer equation, Tafel plots)
- Mass transport (diffusion, migration, convection)
- Double layer theory and interfacial phenomena

### Battery Chemistry and Types
- Primary vs. secondary batteries
- Lead-acid batteries (chemistry, structure, applications)
- Nickel-based batteries (NiMH, NiCd)
- Lithium-ion batteries (intercalation chemistry, components)
- Emerging technologies (solid-state, lithium-sulfur, sodium-ion)

### Battery Performance and Degradation
- Capacity, energy density, power density
- Solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation
- Degradation mechanisms (cycling, calendar aging)
- Thermal management and safety

### Characterization and Modeling
- Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS)
- Cyclic voltammetry
- Mathematical modeling (porous electrode theory, Newman models)
- Equivalent circuit models

- **Bard & Faulkner: "Electrochemical Methods: Fundamentals and Applications"** (3rd edition)
  - Gold standard comprehensive electrochemistry textbook

- **Newman & Thomas-Alyea: "Electrochemical Systems"**
  - Essential for battery modeling
  - More mathematically rigorous than Bard & Faulkner
  - Used as bridge to advanced modeling

- **Huggins: "Advanced Batteries: Materials Science Aspects"**
  - Recommended in MIT course
  - Materials science perspective on battery technology

## Recommended Approach for Intermediate Level

For an intermediate curriculum, bridge the gap between basic electrochemistry (general chemistry level) and advanced battery engineering:
1. Start with fundamental electrochemical principles (using Khan Academy for visual reinforcement)
2. Build mathematical sophistication gradually (Nernst → Butler-Volmer → transport)
3. Connect theory to real battery systems (lead-acid as pedagogical anchor, then Li-ion)
4. Introduce characterization techniques alongside theory
5. End with emerging technologies and real-world challenges

The MIT OCW course provides excellent structure and depth for intermediate-to-advanced learners, while Khan Academy fills visualization and basic concept needs.
