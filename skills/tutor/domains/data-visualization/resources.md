# Information Design and Data Visualization — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Books
- **Edward Tufte — The Visual Display of Quantitative Information** (https://www.edwardtufte.com/tufte/books_vdqi) — The classic. Data-ink ratio, chartjunk, small multiples. Dense but essential. Best for intermediate students who want rigor.
- **Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic — Storytelling with Data** (https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/books) — Practical, accessible, business-focused. Great for learning to communicate, not just analyze. Excellent for lessons 19-22.
- **Alberto Cairo — The Truthful Art** (https://www.albertocairo.com/books.html) — Balance of theory and practice. Strong on ethics and statistical thinking. Intermediate-friendly.
- **Stephen Few — Show Me the Numbers** (https://www.stephen-few.com/smtn.php) — Dashboard and table design. Detailed, prescriptive. Best for lessons 11, 26.
- **Tamara Munzner — Visualization Analysis and Design** (https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~tmm/vadbook/) — Academic textbook. Comprehensive theory of marks, channels, tasks. Advanced but worth it for serious students.
- **Isabel Meirelles — Design for Information** (https://isabelmeirelles.com/book-design-for-information/) — Beautiful examples, organized by data type. Visual reference.

### Research Papers (accessible to intermediate students)
- **Cleveland & McGill (1984) — Graphical Perception** (https://www.jstor.org/stable/2288400) — The foundational study ranking visual encodings. Cited everywhere.
- **Heer & Bostock (2010) — Crowdsourcing Graphical Perception** (http://vis.stanford.edu/files/2010-MTurk-CHI.pdf) — Modern replication and extension of Cleveland-McGill using Mechanical Turk.
- **Borkin et al. (2013) — What Makes a Visualization Memorable?** (https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/what-makes-a-visualization-memorable) — Eye-tracking study on memorability vs. effectiveness.

### Online Courses
- **UW CSE 442 — Data Visualization** (https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse442/) — Excellent syllabus, D3-focused, publicly available lectures and assignments
- **Datawrapper Academy** (https://academy.datawrapper.de/) — Free, practical, chart-by-chart guides. Perfect for beginners to intermediate.
- **Curran Kelleher's YouTube — Data Visualization** (https://www.youtube.com/c/currankelleher) — D3.js tutorials, Observable notebooks, visualization theory
- **Storytelling with Data Blog** (https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog) — Monthly makeovers, challenges, and exercises

### Interactive Tools and Playgrounds
- **Observable** (https://observablehq.com) — Collaborative JavaScript notebooks. Best platform for learning D3.js and modern web viz. Free tier is generous.
- **D3.js Gallery** (https://observablehq.com/@d3/gallery) — Canonical examples of D3 techniques. Fork and modify to learn.
- **Observable Plot** (https://observablehq.com/plot) — High-level charting library. Like ggplot2 for JavaScript. Great for quick exploration.
- **Vega-Lite** (https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/) — Declarative grammar of graphics. Write JSON, get charts. Good for understanding the theory.
- **RAWGraphs** (https://www.rawgraphs.io/) — No-code tool for unusual chart types (alluvial, parallel coordinates). Great for exploration.
- **ColorBrewer** (https://colorbrewer2.org/) — Evidence-based color schemes for maps and charts. Essential reference.
- **Colorgorical** (http://vrl.cs.brown.edu/color) — Generate categorical color palettes optimized for perceptual distance
- **Viz Palette** (https://projects.susielu.com/viz-palette) — Test your color choices for accessibility and differentiation
- **Data-to-Viz** (https://www.data-to-viz.com/) — Decision tree for chart selection. Input data type and question, get recommendations.

## Tools and Libraries (organized by use case)

### For web-based interactive visualization
- **D3.js** (https://d3js.org/) — Low-level, full control, steep learning curve. Industry standard for custom work.
- **Observable Plot** (https://observablehq.com/plot) — High-level, concise, opinionated. Like ggplot2 for the web.
- **Vega-Lite** (https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/) — Declarative JSON specs. Good for embedding and programmatic generation.
- **Chart.js** (https://www.chartjs.org/) — Simple, canvas-based, limited customization. Good for basic dashboards.
- **Plotly.js** (https://plotly.com/javascript/) — High-level, lots of chart types, 3D support. Can feel heavy.

### For Python
- **Matplotlib** (https://matplotlib.org/) — Low-level, MATLAB-like, full control. Verbose but powerful.
- **Seaborn** (https://seaborn.pydata.org/) — High-level wrapper over matplotlib. Statistical focus, beautiful defaults.
- **Plotly** (https://plotly.com/python/) — Interactive charts, Dash for dashboards. Good for exploration and sharing.
- **Altair** (https://altair-viz.github.io/) — Declarative, Vega-Lite backend. Clean syntax, interactive by default.
- **Bokeh** (https://bokeh.org/) — Interactive, server-backed, streaming data. Good for dashboards.

### For R
- **ggplot2** (https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/) — Grammar of graphics, publication-ready, massive ecosystem. Gold standard for R.
- **plotly (R)** (https://plotly.com/r/) — Interactive, ggplotly converts ggplot2 to interactive.
- **Shiny** (https://shiny.rstudio.com/) — Interactive dashboards and apps. Mature ecosystem.
- **gganimate** (https://gganimate.com/) — Animation extension for ggplot2
- **patchwork** (https://patchwork.data-imaginist.com/) — Composing multiple ggplot2 plots

### For business intelligence / no-code
- **Tableau** (https://www.tableau.com/) — Industry leader, drag-and-drop, fast iteration. Expensive but powerful.
- **Power BI** (https://powerbi.microsoft.com/) — Microsoft's answer to Tableau. Good MS Office integration.
- **Datawrapper** (https://www.datawrapper.de/) — Journalist-friendly, embeddable, accessible by default. Free tier.
- **Flourish** (https://flourish.studio/) — Templates for storytelling, animations, embeds. Free tier.
- **RAWGraphs** (https://www.rawgraphs.io/) — Open source, unusual chart types, no-code. Good for exploration.

### For design and illustration
- **Adobe Illustrator** — Vector graphics, publication-ready. Industry standard for polished work.
- **Figma** (https://www.figma.com/) — Collaborative design tool, vector graphics, prototyping
- **Inkscape** (https://inkscape.org/) — Free, open source vector graphics
- **Affinity Designer** (https://affinity.serif.com/) — One-time purchase alternative to Illustrator
