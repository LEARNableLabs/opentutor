# Information Design and Data Visualization — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 8 lessons (26%)
- **Socratic questions** — 7 lessons (23%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 7 lessons (23%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (13%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 3 lessons (10%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (6%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 32% accessible (1-2), 32% standard (3), 35% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 9: "Why are treemaps and sunbursts better than nested pie charts?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 11: "How would you visualize this messy dataset?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 15: "How do aspect ratio and scale distort perception?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 19: "What makes a visualization accessible beyond color?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 23: "Why does The Pudding's work feel like a story and not a report?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers information design and data visualization, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: pre-attentive processing, visual encoding, cognitive load, marks and channels, encoding effectiveness, Cleveland-McGill hierarchy, Gestalt principles, proximity, similarity, continuity, quantitative vs categorical, ordinal data, temporal and spatial data, angle perception, area perception (and 88 more).
