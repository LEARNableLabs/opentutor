# History of Jazz — Improvisation as a Practice (Research Summary)

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Origins and Cultural Roots (late 1800s - 1910s)
- Blues tradition (emotional expression, call-and-response)
- Ragtime (syncopated rhythms, melodic embellishment)
- African rhythmic traditions and polyrhythms
- Brass band music and marching traditions
- New Orleans melting pot

### 2. Early Jazz and Collective Improvisation (1910s - 1920s)
- New Orleans style and "collective improvisation"
- Early teaching methods: "ragging" melodies, inserting "breaks"
- Transition from embellishment to improvisation
- Louis Armstrong's revolutionary solo approach

### 3. Swing Era and the Rise of Soloists (1930s - 1940s)
- Big band arrangements vs. improvised solos
- Vincent Lopez Modern Piano Method (1933-34): first method teaching "hot" playing
- Emergence of improvisation over chord changes
- Coleman Hawkins, Lester Young, and tenor saxophone lineage

### 4. Bebop Revolution (1940s - 1950s)
- Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, Thelonious Monk
- Harmonic complexity and chord substitutions
- Melodic improvisation over form and harmony
- Lennie Tristano's pedagogical methods (transcription, patterns, ear training)

### 5. Modal Jazz and Harmonic Freedom (1950s - 1960s)
- Miles Davis: *Kind of Blue* and modal thinking
- John Coltrane: sheets of sound and modal exploration
- Improvising on modes rather than chord changes
- Herbie Hancock's approach to modal improvisation

### 6. Free Jazz and Avant-Garde (1960s - 1970s)
- Ornette Coleman and harmonic liberation
- Collective free improvisation
- Extended techniques and timbral exploration
- Albert Ayler, Cecil Taylor, Sun Ra

### 7. Contemporary Practice and Pedagogy (1970s - Present)
- Modern teaching methods (70+ years of formal pedagogy)
- Transcription as core practice
- Learning tunes in all 12 keys
- Scale/mode mastery and pattern practice
- Stylistic synthesis and genre-crossing
