# History of Jazz — Improvisation as a Practice — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### University Course Materials
- **[Columbia University: Jazz Improvisation: Theory, History, Practice](https://music.columbia.edu/course/jazz-improvisation-theory-history-practice)** — Comprehensive course integrating theory, history, and performance. Ideal for intermediate students wanting academic rigor.
- **[University of Oregon Jazz Studies Course Offerings](https://jazz.uoregon.edu/course-offerings/)** — History of Jazz 1900-1950, Jazz Pedagogy covering teaching methods. Strong on historical context and pedagogical evolution.
- **[University of Colorado Boulder Jazz Studies](https://www.colorado.edu/music/academics/departments/jazz-studies/jazz-studies-courses)** — History of Jazz 1 and Advanced Improvisation courses with transcription and analysis focus.
- **[NYU Jazz Syllabus](https://cas.nyu.edu/content/dam/nyu-as/casCore/documents/syllabi2023-24/730_Jazz_Daughtry.pdf)** — Detailed syllabus with readings, listening assignments, and critical perspectives on jazz history.
- **[UNLV MUS 258 Elementary Jazz Improvisation](https://www.unlv.edu/sites/default/files/media/document/2023-01/MUS258-Syllabus.pdf)** — Techniques across all jazz styles, good for understanding style-specific approaches.

### Academic Articles and Deep Dives
- **[Music Theory Online: Improvisation in Early Jazz Piano](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.25.31.3/mto.25.31.3.martin.html)** — Scholarly analysis of how early improvisation pedagogy evolved from ragging to playing hot. Essential for lessons 3, 5, 8.
- **[Breve Music: Deep Dive into History and Techniques of Jazz Improvisation](https://brevemusicrecords.com/a-deep-dive-into-the-history-and-techniques-of-jazz-improvisation/)** — Accessible overview of historical evolution with technical analysis. Good comprehensive reference.

### Textbooks
- **[Routledge: Jazz Improvisation Using Simple Melodic Embellishment](https://www.routledge.com/Jazz-Improvisation-Using-Simple-Melodic-Embellishment/Titlebaum/p/book/9780367427429)** — Jargon-free approach with backing audio, focuses on melodic development. Good for understanding embellishment-to-improvisation evolution.
- **Mark Levine: The Jazz Theory Book** — Industry-standard reference for harmony, scales, and theoretical underpinnings (not publicly available for free, but widely owned by intermediate students).

## Supplementary (for engagement and practice)

### Video Courses and Lectures
- **[Coursera: Jazz Improvisation (Gary Burton)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/jazz-improvisation)** — One of the greatest improvisers explains mental, melodic, and harmonic processes. Excellent for lessons 10-16 on bebop and modal jazz.
- **[New York Jazz Academy: Introduction to Improvisation and Jazz Theory](https://www.nyjazzacademy.com/program/introduction-to-improvisation-and-jazz-theory-full-online-course-content-only/)** — Full online course with video lectures and bibliographic references. Structured learning path.
- **[Jazz Video Lessons](https://jazzvideolessons.net/)** — Library of jazz courses, masterclasses, and live practice sessions. Over 1,000 students since 2013. Good for seeing techniques in action.
- **Herbie Hancock MasterClass** (subscription service) — 25 video lessons covering improvisation philosophy, modal thinking, harmonic freedom. Mentioned in research; excellent for modal jazz unit.

### Practice Systems and Methods
- **[Learn Jazz Standards: Zero to Improv](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/zerotoimprov/)** — Basic theory eBook for all instruments (C, Bb, Eb, Bass Clef) with audio examples. Practical approach to building improvisational skills.
- **[Jazz Advice: Ultimate Guide to Jazz Improvisation](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/jazz-improvisation-ultimate-guide/)** — Comprehensive online guide covering listening, transcription, scales, practicing with recordings. Excellent contemporary synthesis of historical methods.
- **[Jazz Lesson Videos](https://www.jazzlessonvideos.com/)** — Educational PDF packages, masterclasses, lessons, workshops for improving improvisation. Good for pattern study and vocabulary building.
- **[This Is Jazz: Complete Jazz Education System](https://thisisjazz.com/)** — System for teaching jazz and improvisation, particularly strong on pedagogical methods. Useful for lessons 20-22 on contemporary practice.
- **[eJazz News: Jazz Improvisation Complete Guide](https://ejazznews.com/jazz-education/jazz-improvisation/)** — News and educational content on contemporary jazz improvisation approaches.

### Teaching Resources (for understanding pedagogy)
- **[Allan Chase: Teaching Free Improvisation Resources](https://allan-chase.com/2019/04/29/teaching-free-improvisation-part-3-resources/)** — Detailed resources on teaching free improvisation, essential for lessons 18-19. Includes philosophy, exercises, and reading lists.
- **[Jazz Studies Online: Teaching Tools](https://www.jazzstudiesonline.org/resource/teaching-tools/list)** — Curated teaching materials and resources. Good for understanding how improvisation is taught today.

### Interactive and Listening Tools
- **Jazz Standards Database** (various sites) — Essential for learning tunes, finding recordings, accessing lead sheets. Critical for 12-key practice.
- **YouTube** — Indispensable for finding specific recordings mentioned in lessons. Search for specific performances: "Charlie Parker Ko Ko," "Miles Davis So What," "Ornette Coleman Lonely Woman."
- **Spotify / Apple Music / Archive.org** — Access to historical recordings for listening assignments. Create era-specific playlists.

## Code and Interactive Tools

- **iReal Pro** (app, paid) — Play-along tool with chord changes for thousands of jazz standards. Essential for practice.
- **Transcribe!** (software, paid) — Slow down recordings, loop sections, isolate frequencies. Standard transcription tool.
- **Amazing Slow Downer** (app) — Alternative to Transcribe for slowing down audio without pitch change.
- **Music21** (Python library) — For computationally analyzing transcribed solos (advanced students).
- **MuseScore / Finale / Sibelius** — Notation software for writing out transcriptions.

## People to Explore (Beyond Core Curriculum)

### Early Innovators
- Jelly Roll Morton — Early stride piano and compositional approach
- Sidney Bechet — Soprano saxophone virtuoso, contemporary of Armstrong
- Art Tatum — Harmonic sophistication that prefigured bebop

### Bebop Era
- Kenny Clarke — Drum innovations that enabled bebop
- Max Roach — Melodic drumming approach
- Fats Navarro — Trumpet virtuosity, influenced later hard bop

### Modal and Beyond
- Bill Evans — Harmonic voicings and interactive trio concept
- Wayne Shorter — Composition and improvisation integration
- McCoy Tyner — Modal piano vocabulary with Coltrane quartet

### Free Jazz
- Sun Ra — Cosmic philosophy and collective improvisation
- Anthony Braxton — Solo saxophone and compositional systems
- The Art Ensemble of Chicago — Theater and multi-instrumentalism

### Contemporary
- Jason Moran — Hip-hop influence and stride piano synthesis
- Steve Lehman — Spectral harmony and electronic integration
- Mary Halvorson — Extended guitar techniques and composition

### Educators and Methodologists
- Lennie Tristano — Lesson 12, revolutionary pedagogy
- Jamey Aebersold — Play-along method books (not in curriculum but widely used)
- Jerry Coker — Improvising Jazz Piano (textbook method)
- George Russell — Lydian Chromatic Concept (theoretical system)

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Cross-Genre Influences
- **Hip-hop sampling** — How jazz improvisation influenced rap flow and beat making. Explore J Dilla, Madlib, Robert Glasper Experiment.
- **Electronic music** — How loop-based improvisation in techno and ambient relates to jazz. Explore Jan Jelinek, Nils Frahm.
- **Contemporary classical** — Improvisation in new music. Explore George Lewis (trombonist and theorist), Tyshawn Sorey.

### Cultural Studies
- **The Great Migration** — How movement of Black Americans from South to North cities shaped jazz geography and style.
- **Cold War cultural diplomacy** — Jazz as American export, State Department tours, global influence.
- **The "Jazz Age" myth** — How 1920s cultural discourse both celebrated and commodified jazz.

### Technology and Improvisation
- **Recording technology timeline** — 78s → LPs → digital. How format shaped solo length and complexity.
- **Amplification** — Electric instruments (guitar, bass, keyboards) changed ensemble dynamics and improvisation possibilities.
- **AI and machine learning** — Contemporary algorithmic improvisation partners. Explore Google Magenta, AIVA.

### Philosophical Dimensions
- **Improvisation as freedom** — Political and aesthetic dimensions of spontaneity. Read George Lewis, "A Power Stronger Than Itself."
- **Oral tradition vs. notation** — What gets lost/gained when improvisation is written down?
- **Collective vs. individual creativity** — Tension throughout jazz history between solo virtuosity and ensemble interaction.
