# World Mythology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Comparative Mythology Framework
- Theoretical approaches (structuralism, psychoanalytic, functionalist)
- Joseph Campbell's monomyth and archetypal patterns
- Cross-cultural narrative structures
- Universal vs. culturally-specific elements

### 2. Creation and Cosmogony
- Ex nihilo creation (from nothing)
- Emergence myths (from chaos/void)
- Earth-diver narratives
- World parent myths (sky/earth separation)
- Comparative analysis across cultures (Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Norse, Hindu, Chinese, Mesoamerican, Polynesian)

### 3. Hero Archetypes and Journey Patterns
- Monomyth stages (departure, initiation, return)
- Hero types across cultures (divine heroes, cultural heroes, trickster-heroes)
- Comparative examples: Gilgamesh, Odysseus, Arjuna, Quetzalcoatl, Maui, Beowulf
- Gender dimensions of heroism

### 4. Divine Pantheons and Archetypes
- Supreme deities and creator gods
- Mother goddess archetypes
- Trickster figures (Loki, Anansi, Coyote, Eshu, Hermes)
- War, wisdom, and fertility deities
- Comparative polytheism vs. monotheism

### 5. Death, Afterlife, and Transformation
- Underworld journeys (katabasis)
- Resurrection and rebirth myths
- Psychopomp figures (guides to the afterlife)
- Cultural variations: Egyptian Book of the Dead, Greek Hades, Norse Hel, Aztec Mictlan, Buddhist realms

### 6. Flood Myths and Apocalypse
- Universal flood narratives (Mesopotamian, Biblical, Hindu, Native American)
- Ragnarok, Kali Yuga, and cyclical destruction
- Survival and renewal patterns

### 7. Sacred Marriage and Fertility Myths
- Hieros gamos (sacred marriage)
- Seasonal cycles and agricultural deities
- Inanna/Ishtar, Demeter/Persephone, Osiris/Isis parallels

### 8. Modern Applications
- Myths in contemporary storytelling (Star Wars, Lord of the Rings, Marvel)
- Psychological interpretations (Jung, Freud)
- Myth and identity formation
- Neo-mythology and world-building

## Key Sources

### Academic Courses
- **HRS 151 World Mythology** (California State University, Sacramento) — covers Mesopotamian (Gilgamesh), Egyptian, Biblical, Celtic, Germanic (Beowulf), Norse, Mesoamerican, Native American, African, Chinese, Indian (Ramayana), Polynesian, and Arabian/Persian myths
- **HUM 256 Comparative Mythology** (Tidewater Community College) — focuses on archetypes, creation/destruction, afterlife perspectives, rituals, folk stories
- **RELS 1502 Comparative Mythology** (UNC Charlotte) — comparative exploration of how societies imaginatively constructed their worlds
- **Classical Mythology** (University of Pennsylvania) — Greek/Roman focus with Hesiod, Homeric Hymns, tragic drama

### Foundational Texts
- Joseph Campbell: *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949), *The Masks of God* (4 volumes), *Myths to Live By*, *The Historical Atlas of World Mythology*
- Comparative mythology theory by scholars including Plato, Euhemerus, James Frazer, Ernst Cassirer

### Online Courses
- **Greek and Roman Mythology** (Coursera / University of Pennsylvania) — video lectures, graded assignments, free preview
- **Mythology courses on edX** — various cultural traditions and time periods
- **Great Mythologies of the World** (The Great Courses Plus)

## Research Gaps Identified
- Limited coverage of African diaspora mythology
- Indigenous perspectives often filtered through colonial sources
- Feminist and gender-inclusive reinterpretations
- Contemporary myth-making in digital cultures
