# World Mythology — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Texts

- **Joseph Campbell, *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*** (1949) — The definitive work on the monomyth. Essential for understanding archetypal hero journey patterns across cultures. Best for intermediate students already familiar with multiple mythological traditions who can evaluate Campbell's claims critically.
  - Wikipedia overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hero_with_a_Thousand_Faces
  - Available on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Comparative-Mythology-Singles-Joseph-Campbell-ebook/dp/B005DTSHUI

- **Joseph Campbell, *The Masks of God*** (4-volume series) — Comprehensive survey of world mythology: Primitive Mythology, Oriental Mythology, Occidental Mythology, Creative Mythology. More detailed and nuanced than *Hero*, covering specific cultural traditions in depth.

- **Joseph Campbell, *Myths to Live By*** — More accessible introduction to Campbell's ideas, focusing on practical applications of mythic thinking to modern life.

- **Kenneth Golden (ed.), *Uses of Comparative Mythology: Essays on the Work of Joseph Campbell*** (Routledge, 1992) — Critical-interpretive essays assessing Campbell's contributions and limitations. Essential for intermediate students to engage scholarly debate around monomyth theory.
  - Publisher page: https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/edit/10.4324/9781315860602/uses-comparative-mythology-kenneth-golden
  - Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/Routledge-Library-Editions-Comparative-Mythology/dp/1138843245

### Primary Myth Texts (in translation)

- **Epic of Gilgamesh** (N.K. Sandars translation recommended) — Oldest surviving epic, Mesopotamian hero journey
- **Hesiod, *Theogony*** — Greek creation myth and divine genealogy
- **Homer, *The Odyssey*** (Lattimore or Fagles translation) — Quintessential nostos narrative
- ***Prose Edda*** and ***Poetic Edda*** (Snorri Sturluson) — Norse mythology, creation through cosmic violence
- ***Ramayana*** and ***Mahabharata*** — Indian epics, dharmic heroism
- ***Popol Vuh*** — Maya creation narrative
- ***Egyptian Book of the Dead*** (Budge or Faulkner translation) — Underworld journey and transformation

## Interactive Tools and Media

### Museums and Digital Collections

- **British Museum Mesopotamia Collection** — Digital access to Epic of Gilgamesh tablets and cuneiform resources.

- **Metropolitan Museum of Art (The Met)** — Search "mythology" for thousands of artworks depicting mythic scenes across cultures. Use for visual analysis of archetypal representation.

- **Theoi Greek Mythology** (theoi.com) — Comprehensive encyclopedia of Greek gods, heroes, creatures. Includes primary text citations.

### Modern Myth Applications

- **Star Wars** (Original Trilogy) — George Lucas explicitly used Campbell's monomyth as structural template. Perfect for teaching modern myth-making and archetypal persistence.

- **Marvel Cinematic Universe** — Contemporary pantheon with divine archetypes (Thor as Norse god reimagined, hero journeys across 20+ films). Use to discuss serialized mythology.

- **Studio Ghibli Films** (*Princess Mononoke*, *Spirited Away*) — Miyazaki draws on Shinto mythology and animism. Shows non-Western mythic frameworks.

- **Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson series** — YA fiction that translates Greek myths to contemporary settings. Accessible entry point for students unfamiliar with classics.

- **Neil Gaiman's *American Gods*** — Novel exploring how myths migrate and transform in new cultural contexts. Great for discussions of living mythology.

- **God of War** (video game series) — Interactive engagement with Norse and Greek mythology. Complex enough for analysis of adaptation and reinterpretation.

### Comparison and Analysis Tools

- **Comparative mythology charts** — Create or use existing comparison tables for creation myths, hero journeys, trickster figures, flood narratives. Visual organization helps pattern recognition.

- **Pantheon family trees** — Diagram divine genealogies (Greek, Norse, Egyptian) to understand kinship structures and divine relationships.

- **Monomyth mapping worksheets** — Templates for plotting narrative stages (departure, initiation, return) across different hero stories.

## People (Scholars and Myth Theorists)

### Foundational Theorists

- **Joseph Campbell** (1904-1987) — Comparative mythologist, monomyth theory, influence on contemporary storytelling
- **Carl Jung** (1875-1961) — Psychologist, collective unconscious, archetypal theory
- **Sigmund Freud** (1856-1939) — Psychoanalytic interpretation of myth (Oedipus complex, primal horde)
- **Claude Lévi-Strauss** (1908-2009) — Structuralist anthropology, myth as unconscious logic
- **Mircea Eliade** (1907-1986) — Historian of religion, sacred vs. profane, eternal return
- **James George Frazer** (1854-1941) — *The Golden Bough*, comparative mythology (now dated/problematic but historically influential)
- **Georges Dumézil** (1898-1986) — Indo-European trifunctional hypothesis

### Contemporary Scholars

- **Wendy Doniger** — Hindu mythology, comparative erotics, *The Implied Spider*
- **Bruce Lincoln** — Myth and ideology, critical myth theory, *Theorizing Myth*
- **Robert Segal** — History of myth theory, *Myth: A Very Short Introduction*
- **Marina Warner** — Fairy tales and myth, feminist reinterpretation
- **Maureen Murdock** — Feminist alternative to monomyth, *The Heroine's Journey*
- **Kim Hudson** — *The Virgin's Promise*, alternative narrative structure
- **Michael Witzel** — Comparative mythology and linguistics, global myth origins

### Diverse Cultural Voices

- **Devdutt Pattanaik** — Indian mythology from insider perspective, critiques of Western frameworks
- **Chinua Achebe** — African storytelling, postcolonial critique of Western myth interpretation
- **Leslie Marmon Silko** — Native American (Laguna Pueblo) storytelling and myth
- **Octavia Butler** — Afrofuturist myth-making, *Parable* series

## Unexpected Connections (cross-discipline links)

### Myth and Psychology

- **Jungian therapy and active imagination** — Using mythic archetypes in therapeutic practice
- **Narrative therapy** — Reauthoring personal stories using mythic frameworks
- **Shadow work** — Engaging repressed aspects through trickster and underworld archetypes

### Myth and Politics

- **National founding myths** — American exceptionalism, Manifest Destiny, European nationalist myths
- **Political mythology** — Roland Barthes' *Mythologies*, how contemporary culture creates "myths" (naturalizing ideology)
- **Myth and colonialism** — How empire used comparative mythology to construct racial hierarchies

### Myth and Science

- **Cognitive science of religion** — Why humans create gods (hyperactive agency detection, theory of mind)
- **Evolutionary psychology** — Are archetypes adaptive? Do narrative preferences reflect evolutionary pressures?
- **Astrobiology and myth** — How might alien cultures create myths? Universal vs. contingent narrative structures

### Myth and Digital Culture

- **Creepypasta and internet folklore** — Slenderman, SCP Foundation as contemporary myth-making
- **Fandom and shared universes** — Marvel, Star Wars, Tolkien as modern pantheons with canon wars
- **Conspiracy theories as mythology** — QAnon's apocalyptic battle narrative, mythic structure of conspiracism

### Myth and Ecology

- **Mythic relationship to land** — Indigenous creation myths as ecological knowledge systems
- **Anthropocene myths** — Climate narratives, extinction stories, technological sublime
- **Rewilding and myth** — Reconnecting to place through local mythic traditions
