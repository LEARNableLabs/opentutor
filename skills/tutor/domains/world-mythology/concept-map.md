# World Mythology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Comparative mythology** — systematic study of myths from different cultures to identify patterns and differences
2. **Universal patterns** — recurring themes, motifs, and structures found across unrelated cultures
3. **Cultural diffusion vs independent invention** — debate over whether similar myths spread geographically or emerged independently
4. **Collective unconscious** — Jung's theory of shared psychological substrate producing archetypal images (depends on: comparative mythology)
5. **Archetypes** — universal symbolic patterns (hero, mother, shadow, trickster) recurring across cultures (depends on: collective unconscious)
6. **Psychoanalytic interpretation** — reading myths as expressions of unconscious desires and conflicts (depends on: archetypes)
7. **Structuralism** — analyzing myths as language-like systems of binary oppositions (depends on: comparative mythology)
8. **Myth definition** — distinguishing myth from legend, folklore, and fairy tale based on sacred function
9. **Cosmogony types** — classification of creation myths by structure (ex nihilo, chaos, world parent, earth-diver, emergence)
10. **Chaos to order** — pattern of creation beginning with undifferentiated state and progressing to cosmos (depends on: cosmogony types)
11. **Ex nihilo creation** — creation from nothing by divine will or thought (depends on: cosmogony types)
12. **Creation through conflict** — cosmogony involving battle between divine forces, especially Mesopotamian models (depends on: chaos to order)
13. **World parent myths** — creation via separation of primordial sky father and earth mother (depends on: cosmogony types)
14. **Earth-diver narrative** — creation via diving into primordial waters to bring up earth (depends on: cosmogony types)
15. **Emergence myths** — creation via movement through successive underworlds to current world (depends on: cosmogony types)
16. **Monomyth** — Campbell's theory of universal hero journey structure: departure, initiation, return (depends on: archetypes, universal patterns)
17. **Threshold crossing** — hero's movement from ordinary to special world (depends on: monomyth)
18. **Nostos** — Greek concept of homecoming journey after trials (depends on: monomyth)
19. **Transformation through loss** — pattern where hero's failure or grief enables wisdom/growth (depends on: monomyth)
20. **Heroine's journey** — alternative or complementary pattern emphasizing descent, reclamation, integration (depends on: monomyth)
21. **Modern myth-making** — application of archetypal patterns in contemporary storytelling (depends on: monomyth, archetypes)
22. **Trickster archetype** — boundary-crossing divine figure who disrupts order creatively (depends on: archetypes)
23. **Trickster-culture hero** — figure who steals/creates benefits for humanity through transgression (depends on: trickster archetype)
24. **Divine gender archetypes** — recurring patterns of masculine/feminine divine roles and powers (depends on: archetypes)
25. **War deity archetypes** — divine figures embodying martial values specific to their cultures (depends on: divine gender archetypes)
26. **Katabasis** — descent to underworld and return, symbolizing transformation (depends on: threshold crossing, monomyth)
27. **Flood narratives** — cross-cultural myths of world destruction and renewal by water (depends on: universal patterns)
28. **Dying god archetype** — deity whose death and resurrection mirrors seasonal or cosmic cycles (depends on: archetypes, transformation through loss)
29. **Seasonal myth** — narratives linking divine/heroic events to agricultural cycles (depends on: dying god archetype)
30. **Archetypal persistence** — continued appearance of mythic patterns in secular modern media (depends on: modern myth-making)
31. **Universalism vs cultural specificity** — tension between emphasizing common patterns vs unique cultural meanings (depends on: comparative mythology, structural analysis)

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies
- **Archetypes** require understanding **collective unconscious** because archetypes are the specific content of the collective unconscious—the forms it takes across cultures
- **Psychoanalytic interpretation** builds on **archetypes** because it uses archetypal patterns to decode unconscious meanings in myths
- **Structuralism** depends on **comparative mythology** because structural analysis requires comparing multiple versions to identify underlying systems

### Creation Myth Dependencies
- All specific **cosmogony types** (ex nihilo, chaos to order, world parent, earth-diver, emergence) depend on understanding the general concept of **cosmogony types** as a classification system
- **Creation through conflict** requires **chaos to order** because conflict-creation is a specific mechanism for moving from chaos to cosmos
- **Mesopotamian creation** concepts build on **creation through conflict** and **chaos to order** as these describe the Enuma Elish pattern

### Hero Journey Dependencies
- **Threshold crossing**, **nostos**, **transformation through loss**, and **heroine's journey** all depend on **monomyth** as they are components or variations of Campbell's framework
- **Modern myth-making** requires both **monomyth** and **archetypes** because contemporary stories apply both specific journey patterns and broader symbolic templates
- **Katabasis** builds on both **threshold crossing** and **monomyth** because underworld descent is a special type of threshold experience within the hero pattern

### Divine Archetype Dependencies
- **Trickster-culture hero** depends on **trickster archetype** because it represents a specific function (cultural benefactor) of the trickster figure
- **War deity archetypes** and other specific divine types depend on **divine gender archetypes** because gender often structures divine roles
- **Dying god archetype** requires **transformation through loss** because death and resurrection represent the ultimate transformation pattern

### Synthesis Dependencies
- **Archetypal persistence** depends on **modern myth-making** because it examines how ancient patterns persist in contemporary contexts
- **Universalism vs cultural specificity** depends on all previous concepts because it represents the meta-level debate about the comparative method itself

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic familiarity with one mythological tradition** (Greek, Norse, Hindu, etc.) — provides concrete examples for comparison and prevents abstract framework from being taught in vacuum
- **Reading comprehension for translated texts** — needed for engaging primary sources like Gilgamesh, Homeric epics, Eddas
- **Cultural sensitivity and openness** — required for respecting sacred narratives from living traditions and avoiding appropriation

## Bottlenecks

### Conceptual Bottlenecks
1. **Archetypes/Collective Unconscious** (Lessons 2, 5) — if students don't grasp Jung's framework, they'll struggle with all subsequent pattern recognition and the entire comparative project becomes mechanical classification
2. **Monomyth** (Lesson 10) — Campbell's framework structures half the curriculum; weak understanding here cripples Lessons 11-15 and 21
3. **Universalism vs Cultural Specificity** (Lesson 25) — this meta-critical concept requires comfort with all previous frameworks to meaningfully engage the debate

### Skill Bottlenecks
1. **Pattern recognition across cultures** — begins with creation myths (Lessons 5-9) and must be solid before hero journey analysis (Lessons 10-15)
2. **Close reading of primary texts** — introduced with Gilgamesh (Lesson 11) and Odyssey (Lesson 12); students who struggle here may need scaffolding or modern retellings
3. **Critical evaluation of theory** — first challenged with monomyth critique (Lesson 10) and gender (Lesson 14); essential for synthesis in Lessons 24-25

## Common Misconceptions by Concept Cluster

### Comparative Method
- **"All myths are the same"** — conflating pattern recognition with reductionism; misses cultural specificity
- **"Similarities prove diffusion"** — assuming geographic contact rather than considering independent invention or universal human experiences

### Archetypes
- **"Archetypes are prescriptive"** — treating archetypal patterns as rules rather than descriptive observations
- **"Jung and Campbell are uncontested"** — not recognizing critiques around gender bias, Western-centrism, and oversimplification

### Hero Journey
- **"All heroes are male"** — overlooking female heroes or forcing them awkwardly into monomyth
- **"The monomyth is the only valid framework"** — privileging Campbell over other comparative approaches (structuralist, functionalist, feminist)

### Creation Myths
- **"Creation myths are primitive science"** — missing their existential, social, and cosmological functions beyond explanation
- **"One cosmogony type per culture"** — not recognizing that traditions often have multiple creation accounts with different emphases

### Divine Archetypes
- **"Tricksters are villains"** — missing their creative, boundary-dissolving, culture-building functions
- **"Divine pantheons are like modern org charts"** — imposing rigid hierarchies on fluid, context-dependent divine relationships

### Death and Transformation
- **"Underworld journeys are about literal afterlife"** — missing the psychological transformation symbolism
- **"Flood myths remember actual global flood"** — privileging pseudo-scientific explanations over symbolic and social functions
