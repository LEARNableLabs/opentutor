# International Law and Human Rights — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Foundational Texts
- **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)** — https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights — the foundational document; read the full text at least once
- **International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)** — https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights
- **International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)** — https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-economic-social-and-cultural-rights
- **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969)** — https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/vclt/vclt.html — the "treaty on treaties"
- **Geneva Conventions (1949) and Additional Protocols** — https://www.icrc.org/en/war-and-law/treaties-customary-law/geneva-conventions

### Treaty Collections and Legal Databases
- **UN Treaty Collection** — https://treaties.un.org/ — searchable database of all multilateral treaties deposited with the UN
- **OHCHR Treaty Body Database** — https://tbinternet.ohchr.org/ — state reports, concluding observations, individual complaints
- **IHL Database (ICRC)** — https://ihl-databases.icrc.org/en/ihl-treaties — full texts of IHL treaties and customary IHL study
- **Refworld (UNHCR)** — https://www.refworld.org/ — legal and policy documents on refugees and asylum
- **UN Audiovisual Library of International Law** — https://legal.un.org/avl/ — lecture series, historic archives, research guides

### Case Law
- **International Court of Justice** — https://www.icj-cij.org/en/cases — full judgments, advisory opinions, case summaries
- **International Criminal Court** — https://www.icc-cpi.int/cases — ongoing and completed cases, trial transcripts
- **European Court of Human Rights** — https://www.echr.coe.int/hudoc — searchable HUDOC database
- **Inter-American Court of Human Rights** — https://www.corteidh.or.cr/index-en.cfm — judgments, advisory opinions
- **African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights** — https://www.african-court.org/ — decisions and provisional measures

## Textbooks and Academic Resources

### Comprehensive Treatises (Intermediate Level)
- **Malcolm Shaw, "International Law" (9th ed., Cambridge, 2021)** — standard reference, excellent for doctrinal foundations
- **Antonio Cassese, "International Law" (2nd ed., Oxford, 2005)** — influential European perspective; Cassese was first ICC president
- **Dinah Shelton, "Advanced Introduction to International Human Rights Law" (Edward Elgar, 2014)** — concise, accessible, perfect for intermediate students
- **David Forsythe, "Human Rights in International Relations" (4th ed., Cambridge, 2017)** — political science lens; great for understanding enforcement limits

### Specialized Texts
- **Nils Melzer, "International Humanitarian Law: A Comprehensive Introduction" (ICRC, 2016)** — free PDF from ICRC, authoritative IHL primer
- **Rhona Smith, "Textbook on International Human Rights" (9th ed., Oxford, 2022)** — excellent coverage of regional systems
- **William Schabas, "The International Criminal Court: A Commentary on the Rome Statute" (2nd ed., Oxford, 2016)** — deep dive into ICC law

### Open Access Resources
- **ICRC Casebook on IHL** — https://casebook.icrc.org/ — searchable case studies for teaching IHL
- **Opinio Juris** — https://opiniojuris.org/ — blog by international law professors; current events analysis
- **EJIL: Talk!** — https://www.ejiltalk.org/ — blog of the European Journal of International Law; scholarly but accessible

## Interactive Tools and Databases

### Research and Monitoring Tools

- **Amnesty International** — https://www.amnesty.org/ — urgent actions, country reports, campaigns
- **Freedom House** — https://freedomhouse.org/ — annual freedom in the world rankings and reports

- **Rule of Law in Armed Conflicts (RULAC)** — https://www.rulac.org/ — Geneva Academy database classifying armed conflicts worldwide
- **Coalition for the International Criminal Court** — https://www.coalitionfortheicc.org/ — tracker of ICC ratifications, national implementing legislation
- **Humanitarian Data Exchange** — https://data.humdata.org/ — datasets on crises, displacement, humanitarian needs

### Simulation and Educational Games
- **Facing History and Ourselves** — https://www.facinghistory.org/ — educational resources on genocide prevention, human rights education
- **iCourts (University of Copenhagen)** — https://jura.ku.dk/icourts/ — research on international courts; some teaching materials available

## People to Follow (Scholars, Practitioners, Advocates)

### Leading Scholars
- **Diane Orentlicher (American University)** — expert on accountability, self-determination, and the former Yugoslavia
- **David Scheffer (Northwestern)** — former US Ambassador-at-Large for War Crimes Issues; coined "atrocity crimes"
- **Mónica Pinto (Argentina)** — former UN Special Rapporteur on judicial independence; expert on state responsibility
- **Ruti Teitel (NYU)** — transitional justice and human rights in conflict
- **Ryan Goodman (NYU, Just Security)** — US national security and international law; co-editor of Just Security blog

### Practitioners and Advocates
- **Kenneth Roth** — former longtime executive director of Human Rights Watch; prolific on Twitter (@KenRoth)
- **Kate Mackintosh (MSF)** — Médecins Sans Frontières expert on IHL and humanitarian access
- **Philippe Sands (UCL)** — international lawyer, author of "East West Street" (origins of genocide and crimes against humanity concepts)
- **Navi Pillay** — former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, former ICTR judge
- **Zeid Ra'ad Al Hussein** — former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights (2014-2018); outspoken on accountability

### Emerging Voices and Activists
- **Ilwad Elman (Somalia)** — peacebuilding and women's rights activist; follow for African perspectives
- **Tirana Hassan (Human Rights Watch)** — Executive Director; strong on Middle East and accountability
- **Budour Hassan** — Palestinian human rights lawyer; critical perspective on international law and power
- **Samuel Moyn (Yale)** — historian and legal scholar; critical view of human rights movement (useful counterpoint)

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Discipline Links)

### Literature and Human Rights
- **"The Sunflower" by Simon Wiesenthal** — ethics of forgiveness; relevant to transitional justice
- **"A Long Way Gone" by Ishmael Beah** — child soldiers memoir; connects to CRC, IHL, ICC jurisdiction
- **"Half of a Yellow Sun" by Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie** — Biafra war; IHL, humanitarian intervention, self-determination

### Film and Documentaries
- **"The Act of Killing" (2012)** — Indonesian mass killings; impunity, historical memory, transitional justice
- **"The Square" (2013)** — Egyptian revolution; right to protest, state violence, digital activism
- **"The Reckoning" (2009, PBS Frontline)** — ICC's first case (Thomas Lubanga); how international justice works in practice
- **"Nuremberg: Its Lesson for Today" (1948, restored 2010)** — original footage from Nuremberg trials

### Data Science and Human Rights
- **Syrian Archive** — https://syrianarchive.org/ — preserving open-source documentation of HR violations; digital evidence in international law
- **Bellingcat** — https://www.bellingcat.com/ — open-source investigative journalism; techniques used for HR documentation
- **Human Rights Data Analysis Group** — https://hrdag.org/ — statistical analysis of mass atrocities; truth commissions use their methods

### Philosophy and Ethics
- **"The Idea of Human Rights" by Charles Beitz** — philosophical foundations; what grounds human rights claims?
- **"Creating Capabilities" by Martha Nussbaum** — capabilities approach; alternative foundation for economic and social rights
- **"The Law of Peoples" by John Rawls** — political philosophy; how liberal principles extend to international realm

### Technology and Digital Rights
- **Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF)** — https://www.eff.org/ — digital privacy, free expression online; emerging human rights frontier
- **Access Now** — https://www.accessnow.org/ — digital rights advocacy; internet shutdowns, surveillance
- **Article 19** — https://www.article19.org/ — freedom of expression and information in digital age
