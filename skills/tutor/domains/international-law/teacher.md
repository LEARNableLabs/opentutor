# International Law and Human Rights — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 10 lessons (34%)
- **Socratic questions** — 6 lessons (21%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 5 lessons (17%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (14%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 2 lessons (7%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 2 lessons (7%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 38% accessible (1-2), 45% standard (3), 17% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 9: "What happens when treaties conflict?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 19: "What makes a war crime different from a crime against humanity?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 22: "Can the ICC prosecute anyone for atrocities?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 24: "When can states prosecute foreign officials?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 29: "Can corporations be held accountable for human rights abuses?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers international law and human rights, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: sovereignty, consent, international legal order, monism, dualism, hierarchy of norms, Universal Declaration of Human Rights, post-WWII order, Nuremberg principles, individual standing, locus standi, international legal personality, treaty formation, Vienna Convention, pacta sunt servanda (and 68 more).
