# Art History (Renaissance to Contemporary) — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Renaissance & Mannerism (1400-1600)**
   - Early Renaissance: perspective, humanism, classical revival
   - High Renaissance: Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael
   - Northern Renaissance: Dürer, van Eyck, Bosch
   - Mannerism: elongation, artifice, emotional intensity

2. **Baroque & Rococo (1600-1780)**
   - Baroque: drama, movement, chiaroscuro (Caravaggio, Bernini, Rembrandt)
   - Dutch Golden Age: Vermeer, Hals
   - Rococo: lightness, ornamentation, aristocratic themes

3. **Neoclassicism & Romanticism (1750-1850)**
   - Neoclassicism: return to classical order, political themes
   - Romanticism: emotion, nature, sublime, individual expression

4. **Realism & Impressionism (1850-1900)**
   - Realism: everyday life, social commentary
   - Impressionism: light, color, modern life, plein-air painting
   - Post-Impressionism: structure, symbolism, subjective color

5. **Early Modernism (1900-1940)**
   - Fauvism, Expressionism, Cubism, Futurism
   - Dada, Surrealism, Abstract art
   - Social Realism, Regionalism

6. **High Modernism (1940-1970)**
   - Abstract Expressionism, Color Field painting
   - Pop Art, Minimalism, Conceptual art
   - Performance art emergence

7. **Contemporary Art (1970-present)**
   - Postmodernism, appropriation, identity politics
   - Installation, video, new media
   - Globalization, decolonization, social practice

## Key Educational Resources

### Comprehensive Platforms
- **Smarthistory** (https://smarthistory.org/) — peer-reviewed art history content, movement-by-movement coverage
- **Khan Academy Art History** (https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/art-history) — structured video courses
- **Google Arts & Culture** (https://artsandculture.google.com/) — high-resolution images, virtual museum tours

### Museum Collections & Educational Resources
- **Metropolitan Museum of Art** (https://www.metmuseum.org/learn) — Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History
- **MoMA Learning** (https://www.moma.org/learn/moma_learning/) — modern and contemporary focus
- **Tate** (https://www.tate.org.uk/art) — British and international modern art
- **National Gallery, London** (https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/) — Renaissance to Post-Impressionism
- **Getty Museum** (https://www.getty.edu/education/) — educational resources, virtual exhibitions

### Academic Courses
- **Yale University Open Courses** — Introduction to Art History (Renaissance to Contemporary)
- **Coursera** — Modern and Contemporary Art and Design specialization (MoMA)
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** — Introduction to Western and Non-Western Art

### Textbooks & Key Sources
- Gardner's Art Through the Ages (standard survey text)
- Janson's History of Art
- Arnason's History of Modern Art
- Art Since 1900 (Foster, Krauss, Bois, Buchloh)

## Available Interactive Tools
- **Google Arts & Culture** — zoom into ultra-high-resolution images, virtual museum walks
- **Smarthistory 3D models** — architectural and sculptural works
- **Museum virtual tours** — Louvre, Uffizi, Prado, Vatican Museums
- **Timeline tools** — Art History Timeline (various museums)

## Pedagogical Approaches Found
- Movement-based organization (most common in surveys)
- Formal analysis emphasis (close looking, visual literacy)
- Contextual approach (historical, political, social context)
- Comparative method (contrasting works across periods)
- Artist biography integration
- Theme-based connections (patronage, gender, technology, globalization)
