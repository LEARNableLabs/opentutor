# Art History (Renaissance to Contemporary) — Resources

## Primary Educational Platforms

### Comprehensive Art History Resources

**Smarthistory** — https://smarthistory.org/
- Peer-reviewed, movement-by-movement coverage from ancient to contemporary
- Excellent videos with expert analysis
- Strong on context and close looking methodology
- Best single resource for this curriculum
- **Use for:** Every period; especially good for Renaissance, Baroque, Modernism

**Khan Academy Art History** — https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/art-history
- Structured video courses with practice exercises
- Partnership with major museums
- Good for beginners and intermediate learners
- **Use for:** Foundational concepts, movement introductions

**Google Arts & Culture** — https://artsandculture.google.com/
- Ultra-high-resolution images (gigapixel zoom)
- Virtual museum tours
- Curated collections and stories
- **Use for:** Close looking at specific works, virtual museum visits

## Museum Collections & Educational Resources

### Major Museums with Educational Content

**Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York** — https://www.metmuseum.org/learn
- Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History (comprehensive, scholarly)
- Collection covers all periods in curriculum
- Thematic essays and object entries
- **Use for:** Renaissance to 19th century especially strong

**Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York** — https://www.moma.org/learn/
- MoMA Learning platform with interactive courses
- Excellent on modern and contemporary art (Impressionism forward)
- Audio guides and artist interviews
- **Use for:** Lessons 15-33 (Impressionism through Contemporary)

**Tate (Tate Modern, Tate Britain)** — https://www.tate.org.uk/
- Strong British and international modern/contemporary collection
- Art Terms glossary (excellent for vocabulary)
- Artist rooms and thematic exhibitions
- **Use for:** Romanticism (Turner, Blake), Modern and Contemporary movements

**National Gallery, London** — https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/
- Renaissance to Post-Impressionism focus
- Excellent conservation and technical analysis resources
- Virtual tours and close-looking videos
- **Use for:** Lessons 1-18 (Renaissance through Post-Impressionism)

**J. Paul Getty Museum** — https://www.getty.edu/
- Getty Iris blog with scholarly but accessible essays
- Education resources and virtual exhibitions
- Strong on European art and photography
- **Use for:** Baroque, Neoclassicism, photography context

**Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam** — https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en
- Dutch Golden Age (Rembrandt, Vermeer)
- Ultra-high-resolution downloads (Rijksstudio)
- **Use for:** Lesson 8 (Vermeer and Dutch painting)

**Louvre, Paris** — https://www.louvre.fr/en
- Virtual tours and collection database
- Renaissance to 19th century French art
- **Use for:** Neoclassicism, Romanticism, French painting

**Guggenheim Museums** — https://www.guggenheim.org/
- Modern and contemporary art
- Collection database with educational resources
- **Use for:** Kandinsky, Abstract Expressionism, Minimalism

**Whitney Museum of American Art** — https://whitney.org/
- American art 20th-21st centuries
- Contemporary art focus
- **Use for:** Abstract Expressionism, Pop Art, Contemporary art

## Video Resources

### Lecture Series & Channels

**The Art Assignment (PBS Digital Studios)** — https://www.youtube.com/theartassignment
- Contemporary art focus
- Accessible, engaging format
- Explores "what is art?" questions
- **Use for:** Contemporary art lessons, conceptual challenges

**Great Art Explained** — https://www.youtube.com/c/GreatArtExplained
- 15-minute deep-dives on individual masterworks
- Excellent visual analysis and historical context
- Covers Renaissance to contemporary
- **Use for:** Supplementing specific lessons with focused work analysis

**National Gallery Video Guides** — https://www.youtube.com/nationalgallery
- Expert curators discussing works
- Close looking emphasis
- **Use for:** Renaissance through Post-Impressionism

**MoMA Videos** — https://www.youtube.com/MoMAvideos
- Artist interviews, exhibition walkthroughs
- Conservation and curatorial insights
- **Use for:** Modern and contemporary art

**Tate Shots** — https://www.youtube.com/user/tate
- Short artist interviews and exhibition previews
- Contemporary art focus
- **Use for:** Contemporary art, artist perspectives

## Textbooks & Key Reading

### Survey Textbooks

**Gardner's Art Through the Ages**
- Standard comprehensive survey
- Strong images and contextual essays
- Available in various editions (Western Art or Global volumes)
- **Use for:** Reference for all periods

**Janson's History of Art**
- Another standard survey text
- Good balance of formal analysis and context
- **Use for:** Alternative perspective to Gardner

**Art Since 1900: Modernism, Antimodernism, Postmodernism**
by Hal Foster, Rosalind Krauss, Yve-Alain Bois, Benjamin Buchloh
- Advanced, theoretical approach to modern/contemporary
- Year-by-year key moments
- **Use for:** Advanced students, Lessons 20-33

### Period-Specific Texts

**The Renaissance** (Oxford History of Art series)
https://global.oup.com/academic/
- Accessible scholarly treatment
- **Use for:** Lessons 1-5

**Baroque and Rococo** by Gauvin Alexander Bailey
- Comprehensive overview with global perspective
- **Use for:** Lessons 6-9

**Modern Art** by Amy Dempsey
- Movement-by-movement guide with timelines
- **Use for:** Lessons 20-29

**What Is Contemporary Art?** by Terry Smith
- Theoretical framework for contemporary
- **Use for:** Lessons 30-33

## Unexpected Connections & Rabbit Holes

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

**Science & Art**
- Color theory and optics (Impressionism)
- X-ray and infrared imaging revealing underpaintings
- Conservation science (how do we preserve art?)
- Neuroscience of aesthetic experience

**Technology & Art**
- Camera obscura and Renaissance perspective
- Photography's challenge to painting (19th century)
- Film and video art (20th century)
- AI-generated art and NFTs (contemporary)

**Politics & Art**
- Propaganda and state patronage (Neoclassicism, Socialist Realism)
- Censorship and freedom of expression (Dada, Guerrilla Girls)
- Cultural heritage and repatriation debates
- Art market and financialization

### Contemporary Relevance

**Street Art & Graffiti**
- Connection to Dada, Pop Art, Conceptual art
- Banksy, Jean-Michel Basquiat, Keith Haring
- https://www.streetartbio.com/

**Meme Culture**
- Appropriation, remix, democratization
- Connection to Dada absurdity, Pop Art mass culture

**Museum Controversies**
- Decolonization movements
- Sackler family and opioid crisis (museum funding ethics)
- Who gets exhibited? Canon revision ongoing

**Climate Art**
- Olafur Eliasson's ice blocks
- Agnes Denes's "Wheatfield — A Confrontation"
- Contemporary artists addressing environmental crisis

## For Advanced Exploration

**Primary Sources**
- Artist manifestos (Futurist Manifesto, Surrealist Manifesto, etc.)

- John Berger, *Ways of Seeing* (accessible Marxist art criticism)
- Linda Nochlin, "Why Have There Been No Great Women Artists?"

- Rosalind Krauss, "Sculpture in the Expanded Field"

**Podcasts**
- *The Art Newspaper Podcast*
- *The Lonely Palette* (close looking at individual works)
- *Art Matters* (National Gallery of Art)
