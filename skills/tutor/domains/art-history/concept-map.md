# Art History (Renaissance to Contemporary) — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Linear perspective** — mathematical system for creating illusion of depth on flat surface
2. **Humanism** — Renaissance philosophy emphasizing human potential and classical learning
3. **Fresco technique** — painting on wet plaster, crucial for monumental Renaissance works
4. **Oil painting technique** — Northern innovation allowing glazing, detail, and luminosity. Depends on: basic painting knowledge
5. **Symbolism** — use of objects/motifs to convey deeper meaning, especially in Northern Renaissance
6. **Mannerism** — late Renaissance style emphasizing artifice and emotional intensity. Depends on: classical Renaissance norms to break from
7. **Formal analysis** — method of analyzing artworks through visual elements (line, color, composition). Depends on: familiarity with basic art terminology
8. **Chiaroscuro** — dramatic contrast of light and shadow. Depends on: understanding of pictorial space
9. **Tenebrism** — extreme form of chiaroscuro (Caravaggio). Depends on: chiaroscuro
10. **Baroque naturalism** — dramatic realism in service of religious or political themes. Depends on: Counter-Reformation context
11. **Genre painting** — scenes from everyday life, especially in Dutch Golden Age. Depends on: understanding of painting hierarchies
12. **Rococo style** — 18th century decorative, ornamental style emphasizing pleasure and aristocratic leisure
13. **Neoclassicism** — return to classical order, clarity, and political themes. Depends on: knowledge of classical antiquity, Enlightenment values
14. **Romanticism** — emphasis on emotion, individual expression, nature, and the sublime. Depends on: Neoclassicism as reaction against
15. **The sublime** — aesthetic concept of awe mixed with terror in face of nature's power
16. **Realism** — 19th century movement depicting ordinary life without idealization. Depends on: Romantic idealism to reject
17. **Salon system** — official French exhibition system that avant-garde artists challenged
18. **Impressionism** — capturing fleeting light effects and modern life through broken color. Depends on: Realism, plein-air practice
19. **Plein-air painting** — painting outdoors to capture natural light directly
20. **Broken color** — technique of placing unmixed colors side-by-side. Depends on: color theory
21. **Post-Impressionism** — diverse responses to Impressionism emphasizing structure, symbolism, or subjective color. Depends on: Impressionism
22. **Cubism** — fracturing form into geometric planes, showing multiple perspectives. Depends on: rejection of single-point perspective
23. **Fourth dimension** — concept influencing Cubist exploration of time and space
24. **Expressionism** — using distortion and intense color to convey emotional/psychological states. Depends on: Post-Impressionist precedents
25. **Fauvism** — "wild beasts," using non-naturalistic, vibrant color. Depends on: color as independent element
26. **Dada** — anti-art movement rejecting logic and convention (WWI response). Depends on: understanding of artistic traditions to reject
27. **Readymades** — everyday objects presented as art (Duchamp). Depends on: Dada philosophy
28. **Surrealism** — visualizing unconscious, dreams, and irrational. Depends on: Dada, Freudian psychology
29. **Automatism** — creating art without conscious control. Depends on: Surrealist theory
30. **Abstraction** — art without representational reference. Depends on: progressive simplification in earlier modern art
31. **Non-objective art** — completely abstract, no reference to visual world
32. **Abstract Expressionism** — gestural abstraction emphasizing spontaneity and existential meaning. Depends on: Surrealist automatism, abstraction
33. **Action painting** — physical, gestural painting process. Depends on: Abstract Expressionism
34. **Pop Art** — incorporating mass media imagery and consumer culture. Depends on: high/low art distinction to blur
35. **Minimalism** — reduction to essential forms, industrial materials. Depends on: abstraction, rejection of expressionism
36. **Phenomenology** — philosophy of perception influencing Minimalist viewer experience
37. **Conceptual art** — prioritizing idea over object. Depends on: Minimalism, Duchamp's legacy
38. **Institutional critique** — questioning art world structures. Depends on: Conceptual art
39. **Postmodernism** — challenging modernist certainties through irony, appropriation, pluralism. Depends on: modernist canon
40. **Appropriation** — borrowing/re-contextualizing existing images. Depends on: Postmodernist strategies
41. **Installation art** — immersive, site-specific environments. Depends on: expansion of what counts as art
42. **Site-specificity** — work designed for particular location. Depends on: installation practice
43. **Global contemporary** — recognition of non-Western art practices and decolonization. Depends on: critique of Western-centric canon
44. **Social practice** — art as social engagement and activism. Depends on: expanded field of art, participatory aesthetics
45. **Relational aesthetics** — art creating social interactions. Depends on: social practice

## Dependencies

### Historical Sequences (Must Learn in Order)
- **Renaissance → Mannerism** — need classical harmony to understand its breakdown
- **Neoclassicism → Romanticism** — Romanticism reacts against Neoclassical order
- **Realism → Impressionism → Post-Impressionism** — each builds on and reacts to previous
- **Dada → Surrealism** — Surrealism inherits Dada's anti-rational stance but channels it differently
- **Abstract Expressionism → Minimalism → Conceptual art** — progressive dematerialization
- **Modernism → Postmodernism** — need modernist certainties to understand Postmodernist critique

### Technical Dependencies
- **Linear perspective** required for understanding how later artists break perspective rules (Cubism, abstraction)
- **Chiaroscuro** is foundation for **tenebrism** and Baroque drama
- **Oil painting technique** enables Northern Renaissance detail and later glazing traditions
- **Broken color** (Impressionism) leads to **subjective color** (Post-Impressionism, Fauvism)
- **Automatism** (Surrealism) influences **action painting** (Abstract Expressionism)

### Conceptual Dependencies
- **Formal analysis** required throughout — students need this early to discuss any artwork
- **Salon system** understanding necessary to grasp avant-garde strategies and rejection
- **Readymades** concept crucial for understanding Conceptual art and institutional critique
- **Phenomenology** helps understand Minimalist and installation art's emphasis on viewer experience
- **Global contemporary** requires understanding Western canon that's being decentered

## Bottlenecks

1. **Formal analysis** (Lesson 5) — students must be comfortable describing what they see before interpreting meaning. If weak here, pause for practice.

2. **Post-Impressionism** (Lesson 18) — gateway to modernism. Students need to grasp how Cézanne, van Gogh, Gauguin diverge in different directions (structure, emotion, symbolism) to understand early 20th century explosions.

3. **Abstraction** (Lesson 24) — major conceptual shift. If students struggle with "what does non-representational art mean?", address philosophy of abstract art before proceeding to high modernism.

4. **Conceptual art** (Lesson 28) — hardest shift for many students. Idea that "art doesn't have to be beautiful or even visual" challenges deep assumptions. May need extra time.

5. **Postmodernism** (Lesson 30) — requires solid grasp of modernist principles to understand what's being questioned. Review modernist assumptions if needed.

## Common Misconceptions

### Technical Misconceptions
- **"Impressionism is just pretty pictures"** — students miss the radical break with academic tradition, the emphasis on modern life, and optical color theory
- **"Abstract art is random/anyone could do it"** — need to teach deliberate composition, color relationships, historical context
- **"Perspective is just realism"** — perspective is a convention, not natural vision; other cultures used different systems

### Historical Misconceptions
- **"Art movements follow neat chronological boundaries"** — overlapping, contradictory movements coexist; periodization is retrospective scholarly construction
- **"Modern art progressively got better/worse"** — avoid teleological thinking; each movement responds to specific context
- **"The Renaissance invented realism"** — various cultures achieved naturalism; Renaissance codified specific techniques like linear perspective

### Conceptual Misconceptions
- **"Contemporary art has 'gone too far' from tradition"** — contemporary art engages tradition, just differently; need historical context for each innovation
- **"Art history is just memorizing artists and dates"** — it's about understanding visual culture, context, and ideas
- **"Only painting and sculpture count as art"** — expanded field includes performance, installation, new media, social practice

### Movement-Specific Misconceptions
- **"Baroque is just overly ornate"** — miss the emotional and religious intensity, the theatrical engagement with viewer
- **"Romanticism is just sentimental"** — miss the philosophical depth, political dimensions, engagement with sublime
- **"Pop Art just copies advertisements"** — miss the irony, critique of consumer culture, appropriation strategy
- **"Minimalism is boring or easy"** — miss phenomenological engagement, relationship to space, industrial labor challenge to expressive genius

## Critical Junctures (Where Students Often Struggle)

1. **Transition to abstraction** (Lessons 20-24) — "why abandon representation?" needs solid grounding in progressive simplification from Post-Impressionism forward

2. **Understanding Conceptual art** (Lesson 28) — many students resistant to dematerialization; need to build up through Minimalism and Duchamp's readymades

3. **Engaging with contemporary art** (Lessons 30-33) — can feel chaotic or meaningless without framework; emphasize critical tools and openness to new forms

4. **Distinguishing similar movements** — students confuse Impressionism/Post-Impressionism, Cubism/Futurism, Abstract Expressionism/Color Field, etc. Use comparative analysis throughout.
