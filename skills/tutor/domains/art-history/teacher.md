# Art history — Renaissance to contemporary — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 11 lessons (33%)
- **Socratic questions** — 8 lessons (24%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 6 lessons (18%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 4 lessons (12%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 4 lessons (12%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 30% accessible (1-2), 45% standard (3), 24% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 4: "What happens when classical harmony gives way to emotional intensity?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 18: "How did Post-Impressionists break with visual reality?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 20: "Why did Picasso and Braque fracture form into geometric planes?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 22: "What happens when artists reject logic and embrace absurdity?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 24: "When did painting break free from representation entirely?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers art history — renaissance to contemporary, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: linear perspective, humanism, classical revival, Michelangelo, fresco technique, biblical narrative, human anatomy, oil painting technique, symbolism, domestic interiors, religious reform, Mannerism, elongation, artifice, courtly culture (and 102 more).
