# Mechatronics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Sensors & Transducers
- Position, velocity, acceleration sensors (encoders, potentiometers, IMUs)
- Force and pressure sensors (load cells, strain gauges)
- Temperature, proximity, and optical sensors
- Signal conditioning and noise reduction

### 2. Actuators & Motion Control
- DC motors, stepper motors, servo motors
- Motor drivers and H-bridges
- Pneumatic and hydraulic actuators
- Motion profiles and trajectory planning

### 3. Microcontrollers & Embedded Systems
- Arduino, Raspberry Pi, STM32 platforms
- Digital I/O, PWM, ADC/DAC
- Real-time operating systems (RTOS) basics
- Communication protocols (UART, I2C, SPI, CAN)

### 4. Control Systems
- Feedback control fundamentals (PID)
- System modeling and transfer functions
- Stability and tuning
- State-space methods (intermediate level)

### 5. Mechanical Systems Integration
- Kinematics and dynamics of mechanisms
- Power transmission (gears, belts, linkages)
- Mechanical design for automation
- Tolerance and backlash management

### 6. System Integration & Design
- System architecture and modularity
- Signal flow and interface design
- Testing, debugging, and validation
- Real-world applications (robotics, automation, automotive)

## Research Notes

Mechatronics is inherently interdisciplinary — success requires balancing three domains (mechanical, electrical, software) without getting lost in any one. The intermediate level should assume comfort with basic electronics (Ohm's law, transistors) and programming (loops, functions), but may need review of control theory fundamentals.

Key pedagogical challenge: students often know pieces (e.g., Arduino programming OR control theory) but struggle with *integration*. Curriculum should emphasize end-to-end systems thinking from day one.
