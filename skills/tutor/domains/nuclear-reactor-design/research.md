# Nuclear Reactor Design — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Reactor Physics Fundamentals**
   - Neutron transport and diffusion theory
   - Criticality and reactivity control
   - Burnup and fuel cycles
   - Reactor kinetics and dynamics

2. **Core Design Principles**
   - Lattice design and fuel assembly geometry
   - Power distribution and peaking factors
   - Core loading patterns
   - Reactivity coefficients and stability

3. **Thermal-Hydraulics**
   - Heat generation and removal
   - Single-phase and two-phase flow
   - Critical heat flux and departure from nucleate boiling (DNB)
   - Natural circulation and passive cooling

4. **Safety Systems and Analysis**
   - Defense-in-depth philosophy
   - Accident scenarios (LOCA, ATWS, etc.)
   - Emergency core cooling systems (ECCS)
   - Containment design
   - Probabilistic risk assessment (PRA)

5. **Materials and Fuel**
   - Fuel types (UO2, MOX, TRISO)
   - Cladding materials (Zircaloy, SiC)
   - Structural materials
   - Radiation damage and material degradation

6. **Control and Instrumentation**
   - Control rod design and positioning
   - Reactor protection systems
   - In-core instrumentation
   - Digital I&C systems

7. **Advanced Reactor Concepts**
   - Generation IV designs (SFR, MSR, VHTR, etc.)
   - Small modular reactors (SMRs)
   - Passive safety features
   - Fusion-fission hybrids

## Key Sources

### Academic Courses
- **MIT OCW 22.05**: Neutron Interactions and Applications
- **MIT OCW 22.06**: Engineering of Nuclear Systems
- **MIT OCW 22.091**: Nuclear Reactor Safety

### Standard Textbooks
- **Lamarsh & Baratta**: "Introduction to Nuclear Engineering" (3rd ed.) — comprehensive undergraduate text
- **Todreas & Kazimi**: "Nuclear Systems Vol. I & II" — thermal-hydraulics and design fundamentals
- **Duderstadt & Hamilton**: "Nuclear Reactor Analysis" — classic reactor physics text
- **Stacey**: "Nuclear Reactor Physics" — modern treatment with computational methods
- **Glasstone & Sesonske**: "Nuclear Reactor Engineering" — comprehensive design reference
- **NRC**: "Reactor Concepts Manual" — practical regulatory perspective

### Online Resources
- **IAEA Nuclear Energy Series**: Technical documents and safety guides
- **NRC NUREG series**: Regulatory guides and technical reports
- **ANS (American Nuclear Society)**: Conference proceedings and journals
- **World Nuclear Association**: Educational materials and technology briefings
- **Khan Academy**: Basic nuclear physics foundations

- **PhysicsForums Nuclear Engineering subforum**: Active community for Q&A

### Video Lectures
- **Illinois EnergyProf (YouTube)**: Nuclear engineering fundamentals

## Available Resources Quality

**Excellent** — MIT OCW provides complete courses with lecture notes, problem sets, and exams. Textbooks by Lamarsh and Todreas are industry standards. IAEA and NRC provide authoritative technical documents.

**Good** — Interactive simulation tools exist but require specialized software. Some are open-source (OpenMC, SERPENT).

**Moderate** — General public understanding of reactor design is limited; most resources assume engineering background. Video content less abundant than for pure physics topics.

## Gaps Identified

- Limited interactive visualizations for neutron transport (mostly static diagrams)
- Few publicly accessible reactor design case studies with full engineering details
- Proprietary codes dominate industry; open-source alternatives growing but less mature
- Advanced reactor concepts still in development; limited operational data
