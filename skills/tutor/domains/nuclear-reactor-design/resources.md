# Nuclear Reactor Design — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Lamarsh & Baratta, "Introduction to Nuclear Engineering" (3rd ed.)** — The standard undergraduate text. Covers reactor physics, thermal-hydraulics, safety, and fuel cycles comprehensively. Excellent problem sets. Ideal for intermediate students — rigorous but accessible. [Pearson]

- **Todreas & Kazimi, "Nuclear Systems Vol. I: Thermal Hydraulic Fundamentals"** — Definitive reference on reactor thermal-hydraulics. Covers single-phase flow, two-phase flow, heat transfer, and subchannel analysis. More advanced than Lamarsh; use selectively for thermal-hydraulics module. [CRC Press]

- **Todreas & Kazimi, "Nuclear Systems Vol. II: Elements of Thermal Hydraulic Design"** — Companion volume focusing on design applications, LOCA analysis, natural circulation. Excellent for safety module. [CRC Press]

- **Duderstadt & Hamilton, "Nuclear Reactor Analysis"** — Classic text on reactor physics. Strong on diffusion and transport theory, criticality calculations, and perturbation theory. Mathematical depth appropriate for intermediate students. [Wiley]

- **Stacey, "Nuclear Reactor Physics" (3rd ed.)** — Modern treatment incorporating computational methods. Covers Monte Carlo, deterministic methods, and burnup analysis. Good bridge to research-level work. [Wiley-VCH]

- **Glasstone & Sesonske, "Nuclear Reactor Engineering" (4th ed.)** — Comprehensive design reference covering reactor types, core design, materials, and safety. Dated but encyclopedic. [Springer]

- **OpenMC** — Open-source Monte Carlo reactor physics code with Python API. Best for students who want hands-on simulation experience. https://docs.openmc.org/

- **SCALE** — Oak Ridge comprehensive modeling suite for criticality, shielding, and depletion. Free for US institutions. https://www.ornl.gov/scale

- **RELAP5** — Thermal-hydraulics system code for transient analysis. Free for academic use through OECD/NEA. https://www.oecd-nea.org/tools/abstract/detail/nea-1739/

- **Reactor Lab (online)** — Simple visual reactor simulator showing reactivity control and power response. Good for introductory understanding. http://reactorlab.net/

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Nuclear Forensics** — Using reactor physics and burnup calculations to trace nuclear materials back to their production reactor. Connects to nonproliferation.

- **Space Nuclear Power** — Kilopower reactors for Mars missions, nuclear thermal propulsion. Same physics, completely different engineering constraints.

- **Medical Isotope Production** — Research reactors optimized for producing medical isotopes (Mo-99, I-131). Unusual mission drives unusual design.

- **Nuclear Archaeology** — Dating ancient artifacts using reactor-produced isotopes. Connects neutron physics to archaeology.

- **Oklo Natural Reactor** — 2-billion-year-old natural fission reactor discovered in Gabon. Criticality achieved naturally. Mind-bending implications for geological time and neutron physics.

- **Nuclear Batteries (RTGs)** — Radioisotope thermoelectric generators using decay heat. No fission, but similar heat-to-electricity conversion. Powers deep space probes.

- **Accelerator-Driven Subcritical Reactors** — External neutron source drives subcritical core. Inherently safe but requires particle accelerator. Research frontier.

- **Traveling Wave Reactors** — Fission wave propagates through depleted uranium, "breeding" fuel as it goes. TerraPower's ambitious design. Connects burnup, breeding, and reactor control.

## Topic-Specific Learning Resources

### For visual learners

- **Reactor cross-section diagrams** — cutaway views of PWR, BWR, CANDU, fast reactors (available in all textbooks and NRC materials)
- **Power distribution visualizations** — 3D plots of power density (use MATLAB/Python to generate from example data)
- **Flow regime maps** — visual classification of two-phase flow patterns (Todreas & Kazimi Vol. I)
- **Nuclear fuel cycle flowcharts** — from mining to disposal (World Nuclear Association)

### For hands-on learners

- **OpenMC tutorials** — build and run simple reactor models
- **Python reactor physics** — write simple diffusion solvers, burnup trackers
- **Visit a reactor** — many universities with research reactors offer tours (MIT, Penn State, Oregon State)
- **Operator training simulators** — some utilities offer public demonstrations

### For theory-first learners

- **Derive key equations** — diffusion equation from transport, six-factor formula, reactivity coefficients
- **Read seminal papers** — Fermi's reactor theory papers, Weinberg on molten salt reactors
- **Work through Lamarsh problem sets** — comprehensive and well-structured

## Curated Reading List (for deep dives)

1. **NRC Reactor Concepts Manual** — best starting point for overview
2. **Lamarsh & Baratta Chapters 1-7** — core reactor physics
3. **MIT OCW 22.06 lecture notes** — design synthesis
4. **Todreas & Kazimi Vol. I Chapters 1-10** — thermal-hydraulics fundamentals
5. **NRC NUREG/CR-2800 PRA Procedures Guide** — safety analysis methods
6. **IAEA-TECDOC-1391 on SMRs** — advanced reactor concepts
7. **Glasstone & Sesonske for historical context** — how we got here

## Where to Go Next

After completing this curriculum, students ready for more can pursue:

- **Advanced reactor physics**: transport theory, Monte Carlo methods, lattice codes
- **Multiphysics coupling**: neutronics + thermal-hydraulics + structural mechanics
- **Fuel cycle analysis**: economics, proliferation resistance, waste minimization
- **Advanced safety analysis**: severe accidents, source term analysis, uncertainty quantification
- **Specialized topics**: fast reactors, molten salt reactors, fusion-fission hybrids
- **Experimental work**: research reactor experiments, material testing
- **Regulatory pathway**: NRC regulations, licensing, quality assurance

Graduate programs to consider: MIT, Michigan, Wisconsin, UC Berkeley, Texas A&M, NCSU, Penn State, Idaho, Tennessee.
