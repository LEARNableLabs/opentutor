# Logic — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics Covered

This curriculum traces the development of logic from ancient Greece through the 20th century, covering:

1. **Ancient Logic** — Aristotle's syllogistic logic, categorical propositions, the Organon
2. **Medieval Logic** — Scholastic developments, paradoxes, supposition theory
3. **Boolean Algebra** — George Boole's algebraic approach to logic
4. **Frege's Revolution** — First-order predicate logic, quantifiers, formal proof systems
5. **Hilbert's Program** — Formalism, axiomatization, the quest for consistency
6. **Gödel's Completeness Theorem** — First-order logic is complete
7. **Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems** — Limits of formal systems, undecidability
8. **Aftermath** — Church-Turing thesis, Tarski's undefinability, modern implications

## Key Educational Sources Found

### University Courses & Lecture Notes

- **History of Logic from Aristotle to Gödel** (historyoflogic.com) — Comprehensive survey of logical problems from Aristotle through modern developments
- **MIT Logic II** (24.242) — Covers computability theory and Gödel's incompleteness theorems extensively
- **MIT Theory of Computation** (18.404J) — Includes Gödel's first incompleteness theorem
- **Gödel's Basic Logic Course at Notre Dame** (arxiv.org/abs/1705.02601) — Historical manuscript covering completeness, decidability, independence of axioms, natural deduction
- **Computational Logic Lecture Notes** by Michael Kohlhase (kwarc.info) — Traces logic from Greek philosophy through Aristotle to Frege

### Textbooks & Open Educational Resources

- **"Incompleteness and Computability: An Open Introduction to Gödel's Theorems"** — Open Logic Project (ic.openlogicproject.org)
- **"A Course in Mathematical Logic for Mathematicians"** by Manin — Includes chapters on completeness and incompleteness
- **Handbook of the History of Logic** — Multi-volume series addressing research communities in logic, history, and philosophy

### Interactive Tools & Proof Assistants

- **Natural Deduction Proof Editor** (proofs.openlogicproject.org) — Fitch-style proof checker for propositional and first-order logic
- **Logictools** (logictools.org) — First-order predicate logic toolkit, runs in browser via Wasm
- **LogicProof** (arxiv.org/abs/2607.04321) — Web-based theorem prover for natural deduction and sequent calculus
- **The Logic Machine** (logic.tamu.edu) — Daemon proof checker with hints for students

### Key Researchers & Historical Figures

The "five greatest logicians of history":
1. **Aristotle** — Founded formal logic with syllogistic reasoning
2. **George Boole** — Algebraic logic, foundation of modern computation
3. **Gottlob Frege** — Predicate logic, quantifiers, modern formal systems
4. **Alfred Tarski** — Model theory, semantic conception of truth
5. **Kurt Gödel** — Completeness and incompleteness theorems, limits of formal systems

Additional key figures: Hilbert (formalism, proof theory), Church (lambda calculus, undecidability), Turing (computability)

## Pedagogical Approach Considerations

- **Historical narrative** provides motivation and context for technical developments
- **Interactive proof tools** allow hands-on practice with formal systems
- **Balance theory and practice** — understanding both what Gödel proved and why it matters
- **Connect to computer science** — Boolean logic, computability, decidability
- **Emphasize revolutionary moments** — Frege's quantifiers, Gödel's diagonal argument
