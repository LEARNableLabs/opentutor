# Nutrition Science — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Cellular respiration** — how cells extract energy from nutrients to produce ATP
2. **Metabolic energy** — the relationship between food calories and cellular work
3. **Caloric measurement** — how energy content of macronutrients is quantified
4. **Digestion and absorption** — breakdown of food and transport of nutrients into the body
5. **Basal metabolic rate (BMR)** — baseline energy expenditure for maintaining vital functions
6. **Anabolic vs catabolic pathways** — building up (storage) vs breaking down (release) of molecules
7. **Fed vs fasted state** — hormonal and metabolic transitions between nutrient availability states
8. **Glycolysis** — breakdown of glucose to pyruvate with net ATP production. Depends on: cellular respiration
9. **Citric acid cycle** — central oxidative pathway producing electron carriers. Depends on: glycolysis
10. **Glycogen metabolism** — storage and mobilization of glucose polymers. Depends on: glycolysis, fed/fasted states
11. **Gluconeogenesis** — synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors. Depends on: glycolysis, fasted state
12. **Dietary fiber** — non-digestible carbohydrates and their effects on metabolism and microbiome. Depends on: digestion
13. **Beta-oxidation** — stepwise breakdown of fatty acids to acetyl-CoA. Depends on: cellular respiration
14. **Ketogenesis** — production of ketone bodies from acetyl-CoA during low carbohydrate availability. Depends on: beta-oxidation, fasted state
15. **Lipogenesis** — synthesis of fatty acids from excess carbohydrate or protein. Depends on: fed state, citric acid cycle
16. **Dietary fat types** — saturated, unsaturated, trans, and essential fatty acids and their metabolic effects
17. **Protein digestion** — breakdown of dietary proteins to amino acids. Depends on: digestion
18. **Amino acid catabolism** — use of amino acids for energy and gluconeogenesis. Depends on: gluconeogenesis, citric acid cycle
19. **Essential amino acids** — amino acids that must be obtained from diet. Depends on: protein digestion
20. **Vitamins as coenzymes** — role of B vitamins and other vitamins in metabolic enzyme function. Depends on: glycolysis, citric acid cycle, beta-oxidation
21. **Mineral cofactors** — role of minerals (especially iron) in electron transport and metabolic enzymes. Depends on: cellular respiration
22. **Circadian metabolism** — time-dependent metabolic regulation and meal timing effects. Depends on: fed/fasted states
23. **Metabolic flexibility** — ability to switch between glucose and fat oxidation. Depends on: glycolysis, beta-oxidation, ketogenesis
24. **Insulin signaling** — hormonal control of glucose uptake and metabolic state transitions. Depends on: fed/fasted states, glycogen metabolism
25. **Insulin resistance** — impaired cellular response to insulin leading to metabolic dysfunction. Depends on: insulin signaling
26. **Evidence-based nutrition** — evaluating nutritional claims using scientific reasoning and research hierarchy

## Dependencies

### Foundational metabolic understanding
- **Anabolic and catabolic pathways** require understanding cellular respiration and metabolic energy, because these define the direction of energy flow (storage vs release)
- **Fed vs fasted state** builds on anabolic/catabolic concepts and introduces hormonal control, because metabolic pathways switch direction based on nutrient availability

### Carbohydrate metabolism sequence
- **Glycolysis** is the entry point for all carbohydrate metabolism and must be understood before downstream pathways
- **Citric acid cycle** depends on glycolysis because it processes the pyruvate output from glycolysis
- **Glycogen metabolism** makes sense only after understanding glycolysis and fed/fasted states, because it explains glucose storage and mobilization
- **Gluconeogenesis** is the reverse of glycolysis and requires understanding both glycolysis and the fasted state when it becomes active

### Fat metabolism integration
- **Beta-oxidation** parallels glycolysis as a catabolic pathway and feeds into the citric acid cycle via acetyl-CoA
- **Ketogenesis** depends on beta-oxidation because ketones are made when acetyl-CoA accumulates faster than the citric acid cycle can process it (during fasting or very low carb intake)
- **Lipogenesis** is the anabolic counterpart to beta-oxidation and requires understanding the fed state and citric acid cycle intermediates

### Protein metabolism connections
- **Amino acid catabolism** connects to both gluconeogenesis (for glucogenic amino acids) and ketogenesis (for ketogenic amino acids)
- **Essential amino acids** concept depends on understanding protein digestion but is independent of energy metabolism

### Micronutrient integration
- **Vitamins as coenzymes** cannot be understood without first learning the metabolic pathways (glycolysis, citric acid cycle, beta-oxidation) where they function
- **Mineral cofactors** especially iron, are crucial for cellular respiration's electron transport chain

### Applied nutrition concepts
- **Circadian metabolism** builds on fed/fasted state transitions and adds temporal regulation
- **Metabolic flexibility** requires understanding both glycolysis and beta-oxidation as alternative fuel pathways
- **Insulin signaling and resistance** tie together glucose metabolism, glycogen storage, and fed/fasted states as a unified hormonal control system

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Acetyl-CoA hub
Acetyl-CoA is the central metabolic intermediate where all three macronutrient pathways converge:
- Carbohydrates → pyruvate → acetyl-CoA (via pyruvate dehydrogenase)
- Fats → acetyl-CoA (via beta-oxidation)
- Proteins → acetyl-CoA (for ketogenic amino acids)

Understanding this convergence is essential for grasping metabolic integration.

### Fed/fasted hormonal switch
The fed vs fasted state transition (controlled primarily by insulin and glucagon) determines which pathways are active:
- Fed state: insulin high → glycolysis, glycogen synthesis, lipogenesis active
- Fasted state: glucagon high → gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis, beta-oxidation, ketogenesis active

This hormonal regulation must be clear before understanding dietary interventions.

### Oxidative phosphorylation as the energy sink
All catabolic pathways ultimately produce electron carriers (NADH, FADH2) that feed into the electron transport chain. Understanding that cellular respiration is the final common pathway for ATP production prevents confusion about why different fuels (glucose, fat, amino acids) all ultimately produce ATP.

## Common Misconception Prevention

The concept map deliberately sequences topics to prevent common errors:

1. **Energy balance before specific pathways** — students understand *why* metabolism exists (to produce ATP) before diving into *how* each pathway works
2. **Catabolic before anabolic** — breakdown pathways (glycolysis, beta-oxidation) are introduced before synthesis pathways (lipogenesis, gluconeogenesis) to match physiological priority
3. **Integration points highlighted** — acetyl-CoA hub and hormonal regulation are explicitly called out to prevent "silo thinking" where students see pathways as independent
4. **Micronutrients placed after pathways** — vitamins and minerals are introduced only after the pathways they participate in are understood, preventing rote memorization without mechanistic understanding

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic cell biology** — needed for understanding cellular respiration, membrane transport, and organelle function (especially mitochondria)
- **Basic organic chemistry** — needed for understanding functional groups, oxidation/reduction, and basic reaction mechanisms
- **Enzyme function** — needed for understanding how catalysts enable metabolic reactions and how cofactors/coenzymes work

Students lacking these prerequisites will struggle with:
- The mechanism of oxidative phosphorylation (requires understanding of electron transfer)
- Why vitamins function as coenzymes (requires understanding enzyme catalysis)
- The structural differences between macronutrients (requires basic organic chemistry)
