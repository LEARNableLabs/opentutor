# Nutrition Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Gropper, Smith & Carr, "Advanced Nutrition and Human Metabolism" (8th edition, 2021)**
  - Comprehensive coverage of biochemical pathways and nutrient metabolism
  - Excellent for intermediate learners; assumes basic biochemistry background
  - Strong integration of physiology with molecular mechanisms
  - https://www.cengage.com/c/advanced-nutrition-and-human-metabolism-8e-gropper/9781337679459/

- **Gibney, Lanham-New, Cassidy & Vorster, "Introduction to Human Nutrition" (3rd edition, 2019)**
  - Nutrition Society's official textbook
  - Balanced coverage of biochemistry, physiology, and public health perspectives
  - Intermediate level, accessible writing
  - https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Introduction+to+Human+Nutrition%2C+3rd+Edition-p-9781118660959

- **Berg, Tymoczko & Stryer, "Biochemistry" (9th edition, 2019)**
  - Canonical biochemistry reference
  - In-depth coverage of metabolic pathways with excellent diagrams
  - Use for detailed pathway mechanisms
  - https://www.macmillanlearning.com/college/us/product/Biochemistry/p/1319114652

- **Whitney & Rolfes, "Understanding Nutrition" (15th edition, 2018)**
  - More accessible than Gropper; good for reviewing fundamentals
  - Strong practical application sections
  - https://www.cengage.com/c/understanding-nutrition-15e-whitney/9781337392693/

### Open Educational Resources

- **MIT OCW 7.06 Cell Biology (Spring 2007)**

  - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK22436/

## Recommended Study Flow

1. **Start with textbook chapters** (Gropper or Gibney) for foundational understanding
2. **Watch Khan Academy videos** for visual reinforcement of pathways
3. **Explore KEGG pathways** to see how pathways integrate
4. **Read NIH fact sheets** for evidence-based micronutrient information
5. **Use Examine.com** to evaluate specific nutrition claims or interventions
6. **Read primary literature** on topics of interest (via PubMed)
7. **Apply learning** with Cronometer or dietary analysis tools
8. **Deepen understanding** with textbooks like Berg for detailed mechanisms

## Warning: Sources to Approach with Caution

- **Social media influencers** without scientific credentials — look for peer-reviewed publications
- **Supplement company blogs** — conflict of interest in promoting products
- **Documentary films on nutrition** — often cherry-pick evidence; verify claims with primary literature
- **Popular diet books** — may oversimplify or misrepresent metabolic science; cross-reference with textbooks

Always prioritize peer-reviewed research, systematic reviews, and textbooks over blogs and popular media.
