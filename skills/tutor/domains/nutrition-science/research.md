# Nutrition Science — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Macronutrient Metabolism
- Carbohydrate metabolism (glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, glycogen)
- Lipid metabolism (beta-oxidation, ketogenesis, lipogenesis)
- Protein metabolism (amino acid catabolism, protein synthesis, nitrogen balance)
- Integration and regulation of metabolic pathways

### 2. Micronutrients and Cofactors
- Vitamins (fat-soluble: A, D, E, K; water-soluble: B-complex, C)
- Minerals (macro: Ca, Mg, P; trace: Fe, Zn, Se, I)
- Role in enzymatic reactions and metabolic regulation

### 3. Energy Balance and Body Composition
- Caloric intake vs expenditure (BMR, TDEE, TEF)
- Body composition measurement and significance
- Metabolic adaptation and set-point theory

### 4. Dietary Patterns and Health Outcomes
- Macronutrient distribution (low-carb, low-fat, high-protein diets)
- Meal timing and frequency (intermittent fasting, circadian rhythm)
- Whole foods vs processed foods
- Evidence-based dietary guidelines

### 5. Clinical Applications
- Metabolic disorders (diabetes, metabolic syndrome, obesity)
- Nutrient deficiencies and toxicities
- Personalized nutrition and nutrigenomics

## Key Sources

### Academic Textbooks
- **Gropper & Smith, "Advanced Nutrition and Human Metabolism"** (8th ed, 2021) — comprehensive coverage of biochemical pathways and nutrient metabolism

## Teaching Considerations

This intermediate curriculum assumes:
- Basic biology (cell structure, enzymes)
- Basic chemistry (organic chemistry fundamentals)
- Familiarity with common dietary concepts

Focus areas for intermediate learners:
- Mechanistic understanding (not just "what" but "why" and "how")
- Integration across organ systems
- Critical evaluation of dietary claims
- Application to real-world nutrition decisions
