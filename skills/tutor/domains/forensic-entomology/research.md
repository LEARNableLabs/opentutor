# Forensic Entomology — Research Summary

## Topic Overview

Forensic entomology applies the study of insects and other arthropods to legal investigations, primarily in death investigation. At the intermediate level, students explore PMI (postmortem interval) estimation, insect succession patterns, developmental biology, collection protocols, and real-world case applications.

## Major Subtopics

1. **Insect Biology & Life Cycles** — developmental stages, temperature dependence, species identification
2. **Succession Patterns** — predictable colonization sequences on remains
3. **PMI Estimation Methods** — accumulated degree hours (ADH), development rates, succession-based methods
4. **Collection & Preservation** — field protocols, chain of custody, specimen handling
5. **Environmental Factors** — temperature, habitat, season, geography
6. **Case Studies & Court Testimony** — applying science to real investigations, expert witness protocols
7. **Advanced Topics** — toxicology effects on development, buried/aquatic remains, DNA analysis

## Key Textbooks

- **"A Fly for the Prosecution"** by M. Lee Goff — narrative introduction to the field, case-based approach
- **"Forensic Entomology: The Utility of Arthropods in Legal Investigations"** edited by Jason H. Byrd and James L. Castner — comprehensive reference, technical depth
- **"Forensic Entomology: An Introduction"** by Dorothy E. Gennard — structured educational approach
- **"The Science of Forensic Entomology"** by David B. Rivers and Gregory A. Dahlem — modern overview with applied focus

## Key Researchers & Practitioners

- **M. Lee Goff** (University of Hawaii) — pioneer in applying entomology to forensics, author of foundational texts
- **Jason H. Byrd** (University of Florida) — leading researcher, board-certified forensic entomologist
- **Gail S. Anderson** (Simon Fraser University) — succession patterns, environmental factors
- **Jeffery K. Tomberlin** (Texas A&M) — insect behavior, decomposition ecology
- **Zakaria Erzinçlioğlu** (1951-2002) — British forensic entomologist, public communicator

- **Taphonomy** — study of decomposition processes
- **Microbial ecology** — bacteria, fungi interactions with insects
- **Climatology** — historical weather data for ADH calculations
- **Legal studies** — Daubert standard, expert testimony
- **Chemistry** — toxicology effects on insect development

## Pedagogical Notes

At the intermediate level, students should:
- Have basic entomology background (insect anatomy, life cycles, taxonomy)
- Understand biological principles (development, ecology, physiology)
- Be ready for quantitative methods (degree-day calculations, statistical analysis)
- Engage with ethical dimensions (working with death, court testimony)

The field is highly visual (requires species ID) and case-driven (real investigations motivate theory).
