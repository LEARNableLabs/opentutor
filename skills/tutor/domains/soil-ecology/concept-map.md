# Soil Ecology and Regenerative Agriculture — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Soil as ecosystem** — soil is a living system, not inert substrate
2. **Soil biodiversity** — bacteria, archaea, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, arthropods, earthworms
3. **Soil layers and horizons** — O, A, B, C horizons; organic vs mineral
4. **Aggregation** — how soil particles bind into stable structures
5. **Microbial glues** — exopolysaccharides and glomalin create soil architecture
6. **Root exudates** — plants secrete sugars and compounds to feed microbes
7. **Plant-microbe symbiosis** — mutualistic relationships, especially mycorrhizae
8. **Mycorrhizae** — fungal-plant root associations; arbuscular vs ectomycorrhizal
9. **Fungal networks** — mycelial networks connect plants, transfer nutrients and signals
10. **Bacteria:fungi ratio** — dominance shifts ecosystem function
11. **Bioturbation** — soil fauna physically mix and restructure soil
12. **Microbivores** — protozoa and nematodes eat microbes, release nutrients
13. **Trophic levels** — energy and nutrient flow through food web
14. **Nutrient immobilization and mineralization** — microbes lock up vs release nutrients
15. **C:N ratio** — carbon-to-nitrogen ratio determines decomposition rate
16. **Decomposition pathways** — bacterial vs fungal breakdown of organic matter
17. **Soil health indicators** — physical, chemical, and biological measures
18. **Aggregate stability** — resistance of aggregates to water and mechanical stress
19. **Visual soil assessment** — field-based observation methods (slake test, smell, color)
20. **Soil respiration** — CO2 release as indicator of microbial activity
21. **Organic matter** — master variable affecting CEC, WHC, aggregation, biology
22. **Cation exchange capacity (CEC)** — soil's ability to hold nutrients
23. **Water holding capacity (WHC)** — depends on organic matter and structure
24. **Cover crops** — plants grown for soil health, not harvest
25. **Nitrogen fixation** — legumes and bacteria convert N2 to plant-available forms
26. **Living roots year-round** — continuous carbon input to soil food web
27. **Tillage effects** — disrupts aggregates, destroys fungal networks, oxidizes OM
28. **No-till farming** — minimal soil disturbance preserves structure and biology
29. **Crop rotation** — temporal diversity breaks pest cycles, balances nutrients
30. **Rotational grazing** — mob grazing with recovery periods mimics wild herds
31. **Trampling and manure distribution** — grazing impacts on soil carbon and fertility
32. **Composting** — controlled decomposition; reduces pathogens, balances C:N
33. **Agroforestry** — integrating trees with crops/livestock
34. **Silvopasture** — trees + pasture + livestock
35. **Deep roots** — tree and perennial roots build soil carbon in subsoil
36. **Carbon sequestration** — long-term storage of atmospheric CO2 as soil organic carbon
37. **Soil organic carbon (SOC)** — carbon stored in organic matter
38. **Permanence** — how long sequestered carbon stays in soil
39. **Carbon markets** — trading soil carbon credits
40. **Additionality** — proving carbon would not have been sequestered without intervention
41. **Transition challenges** — economic, knowledge, and infrastructure barriers to adoption
42. **Scaling regenerative agriculture** — policy, networks, research needed

## Bottlenecks

2. **Nutrient cycling through trophic levels** (Lessons 10-11) — unlocks understanding of soil fertility and management
3. **Organic matter as master variable** (Lesson 18) — connects all physical, chemical, and biological indicators
4. **C:N ratio** (Lesson 11, 24) — critical for understanding decomposition, composting, and nutrient availability

### Skill Bottlenecks (application points)
- **Visual soil assessment** (Lesson 16) — first hands-on skill, builds confidence
- **Systems design** (Lesson 26) — integration of all practices into context-specific plans

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Soil is just dirt"** — missing the biological complexity and living nature
2. **"More fertilizer = healthier soil"** — confusing nutrient inputs with soil health
3. **"Tillage aerates and improves soil"** — not recognizing destruction of structure and biology
4. **"Organic matter is just compost"** — missing the living biomass component
5. **"Bacteria are bad for soil"** — not understanding beneficial vs pathogenic roles
6. **"Mycorrhizae are optional"** — underestimating their centrality to plant health
7. **"Carbon sequestration is permanent"** — not understanding SOC turnover and stability
8. **"One practice fixes everything"** — missing the systems/integration approach
9. **"Regenerative = organic"** — conflating certification with soil health outcomes
10. **"Soil health is expensive"** — not seeing long-term cost savings and resilience

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic biology** — needed for understanding cells, organisms, ecosystem concepts, energy flow
- **Basic chemistry** — needed for pH, chemical bonds, nutrient elements (N, P, K, C)
- **General ecology** — needed for food webs, nutrient cycles, succession, diversity concepts
- **(Helpful but not required) Agriculture basics** — crop types, livestock, farming systems

## Cross-Disciplinary Connections

- **Climate science** — carbon cycle, greenhouse gases, mitigation strategies
- **Hydrology** — water infiltration, runoff, drought resilience
- **Economics** — transition costs, carbon markets, long-term profitability
- **Policy** — agricultural subsidies, conservation programs, climate policy
- **Social science** — farmer knowledge, community networks, indigenous practices
- **Microbiology** — genomics, metagenomics, microbial ecology methods
- **Chemistry** — soil chemistry, nutrient transformations, organic chemistry
- **Physics** — soil structure, water movement, aggregation mechanics
