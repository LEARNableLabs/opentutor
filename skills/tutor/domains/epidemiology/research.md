# Epidemiology Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Descriptive Epidemiology** — Patterns of disease occurrence (person, place, time)
2. **Study Designs** — Cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, randomized trials
3. **Measures of Disease Frequency** — Incidence, prevalence, mortality rates
4. **Measures of Association** — Risk ratios, odds ratios, attributable risk
5. **Bias and Confounding** — Selection bias, information bias, confounding variables
6. **Causal Inference** — Hill's criteria, counterfactuals, directed acyclic graphs (DAGs)
7. **Screening and Surveillance** — Sensitivity, specificity, predictive values
8. **Infectious Disease Epidemiology** — Transmission dynamics, outbreak investigation, R0
9. **Chronic Disease Epidemiology** — Risk factors, prevention strategies
10. **Environmental and Occupational Epidemiology** — Exposure assessment, dose-response

## Key Sources Found

### Textbooks
- **Gordis Epidemiology** (Leon Gordis, updated editions) — Gold standard introductory text, widely used in MPH programs
- **Modern Epidemiology** (Rothman, Greenland, Lash) — Comprehensive advanced reference for epidemiologic and biostatistical methods
- **Epidemiology: An Introduction** (Kenneth J. Rothman) — Clear conceptual introduction suitable for intermediate learners

### University Courses
- **Harvard STAT S-103** — Introduction to Epidemiology and Biostatistics with hands-on R programming using NHANES data
- **Johns Hopkins PH.340** — Principles of Epidemiology covering infectious and noninfectious disease investigation
- **Johns Hopkins EPI 340.721** — Epidemiologic Inference covering randomized trials, cohort/case-control studies, causal inference
- **UNC Chapel Hill Coursera** — Epidemiology: The Basic Science of Public Health

### Online Platforms
- **Coursera** — Multiple epidemiology courses from leading institutions (Johns Hopkins, UNC)
- **edX** — Epidemiology fundamentals and advanced courses
- **Biostatistics.ca** — Epidemiology Fundamentals online modules

### Interactive Tools & Simulators
- **Operation Outbreak** (https://operationoutbreak.org/) — Mobile app-based outbreak simulator using Bluetooth proximity to model pathogen spread
- **BioInteractive Modeling Disease Spread** (https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/modeling-disease-spread) — SIR model simulators for small and large populations
- **CDC epiENGAGE Outbreak Simulator** — Real-time pandemic scenario simulator from Meyers Lab at UT Austin
- **Disease Detective Cards** — Card-based training for foodborne outbreak investigations

### Key Researchers & Institutions
- **Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health** — Leading epidemiology department, extensive training programs
- **Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health** — Top-tier research and education in epidemiology and biostatistics
- **CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)** — Outbreak analytics, forecasting, and practical field epidemiology
- **Meyers Lab at UT Austin** — Infectious disease modeling and simulation tools

### Educational Resources

## Notes for Curriculum Design

- Intermediate level assumes basic statistics and probability background
- Strong emphasis on study design and critical appraisal of literature
- Interactive outbreak simulations provide excellent hands-on learning
- Balance between infectious and chronic disease epidemiology
- Causal inference and confounding are conceptually challenging — need careful scaffolding
- Real-world applications through outbreak investigations highly engaging
- Computational tools (R, outbreak simulators) enhance understanding of abstract concepts
