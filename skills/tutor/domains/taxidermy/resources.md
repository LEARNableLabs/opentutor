# Taxidermy — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Comprehensive Guides
- **Breakthrough Taxidermy School** — Professional certification programs, online courses, and DVDs covering mammal, bird, and fish taxidermy from beginner to advanced. Strong focus on competition-quality work.
  - https://www.breakthroughtaxidermy.com/

- **McKenzie Taxidermy Supply Educational Resources** — Industry-leading supplier with extensive instructional videos, guides, and technical documentation. Covers anatomy, tools, techniques, and troubleshooting.
  - https://www.mckenziesp.com/Instruction/

- **Taxidermy.net Forums** — Long-running community with thousands of threads on techniques, troubleshooting, legal questions, and critiques. Searchable archive dating back 20+ years.
  - https://www.taxidermy.net/forum/

### Specialized Texts
- **"Taxidermy: A Complete Manual" by Gerald J. Grantz** — Comprehensive technical manual covering mammals, birds, fish, and reptiles. Strong on traditional techniques and chemistry. Available through taxidermy suppliers and used bookstores.

- **"The Complete Guide to Bird Taxidermy" by John Janelli** — Definitive text on avian taxidermy, covering anatomy, skinning, wiring, and feather management.
  - https://www.amazon.com/Complete-Guide-Bird-Taxidermy-Janelli/dp/0811732002

- **"Taxidermy and Museum Exhibition" by John Rowley** — Historical perspective on museum preparation and display. Out of print but findable used; excellent context for understanding museum vs. commercial practice.

### Scientific/Academic Sources
- **Smithsonian Museum Conservation Institute** — Publications on conservation science, including preservation chemistry, pest management, and long-term specimen care.
  - https://www.si.edu/mci

- **Journal of the American Institute for Conservation (JAIC)** — Peer-reviewed articles on preservation chemistry, materials science, and conservation ethics. Some articles freely available.
  - https://www.culturalheritage.org/publications/jaic

- **Studies in Conservation** — International conservation science journal. Covers historical preservation techniques and modern materials analysis.
  - https://www.iiconservation.org/publications/studies-in-conservation

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Video Channels & Tutorials
- **Taxidermy Instruction Network (YouTube)** — Step-by-step tutorials on skinning, fleshing, form fitting, and mounting for various species. Well-produced, clear explanations.
  - https://www.youtube.com/c/TaxidermyInstructionNetwork

- **Chuck Testa (YouTube)** — Professional taxidermist known for high-quality big game work. Occasional tutorials and shop tours.
  - https://www.youtube.com/@ChuckTesta

- **World Taxidermy & Fish Carving Championships Videos** — Competition footage and champion presentations. Great for seeing professional standards and judging criteria in action.
  - https://www.worldtaxidermychampionships.com/

### Interactive Tools & References
- **McKenzie Anatomy References** — Photos, diagrams, and measurement guides for various game animals. Essential for form selection and posture accuracy.
  - https://www.mckenziesp.com/Anatomy-Resources/

- **Cornell Lab of Ornithology — All About Birds** — Comprehensive bird ID, behavior, and anatomy reference. Essential for bird taxidermy posture and habitat context.
  - https://www.allaboutbirds.org/

- **iNaturalist** — Photo database of living animals in natural poses. Invaluable for reference when designing taxidermy mounts.
  - https://www.inaturalist.org/

### Professional Organizations
- **Society of American Taxidermists (SAT)** — Professional association with code of ethics, educational resources, and networking. Offers certification and hosts annual convention.
  - https://www.taxidermists.com/

- **National Taxidermists Association (NTA)** — Hosts competitions, publishes technical journals, offers business resources and certification.
  - https://www.nationaltaxidermists.com/

- **Guild of Taxidermists (UK)** — British/European perspective, strong museum and conservation focus. International membership available.
  - https://www.taxidermy.org.uk/

## Legal & Regulatory Resources

### Federal Regulations
- **U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service — Migratory Bird Treaty Act** — Full text and interpretation of MBTA, which governs possession of native birds.
  - https://www.fws.gov/law/migratory-bird-treaty-act-1918

- **CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species)** — International treaty governing trade in threatened/endangered species. Searchable species database.
  - https://cites.org/eng

- **U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service — Permits** — Information on scientific, educational, and falconry permits that may allow legal possession of otherwise restricted species.
  - https://www.fws.gov/program/permits

### State Regulations
- **State Wildlife Agency Contacts** — Each state has different regulations for game animals, furbearers, and commercial taxidermy. Check state-specific rules.
  - Find via: https://www.fishwildlife.org/afwa-informs/state-agency-directory

## People & Practitioners to Follow

### Competition Champions & Educators
- **Ken Walker** — Multi-time world champion, especially known for bird taxidermy. Frequent seminar presenter.
- **Greg Crain** — Champion mammal taxidermist and instructor. Known for anatomical accuracy and naturalism.
- **Frank Newmyer** — Small mammal specialist and educator. Excellent tutorials on anatomy and form alteration.

### Museum Taxidermists & Conservators
- **George Dante** — Chief taxidermist at Smithsonian's National Museum of Natural History. Focus on museum preparation and conservation.
- **Martha Maxwell** (historical) — 19th-century pioneering female taxidermist, known for naturalistic habitat groups. Important historical figure.

### Rogue/Artistic Taxidermy
- **Sarina Brewer** — Co-founder of the Minnesota Association of Rogue Taxidermists. Artistic and surreal taxidermy using ethically sourced specimens.
  - http://www.sarinabrewer.com/

- **Divya Anantharaman** — Brooklyn-based artist and educator, known for accessible, ethical approach to artistic taxidermy.
  - https://www.divyaanantharaman.com/

### Conservation & Ethics Voices
- **Pat Morris** — UK-based taxidermist and conservation biologist. Writes extensively on taxidermy history and ethics.
- **American Alliance of Museums — Taxidermy Working Group** — Discussions on ethical collection, display, and interpretation of taxidermy in museums.

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Disciplinary)

### Material Science & Chemistry
- **Leather Chemistry** — LeatherChem.org has resources on tanning chemistry that apply directly to hide preservation.
  - https://www.leatherchem.org/

- **Conservation Science** — Techniques from art conservation (painting restoration, textile preservation) overlap with taxidermy finishing and maintenance.

### Anatomy & Morphology
- **Veterinary Anatomy Resources** — Vet school anatomy texts and references (esp. *Miller's Anatomy of the Dog*) are invaluable for understanding musculature and skeletal structure.

- **Comparative Anatomy Collections** — University and museum osteology collections (skeletons) are critical references for understanding species-specific skeletal variation.

### Art & Design
- **Natural History Illustration** — Artists like John James Audubon, Ernst Haeckel, and contemporary scientific illustrators provide reference for posture, anatomy, and naturalism.

- **Wildlife Photography** — Professional wildlife photographers capture animals in natural behavior and lighting. Essential reference for taxidermy posture and habitat design.

### History & Culture
- **"Still Life: Adventures in Taxidermy" by Melissa Milgrom** — Engaging narrative nonfiction exploring taxidermy culture, history, and personalities.
  - https://www.amazon.com/Still-Life-Adventures-Melissa-Milgrom/dp/0547572409

- **"Stuffed Animals & Pickled Heads" by Stephen T. Asma** — Philosophical exploration of natural history museums, curiosity cabinets, and the human impulse to collect and preserve.
  - https://www.amazon.com/Stuffed-Animals-Pickled-Heads-Exploring/dp/0195381750

## Tools for Self-Directed Learning

### Documentation & Portfolio
- **Instagram taxidermy community** — Search hashtags like #taxidermy, #taxidermyart, #taxidermist for contemporary work and inspiration. Mix of commercial, museum, and artistic practitioners.

- **Competition photography** — Document work in progress and finished pieces. Photography reveals flaws invisible to tired eyes and builds portfolio for professional practice.

### Peer Critique & Feedback
- **Taxidermy.net forums** — Post work-in-progress for community feedback and troubleshooting help.

- **Local/regional taxidermy associations** — Many states have associations with workshops, mentorships, and critique sessions. Google "[your state] taxidermists association."

### Competitions (Observation & Entry)
- **World Taxidermy Championships** — Annual event with competition categories, educational seminars, and vendor hall. Observing competitions is highly educational even before entering.
  - https://www.worldtaxidermychampionships.com/

- **State & Regional Competitions** — Lower-stakes opportunities to test work against peers and receive judges' critiques.

## Safety & Health Resources

### Zoonotic Disease Information
- **CDC — Diseases from Wildlife** — Guides on rabies, tularemia, hantavirus, and other diseases transmissible from animals to humans.
  - https://www.cdc.gov/healthypets/wildlife/

### Chemical Safety
- **OSHA Safety Data Sheets** — Required safety information for all commercial chemicals. Search by product name to find hazards, handling, and first aid.
  - https://www.osha.gov/safety-data-sheets

- **Ventilation & Respiratory Protection** — Guidelines for fume hood use and respirator selection when working with tanning agents, solvents, and paints.
  - https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/respirators/

## Museums with Notable Taxidermy Collections

### For Study & Inspiration
- **American Museum of Natural History (NYC)** — Legendary dioramas by Carl Akeley and team. Hall of North American Mammals is a masterclass in naturalistic habitat taxidermy.
  - https://www.amnh.org/

- **Field Museum (Chicago)** — Extensive taxidermy collection spanning historical and contemporary work. Strong focus on scientific accuracy.
  - https://www.fieldmuseum.org/

- **Natural History Museum (London)** — Historical and contemporary taxidermy, including extinct species. UK perspective on museum practice.
  - https://www.nhm.ac.uk/

- **Harvard Museum of Natural History** — Glass flowers and taxidermy collection, accessible to students for close study with appointment.
  - https://hmnh.harvard.edu/
