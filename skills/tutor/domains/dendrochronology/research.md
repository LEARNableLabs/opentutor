# Dendrochronology Research Summary

## Overview

Dendrochronology (tree-ring dating) is an interdisciplinary field combining botany, climate science, archaeology, and statistics. At the intermediate level, students need hands-on skills in ring measurement, cross-dating, and climate reconstruction alongside theoretical understanding of tree growth processes.

## Major Subtopics

1. **Tree Anatomy and Ring Formation** — cellular structure, cambium activity, earlywood/latewood differentiation, environmental controls on growth
2. **Measurement and Cross-Dating** — core sampling, skeleton plotting, master chronologies, statistical validation
3. **Climate Signals** — temperature vs. precipitation sensitivity, pointer years, common signal extraction
4. **Statistical Methods** — standardization, chronology building, correlation analysis, response functions
5. **Applications** — archaeological dating, climate reconstruction, fire history, ecological studies
6. **Laboratory Techniques** — sample preparation, measurement systems (WinDENDRO, CooRecorder), quality control
7. **Advanced Methods** — isotope dendrochronology, blue intensity, quantitative wood anatomy

## Teaching Approach

Intermediate students should balance:
- Theoretical understanding of tree physiology and climate response
- Practical skills in measurement and cross-dating
- Statistical rigor in chronology development
- Real-world applications across archaeology and climate science

The curriculum emphasizes hands-on problem-solving with real datasets, visual pattern recognition, and critical evaluation of tree-ring signals.
