## Domain Hooks
- **Exceptional preservation (Lagerstätten)** — Drop in Burgess Shale, Chengjiang, Solnhofen, Jehol Biota when discussing taphonomic bias. Show how rare preservation windows reveal soft anatomy, behavior, color.

- **Convergent evolution** — Ichthyosaurs, dolphins, and sharks look alike but evolved from different ancestors. Brings in functional morphology and ecological constraints. Discuss marsupial vs. placental mammal convergence (thylacine vs. wolf).

- **Evo-devo and fossils** — Tiktaalik, Archaeopteryx, and feathered dinosaurs have become model systems for understanding developmental genetics. Connect deep time to modern molecular biology.

- **Paleontology and climate science** — Past warm periods (PETM, Cretaceous hothouse) inform climate projections. Ice core data, foram isotopes, tree rings — these are "fossils" of climate.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Fossils are rare lucky finds"** — Students underestimate how systematic fossil discovery is. Modern paleontology uses predictive models based on geology, ancient environments, and sampling theory. Correct by discussing prospecting strategies and the Paleobiology Database as a quantitative resource.

2. **"The fossil record is complete enough to answer any question"** — Opposite problem: students may trust fossil evidence too much. Emphasize taphonomic bias heavily: soft bodies don't fossilize, terrestrial environments preserve poorly, recent fossils are over-represented. Use Lagerstätten (Burgess Shale, Solnhofen) as exceptional preservation examples that reveal how much we normally miss.

3. **"Evolution proceeds linearly toward increasing complexity"** — Deep-rooted misconception. Emphasize branching trees, dead-end lineages, and the role of contingency. Gould's *Wonderful Life* argument helps: replay the tape of life and you get different outcomes.
