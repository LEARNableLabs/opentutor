# Stained Glass Research — Lead Came & Copper Foil Techniques

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Lead Came Technique
Traditional method using H-channel lead strips to join glass pieces. Dominant for architectural and traditional panel work. Key skills: pattern making, cutting, leading, soldering, cementing/puttying.

### 2. Copper Foil (Tiffany) Technique
Developed by Louis Comfort Tiffany in the 1880s. Uses copper tape wrapped around glass edges, then soldered. Allows for intricate 3D forms and smaller pieces. Key skills: foiling, burnishing, flux application, three-dimensional construction.

### 3. Glass Selection & Cutting
Both methods require mastery of: glass scoring, breaking, grinding, pattern transfer. Different glass types (cathedral, opalescent, textured) suit different techniques.

### 4. Finishing & Patina
Soldering quality, cleaning, patina application (black, copper), frame construction, hanging systems.

### 5. Design Principles
Line quality, color theory for translucent materials, structural integrity, light consideration.

## Intermediate Student Assumptions

At intermediate level, students typically have:
- Basic glass cutting and grinding skills
- Completed 2-3 simple copper foil projects (sun catchers, small panels)
- Understanding of soldering basics
- May have little/no lead came experience (copper foil is more common for beginners)

They need:
- Formal understanding of when to use each technique
- Advanced soldering and finishing skills
- Ability to handle larger, more complex projects
- Understanding of structural considerations
- Introduction to architectural/installation work
