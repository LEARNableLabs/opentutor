## Domain Hooks
- **Glass chemistry and manufacturing** — Why does opalescent glass behave differently than cathedral glass? Trace it to the manufacturing process and chemical composition. Great for students interested in materials science.

- **Medieval cathedral windows** — How did 12th-century craftspeople build structurally sound 40-foot windows without modern tools? Dive into historical engineering, ferramenta (armatures), and deterioration science. Connects to art history and architecture.

- **Tiffany's business empire** — Louis Comfort Tiffany didn't just invent copper foil — he industrialized luxury glass production. Explore the intersection of craft, art, and capitalism. Fascinating for students interested in design business.

- **Light and color physics** — Why do stained glass windows look different from inside vs outside? Why does time of day matter? Deep dive into transmission vs reflection, glass color mixing, and architectural lighting. Connects to optics and physics.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Lead came is just old-fashioned copper foil"** — Students often think lead came is an outdated technique that copper foil replaced. In reality, lead came is structurally superior for large architectural panels, has a distinct aesthetic appropriate for traditional designs, and is the standard for historical restoration. Copper foil enables intricate 3D work that lead cannot match. They are complementary, not competitive.

2. **"More solder makes stronger joints"** — Beginners often "glob" solder thinking it increases strength. In reality, a thin, properly adhered bead is stronger than a thick, poorly flowed one. Excess solder looks amateur and adds unnecessary weight. Teach that solder strength comes from proper flux, heat, and flow — not volume.
