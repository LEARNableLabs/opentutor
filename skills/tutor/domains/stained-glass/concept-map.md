# Stained Glass — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Technique selection** — understanding when to use lead came vs copper foil based on design, scale, and aesthetic goals
2. **Structural properties** — how each technique provides different strength characteristics
3. **Visual weight** — the appearance and design impact of lead lines vs copper foil seams
4. **Pattern making** — translating designs into accurate cutting patterns with proper registration
5. **Material selection** — choosing appropriate glass types for each technique
6. **Lead came profiles** — H-channel, U-channel, and width variations. Depends on: structural properties
7. **Came preparation** — stretching and preparing lead for use. Depends on: lead came profiles
8. **Came cutting and fitting** — miter joints, T-joints, corner techniques. Depends on: came preparation
9. **Lead soldering** — flux selection, heat control, bead formation on lead. Depends on: came cutting and fitting
10. **Panel cementing** — weatherproofing and reinforcement of lead came panels. Depends on: lead soldering
11. **Copper foil sizing** — selecting appropriate foil width for glass thickness. Depends on: material selection
12. **Foil application** — wrapping and burnishing technique. Depends on: copper foil sizing
13. **Flux chemistry** — understanding flux types and their interaction with copper. Depends on: foil application
14. **Copper foil soldering** — tinning, bead building, temperature control. Depends on: flux chemistry
15. **Three-dimensional construction** — building stable 3D forms with copper foil. Depends on: copper foil soldering, structural properties
16. **Framing methods** — zinc and brass came for panel edges. Depends on: lead soldering, copper foil soldering
17. **Structural reinforcement** — rebar placement for large panels. Depends on: structural properties, framing methods
18. **Patina chemistry** — black and copper sulfate patinas, application and sealing. Depends on: lead soldering, copper foil soldering
19. **Light physics** — designing for transmission vs reflection. Depends on: material selection, visual weight
20. **Hybrid techniques** — combining lead came and copper foil in one piece. Depends on: all prior concepts

## Dependencies

### Foundation Phase (Lessons 1-5)
- **Material selection** depends on **technique selection** because different glass types behave differently with lead vs foil
- **Pattern making** is foundational to both techniques and must account for **structural properties**
- **Visual weight** understanding influences **pattern making** and **technique selection**

### Lead Came Phase (Lessons 6-11)
- **Came cutting and fitting** requires proper **came preparation** (stretching) to ensure joints are tight
- **Lead soldering** quality depends on precise **came cutting** — poor joints can't be fixed with solder
- **Panel cementing** can only be done after **lead soldering** is complete and cooled
- All lead concepts build sequentially — skipping **came preparation** will cause failures in **came fitting**

### Copper Foil Phase (Lessons 12-17)
- **Foil application** quality directly impacts **copper foil soldering** success — poor burnishing causes lifting
- **Flux chemistry** must be understood before **copper foil soldering** to prevent cold joints and poor adhesion
- **Three-dimensional construction** requires mastery of **copper foil soldering** plus understanding of **structural properties** from foundations
- **Tinning** (part of flux chemistry) must happen before building final **solder beads**

### Advanced Integration (Lessons 18-23)
- **Framing methods** require proficiency in both **lead soldering** and **copper foil soldering** depending on the project
- **Structural reinforcement** placement requires understanding of **structural properties** and **framing methods**
- **Patina chemistry** depends on proper **lead soldering** and **copper foil soldering** — patina won't adhere to poorly cleaned or fluxed joints
- **Hybrid techniques** require mastery of all previous concepts — this is the capstone skill

## Bottlenecks

### Critical Bottleneck: Soldering Quality
Both **lead soldering** and **copper foil soldering** are bottlenecks that affect all downstream work:
- Poor soldering prevents proper **patina** application
- Weak solder joints make **structural reinforcement** insufficient
- Bad soldering ruins **framing methods**
- **Three-dimensional construction** is impossible without clean, strong solder joints

Students who struggle here must return to fundamentals (heat control, flux use, iron maintenance) before proceeding.

### Critical Bottleneck: Pattern Making
**Pattern making** errors cascade through the entire project:
- Inaccurate patterns cause cutting errors
- Poor registration marks make assembly difficult
- Missing allowances for **came width** or **foil thickness** cause fit problems

This is the first place to troubleshoot when later assembly goes wrong.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic glass cutting and scoring** — needed for all lessons, especially pattern execution
- **Familiarity with copper foil method** — assumed for lessons 12-17; students without this will need extra time
- **Soldering fundamentals** — essential for lessons 9, 14-15, 18, 22
- **Use of glass grinder** — required for fitting pieces in both lead came and copper foil work

## Common Dependency Violations

Students often want to skip:
- **Came preparation** (stretching) → leads to loose, weak panels
- **Flux chemistry** understanding → causes poor solder adhesion and frustration
- **Panel cementing** on lead work → results in weak, non-weatherproof panels
- **Burnishing** in copper foil → foil lifts during soldering

Enforce the dependency order strictly for these concepts.
