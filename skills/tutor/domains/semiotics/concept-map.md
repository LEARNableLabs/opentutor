# Semiotics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Sign** — anything that stands for something else; the fundamental unit of meaning
2. **Signifier** — the form a sign takes (sound, image, word). Depends on: Sign
3. **Signified** — the concept or meaning the signifier evokes. Depends on: Sign, Signifier
4. **Arbitrariness** — the lack of natural connection between signifier and signified. Depends on: Signifier, Signified
5. **Dyadic model (Saussure)** — sign as two-part unity of signifier/signified. Depends on: Signifier, Signified
6. **Langue vs. Parole** — language system vs. individual speech acts. Depends on: Dyadic model
7. **Index** — sign that has causal/physical connection to its object (smoke → fire). Depends on: Sign
8. **Icon** — sign that resembles its object (portrait, diagram). Depends on: Sign
9. **Symbol** — sign whose meaning is learned by convention (words, flags). Depends on: Sign, Arbitrariness
10. **Triadic model (Peirce)** — sign as representamen/object/interpretant relation. Depends on: Icon, Index, Symbol
11. **Object** — what the sign refers to. Depends on: Triadic model
12. **Representamen** — the sign vehicle itself. Depends on: Triadic model
13. **Interpretant** — the meaning created in the mind. Depends on: Triadic model
14. **Unlimited semiosis** — signs lead to more interpretants, which become new signs. Depends on: Interpretant
15. **Syntagmatic relations** — how signs combine in sequence. Depends on: Dyadic model
16. **Paradigmatic relations** — how signs relate by substitution/choice. Depends on: Dyadic model
17. **Codes** — systems of rules for combining signs. Depends on: Syntagmatic relations, Paradigmatic relations
18. **Conventions** — socially agreed-upon sign meanings. Depends on: Symbol, Codes
19. **Context dependency** — how meaning shifts with situation. Depends on: Interpretant, Codes
20. **Polysemy** — signs with multiple meanings. Depends on: Context dependency
21. **Intertextuality** — signs referencing other signs/texts. Depends on: Unlimited semiosis
22. **Denotation** — literal, surface-level meaning. Depends on: Signified
23. **Connotation** — associated, cultural meanings. Depends on: Denotation
24. **Second-order signification** — when a sign becomes a signifier for another sign. Depends on: Connotation
25. **Myth (Barthes)** — ideology naturalized through second-order signification. Depends on: Second-order signification
26. **Cultural codes** — shared meaning systems in a culture. Depends on: Codes, Connotation
27. **Genre codes** — conventions that define types of texts. Depends on: Cultural codes
28. **Visual rhetoric** — persuasive use of images. Depends on: Icon, Cultural codes
29. **Photographic indexicality** — photos as indexes of what was in front of the camera. Depends on: Index
30. **Framing** — how composition shapes meaning. Depends on: Visual rhetoric
31. **Color codes** — culturally specific color meanings. Depends on: Cultural codes, Symbol
32. **Metaphor** — understanding one thing in terms of another. Depends on: Icon
33. **Skeuomorphism** — digital objects that mimic physical ones. Depends on: Icon, Metaphor
34. **Semiotic square (Greimas)** — structural diagram of meaning oppositions. Depends on: Paradigmatic relations
35. **Deep structure** — underlying logical relations beneath surface content. Depends on: Semiotic square
36. **Interpretive communities** — groups that share reading strategies. Depends on: Context dependency, Cultural codes
37. **Biosemiotics** — study of sign processes in living systems. Depends on: Triadic model

## Dependencies

### Foundational Dependencies
- **Dyadic model** requires understanding **signifier** and **signified** because it defines the sign as the inseparable unity of these two elements
- **Triadic model** requires understanding **icon**, **index**, and **symbol** because Peirce's model expands the simple sign into a three-way relation
- **Arbitrariness** is crucial for understanding **symbol** because symbolic meaning is conventional, not natural

### Sign Type Dependencies
- **Index**, **icon**, and **symbol** all depend on the basic concept of **sign** but are independent of each other
- **Triadic model** integrates all three types into a unified framework, so it depends on knowing them first

### Relations Dependencies
- **Syntagmatic** and **paradigmatic relations** both build on the **dyadic model** and illuminate how signs create meaning through combination and choice
- **Codes** depend on both types of relations because codes govern both sequence and selection

### Signification Levels
- **Connotation** depends on **denotation** because it's the layer of meaning beyond the literal
- **Second-order signification** depends on **connotation** because it treats the entire first-order sign as a new signifier
- **Myth** depends on **second-order signification** because it's the cultural/ideological level of meaning

### Application Dependencies
- **Visual rhetoric** depends on both **icon** (resemblance) and **cultural codes** (learned meanings)
- **Photographic indexicality** specifically applies **index** to photography
- **Skeuomorphism** combines **icon** (resemblance) and **metaphor** (conceptual mapping)

### Advanced Concepts
- **Semiotic square** builds on **paradigmatic relations** to reveal structural oppositions
- **Interpretive communities** depends on **context dependency** and **cultural codes** because different groups activate different meanings
- **Biosemiotics** extends the **triadic model** to non-human sign processes

## Bottlenecks

### Critical Concepts (must master to progress)
1. **Sign** — everything builds on this
2. **Dyadic vs. Triadic models** — two competing frameworks that structure the entire field
3. **Icon/Index/Symbol** — core typology used throughout analysis
4. **Denotation/Connotation** — foundation for cultural analysis

### Common Sticking Points
- **Arbitrariness** — students often resist that most meanings are conventional, not natural
- **Interpretant** — easily confused with "interpretation"; it's the meaning-effect in the mind
- **Unlimited semiosis** — can feel infinite/overwhelming; needs grounding in examples
- **Second-order signification** — the meta-level jump is conceptually difficult

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic linguistics** — needed for understanding signifier/signified, language structure
- **Introductory philosophy** — helpful for Peirce's logic, pragmatism background
- **Media literacy** — needed for analyzing ads, films, images
- **Cultural awareness** — needed for recognizing how codes vary across cultures

## Conceptual Milestones

1. **Can distinguish signifier from signified** (Lesson 1-2)
2. **Can classify signs as icon/index/symbol** (Lesson 3-5)
3. **Can compare Saussure and Peirce frameworks** (Lesson 5-6)
4. **Can identify codes and conventions in everyday contexts** (Lesson 7-11)
5. **Can separate denotation from connotation** (Lesson 12-14)
6. **Can decode myth in cultural artifacts** (Lesson 13-16)
7. **Can perform visual semiotic analysis** (Lesson 17-21)
8. **Can apply advanced frameworks (semiotic square, interpretive communities)** (Lesson 22-25)
