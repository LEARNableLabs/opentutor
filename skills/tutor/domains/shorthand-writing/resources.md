# Shorthand Writing Systems — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Gregg Shorthand
- **Gregg Shorthand Manual Simplified** (McGraw-Hill, 1949/1955) — The definitive textbook for Gregg Simplified, most widely taught edition. Available on archive.org.
- **Gregg Speed Studies** (Gregg Publishing, 1955) — Advanced speed building drills for 80-120 wpm progression.
- **Gregg Shorthand Dictionary Simplified** (McGraw-Hill, 1963) — 26,000+ brief forms and special outlines; essential reference for intermediate learners.
- **gregg.angelfishy.net** — Comprehensive online resource with alphabet charts, brief form lists, and practice materials. Best free Gregg resource.
- **greggshorthand.github.io** — Community-maintained practice drills, dictation files, and reading exercises.

### Pitman Shorthand
- **Pitman 2000 Shorthand** (Pearson, 1993) — Modern standard edition; clearer than New Era for self-study.
- **Pitman's New Era Shorthand Instructor** (Pitman Publishing, 1975) — Classic edition, more complex but faster at expert level.
- **Pitman Shorthand Instructor and Key** (Isaac Pitman & Sons) — Historical edition with complete key; valuable for reading old documents.
- **long-live-pitmans-shorthand.org.uk** — Most complete online Pitman resource; includes consonant charts, vowel placement guides, and grammologue lists.
- **pitmanshorthand.homestead.com** — Practice exercises and speed building materials.

### Teeline Shorthand
- **Teeline Gold: The Course Book** (Heinemann, 2018) — Official NCTJ (UK journalism) standard textbook; best structured Teeline course.
- **Teeline Fast** (Heinemann, 2002) — Accelerated course for students with tight timelines.
- **Handbook for Teeline Teachers** (NCTJ) — Pedagogical guide; useful for understanding common errors and teaching progressions.
- **www.nctj.com/journalism-skills/teeline** — Official NCTJ Teeline resources and certification information.
- **www.teeline.org.uk** — UK Teeline community site (if active; check archive.org if link is broken).

## People (to follow/research)

### Historical Figures
- **John Robert Gregg (1867-1948)** — Inventor of Gregg Shorthand; Irish-American publisher and educator. His autobiography *Light-Line Phonography* details the system's philosophical origins.
- **Sir Isaac Pitman (1813-1897)** — English educator who standardized Pitman Shorthand; also invented postal stamps and phonetic spelling reform.
- **James Hill (1854-1927)** — Pitman expert who created teaching materials still used today.
- **Alfred Reed (1908-1991)** — Invented Teeline in 1968; designed it specifically for journalism training.

### Modern Practitioners

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

### Cross-Discipline Links
- **Musical notation** — Both music and shorthand encode temporal information spatially; understanding one helps with the other. Musicians often excel at shorthand.
- **Sign language** — Phonetic thinking in shorthand mirrors phoneme-based sign systems; ASL users often find Gregg intuitive.
- **Conlangs (constructed languages)** — Shorthand systems are essentially constructed writing systems; conlang enthusiasts (Esperanto, Tolkien's Tengwar) appreciate the design logic.
- **Cryptography** — Shorthand was historically used for encryption (Samuel Pepys, WWII intelligence). Modern "journaling codes" communities on Reddit use shorthand for privacy.
- **Calligraphy** — While shorthand prioritizes speed over beauty, the motor control from calligraphy practice transfers well to shorthand stroke consistency.

### Modern Communities
- **r/Journaling** — Many journalers learn shorthand for privacy or aesthetic variety.
- **Digital detox communities** — Shorthand appeals to people reducing screen time; handwritten notes without the slowness of longhand.

## Regional and Professional Standards

### Journalism
- **UK:** Teeline is the standard (NCTJ certification requires 100 wpm).
- **US:** Gregg historically dominated; now mostly digital tools (typing), but some programs still teach Gregg.

### Legal/Court Reporting
- **Stenotype machines** have largely replaced manual shorthand, but some jurisdictions still certify Pitman/Gregg stenographers.
- **Deposition reporters** may use shorthand for sidebar notes even if primary transcription is stenotype.

### Business/Secretarial
- **Gregg** was US corporate standard (1900-1980s).
- **Pitman** was UK/Commonwealth standard.
- Largely obsolete post-1990s due to typing/word processing, but executive assistants in high-security environments (government, finance) sometimes still use shorthand for confidential notes.

## Where to Find Historical Materials

- **HathiTrust Digital Library** — Academic repository; many vintage textbooks available for free.
- **University libraries** — Harvard, Yale, and British Library have extensive shorthand collections (some digitized).
- **eBay/AbeBooks** — Vintage textbooks are cheap ($5-15); often better quality than modern reprints.

## Certification and Testing (if applicable)
- **NCTJ Teeline exams** (UK) — 60/80/100/120 wpm certification levels.
- **National Shorthand Reporters Association** (historical, mostly defunct) — Used to certify Gregg/Pitman speeds.
- **Self-testing** — Most modern learners track speed via self-administered dictation tests (record audio, write it down, time yourself, check accuracy).

## Learning Path Recommendations

### For Quick Wins (3-6 months to 40 wpm)
Focus on **Teeline**. Use *Teeline Gold* book + YouTube tutorials + r/shorthand community. 15 min/day practice.

### For Maximum Speed (12-24 months to 100+ wpm)
Choose **Gregg Simplified** or **Pitman 2000** based on personal preference (try both for 1 week each). Commit to 30+ min/day. Join a Discord practice group for accountability.

### For Historical Document Reading
Learn **Pitman New Era** (most common in UK archives 1920-1980) or **Gregg Anniversary** (most common in US archives 1929-1963). Archive.org has the manuals.

### For Journaling/Privacy
Any system works; **Gregg** is most aesthetic, **Teeline** is fastest to learn. No need for high speed; 30-40 wpm is plenty for personal notes.
