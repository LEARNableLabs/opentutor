# Shorthand Writing Systems — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Historical Development
- Evolution of shorthand from ancient systems to modern methods
- Geometric vs. script-based approaches
- Regional adoption patterns (Pitman in UK/Commonwealth, Gregg in US, Teeline in journalism)

### 2. Gregg Shorthand
- Simplified form (1888), Anniversary Edition (1929), Simplified (1949), Diamond Jubilee (1963), Series 90 (1978)
- Phonetic, cursive-based system
- Emphasis on natural writing motion and speed
- Vowel representation strategies
- Brief forms and phrases

### 3. Pitman Shorthand
- Geometric system based on line thickness, position, and length
- Consonant-heavy with positional vowels
- Precise phonetic representation
- Higher initial learning curve but potentially faster at mastery
- Multiple editions (New Era, 2000)

### 4. Teeline Shorthand
- Developed 1968 for journalism
- Alphabetic rather than phonetic
- Faster to learn, widely used in UK journalism schools
- Less standardized than Gregg/Pitman

### 5. Speed Building Techniques
- Dictation practice methodology
- Progressive speed targets (60-120+ wpm)
- Brief forms, phrasing, abbreviations
- Reading back and transcription accuracy

### 6. System Selection Criteria
- Learning curve vs. ultimate speed potential
- Cultural/professional context
- Personal writing style and goals
- Available learning resources
