# Bonsai — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Tree Biology & Physiology
- Vascular system (xylem/phloem) and nutrient transport
- Root development and mycorrhizal relationships
- Apical dominance and auxin distribution
- Photosynthesis under constraint
- Seasonal dormancy cycles (temperate species)
- Wound response and callus formation

### 2. Horticultural Science
- Soil science: particle size, water retention, aeration, pH
- Water management: osmotic pressure, transpiration, humidity
- Nutrient cycles: NPK ratios, micronutrients, fertilization timing
- Root pruning physiology and timing
- Species-specific environmental requirements
- Pest and disease management in containerized systems

### 3. Aesthetic Theory & Design Principles
- Traditional Japanese aesthetic concepts (wabi-sabi, ma, yohaku-no-bi)
- Classical styles: formal/informal upright, slanting, cascade, literati, forest
- Visual balance and the golden ratio in design
- Negative space (yohaku) and viewer perception
- Seasonal display and transformation
- Pot selection and complementarity

### 4. Technical Practice
- Pruning: structural vs. maintenance, timing by species
- Wiring: mechanics, material selection, removal timing
- Repotting: root work, soil replacement, aftercare
- Branch selection and Jin/Shari creation
- Air layering and propagation techniques
- Seasonal care calendars

### 5. Species Knowledge
- Temperate deciduous: maples, elms, hornbeam, zelkova
- Temperate coniferous: pines, junipers, spruce, larch
- Tropical/subtropical: ficus, schefflera, jade
- Species-specific growth patterns and responses
- Matching species to climate zones

## Research Gaps & Opportunities

For intermediate learners, the main challenge is integrating horticultural science with aesthetic practice. Many resources focus on one or the other. This curriculum bridges both domains by:

1. Teaching the **why** (physiology) before the **how** (technique)
2. Connecting each technique to specific tree responses
3. Using seasonal cycles as the organizing framework
4. Emphasizing species-specific knowledge over generic rules
5. Building aesthetic judgment through comparative analysis

The intermediate level assumes basic familiarity with plant care but not specialized knowledge of tree physiology or traditional design principles.
