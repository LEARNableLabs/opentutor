# Bonsai — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Books

- **"Bonsai Techniques I & II"** by John Yoshio Naka  
  The canonical English-language technical reference. Techniques I covers fundamentals (care, styling, potting). Techniques II dives into specific species and advanced refinement. Dense but authoritative. Perfect for intermediate students who want depth.

- **"The Art of Bonsai"** by Yuji Yoshimura & Giovanni M. Halford  
  Classic introduction to Japanese aesthetics and traditional styles. Beautiful photography. Less technical than Naka, more philosophical. Good complement for Module 3 (aesthetic principles).

- **"Modern Bonsai Practice"** by Larry W. Jackel  
  Science-based approach with detailed soil science, root physiology, and fertilization research. Challenges some traditional "rules" with experimental data. Excellent for students who want to understand mechanisms, not just follow procedures.

- **"Bonsai: The Art of Growing and Keeping Miniature Trees"** by Peter Chan  
  Accessible intermediate guide from Herons Bonsai. Species-specific care calendars, clear photography, practical troubleshooting. Good balance of technique and theory.

- **"The Complete Book of Bonsai"** by Harry Tomlinson  
  Comprehensive reference with species directory, technique encyclopedia, and design principles. Good for quick lookups and species comparison. Less narrative than Naka, more encyclopedic.

### Academic & Scientific

- **"The Reference Manual of Woody Plant Propagation"** by Michael A. Dirr & Charles W. Heuser Jr.  
  Not bonsai-specific, but essential for understanding root physiology, cutting propagation, and species-specific rooting requirements. Referenced by professionals.

- **"Plant Physiology and Development"** by Taiz, Zeiger, et al.  
  University-level textbook. Overkill for most students, but chapters on auxin, vascular transport, and photosynthesis are definitive. Use as deep-dive reference for lessons 1, 3, 4.

- **Journal of the American Bonsai Society**  
  Quarterly publication with species studies, technique experiments, and exhibition reviews. Intermediate-to-advanced. Available to ABS members.

## Online Educational Platforms

### Bonsai Empire (bonsaiempire.com)
- **Structure:** Comprehensive learning hub with free articles, courses, and videos
- **Strengths:** Well-organized by topic (care, styling, species), beginner-friendly, international perspective
- **Courses:** "Bonsai Basics" (free), "Advanced Techniques" (paid), species-specific guides
- **Best for:** Self-paced learning, species identification, technique demonstrations
- **Key resources:**  
  - Tree species guide: https://bonsaiempire.com/tree-species  
  - Care basics: https://bonsaiempire.com/origin/bonsai-care  
  - Styling guide: https://bonsaiempire.com/inspiration/bonsai-styles  

### Bonsai Mirai (live.bonsaimirai.com)
- **Structure:** Subscription-based professional instruction by Ryan Neil
- **Strengths:** Highest production quality, deep theory, emphasis on design principles and conifer work
- **Courses:** "Fundamentals," "Design Theory," "Seasonal Approaches," species libraries
- **Best for:** Students ready for professional-level refinement, especially conifer styling
- **Note:** Expensive ($35/month) but unmatched depth. Free trial available.
- **Key resources:**  
  - Design theory: https://live.bonsaimirai.com/library/video/design-theory-balance  
  - Energy systems: https://live.bonsaimirai.com/library/video/fundamentals-energy-systems  

### American Bonsai Society (absbonsai.org)
- **Structure:** Membership organization with regional clubs, symposia, and publications
- **Strengths:** Community connection, hands-on workshops, mentorship opportunities
- **Resources:** Species guides, care sheets, event calendar
- **Best for:** Finding local teachers, attending workshops, seeing trees in person
- **Key resource:** Species care guides: https://www.absbonsai.org/species-guides

### National Bonsai Foundation (nationalbonsai.org)
- **Structure:** Supports the US National Arboretum bonsai collection in Washington, DC
- **Strengths:** World-class collection (many trees 300+ years old), educational programs, documentation
- **Resources:** Tree profiles, care basics, virtual tours
- **Best for:** Inspiration, aesthetic study, historical context
- **Key resources:**  
  - Collection tours: https://www.nationalbonsai.org/collections/  
  - Bonsai basics: https://www.nationalbonsai.org/bonsai-basics/

## Interactive Tools

### Species Selection & Care

- **Bonsai Empire Species Guide** (https://bonsaiempire.com/tree-species)  
  Filterable database by climate, skill level, and tree type. Detailed care sheets for 100+ species.

- **USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map** (https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/)  
  Essential for matching temperate species to your climate. Know your zone before selecting trees.

### Soil Calculators

- **Bonsai Soil Mix Calculator** (various online tools)  
  Input your climate, species, and watering habits to get akadama/pumice/lava ratios. Useful for lesson 7.

### Design & Aesthetics

- **Virtual Bonsai Collections:**  
  - US National Arboretum: https://www.nationalbonsai.org/collections/  
  - Pacific Bonsai Museum: https://pacificbonsaimuseum.org/  
  - Kokufu-ten (Japan's premier exhibition): photos circulate annually on social media and forums

- **BonsaiNut Forum Design Critique Threads** (bonsainut.com)  
  Community feedback on styling decisions. Good for comparative analysis and learning to evaluate trees.

## Unexpected Connections

### Cross-Discipline Links for Wild Cards

- **Ikebana (Japanese flower arrangement)** — shares aesthetic principles (asymmetry, negative space, seasonal emphasis)
- **Suiseki (viewing stones)** — minimalist aesthetic cousin to bonsai, stones chosen for natural form
- **Japanese gardens** — landscape architecture at larger scale, same design principles
- **Zen Buddhism** — wabi-sabi, impermanence, mindfulness practices align with bonsai cultivation
- **Fractals in nature** — ramification patterns, self-similarity at multiple scales
- **Mycorrhizal network research** (Suzanne Simard, "wood-wide web") — interconnected forest systems
- **Biomimicry** — learning from plant adaptation strategies for engineering (e.g., efficient vascular networks)
- **Climate science** — dendrochronology (tree ring analysis), bonsai as climate archives

## Tools & Supplies

Not instructional, but essential for practice:

- **Bonsai tools:** Concave cutters, wire cutters, shears (brands: Kaneshin, Kikuwa, Joshua Roth)
- **Wire:** Aluminum (beginner-friendly, reusable) or copper (stronger hold, harder to use)
- **Soil components:** Akadama, pumice, lava rock (sources: Bonsai Jack, Superfly Bonsai)
- **Pots:** Tokoname (Japan), Chinese mass-market, or handmade ceramics
- **Fertilizer:** Organic (Biogold, Naruko) or chemical (Dyna-Gro, Miracle-Gro diluted)

## Recommended Learning Path

For an intermediate student using this curriculum:

1. **Start:** Read Naka's "Bonsai Techniques I" alongside lessons 1-12 (biology and horticulture)
2. **Supplement:** Watch Bonsai Empire and Herons videos for technique demonstrations
3. **Practice:** Get 2-3 beginner-friendly trees (ficus, Chinese elm, juniper) and follow along with lessons in real time
4. **Community:** Join local ABS club or BonsaiNut forum for feedback and troubleshooting
5. **Deepen:** After completing curriculum, consider Bonsai Mirai subscription for advanced refinement
6. **Visit:** Plan a trip to US National Arboretum, Pacific Bonsai Museum, or major collection to see masterwork trees in person

## Resource Quality Tiers

**Tier 1 (Essential):**  
- Bonsai Empire (free content)  
- Herons Bonsai YouTube  
- One species-appropriate tree to practice on  
- Basic tools (concave cutter, wire cutter, wire)  

**Tier 2 (Recommended):**  
- John Naka's "Bonsai Techniques I"  
- ABS membership + local club  
- BonsaiNut forum participation  
- Visit to a national bonsai collection  

**Tier 3 (Advanced):**  
- Bonsai Mirai subscription  
- "Modern Bonsai Practice" by Jackel  
- Workshop with a professional (Ryan Neil, Bjorn Bjorholm, regional professionals)  
- Yamadori collecting with experienced mentor  

Start with Tier 1, expand as interest and budget allow. The curriculum is designed to work with free resources, but depth improves with investment.
