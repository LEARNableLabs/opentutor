# Phycology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Diversity and Systematics
- **Cyanobacteria** (blue-green algae) — prokaryotic photosynthesizers, nitrogen fixation, evolutionary importance
- **Chlorophyta** (green algae) — closest relatives to land plants, diverse morphology
- **Rhodophyta** (red algae) — complex life cycles, deep-water adaptation, commercial importance
- **Phaeophyceae** (brown algae) — kelps, complex multicellularity, ecological dominance
- **Bacillariophyta** (diatoms) — silica frustules, ecological importance, paleoclimate proxies
- **Dinoflagellata** — bioluminescence, coral symbiosis, harmful algal blooms
- **Other groups** — chrysophytes, euglenoids, cryptophytes

### 2. Cell Biology and Physiology
- Photosynthetic pigments (chlorophylls, phycobilins, carotenoids)
- Cell wall composition and structure
- Storage products (starch, laminarin, chrysolaminarin)
- Motility and flagellar apparatus
- Chloroplast structure and evolution (primary vs. secondary endosymbiosis)

### 3. Reproduction and Life Cycles
- Asexual reproduction (fragmentation, spores, binary fission)
- Sexual reproduction (isogamy, anisogamy, oogamy)
- Alternation of generations
- Life cycle diversity across groups

### 4. Ecology
- Marine vs. freshwater vs. terrestrial habitats
- Phytoplankton ecology and ocean productivity
- Benthic communities (kelp forests, coral reefs)
- Symbioses (lichens, corals, sea slugs)
- Harmful algal blooms (HABs) — red tides, toxins, eutrophication

### 5. Evolution and Phylogeny
- Endosymbiotic theory and chloroplast origins
- Primary, secondary, and tertiary endosymbiosis
- Molecular phylogeny and algal tree of life
- Transition to land plants

### 6. Applications and Biotechnology
- Commercial products (agar, carrageenan, alginates)
- Aquaculture and mariculture
- Biofuels and bioremediation
- Pharmaceuticals and nutraceuticals
- Climate regulation and carbon sequestration

- **Graham, Graham & Wilcox - Algae (3rd Edition, 2016)** — comprehensive undergraduate/graduate text, excellent phylogenetic framework

## Pedagogical Notes

**At the intermediate level**, students typically have completed:
- General biology (cell biology, genetics, ecology)
- Introductory botany or microbiology
- Basic chemistry (organic preferred)

**Approach for this curriculum**:
- Build from familiar (photosynthesis, plants) to unfamiliar (endosymbiosis, diverse life cycles)
- Emphasize comparative approach across groups
- Balance taxonomy/diversity with ecology/applications
- Use visual resources heavily (algae are best understood by seeing them)
- Connect to current issues (climate change, aquaculture, HABs)

**Challenges**:
- Taxonomic complexity (frequent revisions, paraphyletic groups)
- Misconception that algae = plants (they're polyphyletic!)
- Students may lack microscopy experience
- Intimidating diversity (100,000+ species)

**Opportunities**:
- Highly visual and engaging organisms
- Relevant to climate, food security, biotechnology
- Accessible field and lab work
- Beautiful diversity of form and function
