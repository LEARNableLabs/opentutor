# CNC Machining — Research Summary

## Topic: CNC Machining — Toolpath Generation and Material Science

**Level:** Intermediate  
**Date:** 2026-08-21

## Major Subtopics

### 1. CNC Fundamentals
- G-code programming language
- Machine coordinate systems and work offsets
- Tool selection and tool libraries
- Feeds, speeds, and cutting parameters
- Safety and setup procedures

### 2. Toolpath Generation
- CAM (Computer-Aided Manufacturing) software workflow
- 2D vs 3D toolpaths
- Contouring, pocketing, and facing strategies
- Adaptive clearing and trochoidal milling
- Multi-axis toolpath strategies (4-axis, 5-axis)
- Toolpath optimization for cycle time reduction
- Collision detection and avoidance
- Post-processing for specific machines

### 3. Material Science for Machining
- Material properties affecting machinability
- Chip formation mechanics
- Tool wear mechanisms (abrasive, adhesive, diffusion)
- Cutting forces and power requirements
- Heat generation and thermal management
- Surface finish and dimensional accuracy
- Material-specific cutting strategies (aluminum, steel, titanium, plastics, composites)
- Tool coatings and geometries for different materials

### 4. Advanced Topics
- High-speed machining (HSM) principles
- Climb vs conventional milling
- Tool deflection and compensation
- Fixture design for machining
- Metrology and quality control
- Process optimization

## Prerequisites Identified
- Basic algebra and trigonometry
- Understanding of 3D coordinate systems
- Comfort with CAD software (reading technical drawings)
- Basic physics (forces, friction, heat)
- No prior machining experience required at intermediate level

## Learning Objectives
By completion, students should be able to:
- Generate 2D and 3D toolpaths using CAM software
- Select appropriate cutting tools and parameters for different materials
- Understand chip formation and cutting forces
- Optimize toolpaths for cycle time and tool life
- Troubleshoot common machining problems (chatter, poor surface finish, tool breakage)
- Apply material science principles to machining strategy selection
- Simulate and verify toolpaths before running on actual machines

## Pedagogical Notes
- This topic combines theoretical material science with practical CAM software skills
- Best learned with hands-on CAM software practice (Fusion 360 is free and accessible)
- Visual simulations are critical for understanding toolpath strategies
- Real-world examples of machining failures help cement material science concepts
- Intermediate level assumes no prior machining but some technical background
