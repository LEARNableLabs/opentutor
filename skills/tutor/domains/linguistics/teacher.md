## Domain Hooks
1. **Garden path sentences reveal real-time parsing**
   - Example: "The horse raced past the barn fell" is grammatical but hard to parse
   - When to drop this in: Lesson 27 (interface phenomena) or during syntax review
   - Why it's fascinating: Shows how syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic information interact during sentence processing. Connects to psychology and computational linguistics.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Syntax is about correctness; linguists judge which sentences are wrong"**
   - **Why students get this wrong**: Confusion between prescriptive grammar (school rules) and descriptive syntax (mental representations)
   - **How to correct it**: Show that linguists study all varieties of language equally. "I ain't got none" is syntactically systematic. The question isn't "is it correct?" but "what's its structure?"

2. **"Tree diagrams are just a fancy way to diagram sentences like in elementary school"**
   - **Why students get this wrong**: Superficial similarity to traditional sentence diagramming
   - **How to correct it**: Emphasize that trees make empirical claims. They predict constituency tests, movement possibilities, binding domains. Show how different tree structures for "old men and women" predict different meanings.
