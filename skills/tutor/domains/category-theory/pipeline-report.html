Audience Profile

Audience
Math or CS undergrads (junior/senior), early graduate students, and software engineers with functional programming experience who have already encountered basic categorical concepts (categories, functors, natural transformations) and want to develop working fluency with the full intermediate toolkit.
Goal
Build fluency with the core categorical toolkit -- limits/colimits, adjunctions, the Yoneda lemma, monads, and introductory topos theory -- so the student can read mathematical papers that use categorical language, apply categorical reasoning in their own domain (pure math, typed programming, or applied science), and have a solid foundation for approaching advanced topics like higher category theory or applied CT independently.
Background
Assumes: (1) prior exposure to basic category theory at the level of Leinster chapters 1-3 or Milewski season 1 -- comfortable with the definitions of category, functor, natural transformation, and universal property; (2) mathematical maturity -- can follow abstract definitions, reason about commutative diagrams, and handle proof sketches; (3) grounding in at least one concrete domain that provides source examples -- abstract algebra (groups, rings, modules), point-set topology, or typed functional programming (Haskell, OCaml, Scala). Does NOT assume prior knowledge of adjunctions, monads (in the categorical sense), limits/colimits beyond products and coproducts, or any topos theory.
Tone
Rigorous but accessible. Lead with motivating examples and concrete intuition from the student's home domain before stating formal definitions and theorems. Use precise mathematical language but always pair it with an informal reading. Proofs should be included for central results (Yoneda, adjunction-monad correspondence) but presented as guided reasoning rather than terse symbol manipulation. Encourage active engagement through frequent "try this" prompts and diagram-drawing exercises.
Depth
Intuition plus key proofs with applied grounding. Each major concept gets: (a) motivating examples from multiple domains, (b) the formal definition with careful unpacking, (c) proof of the central theorem with commentary on proof technique, (d) concrete exercises that require applying the concept. The course should NOT attempt full formal generality (no enriched categories, no infinity-categories, no advanced topos internals) but should gesture toward these as motivation for further study.
Time
15-20 minutes of focused reading per lesson, plus 10-15 minutes of exercises (diagram-drawing, proof completion, or worked examples). Roughly 30 minutes total per session, designed for daily or near-daily engagement.
Summary
The target student is a mathematically literate learner -- upper-level undergrad, early grad, or experienced programmer -- who already knows what categories, functors, and natural transformations are but cannot yet wield adjunctions, the Yoneda lemma, or monads with confidence. The course builds this intermediate fluency through a rigorous-but-intuitive approach: each concept is motivated by concrete examples, stated precisely, proved where central, and exercised across both mathematical and computational domains. By the end of 20-30 lessons, the student should be able to read category-theoretic arguments in papers and textbooks without constant reference lookups.
