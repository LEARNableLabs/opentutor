# Knot Theory — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Foundational Concepts**
   - Knot diagrams and projections
   - Reidemeister moves (equivalence of diagrams)
   - Knot operations (connected sum, mirror image)
   - Tame vs. wild knots

2. **Combinatorial Invariants**
   - Tricolorability and n-colorability
   - Unknotting number
   - Crossing number
   - Bridge number and braid representation

3. **Polynomial Invariants**
   - Jones polynomial
   - Alexander polynomial
   - HOMFLY polynomial
   - Kauffman polynomial

4. **Algebraic Topology Approaches**
   - Knot group (fundamental group of complement)
   - Homology and cohomology of knot complements
   - Seifert surfaces and Seifert matrices
   - Genus and slice genus

5. **Advanced Topics**
   - Knot homologies (Khovanov, knot Floer)
   - Hyperbolic geometry of knot complements
   - Surgery theory and Dehn surgery
   - Quantum invariants

## Key Sources

## Target Audience Assessment

**Intermediate level** assumes:
- Comfort with abstract mathematical reasoning
- Basic group theory (groups, homomorphisms, quotients)
- Point-set topology (compactness, connectedness, fundamental concepts)
- Some exposure to algebraic topology (fundamental group helpful but can review)
- Linear algebra (for Seifert matrices)

## Pedagogical Approach

Knot theory is uniquely **visual and tactile** — use diagrams heavily, encourage physical manipulation. Balance intuition-building (rope tricks, physical knots) with formal proof. Build from combinatorial invariants (easy to compute, build intuition) toward algebraic invariants (more powerful, harder to compute).

## Available Resources for Lessons

- KnotInfo database for examples and invariant calculations
- YouTube demonstrations of Reidemeister moves
- Interactive knot tables (KnotAtlas)
- arXiv papers for advanced topics
- Historical context (Lord Kelvin's vortex atoms, connections to physics)
