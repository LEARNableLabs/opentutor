# Pirate History — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Colonial trade routes** — Caribbean shipping lanes carrying treasure and goods
2. **Spanish treasure fleets** — Regular convoys vulnerable to attack. Depends on: colonial trade routes
3. **Letters of marque** — Legal authorization for privateering
4. **Legal privateering** — State-sponsored raiding during wartime. Depends on: letters of marque
5. **Merchant marine wages** — Economic conditions that drove sailors to piracy
6. **Naval demobilization** — Post-war unemployment creating pirate recruits. Depends on: merchant marine wages
7. **Ship design** — Technological capabilities of 17th-18th century vessels
8. **Navigation tools** — Instruments enabling long-distance piracy. Depends on: ship design
9. **Buccaneers** — Early Caribbean pirates (1650-1680)
10. **Spanish colonial monopoly** — Target of buccaneer raids. Depends on: colonial trade routes
11. **Tortuga haven** — First major pirate base. Depends on: buccaneers
12. **Henry Morgan** — Transition figure from privateer to pirate. Depends on: buccaneers, legal privateering
13. **Political legitimacy** — How pirates gained pardons. Depends on: Henry Morgan, letters of marque
14. **Pirate Round** — Indian Ocean route (1690s). Depends on: buccaneers, navigation tools
15. **Madagascar base** — Safe haven for Pirate Round operations. Depends on: Pirate Round
16. **Post-war unemployment** — War of Spanish Succession demobilization (1713)
17. **Treaty of Utrecht** — Ended privateering opportunities. Depends on: post-war unemployment, legal privateering
18. **Nassau Bahamas** — The 'Pirate Republic' (1715-1726). Depends on: Tortuga haven
19. **Pirate democracy** — Elected captains and codes. Depends on: Nassau Bahamas
20. **Blackbeard** — Iconic pirate using reputation. Depends on: Nassau Bahamas
21. **Psychological warfare** — Terror tactics vs. actual violence. Depends on: Blackbeard
22. **Bartholomew Roberts** — Most successful pirate by captures. Depends on: pirate democracy, ship design
23. **Tactical innovation** — Roberts' operational methods. Depends on: Bartholomew Roberts
24. **Primary sources** — "A General History of the Pyrates" (1724)
25. **Captain Charles Johnson** — Mysterious author/compiler. Depends on: primary sources
26. **Historical reliability** — Evaluating 18th-century accounts. Depends on: Captain Charles Johnson, primary sources
27. **Hostis humani generis** — "Enemy of all mankind" legal doctrine (Roman origin)
28. **Universal jurisdiction** — Any nation can prosecute pirates. Depends on: hostis humani generis
29. **Admiralty courts** — Maritime legal institutions. Depends on: universal jurisdiction
30. **Prize law** — Rules for legal capture. Depends on: admiralty courts, legal privateering
31. **Legal procedures** — Court processes for piracy trials. Depends on: admiralty courts
32. **UNCLOS** — Modern Law of the Sea Convention. Depends on: universal jurisdiction
33. **Somali piracy** — Modern parallel to Golden Age. Depends on: UNCLOS
34. **Legal precedents** — How historical law shapes modern cases. Depends on: hostis humani generis, UNCLOS
35. **Pirate codes** — Written rules aboard pirate ships. Depends on: pirate democracy
36. **Voting rights** — Crew participation in decisions. Depends on: pirate democracy, pirate codes
37. **Officer elections** — Democratic selection of leaders. Depends on: voting rights
38. **Share system** — Equitable wealth distribution. Depends on: pirate codes
39. **Compensation schemes** — Payments for injuries. Depends on: share system
40. **Disability payments** — Support for wounded crew. Depends on: compensation schemes
41. **Women pirates** — Anne Bonny, Mary Read. Depends on: pirate democracy
42. **Gender disguise** — How women passed as men. Depends on: women pirates
43. **Multi-ethnic crews** — Diverse composition. Depends on: pirate democracy
44. **Escaped slaves** — Black sailors in pirate crews. Depends on: multi-ethnic crews
45. **Racial dynamics** — Equality vs. hierarchy. Depends on: escaped slaves, pirate democracy
46. **Woodes Rogers** — Governor who suppressed Nassau pirates. Depends on: Nassau Bahamas
47. **Royal pardons** — Amnesty strategy. Depends on: Woodes Rogers, political legitimacy
48. **Naval enforcement** — Royal Navy anti-piracy campaigns. Depends on: Woodes Rogers
49. **Captain Kidd trial** — Famous piracy prosecution. Depends on: admiralty courts, legal procedures
50. **Political factors** — How politics influenced piracy trials. Depends on: Captain Kidd trial, political legitimacy

## Dependencies

### Foundational Cluster (Geography & Economics)
- **Colonial trade routes** are the geographic foundation — students need to understand where wealth flowed before understanding why pirates targeted those routes
- **Spanish treasure fleets** and **Spanish colonial monopoly** both depend on understanding the trade network
- **Merchant marine wages** and **naval demobilization** explain the labor supply side — why sailors chose piracy

### Technology Cluster
- **Ship design** → **navigation tools** — students need basic ship knowledge before navigation capabilities make sense
- Both enable **Pirate Round** (long-distance operations) and **tactical innovation**

### Legal Framework Cluster (Critical Path)
- **Letters of marque** → **legal privateering** → **prize law** forms the legal foundation
- **Hostis humani generis** → **universal jurisdiction** → **admiralty courts** forms the enforcement side
- These converge in **legal procedures** and **Captain Kidd trial**
- Modern **UNCLOS** builds on **universal jurisdiction** precedent

### Geographic/Political Progression
- **Buccaneers** → **Tortuga haven** → **Nassau Bahamas** shows spatial evolution
- **Post-war unemployment** + **Treaty of Utrecht** → Nassau explosion is the causal chain for peak piracy

### Social/Cultural Cluster
- **Pirate democracy** is foundational to everything in Pirate Society module
- **Pirate codes** → **voting rights**/**officer elections** AND **share system** → **compensation schemes**
- **Multi-ethnic crews** → **escaped slaves** → **racial dynamics** shows progressive depth

### Suppression Cluster
- **Woodes Rogers** synthesizes **naval enforcement**, **royal pardons**, and **political legitimacy**
- **Captain Kidd trial** synthesizes legal frameworks with political realities

## Bottlenecks

1. **Legal privateering** — Students must understand this before distinguishing pirates from privateers or understanding the post-Utrecht crisis
2. **Pirate democracy** — Unlocks all social/cultural concepts; misconceptions here damage everything downstream
3. **Universal jurisdiction** — Foundational to both historical and modern piracy law
4. **Primary sources** — Skills learned here apply to all subsequent historical analysis

## Common Misconceptions by Concept

- **Pirates vs. privateers** — Students conflate them; the legal distinction is crucial
- **Pirate democracy** — Often romanticized; students need nuance (democracy + brutality coexisted)
- **Hostis humani generis** — Students think it's just philosophical, not realizing it's actual legal doctrine still used today
- **Historical sources** — Students may treat "A General History" as gospel truth rather than questionable contemporary account
- **Racial dynamics** — Easy to oversimplify as "pirates were egalitarian" when reality was more complex
- **Psychological warfare** — Students may think pirates were more violent than they actually were (reputation ≠ reality)

## Prerequisite Topics

- **European colonial history (1500s-1700s)** — Needed for: colonial trade routes, Spanish colonial monopoly, political legitimacy
- **Age of Sail technology** — Needed for: ship design, navigation tools, tactical innovation
- **Common law systems** — Needed for: admiralty courts, legal procedures, prize law
