# Pirate History — Resources

## Primary Sources (18th Century)

### Key Historical Texts
- **"A General History of the Pyrates" (1724)** — Attributed to Captain Charles Johnson. The foundational contemporary account, though reliability is debated. Contains most famous pirate biographies.
  - Full text: https://archive.org/details/generalhistoryof00john
  - Gutenberg edition: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/40580

- **Captain Kidd Trial Transcripts (1699)** — Old Bailey proceedings. Shows admiralty court procedures and political interference.
  - https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/browse.jsp?div=t16990510-39

- **Admiralty Court Records** — UK National Archives holdings. Prize law cases and piracy trials.
  - https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C100

- **Colonial Governors' Correspondence** — Letters about pirate threats and suppression efforts.
  - Yale Avalon Project: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/subject_menus/18th.asp

### Legal Documents
- **Letters of Marque Collection** — Examples of privateer authorizations.
  - https://avalon.law.yale.edu/subject_menus/letters.asp

- **Treaty of Utrecht (1713)** — Ended War of Spanish Succession and privateer employment.
  - https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/sp1713.asp

## Secondary Sources (Modern Scholarship)

### Essential Books

- **Marcus Rediker — "Villains of All Nations: Atlantic Pirates in the Golden Age" (2004)**
  - Social history approach, emphasizes pirates as working-class rebels
  - Strong on multi-ethnic crews and democratic practices
  - Level: Intermediate-Advanced

- **David Cordingly — "Under the Black Flag: The Romance and Reality of Life Among the Pirates" (1995)**
  - Comprehensive overview, excellent for myth-busting
  - Accessible narrative style
  - Level: Beginner-Intermediate

- **Angus Konstam — "The History of Pirates" (1999)**
  - Military history focus, strong on tactics and naval warfare
  - Well-illustrated
  - Level: Intermediate

- **Benerson Little — "The Sea Rover's Practice: Pirate Tactics and Techniques, 1630-1730" (2005)**
  - Deep dive into maritime technology and combat methods
  - Primary source-heavy
  - Level: Advanced
  - Archive.org: https://archive.org/details/searoverspracti00litt

- **Colin Woodard — "The Republic of Pirates" (2007)**
  - Focuses on Nassau period and Woodes Rogers
  - Narrative history, very readable
  - Level: Intermediate

- **Virginia W. Lunsford — "Piracy and Privateering in the Golden Age Netherlands" (2005)**
  - Legal framework and Dutch perspective
  - Academic but accessible
  - Level: Advanced

### Academic Articles (JSTOR)

- **Rediker, Marcus — "The Seaman as Pirate: Plunder and Social Banditry at Sea"** (1987)
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/2636690
  - Foundational article on pirates as labor movement

- **Starkey, David J. — "Pirates and Markets"** (2001)
  - Economic analysis of piracy as business
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/2194256

- **Kontorovich, Eugene — "The Piracy Analogy: Modern Universal Jurisdiction's Hollow Foundation"** (2004)
  - Connects historical to modern piracy law
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/43923442

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### Piracy and Labor History
- **Peter Linebaugh and Marcus Rediker — "The Many-Headed Hydra: Sailors, Slaves, Commoners, and the Hidden History of the Revolutionary Atlantic"**
  - Pirates as part of Atlantic working-class resistance
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7ztpf9

### Piracy and Gender Studies
- **Ulrike Klausmann, Marion Meinzerin, Gabriel Kuhn — "Women Pirates and the Politics of the Jolly Roger"**
  - Academic study of women in piracy

### Piracy and Race
- **Arne Bialuschewski — "Black People under the Black Flag"**
  - Role of escaped slaves and free Black sailors
  - Challenges myths of pirate equality

### Modern Piracy
- **Somali Piracy Research** — One Earth Future Foundation
  - Contemporary piracy economics and suppression
  - Legal continuity from Golden Age

### Pirate Fiction vs. Reality
- **Robert Louis Stevenson — "Treasure Island" (1883)** — Created most enduring pirate myths
- **Tim Powers — "On Stranger Tides" (1987)** — Fantasy novel that influenced Pirates of the Caribbean
- **Comparison study**: What did fiction get wrong? What did it capture?

## Research Tips for Students

1. **Start with museums** — NMM Greenwich and Mariners' Museum have curated, accurate information
2. **Trust academic presses** — University presses publish peer-reviewed work (Cambridge, Oxford, Harvard, Chicago)
3. **Be skeptical of popular sources** — Many pirate websites perpetuate myths
4. **Use JSTOR** — Academic articles provide cutting-edge research
5. **Read primary sources critically** — "A General History" is fascinating but biased
6. **Compare across sources** — Different historians emphasize different aspects (economic, social, military)
7. **Follow the footnotes** — Good books cite primary sources; follow citations back
