## Domain Hooks
- **The Whydah shipwreck** — Only authenticated pirate treasure ship (Black Sam Bellamy, 1717). Archaeological evidence confirms/contradicts historical accounts. Drop when discussing historical evidence (Lesson 13).

- **Pirate medicine and disability** — Wooden legs, hooks, eye patches weren't just myths. Pirate codes included sophisticated disability compensation predating any state system. Drop during share system discussion (Lesson 19).

- **The Madagascar pirate kingdom theory** — Did pirates establish a proto-state at Libertalia? Probably fictional (from "A General History") but reveals pirate utopian thinking. Drop when discussing primary source reliability (Lesson 13).

- **Jewish pirates** — The Inquisition created Jewish pirate communities targeting Spanish ships (Samuel Pallache, Moses Cohen Henriques). Revenge + profit. Drop when discussing multi-ethnic crews (Lesson 21).

- **Chinese pirate confederation** — Ching Shih commanded 40,000+ pirates (1807-1810), dwarfing Caribb

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Pirates were freedom fighters or proto-revolutionaries"** — Students romanticize pirate democracy while ignoring the brutality, slave-trading, and murder. Correction: Emphasize that pirates created egalitarian spaces *for themselves* while often engaging in morally reprehensible acts. Democracy and cruelty coexisted.

2. **"All Caribbean raiders were pirates"** — Students lump privateers, buccaneers, and pirates together. Correction: Drive home the legal distinction repeatedly. A privateer with a letter of marque was a legal combatant; the same person without one was a pirate. Context matters.

3. **"The Golden Age lasted centuries"** — Students blur time periods, thinking piracy was constant from Columbus to the American Revolution. Correction: Be precise about the three distinct periods (1650-1680, 1690s, 1715-1726) and the gaps between them. Geography shifted too.
