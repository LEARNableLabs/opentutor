# Synesthesia — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks and Books

- **Cytowic, R.E. & Eagleman, D. (2009). "Wednesday is Indigo Blue: Discovering the Brain of Synesthesia"**
  - Comprehensive, accessible treatment of synesthesia science
  - Perfect for intermediate level — balances phenomenology and mechanism
  - Covers history, types, neural basis, genetics, and cultural implications

- **Ward, J. (2008). "The Frog Who Croaked Blue: Synesthesia and the Mixing of the Senses"**
  - Written by a leading researcher (Jamie Ward, Sussex)
  - Clear explanations of research methods and findings
  - Good on individual differences and cognitive consequences

- **Cytowic, R.E. (1989/2018). "Synesthesia: A Union of the Senses"** (updated edition)
  - Classic text, historical perspective
  - More phenomenology-focused than recent works
  - Shows how the field evolved from dismissal to acceptance

### Key Research Papers

- **Ramachandran, V.S. & Hubbard, E.M. (2001). "Synaesthesia: A window into perception, thought and language." *Journal of Consciousness Studies***
  - Foundational paper on cross-activation theory
  - Highly cited, clearly written
  - Available: https://www.ted.com/talks/vilayanur_ramachandran_3_clues_to_understanding_your_brain (TED talk version)

- **Ward, J. (2013). "Synesthesia." *Annual Review of Psychology*, 64, 49-75**
  - Comprehensive review of the field as of 2013
  - Excellent summary of evidence for genetic, developmental, and neural accounts
  - Ideal for intermediate students

- **Rouw, R. & Scholte, H.S. (2007). "Increased structural connectivity in grapheme-color synesthesia." *Nature Neuroscience***
  - Key DTI study showing white matter differences
  - Evidence for hyperconnectivity hypothesis

- **Baron-Cohen, S., Harrison, J., Goldstein, L.H., & Wyke, M. (1993). "Coloured speech perception: Is synaesthesia what happens when modularity breaks down?" *Perception***
  - Early influential paper on modularity and synesthesia

- **Simner, J. et al. (2006). "Synaesthesia: The prevalence of atypical cross-modal experiences." *Perception***
  - Large-scale prevalence study
  - Important for understanding how common synesthesia actually is

### Interactive Tools

- **The Synesthesia Battery** — https://www.synesthete.org/
  - Gold-standard online testing for grapheme-color synesthesia
  - Students can test themselves, see how consistency is measured
  - Free, scientifically validated
  - Developed by David Eagleman's lab

- **Synesthesia test at University of Sussex**
  - https://www.sussex.ac.uk/profiles/155 (Jamie Ward's page, links to lab resources)
  - Additional testing and information

- **Interactive demonstrations of cross-modal effects:**
  - McGurk effect videos (YouTube: search "McGurk effect")
  - Rubber hand illusion demonstrations
  - Sound symbolism tests (bouba-kiki)

### Code and Data

- **No major public datasets or code repos specifically for synesthesia research**
  - Most data is collected in individual labs and not openly shared
  - Neuroimaging studies use standard fMRI/DTI analysis pipelines (FSL, SPM, FreeSurfer)

- **Potential for student projects:**
  - Create a consistency testing app (similar to Synesthesia Battery)
  - Analyze public neuroimaging datasets for cross-modal activation patterns
  - Build a Stroop-like interference task for synesthetic associations

### Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Art and Synesthesia**
  - Kandinsky — paintings inspired by synesthetic color-sound associations
  - Nabokov — writer with elaborate grapheme-color synesthesia (documented in "Speak, Memory")
  - Messiaen — composer who saw colors for musical keys and chords
  - Caution: Not all artists claiming synesthesia have been verified

- **Synesthesia and Psychedelics**
  - Cross-modal experiences common in LSD, psilocybin, mescaline
  - Serotonergic pathways implicated
  - Link to research on 5-HT2A receptors and perception

- **Sensory Substitution Devices**
  - The vOICe — camera-to-sound device for blind users
  - BrainPort — camera-to-tongue electric stimulation
  - Research on whether trained cross-modal mappings resemble synesthesia

- **Perfect Pitch and Synesthesia**
  - Possible shared mechanisms
  - Both involve automatic, specific bindings (frequency to label, grapheme to color)

- **Cross-Modal Perception in Other Species**
  - Evidence in rats, monkeys
  - Questions about evolutionary origins

- **Autism and Synesthesia**
  - Some overlap in sensory processing differences
  - Potential shared genetic factors
  - Both involve atypical perceptual binding

## Recommended Learning Path

1. **Start with TED talk** (Ramachandran) for motivation and overview
2. **Take Synesthesia Battery** (https://www.synesthete.org/) to see testing methodology
3. **Read Ward (2013) Annual Review paper** for comprehensive, up-to-date summary
4. **Watch BBC Horizon documentary** for rich phenomenology and personal stories
5. **Read "Wednesday is Indigo Blue"** (Cytowic & Eagleman) for deep dive
6. **Explore Jamie Ward's research** (https://www.sussex.ac.uk/profiles/155) for current work
7. **Follow rabbit holes** based on interest (genetics, art, applications, etc.)

## Gaps and Opportunities

- **Limited open datasets** — most synesthesia data is not publicly available
- **Few dedicated courses** — synesthesia is usually a unit within a broader perception/neuroscience course
- **No standardized curriculum** — this OpenTutor curriculum fills a gap
- **Opportunities for student projects**: design tests, run surveys, build tools, meta-analyses of published data
