# Synesthesia — Cross-Modal Perception Research

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Phenomenology of Synesthesia
- Types: grapheme-color, sound-color, lexical-gustatory, number forms, spatial sequence
- Subjective reports and individual differences
- Consistency testing and verification methods

### 2. Neural Mechanisms
- Cross-activation theory (adjacent cortical areas)
- Disinhibited feedback theory
- Hyperconnectivity in synesthetes
- Structural and functional brain differences

### 3. Genetics and Development
- Familial clustering and heritability
- Developmental trajectory (childhood to adulthood)
- Gene candidates and molecular mechanisms
- Pruning and critical periods

### 4. Cross-Modal Perception (General)
- Multisensory integration in typical perception
- McGurk effect and other cross-modal illusions
- Temporal and spatial binding
- Superior colliculus and multisensory convergence zones

### 5. Research Methods
- The Synesthesia Battery and consistency testing
- fMRI and structural imaging
- Behavioral paradigms (Stroop-like tasks)
- Psychophysical methods

### 6. Cognitive Consequences
- Memory advantages in synesthetes
- Creativity and synesthesia
- Perceptual advantages and disadvantages
- Cognitive load and interference

### 7. Applications and Implications
- Art, music, and culture
- Design and accessibility
- Educational implications
- Sensory substitution devices

## Research Landscape

Synesthesia research sits at the intersection of cognitive neuroscience, perception, genetics, and consciousness studies. The field has matured significantly since the 1990s revival, moving from phenomenological description to mechanistic understanding. Current frontiers include genetic mapping, computational modeling of cross-modal binding, and applications in sensory substitution.

The intermediate curriculum should balance phenomenology (what it's like), mechanism (how it works), methodology (how we study it), and broader implications for understanding typical perception.
