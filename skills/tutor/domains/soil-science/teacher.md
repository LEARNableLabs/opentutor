## Domain Hooks
These are fascinating tangents to drop in when the moment is right—topics that reveal unexpected depth or connections and can ignite curiosity:

- **Soil color meanings** — What the Munsell color (e.g., 10YR 4/3) actually tells you about mineralogy, drainage, and organic matter. Why red soils indicate oxidized iron, gray soils indicate reduction, and black soils indicate organic matter. When to drop: lesson on horizons or soil description (lesson 6 or 10).

- **The oldest soils on Earth** — Australian Oxisols that are millions of years old, versus very young soils on recent volcanic islands or glacial till. What happens when the time factor goes to extremes? When to drop: lesson on time factor (lesson 8).

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Soil is just ground-up rock"** — Students underestimate the biological and temporal aspects of soil formation. They miss that soil is a living, dynamic system with ongoing transformations, not a static product. Correct by emphasizing organisms, organic matter, and time as active agents in the five factors.

2. **"Soil horizons are distinct boundaries"** — In reality, horizon boundaries are often gradual and irregular. Students expect neat layers like a geology textbook. Show photos of real soil pits and discuss the judgment involved in horizon designation.

3. **"Clay is just very small particles"** — Students miss that clay-sized particles have fundamentally different properties (surface charge, CEC) due to their colloidal nature, not just size. Emphasize the chemical activity of clays, not just their physical dimensions.
