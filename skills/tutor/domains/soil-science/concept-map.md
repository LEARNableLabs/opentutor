# Soil Science & Pedology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Soil as a system** — soil as interface between lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere
2. **Soil functions** — ecological, agricultural, engineering, environmental roles
3. **Field observation** — techniques for examining and sampling soils
4. **Soil-forming factors (CLORPT)** — climate, organisms, relief, parent material, time
5. **Weathering** — physical, chemical, and biological breakdown of parent material. Depends on: climate, organisms
6. **Soil horizonation** — development of distinct layers through pedogenic processes. Depends on: weathering, time
7. **Eluviation and illuviation** — vertical movement of materials creating horizon differentiation. Depends on: horizonation, water movement
8. **Climate effects on pedogenesis** — temperature and moisture regimes driving soil formation. Depends on: CLORPT, weathering
9. **Soil age and maturity** — temporal dimension of soil development. Depends on: CLORPT, horizonation
10. **Particle size distribution** — sand, silt, clay fractions defining soil texture
11. **Soil texture** — fundamental physical property affecting all other properties. Depends on: particle size, parent material
12. **Soil structure** — arrangement of particles into aggregates. Depends on: texture, organic matter, biological activity
13. **Soil porosity** — void space affecting water and air movement. Depends on: texture, structure
14. **Soil water dynamics** — infiltration, permeability, water-holding capacity. Depends on: texture, structure, porosity
15. **Bulk density** — soil mass per unit volume; indicator of compaction. Depends on: texture, structure, porosity
16. **Soil pH** — acidity/alkalinity affecting nutrient availability and biological activity
17. **Cation exchange capacity (CEC)** — capacity to hold and exchange nutrient cations. Depends on: clay mineralogy, organic matter, pH
18. **Soil organic matter** — decomposed plant and animal residues. Depends on: organisms, climate, management
19. **Carbon cycling** — transformation and storage of carbon in soil. Depends on: organic matter, decomposition, climate
20. **Soil biodiversity** — microorganisms, fauna, and their functional roles. Depends on: organic matter, pH, moisture
21. **Bioturbation** — soil mixing by organisms. Depends on: soil fauna, soil structure
22. **Rhizosphere processes** — plant-microbe interactions in root zone. Depends on: soil biology, chemical properties
23. **Diagnostic horizons** — specific horizon types used in classification. Depends on: horizonation, pedogenic processes
24. **Soil taxonomy** — hierarchical classification system (order to series). Depends on: diagnostic horizons, soil properties
25. **Soil orders** — highest level of classification (12 orders in USDA system). Depends on: taxonomy, diagnostic properties
26. **Soil survey and mapping** — systematic inventory and spatial representation of soils. Depends on: classification, field methods
27. **Soil fertility** — capacity to provide nutrients for plant growth. Depends on: CEC, pH, organic matter, nutrient cycling
28. **Soil conservation** — practices to prevent degradation. Depends on: understanding erosion, structure, organic matter
29. **Soil carbon sequestration** — climate mitigation through soil management. Depends on: carbon cycling, soil health, conservation

## Dependencies

### Foundation → Physical
- **Particle size** is determined by parent material weathering, but doesn't depend on understanding other soil concepts
- **Texture** is the foundation for understanding structure, water dynamics, and many chemical properties
- **Structure** requires understanding texture because it describes how particles aggregate
- **Porosity** depends on both texture (primary pores) and structure (secondary pores)
- **Water dynamics** cannot be understood without texture, structure, and porosity

### Physical → Chemical
- **CEC** is heavily influenced by texture (clay content) and organic matter
- **pH** affects CEC and is affected by climate, parent material, and organic matter
- **Organic matter** accumulation depends on climate but also affects structure and CEC

### Chemical + Physical → Biological
- **Soil biodiversity** requires understanding of habitat (pores, structure) and resources (organic matter, nutrients)
- **Rhizosphere** chemistry requires understanding of CEC, pH, and nutrient availability

### All → Classification
- **Diagnostic horizons** integrate physical, chemical, and biological properties
- **Soil orders** cannot be distinguished without understanding diagnostic features and pedogenic processes

### All → Applications
- **Fertility management** requires integration of physical (texture, water), chemical (CEC, pH), and biological (nutrient cycling) concepts
- **Conservation** requires understanding erosion (physical), organic matter (chemical), and biological processes
- **Carbon sequestration** integrates all aspects: physical protection, chemical stabilization, biological transformation

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic chemistry** — needed for pH, CEC, chemical weathering, nutrient cycling
- **Basic geology** — needed for parent material, weathering, mineralogy
- **Environmental science fundamentals** — needed for ecosystem concepts, biogeochemical cycles

## Conceptual Bottlenecks

1. **Horizonation** — students often struggle to visualize three-dimensional soil development. Success here is critical for classification.

2. **CEC** — abstract concept requiring understanding of charged surfaces, ions, and exchange reactions. Gateway to understanding nutrient dynamics.

3. **Soil taxonomy** — the hierarchical classification system can feel overwhelming. Requires integration of all prior concepts.

4. **Scale thinking** — students must shift between microscale (clay particles, microbes), pedon scale (soil profile), and landscape scale (soil survey). This cognitive shift is challenging but essential.

## Common Learning Paths

- **Agricultural focus** — emphasize fertility, CEC, pH, organic matter, conservation early; classification later
- **Environmental focus** — emphasize carbon cycling, erosion, contamination; integrate throughout
- **Geological focus** — emphasize parent material, weathering, chronosequences; strong foundation in pedogenesis
- **Ecological focus** — emphasize soil as ecosystem, biodiversity, rhizosphere, organic matter dynamics

This curriculum follows a **process-based path**: formation → properties → biology → classification → application. This builds mechanistic understanding before categorization.
