# HEMA Resources

## Primary Historical Sources (Free Online)

### Essential Treatises for Intermediate Students

**[Wiktenauer](https://wiktenauer.com/)**  
The largest open-source collection of digitized fight books with translations. Essential starting point.

**[MS I.33](https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/MS_I.33)** (c. 1300)  
Oldest surviving European fight book. Sword and buckler. Well-illustrated, good entry point for reading fight books.

**[Fiore dei Liberi - Fior di Battaglia](https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/Fiore_de%27i_Liberi)** (1409)  
Italian master's complete system. Multiple manuscripts available. Rich diagrams, covers longsword, dagger, wrestling, armored combat.

**[Johannes Liechtenauer - Zettel](https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/Johannes_Liechtenauer)**  
Foundational German longsword system in cryptic verse. Multiple glosses/commentaries available (Ringeck, Danzig, Lew).

**[Joachim Meyer - Art of Combat](https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/Joachim_Meyer)** (1570)  
Comprehensive German system. Well-structured, pedagogical. Covers longsword, dussack, rapier, staff.

**[Ridolfo Capo Ferro - Gran Simulacro](https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/Ridolfo_Capo_Ferro)** (1610)  
Italian rapier system. Clear illustrations, influential text.

**[Salvator Fabris - Lo Schermo](https://wiktenauer.com/wiki/Salvator_Fabris)** (1606)  
Advanced Italian rapier theory. Detailed discussion of measure and tempo.

## Modern Books (Purchase/Library)

### Interpretation and Practice

**"The Art of Longsword Fighting" by Christian Tobler**  
Scholarly interpretation of Liechtenauer tradition. Excellent for understanding German longsword concepts (Indes, Vor/Nach, winding).

**"The Swordsman's Companion" by Guy Windsor**  
Practical training manual for Fiore's system. Clear progressions, exercise design.

**"Medieval Swordsmanship" by John Clements**  
Foundational text for many HEMA practitioners. Controversial in some interpretations, but influential.

**"Joachim Meyer Frei Fechter" translated by Jeffrey Forgeng**  
Scholarly translation of Meyer with extensive footnotes. Essential for serious study.

### Historical Context

**"The Secret History of the Sword" by J. Christoph Amberger**  
Rapier fencing history, dueling culture, social context. Engaging read.

**"By the Sword" by Richard Cohen**  
Broader history of swordsmanship from ancient times to modern sport fencing. Good background.

### Academic References

**Sydney Anglo - "The Martial Arts of Renaissance Europe"**  
Scholarly treatment of fight books as historical documents. Rigorous source criticism.

**Tobias Capwell - "The Real Fighting Stuff"**  
Armor and armored combat from museum perspective. Connects material culture to fighting techniques.

## Learning Pathways by Interest

### If you want to focus on **German Longsword**:
1. Start with Wiktenauer's Liechtenauer Zettel and Ringeck gloss
2. Read Christian Tobler's "Art of Longsword Fighting"
3. Watch Blood and Iron HEMA YouTube channel
4. Study Meyer's longsword sections for more detailed pedagogy

### If you want to focus on **Italian Rapier**:
1. Start with Capo Ferro's Gran Simulacro (shorter, clearer)
2. Progress to Fabris for deeper theory
3. Read J. Christoph Amberger's "Secret History of the Sword"
4. Watch Scholagladiatoria's rapier videos

### If you want to focus on **Fiore's System**:
1. Read Wiktenauer's Fiore manuscripts (multiple versions available)
2. Get Guy Windsor's "Swordsman's Companion"
3. Join Fiore-focused online communities (School of European Swordsmanship, others)
4. Watch Guy Windsor's instructional videos

### If you want to focus on **Historical Context**:
1. Read Sydney Anglo's "Martial Arts of Renaissance Europe"
2. Listen to Historical Fencing podcast
3. Read academic papers on guilds, Fechtschulen, dueling culture
4. Watch Scholagladiatoria's context-heavy videos

### If you want to **Compete in Tournaments**:
1. Find a local club via HEMA Alliance
2. Watch tournament footage (Longpoint, Swordfish) to understand rulesets
3. Study HEMA Scorecard to see competitive meta
4. Train with qualified instructors (required for safe sparring)

### If you want to **Become an Instructor**:
1. Train extensively in-person (years, not months)
2. Study pedagogy (Guy Windsor's curriculum design, Meyer's teaching progressions)
3. Pursue HEMA Alliance instructor development resources
4. Gain experience as assistant instructor at a club
5. Study source interpretation methodology

## Cautions and Caveats

**Translation Quality Varies**  
Not all Wiktenauer translations are equal. Recent scholarly translations (Forgeng, Tobler, Windsor) are more reliable than older amateur work. Cross-reference when possible.

**Instructor Lineages**  
Unlike Asian martial arts, HEMA has no unbroken lineages. All modern practice is reconstructed. "My instructor learned from X who learned from Y" doesn't guarantee historical accuracy. Judge interpretations on evidence, not lineage.

**Community Debates**  
HEMA has active debates about interpretation, sport vs history, tournament rulesets, etc. This is healthy, but can be overwhelming. Learn to distinguish productive debates (evidence-based, good-faith) from bike-shedding.

**Safety First**  
Videos and books cannot replace in-person instruction for partner drills and sparring. Solo practice is valuable, but safe paired work requires qualified supervision.

**Equipment Costs**  
Full HEMA gear (mask, jacket, gloves, sword) can cost $500-1500. Start with minimal gear (mask, gloves, synthetic sword) and expand as you progress. Many clubs have loaner gear.

**No "One True System"**  
Historical masters disagreed with each other. Modern instructors interpret sources differently. There is no single "correct" HEMA. Embrace the diversity and ambiguity.

## Finding a Local Club

**[HEMA Alliance Club Finder](https://hemaalliance.com/club-finders)**  
Search by location worldwide. Check club's focus (weapon, tradition, sport vs historical).

**Tips**:
- Visit/observe before joining
- Ask about instructor qualifications and training philosophy
- Check if club is sport-focused, history-focused, or balanced (match your goals)
- Inquire about beginner programs and loaner gear
- Look for active community, not just drilling (sparring, discussions, events)

## Resource Update Cycle

HEMA is a growing field. New translations, research, and instructional content appear regularly.

**Stay current**:
- Subscribe to key YouTube channels
- Follow HEMA Alliance news
- Check Wiktenauer for new uploads
- Join online communities for announcements
- Attend (in-person or virtual) HEMA events and workshops

**Older resources may be outdated**:
- Early 2000s HEMA had less scholarship, more speculation
- Some influential early books have been superseded by better translations
- Tournament rulesets evolve
- Equipment standards improve

When in doubt, check publication/upload date and cross-reference with recent discussions.
