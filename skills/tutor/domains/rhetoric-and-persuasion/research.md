# Rhetoric and Persuasion — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Classical Rhetoric Foundations**
   - Aristotelian rhetoric and the three appeals (ethos, pathos, logos)
   - The five canons of rhetoric (invention, arrangement, style, memory, delivery)
   - Sophistic tradition and rhetorical theory evolution

2. **Argument Structure and Analysis**
   - Stasis theory and topoi
   - Logical fallacies and sound reasoning
   - Rhetorical criticism and analysis methods

3. **Style and Delivery**
   - Figures of speech and rhetorical devices
   - Voice, tone, and register
   - Public speaking vs. written persuasion

4. **Contemporary Applications**
   - Digital rhetoric and social media persuasion
   - Visual rhetoric and multimodal communication
   - Generative AI's impact on rhetoric
   - Political and civic discourse

5. **Audience and Context**
   - Audience adaptation and identification
   - Kairos (opportune moment)
   - Cultural and situational context

## Key Sources

### University Courses
- **Harvard University**: "Rhetoric: The Art of Persuasive Writing and Public Speaking" (Professor James Engell) — covers classical foundations through modern applications including AI impact
- **MIT OpenCourseWare**: 21W.747 Rhetoric — examination of theory, practice, and rhetorical criticism
- **University of Iowa**: RHET:2055 "Persuasion and Advocacy" — audience adaptation, reasoned appeals, character
- **Ohio State University**: English 2276 — rhetoric in public and political discourse

### Textbooks and Educational Materials
- **Rhetoric Alive! Book 1: Principles of Persuasion** (Alyssan Barnes) — based on Aristotle's Rhetoric, covers three appeals, three types of speech, five canons
- **The Rhetoric Companion** — stasis theory, ancient texts, Christian perspective on persuasion
- **Persuading People: An Introduction to Rhetoric** (Springer) — introductory college-level text
- **OER Commons and Open Textbook Library** — various open educational resources for rhetoric instruction

### Online Courses and Video Resources
- **Harvard/edX**: Rhetoric course with video lectures, practical assignments (op-eds, speeches), discussion forums
- **Hillsdale College**: "Classical Logic and Rhetoric" — 10 lectures (~30 min each) on valid arguments and persuasive speaking
- **Veritas Press**: Self-paced rhetoric course with Dr. Michael Collender (TEDx speaker)

## Research Gaps for Intermediate Level

Students at intermediate level should already have:
- Basic understanding of argumentation
- Exposure to formal writing
- Some familiarity with logical reasoning

The curriculum should focus on:
- Deepening classical rhetoric knowledge
- Applying theory to real-world scenarios
- Developing analytical skills for rhetorical criticism
- Practicing persuasive composition across media
- Understanding contemporary contexts (digital, visual, AI-influenced)
