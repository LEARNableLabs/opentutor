# Rhetoric and Persuasion — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Rhetoric definition** — the art of discovering available means of persuasion in any situation
2. **Persuasion vs. manipulation** — ethical boundaries between influence and coercion
3. **Rhetorical situation** — speaker, audience, purpose, and context as a system. Depends on: rhetoric definition
4. **Audience analysis** — understanding audience values, knowledge, and expectations. Depends on: rhetorical situation
5. **Rhetorical triangle** — speaker-audience-message relationship. Depends on: rhetorical situation, audience analysis
6. **Kairos** — the opportune moment or timing in rhetoric. Depends on: rhetorical situation
7. **Sophistic tradition** — early Greek teachers of rhetoric focused on winning arguments
8. **Platonic skepticism** — Plato's critique of rhetoric as mere flattery vs. truth-seeking
9. **Aristotelian synthesis** — Aristotle's systematic approach combining ethics and persuasion. Depends on: sophistic tradition, Platonic skepticism
10. **Five canons** — invention, arrangement, style, memory, delivery as organizing framework. Depends on: Aristotelian synthesis
11. **Invention** — discovering arguments and content (canon 1)
12. **Arrangement** — organizing arguments for maximum effect (canon 2). Depends on: invention
13. **Style** — choosing language and rhetorical devices (canon 3). Depends on: invention, arrangement
14. **Memory** — retaining and recalling material (canon 4)
15. **Delivery** — vocal and physical presentation (canon 5). Depends on: style
16. **Stasis theory** — determining the central issue (fact, definition, quality, policy). Depends on: invention
17. **Classical structure** — exordium, narratio, confirmatio, refutatio, peroratio. Depends on: arrangement
18. **Ethos** — credibility and character appeal. Depends on: Aristotelian synthesis
19. **Pathos** — emotional appeal. Depends on: Aristotelian synthesis, audience analysis
20. **Logos** — logical appeal. Depends on: Aristotelian synthesis
21. **Authority** — expertise-based credibility. Depends on: ethos
22. **Goodwill** — perceived benevolence toward audience. Depends on: ethos
23. **Emotional reasoning** — using audience emotions to move them to action. Depends on: pathos
24. **Identification** — creating common ground with audience. Depends on: pathos, audience analysis
25. **Deductive reasoning** — reasoning from general to specific. Depends on: logos
26. **Inductive reasoning** — reasoning from specific to general. Depends on: logos
27. **Enthymeme** — rhetorical syllogism with implied premise. Depends on: logos, deductive reasoning
28. **Integrated appeals** — combining ethos, pathos, logos strategically. Depends on: ethos, pathos, logos
29. **Appeal dominance** — which appeal to emphasize for context. Depends on: integrated appeals, audience analysis
30. **Validity** — logical structure correctness. Depends on: logos
31. **Soundness** — valid structure + true premises. Depends on: validity
32. **Logical fallacies** — errors in reasoning that undermine arguments. Depends on: logos, validity
33. **Straw man** — misrepresenting opponent's argument. Depends on: logical fallacies
34. **Ad hominem** — attacking person instead of argument. Depends on: logical fallacies
35. **False dilemma** — presenting only two options when more exist. Depends on: logical fallacies
36. **Analogy** — comparison-based reasoning. Depends on: logos
37. **Toulmin model** — claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal framework. Depends on: logos, arrangement
38. **Evidence types** — statistical, testimonial, anecdotal, empirical. Depends on: logos
39. **Schemes** — patterns of word arrangement (parallelism, anaphora). Depends on: style
40. **Tropes** — figures of speech that shift meaning (metaphor, irony). Depends on: style
41. **Register** — formality level appropriate to context. Depends on: style, audience analysis
42. **Voice** — distinctive speaker personality in text. Depends on: style
43. **Vocal delivery** — pitch, volume, rate, pause in speaking. Depends on: delivery
44. **Body language** — gesture, posture, movement in speaking. Depends on: delivery
45. **Visual rhetoric** — persuasion through images and design. Depends on: multimodal rhetoric
46. **Multimodal rhetoric** — combining text, image, sound, movement. Depends on: style, delivery
47. **Digital rhetoric** — persuasion in online and social media contexts. Depends on: multimodal rhetoric
48. **Rhetorical ethics** — moral constraints on persuasive practice. Depends on: persuasion vs. manipulation
49. **AI-generated content** — machine-produced persuasive text. Depends on: digital rhetoric

## Dependencies

- **The three appeals (ethos, pathos, logos)** build on Aristotle's systematic framework — understanding his synthesis of earlier traditions is essential before distinguishing the appeals
- **Stasis theory** requires understanding invention because it's a tool for discovering the core issue in any argument
- **Classical structure** depends on arrangement principles — you need to understand how to organize before learning the traditional five-part structure
- **Integrated appeals** requires mastery of individual appeals first — students must know how each works before combining them
- **Toulmin model** builds on both logical reasoning and arrangement — it's a modern framework that systematizes argument structure
- **Logical fallacies** make sense only after understanding what valid logos looks like — they're deviations from sound reasoning
- **Digital rhetoric** requires understanding multimodal communication first — social media combines text, image, video, and interaction
- **Rhetorical ethics** circles back to the opening distinction between persuasion and manipulation, but with much deeper tools for analysis

## Bottlenecks

**Logos foundation (lessons 10, 13)** — if students don't grasp deductive/inductive reasoning and validity/soundness, they'll struggle with fallacy identification, Toulmin model, and evidence evaluation. This is the conceptual foundation for lessons 14-18.

**The three appeals as system (lessons 8-12)** — understanding that ethos, pathos, and logos work together, not in isolation, is essential for all rhetorical analysis. Students who see them as discrete "tricks" rather than integrated strategy will produce mechanical analyses.

**Rhetorical situation (lessons 1-2)** — if students don't internalize that all rhetoric is situational (audience, purpose, context-dependent), they'll miss why strategies shift across contexts. This underlies everything from appeal selection to style choices.

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic argumentation** — needed for understanding logos, validity, premises/conclusions
- **Formal writing skills** — needed for style analysis, revision, composition practice
- **Reading comprehension** — needed for rhetorical analysis, identifying appeals and structure in texts

## Common Learning Paths

Students typically find **pathos** most intuitive (everyone understands emotional manipulation from advertising), then struggle with **logos** (formal reasoning is harder), and often underestimate **ethos** (credibility seems simple but has subtle layers).

The **Toulmin model** is challenging because it requires synthesizing logical reasoning with argument structure — students grasp premises/conclusions but struggle with warrants (the implicit connection).

**Digital rhetoric** helps students see classical concepts as living tools — analyzing a viral tweet through Aristotelian appeals makes ancient rhetoric feel urgent and practical.
