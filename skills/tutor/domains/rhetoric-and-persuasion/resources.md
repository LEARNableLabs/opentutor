## Classical Texts

### Essential Primary Texts

- **Aristotle, *Rhetoric*** — The foundational text. Defines the three appeals, discusses topics and fallacies, addresses style and delivery. Essential for lessons 1-3, 8-10.

- **Cicero, *De Oratore*** — Roman expansion of rhetorical theory, emphasis on the orator as educated citizen. Good for understanding the five canons deeply.

- **Quintilian, *Institutio Oratoria*** — Comprehensive rhetoric and education treatise. Useful for pedagogical perspective on teaching rhetoric.

- **Plato, *Gorgias* and *Phaedrus*** — Platonic critique of rhetoric vs. dialectic. Important for understanding tensions in rhetorical tradition.

### Modern Rhetorical Theory

- **Kenneth Burke, *A Rhetoric of Motives*** — Identification, dramatism, and persuasion as identity-formation. Advanced, but rewarding for rabbit holes.

- **Chaim Perelman & Lucie Olbrechts-Tyteca, *The New Rhetoric*** — Modern argumentation theory, audience-centered approach.

- **Wayne Booth, *The Rhetoric of Rhetoric*** — Contemporary defense of rhetoric as ethical inquiry.

## Supplementary Resources

### Video Lectures and Talks

- **TED Talks on Persuasion and Communication** — Search TED.com for talks by:
  - Julian Treasure (speaking techniques)
  - Amy Cuddy (presence and body language)
  - Simon Sinek (start with why — rhetorical framing)
  - Celeste Headlee (conversation and listening)

- **Yale Open Courses** — Search for rhetoric, persuasion, and classical studies lectures.

- **The Art of Rhetoric** (various YouTube channels) — Many channels offer breakdowns of famous speeches (analyze MLK, Churchill, etc.).

### Interactive Tools and Websites

- **Silva Rhetoricae** — [rhetoric.byu.edu](http://rhetoric.byu.edu) — Comprehensive database of rhetorical terms, figures, and schemes. Excellent reference.

- **American Rhetoric** — [americanrhetoric.com](https://www.americanrhetoric.com) — Archive of famous speeches with audio/video and transcripts. Perfect for analysis practice.

- **Argument Mapping Software**:
  - **Rationale** — Visual argument mapping tool
  - **Arguman** — Collaborative argument mapping platform
  - **Argunet** — Free argument diagramming software

- **Purdue OWL: Logic in Argumentative Writing** — [owl.purdue.edu](https://owl.purdue.edu) — Free guide to logical reasoning, fallacies, and argument structure.

### Code and Digital Rhetoric

- **Persuasive Technology Lab (Stanford)** — Research on how technology persuades (apps, websites, interfaces). Relevant for digital rhetoric.

- **Rhetoric of Code** — GitHub repositories analyzing how code documentation persuades (README files as rhetoric).

### People to Follow

**Contemporary Rhetoricians and Communication Scholars:**

- **Jay Heinrichs** — Author of *Thank You for Arguing*, makes rhetoric accessible and practical. Follow his blog and Twitter.

- **Jeanne Fahnestock** — Rhetoric of science scholar, excellent on how scientific arguments work.

- **Deborah Tannen** — Linguist studying conversational rhetoric and argument patterns.

- **George Lakoff** — Cognitive linguist on metaphor and framing in political rhetoric.

- **Roxane Gay** — Public intellectual who models strong contemporary rhetoric (op-eds, essays).

**Historical Figures to Study:**

- **Frederick Douglass** — Master of abolitionist rhetoric, brilliant use of ethos and pathos.

- **Sojourner Truth** — "Ain't I a Woman?" — powerful example of vernacular rhetoric.

- **Abraham Lincoln** — Gettysburg Address, Second Inaugural — concise, memorable, profound.

- **Martin Luther King Jr.** — "I Have a Dream," "Letter from Birmingham Jail" — integrates biblical, constitutional, and emotional appeals.

- **Winston Churchill** — WWII speeches — demonstrates vocal delivery and motivational rhetoric.

### Unexpected Connections

- **Game Design as Rhetoric** — Ian Bogost's *Persuasive Games* — how games make arguments through their mechanics (e.g., *Papers, Please* on immigration).

- **Architecture and Rhetoric** — How buildings persuade (e.g., courthouse design communicates authority, transparency, or intimidation).

- **Food Writing and Rhetoric** — M.F.K. Fisher, Michael Pollan — how sensory language persuades and creates desire.

- **Data Visualization as Rhetoric** — Edward Tufte on how charts and graphs argue. The design choices make implicit claims.

- **Musical Rhetoric** — Baroque *musica poetica* and how musical structure parallels rhetorical structure (exordium = opening theme, etc.).

## Curated Reading Lists by Module

### Classical Foundations (Lessons 1-7)
- Aristotle, *Rhetoric* Book I (translation: George Kennedy or Joe Sachs)
- Plato, *Phaedrus* (excerpts on true vs. false rhetoric)
- Sharon Crowley & Debra Hawhee, *Ancient Rhetorics for Contemporary Students*

### The Three Appeals (Lessons 8-12)
- Aristotle, *Rhetoric* Book II on pathos and ethos
- Jay Heinrichs, *Thank You for Arguing* (Chapters on logos, pathos, ethos)
- Examples: MLK's "Letter from Birmingham Jail" (integrated appeals)

### Argument Structure (Lessons 13-19)
- Stephen Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument* (excerpts on the Toulmin model)
- Wayne Brockriede & Douglas Ehninger, "Toulmin on Argument: An Interpretation and Application"
- Purdue OWL resources on logical fallacies

### Style and Delivery (Lessons 20-24)
- Aristotle, *Rhetoric* Book III on style and delivery
- Richard Lanham, *A Handlist of Rhetorical Terms*
- TED Talks on speaking (Amy Cuddy, Julian Treasure)

### Applied Rhetoric (Lessons 25-28)
- danah boyd, *It's Complicated: The Social Lives of Networked Teens* (digital rhetoric)
- James Paul Gee, *Social Linguistics and Literacies* (discourse and identity)
- Articles on AI-generated text and authenticity (search recent scholarship, 2024-2026)

## Tools for Practice

- **Hemingway Editor** — Analyzes prose readability, helps students see how sentence structure affects clarity (stylistic rhetoric).
- **Grammarly** — Beyond grammar, identifies tone and formality shifts (register awareness).
- **Toastmasters** — Public speaking practice clubs worldwide (delivery practice).
- **Debate.org** — Online debate platform for practicing argumentation.
- **Google Ngram Viewer** — Track language usage over time (historical rhetoric patterns).
