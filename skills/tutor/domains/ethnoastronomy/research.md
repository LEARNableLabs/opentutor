# Ethnoastronomy — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Indigenous Australian Sky Knowledge
- Emu in the Sky and dark constellations
- Songlines and celestial navigation
- Seasonal calendars tied to celestial events
- Oral transmission and knowledge systems

### 2. Polynesian Wayfinding
- Star compass and oceanic navigation
- Rising/setting points of stars
- Zenith stars and latitude determination
- Wave patterns and bird observation integration

### 3. Native American Astronomy
- Medicine wheels and astronomical alignments
- Pleiades rising and agricultural cycles
- Sun dagger phenomena (Chaco Canyon)
- Varied tribal traditions across regions

### 4. Islamic Astronomy
- Qibla determination and mosque orientation
- Lunar calendar and religious observance
- Astrolabe development and use
- Mathematical astronomy tradition

### 5. Chinese Astronomy
- Circumpolar constellations and the Celestial Empire
- Lunar mansions (28 xiu system)
- Cosmological models and divination
- Historical records of supernovae and comets

### 6. Maya Astronomy
- Venus cycles and ritual calendars
- Dresden Codex astronomical tables
- Architectural alignments (equinoxes, solstices)
- Long Count calendar and world ages

### 7. Archaeoastronomy Methods
- Statistical testing of alignments
- Cultural context and ethnographic records
- Distinction between astronomy and astrology
- Critiques and controversies in the field

## Key Sources

- **Institutions**: International Astronomical Union Working Group on Astronomy and World Heritage
- **Academic programs**: Cornell's cultural astronomy course, Arizona State ethnoastronomy research
- **Museums**: Adler Planetarium Indigenous astronomy programs
- **Books**: "The Star Atlas Companion" (cultural star lore), "Skywatchers" by Anthony Aveni
- **Journals**: Journal of Astronomical History and Heritage, Archaeoastronomy journal
