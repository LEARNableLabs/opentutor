# Ethnoastronomy — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Naked-eye astronomy** — observing celestial objects and motions without instruments
2. **Celestial sphere** — the imaginary sphere of objects surrounding Earth
3. **Cultural astronomy vs ethnoastronomy** — distinction between universal observations and culturally-specific interpretations
4. **Oral traditions** — non-written transmission of astronomical knowledge
5. **Archaeological evidence** — physical alignments, structures, and artifacts
6. **Ethnographic records** — documented cultural practices and beliefs about the sky
7. **Pattern recognition** — how humans identify meaningful groupings of stars
8. **Asterisms vs constellations** — informal star patterns vs formal groupings
9. **Lunar calendars** — timekeeping based on Moon phases
10. **Solar calendars** — timekeeping based on Sun's annual motion
11. **Heliacal rising** — first visible rising of a star before sunrise. Depends on: 2, 10
12. **Seasonal markers** — celestial events indicating seasonal changes. Depends on: 10, 11
13. **Statistical significance** — testing whether alignments are intentional or coincidental
14. **Alignment testing** — archaeoastronomy methodology for evaluating claims. Depends on: 5, 13
15. **Dark constellations** — patterns formed by dust lanes in the Milky Way. Depends on: 8
16. **Songlines** — Aboriginal knowledge systems connecting land and sky. Depends on: 4, 15
17. **Star compass** — Polynesian navigation system using star rising/setting points. Depends on: 2
18. **Zenith stars** — stars passing directly overhead, used for latitude. Depends on: 2, 17
19. **Medicine wheels** — stone structures with astronomical alignments. Depends on: 5, 14
20. **Solstice alignment** — structures oriented to sunrise/sunset at solstices. Depends on: 10, 14
21. **Sun dagger** — light-and-shadow phenomena marking solar events. Depends on: 20
22. **Venus cycle** — observable pattern of Venus as morning/evening star. Depends on: 1
23. **Synodic period** — time between same configuration of planet. Depends on: 22
24. **Dresden Codex** — Maya manuscript with astronomical tables. Depends on: 23
25. **Vigesimal system** — base-20 counting system used by Maya. Depends on: 24
26. **Long Count calendar** — Maya calendar tracking long time periods. Depends on: 25
27. **28 lunar mansions (xiu)** — Chinese system dividing ecliptic. Depends on: 9, 2
28. **Circumpolar stars** — stars that never set, visible year-round. Depends on: 2
29. **Guest stars** — Chinese term for transient phenomena (novae, supernovae). Depends on: 1
30. **Qibla direction** — direction to Mecca, determined astronomically. Depends on: 2
31. **Spherical trigonometry** — mathematics of positions on sphere. Depends on: 2, 30
32. **Astrolabe** — instrument for measuring star positions and time. Depends on: 31
33. **Stereographic projection** — mapping sphere to plane, used in astrolabes. Depends on: 32

## Dependencies

- **Heliacal rising** requires understanding the celestial sphere and solar calendars because it describes the relationship between a star's position and the Sun's annual motion
- **Alignment testing** builds on archaeological evidence and statistical significance because it applies rigorous methods to physical structures
- **Star compass** requires understanding the celestial sphere because navigation depends on knowing where stars rise and set around the horizon
- **Zenith stars** build on celestial sphere and star compass because they represent a special case (stars passing directly overhead) used for determining latitude
- **Synodic period** requires understanding the Venus cycle because it quantifies the observable pattern
- **Dresden Codex** depends on synodic period because the tables record Venus cycle predictions
- **Long Count calendar** builds on the vigesimal system and Dresden Codex because it uses base-20 arithmetic to track astronomical cycles
- **Spherical trigonometry** builds on celestial sphere and qibla direction because calculating direction to a point on Earth requires spherical geometry
- **Astrolabe** depends on spherical trigonometry because its construction and use require mathematical understanding of the celestial sphere
- **Stereographic projection** builds on astrolabe because it explains how the three-dimensional sky is mapped onto the flat instrument face

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic astronomy concepts** — needed for celestial sphere, naked-eye observations, planetary motion
- **Spherical coordinates** — needed for horizon system, equatorial coordinates, zenith stars, qibla calculations
- **Cultural anthropology basics** — needed for oral traditions, ethnographic records, cultural context, pattern recognition

## Bottlenecks

- **Celestial sphere** — foundational concept that appears throughout; without it, navigation, calendars, and instrument use don't make sense
- **Heliacal rising** — key to understanding agricultural calendars across cultures
- **Statistical significance** — critical for evaluating archaeoastronomy claims objectively
- **Cultural context** — essential for distinguishing astronomy (observations) from cosmology (interpretations)

## Common Misconceptions

- Conflating **asterisms** with official **constellations** — different cultures see different patterns in the same stars
- Assuming all ancient structures with vague sky alignments were **intentionally** astronomical — alignment testing required
- Thinking **dark constellations** are the same as star patterns — they're negative space in the Milky Way
- Believing **zenith stars** are always the same stars — they vary by latitude
- Confusing **synodic** period with sidereal period — synodic is Earth-relative, sidereal is absolute
