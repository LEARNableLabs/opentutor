# Antitrust and Competition Law — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
This domain uses a mix of delivery formats:
- **concept-focused mini-lessons** — 8 lessons (29%)
- **Socratic questions** — 8 lessons (29%)
- **real-world application challenges** — 6 lessons (21%)
- **review and consolidation sessions** — 3 lessons (11%)
- **teach-back exercises (student explains)** — 2 lessons (7%)
- **curated resource exploration** — 1 lessons (4%)

Primary format: concept-focused mini-lessons.

## Resource Types
This domain has strong coverage in: textbooks, video lectures, interactive tools, academic papers, code repositories, online courses. Prioritize these when recommending resources.

## Difficulty Curve
Balanced progression — steady difficulty increase with regular consolidation.

Distribution: 39% accessible (1-2), 36% standard (3), 25% challenging (4-5).

Difficulty peaks:
- Day 7: "What made the Apple e-books case a conspiracy?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 11: "How do courts distinguish exclusionary conduct from hard competition?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 12: "Why did Microsoft lose but Apple won in their monopolization cases?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 16: "When does exclusive dealing foreclose competition?" (difficulty 4)
- Day 21: "Why did the FTC block Staples-Office Depot but clear Amazon-Whole Foods?" (difficulty 4)

## Domain Hooks
This field covers antitrust and competition law, with applications across theory and practice.

## Common Failure Modes
Students often struggle with the foundational concepts before building to more advanced material. Reinforce basics before progressing.

## Vocabulary
Key terms for this domain: market failure, monopoly deadweight loss, consumer welfare, market definition, SSNIP test, product and geographic markets, Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, concentration ratios, market shares, Chicago School economics, consumer welfare standard, enforcement philosophy, per se illegality, horizontal agreements, price fixing (and 65 more).
