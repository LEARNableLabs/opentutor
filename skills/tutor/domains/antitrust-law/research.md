# Antitrust and Competition Law — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Foundations and Economic Theory
- Sherman Act (1890) and Clayton Act (1914) fundamentals
- Economic rationale: market power, consumer welfare, allocative efficiency
- Structure-conduct-performance paradigm vs. Chicago School vs. post-Chicago
- Market definition and concentration metrics (HHI, CR4)

### 2. Horizontal Restraints
- Price fixing, bid rigging, market allocation
- Trade associations and information exchanges
- Joint ventures and competitor collaborations
- Rule of reason vs. per se illegality

### 3. Vertical Restraints
- Resale price maintenance (RPM)
- Exclusive dealing and tying arrangements
- Vertical mergers and foreclosure theory
- Most-favored-nation (MFN) clauses

### 4. Monopolization and Dominance
- Section 2 Sherman Act (US) and Article 102 TFEU (EU)
- Essential facilities doctrine
- Predatory pricing and exclusionary conduct
- Refusal to deal and duty to deal

### 5. Merger Control
- Horizontal, vertical, and conglomerate mergers
- Pre-merger notification (HSR Act in US, EU Merger Regulation)
- Competitive effects analysis: unilateral vs. coordinated effects
- Remedies: structural vs. behavioral

### 6. Modern Challenges
- Digital platforms and network effects
- Big Tech: Google, Amazon, Facebook, Apple cases
- Data as competitive advantage
- Algorithmic pricing and tacit collusion
- Two-sided markets and multi-homing

### 7. International and Comparative Systems
- EU competition law (Articles 101, 102 TFEU)
- Extraterritorial application and effects doctrine
- International Cooperation Network (ICN)
- Differences in enforcement philosophy (US vs. EU vs. China)

## Research Gaps Identified

- **Practical enforcement**: Most resources are doctrine-heavy; need more case strategy and procedural guidance
- **Emerging markets**: Limited intermediate-level material on algorithmic pricing, AI competition issues
- **Cross-jurisdictional**: Few resources compare US/EU/China enforcement in accessible format
- **Remedies design**: Sparse coverage of behavioral vs. structural remedies effectiveness

## Recommendations for Curriculum Design

1. **Balance theory and application**: Mix economic foundations with case analysis
2. **Use real cases as anchors**: AT&T, Microsoft, Google Shopping, AB InBev/SABMiller
3. **Integrate modern challenges early**: Students are often motivated by Big Tech issues
4. **Comparative perspective**: Show US vs. EU differences to highlight policy choices
5. **Progressive complexity**: Start with horizontal cartels (clear harms), build to vertical restraints and digital markets (nuanced effects)
6. **Regular "why does this matter?" checks**: Connect doctrine to real-world impact on prices, innovation, consumer choice
