# Biomedical Engineering — Prosthetics and Implants: Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Biomechanics and Materials Science
- **Gait biomechanics** — how prosthetic limbs interact with human movement patterns
- **Materials engineering** — biocompatible metals (titanium, cobalt-chromium), polymers, ceramics, composites
- **Osseointegration** — direct skeletal attachment of prosthetic devices
- **Wear and fatigue** — long-term performance of implanted devices

### 2. Prosthetic Limb Design
- **Passive prosthetics** — mechanical joints, energy storage and return (ESAR) feet
- **Microprocessor-controlled joints** — C-Leg, Rheo Knee technologies
- **Socket design and fit** — interface between residual limb and prosthetic
- **Upper limb prosthetics** — body-powered vs. myoelectric control

### 3. Neural Interfaces and Control
- **Myoelectric control** — surface EMG signal processing for prosthetic control
- **Targeted muscle reinnervation (TMR)** — surgical technique for enhanced control signals
- **Brain-computer interfaces (BCI)** — direct neural control of prosthetic devices
- **Sensory feedback** — haptic and proprioceptive feedback systems

### 4. Implantable Devices
- **Orthopedic implants** — joint replacements (hip, knee, shoulder), bone fixation devices
- **Cardiac devices** — pacemakers, defibrillators, ventricular assist devices
- **Neural implants** — cochlear implants, deep brain stimulators, retinal prostheses
- **Drug delivery systems** — implantable pumps and controlled-release devices

### 5. Regulatory and Clinical Pathways
- **FDA regulatory framework** — Class I/II/III device classifications, 510(k) vs. PMA
- **Clinical trials** — study design, endpoints, patient-reported outcomes
- **Biocompatibility testing** — ISO 10993 standards
- **Post-market surveillance** — tracking device performance and adverse events

## Learning Approach for Intermediate Level
Students at this level should have foundational knowledge of anatomy, physiology, materials science, and circuit design. The curriculum balances:
- **Engineering principles** — mechanics, control theory, signal processing
- **Clinical context** — patient needs, functional outcomes, quality of life
- **Design thinking** — iterative prototyping, human-centered design
- **Ethical considerations** — accessibility, cost, equitable access to advanced devices
