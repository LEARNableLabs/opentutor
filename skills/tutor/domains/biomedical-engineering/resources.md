# Biomedical Engineering — Prosthetics and Implants: Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks
- **Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement (4th ed.)** by David Winter — authoritative reference for gait analysis, joint kinetics, and motion analysis. Excellent for lessons 3-4 on biomechanics foundations. Accessible to intermediate students with calculus background.
  - https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Biomechanics+and+Motor+Control+of+Human+Movement

- **Introduction to Biomedical Engineering (3rd ed.)** by John Enderle, Joseph Bronzino, Susan Blanchard — comprehensive coverage of prosthetics, implants, and medical devices. Good balance of engineering principles and clinical applications.
  - https://www.elsevier.com/books/introduction-to-biomedical-engineering/enderle/978-0-12-374979-6

- **Biomaterials Science: An Introduction to Materials in Medicine (4th ed.)** by Buddy Ratner et al. — definitive reference for biocompatibility, material properties, and tissue response. Essential for lessons 1-2.
  - https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780123746269/biomaterials-science

- **Neural Engineering (2nd ed.)** by Bin He — covers BCIs, neural interfaces, signal processing. Technical but accessible at intermediate level with guided reading.
  - https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-43395-6

- **Orthopaedic Biomechanics** by Donald Bartel, Dwight Davy, Tony Keaveny — focused treatment of joint mechanics and implant design. Strong for lessons 19-20 on joint replacements.
  - https://www.pearson.com/store/p/orthopaedic-biomechanics/P100000154781

### Open Educational Resources
- **MIT OpenCourseWare: Biomedical Device Design (2.75)** — complete course with lecture notes, assignments, and readings. Covers design process, regulatory pathways, prototyping.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mechanical-engineering/2-75-medical-device-design-spring-2004/

- **MIT OCW: Materials for Biomedical Applications (3.051J)** — deep dive into biomaterials selection, biocompatibility testing, surface modification.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/materials-science-and-engineering/3-051j-materials-for-biomedical-applications-spring-2014/

- **Stanford Biodesign Innovation Process** — freely available online book covering needs finding, concept generation, implementation strategy for medical devices.
  - http://biodesign.stanford.edu/resources.html

## Unexpected Connections (for rabbit holes)

### Cross-Disciplinary Links
- **Architecture and Universal Design** — principles from accessible building design inform prosthetic socket ergonomics and device user interfaces
  - Centre for Excellence in Universal Design: http://universaldesign.ie/

- **Music and Haptics** — research on tactile music perception for deaf individuals overlaps with sensory feedback for prosthetics
  - Music and Vibrotactile Feedback: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6566389/

- **Sports Biomechanics** — Paralympic athlete data reveals optimization strategies applicable to prosthetic design
  - International Paralympic Committee Science: https://www.paralympic.org/science

- **Soft Robotics** — compliant actuators and soft grippers from robotics research inform next-gen prosthetic hands
  - Soft Robotics Toolkit: https://softroboticstoolkit.com/

- **Fashion and Wearable Technology** — collaboration between prosthetic designers and fashion designers creates alternative aesthetics
  - Alt Limb Project: https://www.thealternativelimbproject.com/

### Historical Context
- **History of Prosthetics** — from wooden peg legs to myoelectric hands, understanding evolution reveals enduring design principles
  - Smithsonian: https://www.si.edu/exhibitions/prosthetics-past-present-and-future

- **Disability Rights Movement** — how advocacy shaped medical device regulation and accessibility requirements
  - ADA National Network: https://adata.org/
