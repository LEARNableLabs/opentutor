# Psycholinguistics — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Mental lexicon** — the mind's dictionary of words and their properties
2. **Automaticity** — unconscious, fast processing of language
3. **Modularity** — whether language processing is independent from other cognition
4. **Reaction time** — key dependent measure in psycholinguistic experiments
5. **Priming** — facilitation or inhibition from prior exposure to related stimuli. Depends on: reaction time
6. **Categorical perception** — perceiving continuous acoustic signals as discrete phoneme categories
7. **Top-down processing** — using context and expectations to guide perception. Depends on: mental lexicon
8. **Phoneme restoration** — filling in missing speech sounds from context. Depends on: top-down processing
9. **Multimodal integration** — combining auditory and visual cues in speech perception
10. **McGurk effect** — audiovisual illusion demonstrating multimodal speech perception. Depends on: multimodal integration
11. **Lexical access** — the process of retrieving word information from the mental lexicon. Depends on: mental lexicon
12. **Cohort model** — words that match initial sounds compete for recognition. Depends on: lexical access
13. **Word frequency** — how often a word appears in language; affects recognition speed. Depends on: lexical access
14. **Neighborhood effects** — words with many similar-sounding neighbors are harder to recognize. Depends on: lexical access
15. **Spreading activation** — activation spreads from one concept to related concepts in semantic memory. Depends on: mental lexicon, priming
16. **Semantic priming** — faster recognition of related words (e.g., "doctor" → "nurse"). Depends on: spreading activation
17. **Syntactic parsing** — building sentence structure incrementally while reading/listening
18. **Attachment preferences** — biases in how the parser connects phrases. Depends on: syntactic parsing
19. **Garden-path sentences** — sentences that mislead the parser, requiring reanalysis. Depends on: syntactic parsing, incremental processing
20. **Incremental processing** — interpreting each word immediately, not waiting for sentence end. Depends on: syntactic parsing
21. **Eye-tracking** — measuring eye fixations and movements during reading
22. **Reanalysis** — revising incorrect syntactic interpretations. Depends on: garden-path sentences
23. **Language production stages** — conceptualization → formulation → articulation (Levelt's model)
24. **Lexical selection** — choosing the right word to express a concept. Depends on: language production stages
25. **Speech errors** — slips of the tongue that reveal production processes. Depends on: lexical selection, phonological encoding
26. **Phonological encoding** — retrieving sound structure of words during production. Depends on: language production stages
27. **Monitoring** — detecting and correcting errors during production. Depends on: language production stages
28. **Visual word recognition** — accessing meaning from written words
29. **Dual-route model** — two pathways for reading: direct (whole-word) and assembled (phonological). Depends on: visual word recognition
30. **Code-switching** — alternating between languages in bilingual speech
31. **Language control** — mechanisms for selecting and inhibiting languages in bilinguals. Depends on: code-switching
32. **Broca's area** — frontal brain region involved in language production and syntax
33. **Wernicke's area** — temporal brain region involved in language comprehension
34. **Aphasia** — language impairment from brain damage, revealing functional specialization. Depends on: Broca's area, Wernicke's area
35. **Event-related potentials (ERPs)** — brain electrical activity time-locked to language events
36. **N400** — ERP component reflecting semantic processing difficulty. Depends on: event-related potentials
37. **P600** — ERP component reflecting syntactic processing difficulty. Depends on: event-related potentials

## Dependencies

### Foundational Layer
- **Automaticity** and **mental lexicon** are foundational — most phenomena depend on rapid, unconscious access to linguistic knowledge
- **Reaction time** and **priming** are methodological foundations for measuring processes we can't directly observe

### Perception to Recognition
- **Categorical perception** requires understanding that perception isn't passive recording
- **Top-down processing** builds on the mental lexicon — you need stored knowledge to generate predictions
- **Phoneme restoration** and **McGurk effect** both require understanding top-down and multimodal influences

### Lexical Access Cluster
- **Lexical access** is central to both perception (word recognition) and production (lexical selection)
- **Cohort model**, **frequency effects**, and **neighborhood effects** all describe constraints on lexical access
- **Spreading activation** explains **semantic priming** and connects to memory models

### Sentence Processing Chain
- **Syntactic parsing** is incremental, which explains why **garden-path sentences** mislead us
- **Attachment preferences** and **reanalysis** both emerge from incremental parsing constraints
- **Eye-tracking** provides the temporal resolution to observe these processes in real time

### Production Chain
- Production stages are modular: conceptual → lexical → phonological → motor
- **Speech errors** reveal these stages because errors respect stage boundaries (phonological errors swap sounds, not meanings)
- **Monitoring** operates in parallel to detect errors before or after articulation

### Reading and Bilingualism
- **Visual word recognition** shares components with spoken word recognition but adds orthographic processing
- **Dual-route model** explains how skilled readers bypass phonology for familiar words
- **Code-switching** and **language control** reveal that bilinguals don't turn languages on/off but actively inhibit non-target language

### Neural Basis
- **Broca's area** and **Wernicke's area** are classical but oversimplified — modern view emphasizes networks
- **Aphasia** patterns (Broca's vs. Wernicke's) demonstrate double dissociations between production and comprehension
- **N400** and **P600** provide millisecond-resolution evidence for distinct semantic vs. syntactic processes

## Bottlenecks

Critical concepts students must master to progress:

1. **Automaticity** — if students think language processing is conscious and deliberate, they'll misunderstand priming, frequency effects, and production errors
2. **Incremental processing** — crucial for understanding garden-path sentences, eye-tracking data, and why context can't always override initial misinterpretations
3. **Lexical access** — central to both comprehension and production; must be solid before tackling models like cohort or spreading activation
4. **Top-down vs. bottom-up** — needed to understand phoneme restoration, context effects, and debates about modularity

## Common Misconceptions

1. **Language processing is conscious** — Most is automatic and unavailable to introspection
2. **We hear/read words, then build sentences** — Actually incremental; we start building structure before sentences end
3. **Broca's = production, Wernicke's = comprehension** — Oversimplified; real story involves distributed networks
4. **Bilinguals translate between languages** — No, they access both directly; translation is effortful and abnormal
5. **Garden-path sentences are rare or artificial** — Common in natural language; the parser is just good at recovering
6. **Speed-reading works** — Eye-tracking shows you can't comprehend without fixating content words
7. **N400 is "meaning," P600 is "syntax"** — Closer to "expectation violation" and "reanalysis," but not strict semantic/syntactic split

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Cognitive Psychology** — needed for attention, working memory, automaticity, experimental design
- **Linguistics (Phonetics)** — needed for understanding phonemes, acoustic cues, voice onset time, minimal pairs
- **Linguistics (Syntax)** — needed for phrase structure, constituency, grammatical relations, parsing
- **Research Methods** — needed for interpreting reaction time experiments, understanding dependent measures, statistical reasoning
