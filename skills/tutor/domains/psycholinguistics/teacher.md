## Domain Hooks
- **Phoneme restoration and predictive processing** (Lesson 5) — Connect to predictive coding in neuroscience, Bayesian brain hypothesis. Reading: Clark's "Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science."

- **McGurk effect and multisensory integration** (Lesson 7) — Link to ventriloquism effect, rubber hand illusion, out-of-body experiences. Shows perception is unified, not modality-specific.

- **Garden-path sentences and "good enough" processing** (Lesson 14) — Sometimes readers don't fully reanalyze; they settle for shallow parse. Connects to memory illusions, gist vs. verbatim memory.

- **Tip-of-the-tongue and two-stage retrieval** (Lesson 10) — Semantic access succeeds, phonological fails. Connects to anomia in aphasia, proper name retrieval in aging.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Language processing is conscious and deliberate"**  
   **Why students get this wrong**: Introspection makes language feel effortful (e.g., choosing words while writing). But speech comprehension and production are mostly automatic.  
   **How to correct**: Use the Stroop effect — students can't *not* read words. Show reaction time data: lexical access happens in ~200ms, too fast for conscious control. Contrast effortful tasks (translating, puns) with automatic ones (understanding speech).

2. **"We process words sequentially, then build sentences"**  
   **Why students get this wrong**: Reading feels word-by-word. They don't notice incremental parsing.  
   **How to correct**: Garden-path sentences show we commit to structure *before* the sentence ends (otherwise, how would we get garden-pathed?). Eye-tracking shows fixations on each word but regressions when parsing fails.
