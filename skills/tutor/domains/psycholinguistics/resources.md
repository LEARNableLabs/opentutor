# Psycholinguistics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Harley, T. A. (2014). *The Psychology of Language: From Data to Theory* (4th ed.). Psychology Press.**  
  Comprehensive intermediate textbook covering all major areas. Clear explanations of models (cohort, spreading activation, Levelt's stages), good balance of theory and data. Best for lessons on word recognition, sentence processing, and production.

- **Altmann, G. T. M. (1997). *The Ascent of Babel: An Exploration of Language, Mind, and Understanding.* Oxford University Press.**  
  Engaging narrative introduction to psycholinguistics. Less technical than Harley; great for motivation and phenomena-first teaching. Use for introducing garden-path sentences, speech errors, and the "feel" of the field.

- **Traxler, M. J. (2011). *Introduction to Psycholinguistics: Understanding Language Science.* Wiley-Blackwell.**  
  Accessible intermediate text with strong coverage of experimental methods (reaction time, eye-tracking, ERPs). Good for lessons on research design and interpretation.

- **Sedivy, J. (2019). *Language in Mind: An Introduction to Psycholinguistics* (2nd ed.). Oxford University Press.**  
  Modern, research-focused textbook. Excellent coverage of prediction, individual differences, and bilingualism. Use for lessons 22 (code-switching) and 25 (ERPs).

### Advanced References

- **Fernández, E. M., & Cairns, H. S. (Eds.). (2017). *The Handbook of Psycholinguistics.* Wiley-Blackwell.**  
  Comprehensive reference with chapters by leading researchers. Use for instructor background, not student-facing lessons at this level.

- **Gaskell, M. G. (Ed.). (2007). *The Oxford Handbook of Psycholinguistics.* Oxford University Press.**  
  Authoritative chapters on speech perception, word recognition, sentence processing, and production. Good for deepening lesson content.

## Supplementary Resources

### Interactive Tools & Experiments

- **Psytoolkit**  
  [https://www.psytoolkit.org/](https://www.psytoolkit.org/)  
  Run psycholinguistic experiments online: lexical decision, Stroop, semantic priming, self-paced reading. Experiment library has ready-made tasks. Students can run themselves through classic paradigms.

- **CHILDES (Child Language Data Exchange System)**  
  [https://childes.talkbank.org/](https://childes.talkbank.org/)  
  Massive database of child language transcripts. Use for lessons on language acquisition (if expanding curriculum) or speech error analysis.

- **Ibex Farm (Internet Based EXperiments)**  
  [https://spellout.net/ibexfarm/](https://spellout.net/ibexfarm/)  
  Platform for creating self-paced reading and other web-based experiments. Students can design and run studies.

- **PCIbex (PennController for Ibex)**  
  [https://doc.pcibex.net/](https://doc.pcibex.net/)  
  More user-friendly version of Ibex. Good for teaching experimental design (Lesson 16).

- **McGurk Effect Demonstrations**  
  [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2k8fHR9jKVM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2k8fHR9jKVM)  
  Classic demo video. Students experience the effect firsthand (Lesson 7).

- **Praat (Phonetics Software)**  
  [https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/)  
  Acoustic analysis tool. Use for examining voice onset time, spectrograms, formants (Lesson 4). Free and cross-platform.

### Databases & Corpora

- **CELEX Database**  
  Lexical database with frequency, phonology, morphology for English, Dutch, German. Use for discussing frequency effects (Lesson 9).

- **ELP (English Lexicon Project)**  
  [http://elexicon.wustl.edu/](http://elexicon.wustl.edu/)  
  Reaction time and accuracy data for 40,000+ English words. Show students real data on frequency and neighborhood effects.

- **Psycholinguistic Database (UBC)**  
  Word norms: age of acquisition, imageability, concreteness. Use for discussing lexical variables.

## Key Papers (Classic & Foundational)

### Speech Perception

- **Warren, R. M. (1970). "Perceptual restoration of missing speech sounds." *Science*, 167(3917), 392-393.**  
  Original phoneme restoration study (Lesson 5).

- **McGurk, H., & MacDonald, J. (1976). "Hearing lips and seeing voices." *Nature*, 264(5588), 746-748.**  
  Original McGurk effect paper (Lesson 7).

### Word Recognition

- **Marslen-Wilson, W. D., & Welsh, A. (1978). "Processing interactions and lexical access during word recognition in continuous speech." *Cognitive Psychology*, 10(1), 29-63.**  
  Foundation of cohort model (Lesson 8).

- **Neely, J. H. (1977). "Semantic priming and retrieval from lexical memory." *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 106(3), 226.**  
  Classic semantic priming study (Lesson 11).

### Sentence Processing

- **Frazier, L., & Rayner, K. (1982). "Making and correcting errors during sentence comprehension: Eye movements in the analysis of structurally ambiguous sentences." *Cognitive Psychology*, 14(2), 178-210.**  
  Garden-path sentences and eye-tracking (Lessons 14-15).

- **Tanenhaus, M. K., et al. (1995). "Integration of visual and linguistic information in spoken language comprehension." *Science*, 268(5217), 1632-1634.**  
  Visual world paradigm (rabbit hole for Lesson 15).

### Language Production

- **Bock, K. (1986). "Syntactic persistence in language production." *Cognitive Psychology*, 18(3), 355-387.**  
  Structural priming in production (Lesson 17).

- **Levelt, W. J. (1989). *Speaking: From Intention to Articulation.* MIT Press.**  
  Foundational model of language production (Lesson 17).

### ERPs

- **Kutas, M., & Hillyard, S. A. (1980). "Reading senseless sentences: Brain potentials reflect semantic incongruity." *Science*, 207(4427), 203-205.**  
  Original N400 study (Lesson 25).

- **Osterhout, L., & Holcomb, P. J. (1992). "Event-related brain potentials elicited by syntactic anomaly." *Journal of Memory and Language*, 31(6), 785-806.**  
  P600 and syntactic violations (Lesson 25).

## Unexpected Connections (Cross-Discipline)

### Psycholinguistics ↔ Machine Learning
- **Word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe)** learn distributional semantics similar to spreading activation. Semantic priming effects correlate with embedding similarity.
- **Language models (GPT, BERT)** show garden-path effects and frequency biases. Surprisal (model prediction error) predicts human reading times.

### Psycholinguistics ↔ Music Cognition
- **Syntax in music and language** — Similar ERP components (ERAN for music, P600 for language). Shared neural substrates?
- **Statistical learning** — Infants and adults extract patterns from tone sequences (like syllable sequences). Domain-general mechanism?

### Psycholinguistics ↔ Philosophy of Mind
- **Modularity debate** — Fodor's modularity thesis applied to language. Is language processing informationally encapsulated?
- **Embodied cognition** — Do we simulate actions when understanding verbs? Connects to grounded semantics.

### Psycholinguistics ↔ Sociolinguistics
- **Code-switching** — Not just cognitive control, but social identity performance. Why/when do bilinguals switch?
- **Sociolinguistic variation** — Does processing differ for standard vs. non-standard dialects? Implicit bias in language processing.

### Psycholinguistics ↔ Evolution
- **Language origins** — Did syntax evolve from tool use? Broca's area involved in both.
- **Universal Grammar vs. usage-based** — Nativist vs. emergentist accounts of acquisition. Psycholinguistic evidence on both sides.

## Final Note

This is an **intermediate** curriculum. Students should engage directly with phenomena (run Stroop tasks, experience McGurk), interpret data (reaction times, eye movements), and critique models (cohort vs. TRACE). Resources should support **active learning** — not just reading about priming, but designing a priming experiment. Prioritize tools (Psytoolkit, Ibex) and demos (McGurk videos) over passive consumption.
