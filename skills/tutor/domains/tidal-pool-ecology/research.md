# Research Summary — Tidal Pool Ecology

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Physical Environment
- **Tidal cycles and vertical zonation** — astronomical tides, tidal range, exposure gradients
- **Wave action and hydrodynamics** — splash zones, surge channels, wave exposure gradients
- **Temperature extremes** — solar heating, evaporative cooling, thermal stratification in pools
- **Salinity fluctuations** — evaporation, rainfall, freshwater runoff
- **Dissolved oxygen and pH** — diurnal cycles, photosynthesis/respiration balance

### 2. Zonation Patterns
- **Vertical zones** — spray zone, high intertidal, mid-intertidal, low intertidal, subtidal fringe
- **Indicator species** — barnacles, mussels, fucoid algae, kelps
- **Microhabitat heterogeneity** — crevices, overhangs, pools, open rock
- **Substrate effects** — bedrock, boulder fields, cobble

### 3. Physiological Adaptations
- **Desiccation resistance** — mucus production, shell sealing, behavioral refuges
- **Temperature tolerance** — heat shock proteins, thermal limits, behavioral thermoregulation
- **Osmotic regulation** — osmoconformers vs. osmoregulators
- **Oxygen limitation** — anaerobic metabolism, metabolic suppression
- **Wave force resistance** — morphology, attachment strength, flexibility

### 4. Community Ecology
- **Competition** — space competition (barnacles, mussels, algae), Connell's classic studies
- **Predation** — sea stars, whelks, crabs; trophic cascades
- **Facilitation** — canopy effects, nurse plants, ecosystem engineers
- **Keystone species** — Paine's *Pisaster* experiments, diversity maintenance
- **Food webs** — grazing chains, detrital pathways, trophic interactions

### 5. Disturbance and Dynamics
- **Wave disturbance** — patch dynamics, intermediate disturbance hypothesis
- **Recruitment variation** — larval supply, settlement cues, post-settlement mortality
- **Succession** — primary succession on cleared rock, succession trajectories
- **Seasonal changes** — recruitment pulses, seasonal die-offs, winter storms
- **Climate change impacts** — range shifts, heatwaves, ocean acidification

### 6. Human Impacts
- **Harvesting** — recreational collecting, bait harvesting, subsistence use
- **Trampling** — foot traffic effects on algae and invertebrates
- **Pollution** — oil spills, urban runoff, microplastics
- **Invasive species** — *Carcinus maenas*, *Littorina littorea*, Asian shore crabs
- **Marine protected areas** — conservation effectiveness, recovery dynamics

## Learning Progression Notes

For intermediate students:
- Assume basic ecology background (trophic levels, competition, predation)
- Emphasize mechanism over description (WHY zonation happens, not just WHAT zones exist)
- Connect to broader ecological theory (intermediate disturbance, keystone species, environmental stress gradients)
- Include quantitative thinking (stress gradients, tide charts, population dynamics)
- Encourage field observation and hypothesis generation
- Address climate change and conservation applications
