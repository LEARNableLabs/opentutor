# Lie Groups and Lie Algebras — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Matrix groups** — groups of matrices closed under multiplication (GL, SL, O, SO, U, SU)
2. **Continuous symmetry** — topology and smooth structure on matrix groups
3. **Group actions** — how groups act on spaces; homogeneous spaces and cosets
4. **Tangent space at identity** — infinitesimal structure; where the Lie algebra lives. Depends on: matrix groups, continuous symmetry
5. **Lie bracket** — commutator operation measuring non-commutativity. Depends on: tangent space at identity
6. **Exponential map** — connects algebra to group; matrix exponential. Depends on: tangent space at identity
7. **Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula** — composition of exponentials via Lie brackets. Depends on: exponential map, Lie bracket
8. **Computing Lie algebras** — determining tangent spaces of classical groups. Depends on: tangent space at identity, matrix groups
9. **Solvable Lie algebras** — algebras with vanishing derived series. Depends on: Lie bracket
10. **Nilpotent Lie algebras** — algebras with vanishing lower central series. Depends on: solvable Lie algebras
11. **Engel's theorem** — nilpotent algebras have upper-triangular representations. Depends on: nilpotent Lie algebras
12. **Semisimple Lie algebras** — algebras with no solvable ideal. Depends on: solvable Lie algebras
13. **Killing form** — invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra. Depends on: Lie bracket
14. **Cartan's criterion** — Killing form characterizes semisimplicity. Depends on: Killing form, semisimple Lie algebras
15. **Simple Lie algebras** — building blocks; no nontrivial ideals. Depends on: semisimple Lie algebras
16. **Cartan subalgebras** — maximal abelian subalgebras of semisimple algebras. Depends on: semisimple Lie algebras
17. **Root space decomposition** — eigenspace decomposition with respect to Cartan subalgebra. Depends on: Cartan subalgebras
18. **Root systems** — geometric structure of roots in Euclidean space. Depends on: root space decomposition
19. **Weyl group** — symmetry group of root system; generated by reflections. Depends on: root systems
20. **Simple roots** — minimal generating set for all roots. Depends on: root systems
21. **Dynkin diagrams** — combinatorial encoding of root system structure. Depends on: simple roots
22. **Cartan matrix** — numerical data from simple roots; determines Dynkin diagram. Depends on: simple roots
23. **Classification of simple Lie algebras** — complete list (A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n, E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2). Depends on: Dynkin diagrams
24. **Representations** — how Lie groups/algebras act on vector spaces. Depends on: Lie bracket, exponential map
25. **Irreducible representations** — representations with no invariant subspaces. Depends on: representations
26. **Weight spaces** — eigenspaces for Cartan subalgebra action on representations. Depends on: Cartan subalgebras, representations
27. **Highest weight** — classification data for irreducible representations. Depends on: weight spaces, simple roots

## Dependencies

### Foundation Dependencies
- **Lie bracket requires tangent space at identity** — the bracket is defined on the tangent space (Lie algebra)
- **Exponential map requires tangent space at identity** — maps from algebra (tangent space) to group
- **Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula requires both exponential map and Lie bracket** — expresses exp(X)exp(Y) in terms of brackets

### Structure Theory Dependencies
- **Semisimple algebras require understanding solvable algebras** — defined as having no solvable ideal
- **Cartan's criterion links Killing form to semisimplicity** — algebraic test for structure
- **Simple algebras are special cases of semisimple** — irreducible building blocks

### Classification Dependencies
- **Root space decomposition requires Cartan subalgebras** — roots are eigenvalues of Cartan elements
- **Root systems require root space decomposition** — geometric organization of roots
- **Weyl group requires root systems** — symmetries of the root system
- **Dynkin diagrams require simple roots** — nodes are simple roots, edges encode angles
- **Classification requires Dynkin diagrams** — each diagram corresponds to a unique simple Lie algebra

### Representation Theory Dependencies
- **Weight spaces require Cartan subalgebras and representations** — weights are eigenvalues of Cartan action
- **Highest weight theory requires weight spaces and simple roots** — classification via dominant integral weights

## Critical Bottlenecks

### Exponential Map
The exponential map is the bridge between Lie groups and Lie algebras. Students must understand:
- Matrix exponential as a concrete computation
- Why it's a local diffeomorphism (locally invertible)
- How it relates group homomorphisms to algebra homomorphisms

Without this, the connection between groups and algebras remains mysterious.

### Killing Form
The Killing form is the algebraic tool that makes classification possible. It:
- Provides an inner product on the algebra
- Characterizes semisimplicity (Cartan's criterion)
- Defines orthogonality in root space

Without understanding the Killing form, root systems seem unmotivated.

### Root Space Decomposition
This is where abstract structure theory becomes concrete geometry. Students must see:
- How Cartan subalgebra acts by simultaneous diagonalization
- Why roots are linear functionals on the Cartan subalgebra
- How root vectors generate the algebra

This is the gateway to classification.

### Dynkin Diagrams
Dynkin diagrams compress enormous structural information into simple combinatorial data. Students must understand:
- What simple roots are and why they span the root lattice
- How angles between roots encode in the diagram
- Why these diagrams classify all simple Lie algebras

This is the culmination of the classification program.

## Common Prerequisite Gaps

### Linear Algebra
- **Eigenvalues and eigenvectors** — essential for Cartan subalgebras and weight spaces
- **Inner products** — needed for Killing form and root systems
- **Matrix exponential** — computational foundation for exponential map

### Abstract Algebra
- **Group homomorphisms** — how algebraic structure is preserved
- **Ideals and quotients** — needed for solvable/semisimple distinction
- **Vector spaces over C** — most theory works over complex numbers

### Topology
- **Continuous maps** — Lie groups are topological groups
- **Connectedness** — relates to exponential map being surjective
- **Compactness** — important for representation theory (Peter-Weyl theorem)

### Differential Geometry (nice to have, not essential at intermediate level)
- **Manifolds** — Lie groups are smooth manifolds
- **Tangent spaces** — geometric interpretation of Lie algebras
- **Vector fields** — global perspective on Lie algebra elements

## Misconceptions to Address

### "Lie algebras are just vector spaces"
False — they have the Lie bracket operation, which makes them fundamentally different. The bracket encodes infinitesimal group commutation.

### "The exponential map is always surjective"
False — for example, exp: gl(n,C) → GL(n,C) is not surjective (matrices with negative eigenvalues are not in the image). It IS surjective for compact groups.

### "All representations are completely reducible"
False — only for semisimple or compact groups. Solvable groups can have indecomposable representations.

### "Roots and weights are the same thing"
False — roots are weights of the adjoint representation, but general representations have different weight spaces.

### "There are infinitely many simple Lie algebras"
False — over C, there are exactly 4 infinite families (A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n) and 5 exceptional algebras (E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2).

### "Lie theory is purely abstract algebra"
False — it has deep connections to geometry (symmetries of manifolds), physics (gauge theory, quantum mechanics), and analysis (harmonic analysis, PDEs).
