## Domain Hooks
- **Cheese and human evolution** — lactose persistence mutations coevolved with dairying cultures. Cheese (with reduced lactose) may have been consumable before fluid milk was. Drop this when discussing lactose metabolism.

- **Cheese microbes as a model ecosystem** — ecologists study cheese rinds as simplified microbial communities to understand succession, competition, and facilitation. Drop this when discussing cave microbiomes.

- **The "cheese crystal" mystery** — those crunchy crystals in aged Parmigiano aren't salt — they're tyrosine crystals from proteolysis. Drop this when discussing amino acid catabolism.

- **Cheese preservation of ancient DNA** — cheese proteins and fats preserve well archaeologically. We've found 3,000-year-old cheese in Egyptian tombs and Bronze Age graves. Drop this when discussing preservation mechanisms.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"All bacteria in cheese are the same"** — Students often lump LAB together without distinguishing starter vs. secondary vs. environmental cultures. They miss that different species act at different times and produce different outcomes. Correct by emphasizing **microbial succession** and showing how pH, temperature, and oxygen availability select for different communities over time.

2. **"Aging just means waiting"** — Many students think aging is passive time passage rather than active biochemical transformation. They don't realize enzymes are continuously breaking down proteins and fats. Correct by tracking specific compounds over time (e.g., show how peptide profiles change week by week).
