# Cheese Science — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Lactic acid bacteria (LAB)** — primary fermentation agents that convert lactose to lactic acid
2. **Controlled fermentation** — using selected cultures vs. spontaneous fermentation
3. **Acidification** — lowering pH through bacterial acid production; enables coagulation and preservation
4. **Starter cultures** — primary LAB strains (Lactococcus, Lactobacillus) that drive early fermentation. Depends on: LAB, controlled fermentation
5. **Secondary cultures** — microbes that act during aging (Penicillium, Brevibacterium, Propionibacterium). Depends on: starter cultures
6. **Microbial succession** — how microbial communities change over time during aging. Depends on: starter cultures, secondary cultures
7. **Environmental microbiota** — microbes from the aging environment (caves, cellars). Depends on: microbial succession
8. **Microbial terroir** — how place-specific microbes influence cheese characteristics. Depends on: environmental microbiota
9. **Proteolysis** — enzymatic breakdown of casein proteins into peptides and amino acids. Depends on: acidification
10. **Lipolysis** — enzymatic breakdown of milk fats into fatty acids. Depends on: acidification
11. **Amino acid catabolism** — further breakdown of amino acids into flavor compounds. Depends on: proteolysis
12. **Volatile compounds** — flavor and aroma molecules produced during aging. Depends on: amino acid catabolism, lipolysis
13. **Protein matrix** — the structural network of cheese proteins that determines texture. Depends on: proteolysis, acidification
14. **Pasture composition** — how animal diet affects milk chemistry. Foundational
15. **Milk fatty acid profile** — the types and ratios of fats in milk, influenced by diet. Depends on: pasture composition
16. **Cave microbiome** — the complete microbial ecosystem of an aging environment. Depends on: environmental microbiota
17. **Protected Designation of Origin (PDO)** — legal framework protecting geographic specificity. Depends on: microbial terroir
18. **Chymosin/rennet** — enzyme that cleaves κ-casein to initiate coagulation. Foundational
19. **Casein micelles** — protein structures in milk that aggregate during coagulation. Depends on: acidification, chymosin
20. **Syneresis** — process of whey expulsion from curd. Depends on: casein micelles
21. **Salt diffusion** — movement of salt through cheese to control water activity and flavor. Depends on: protein matrix
22. **Water activity** — available water for microbial growth, controlled by salt. Depends on: salt diffusion
23. **Pathogen control** — strategies to prevent harmful bacteria (Listeria, E. coli). Depends on: acidification, water activity
24. **Sensory evaluation** — systematic assessment of cheese quality attributes. Integrates all previous concepts
25. **Probiotic survival** — factors that allow beneficial bacteria to remain viable in cheese. Depends on: protein matrix, water activity

## Dependencies

### Microbiology Foundation
- **Secondary cultures** require **starter cultures** to have acidified the environment and created favorable conditions
- **Microbial succession** builds on understanding both **starter** and **secondary cultures** because it describes their temporal relationship
- **Microbial terroir** requires understanding **environmental microbiota** and how they differ by location

### Biochemistry Chain
- **Proteolysis** depends on acidification because pH affects enzyme activity and protein structure
- **Amino acid catabolism** cannot occur without **proteolysis** first generating amino acids
- **Volatile compounds** are the end products of both **amino acid catabolism** (from proteolysis) and **lipolysis**
- **Protein matrix** structure is determined by the extent of **proteolysis** and the initial **acidification**

### Terroir Pathway
- **Milk fatty acid profile** depends on **pasture composition** because different plants contribute different fatty acid precursors
- **Cave microbiome** is a specific instance of **environmental microbiota** applied to aging environments
- **PDO systems** formalize the concept of **microbial terroir** in regulatory frameworks

### Production Science
- **Casein micelles** destabilize when **acidification** lowers pH and **chymosin** cleaves κ-casein
- **Syneresis** is the physical consequence of **casein micelle** aggregation and contraction
- **Water activity** is controlled by **salt diffusion** into the cheese matrix
- **Pathogen control** relies on **acidification** and **water activity** to create an inhospitable environment

### Integration
- **Sensory evaluation** integrates all concepts: microbiology (culture effects), biochemistry (flavor formation), terroir (environmental influence), and production (defects vs. style)
- **Probiotic survival** depends on **protein matrix** protection and controlled **water activity**

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Basic microbiology** — needed for understanding bacterial growth, fermentation, and culture concepts (lessons 1-5, 11-15)
- **Organic chemistry** — needed for understanding proteolysis, lipolysis, and flavor compound formation (lessons 6-10)
- **pH and acid-base chemistry** — needed for understanding acidification, coagulation, and enzyme activity (lessons 1, 6, 16-18)
- **Enzymatic reactions** — needed for understanding proteolysis, lipolysis, and rennet action (lessons 6-8, 16)

## Bottleneck Concepts

### Acidification
This is the keystone concept. Without understanding how LAB lower pH, students cannot understand:
- Why coagulation works
- How proteolysis is controlled
- Why certain microbes dominate at different stages
- How cheese is preserved

### Microbial Succession
This concept unlocks the entire aging story. Students who grasp succession can predict:
- Which flavors develop when
- How environment shapes final product
- Why timing of interventions matters
- The relationship between terroir and outcome

### Protein Matrix
Understanding the protein structure is essential for connecting:
- Biochemistry (what breaks proteins down) to texture (how matrix changes)
- Production (what builds the matrix) to quality (what matrix properties mean)
- Chemistry (proteolysis) to sensory (mouthfeel)

## Common Misconception Connections

- Students often think **secondary cultures** act simultaneously with **starters**, missing the succession concept
- The relationship between **proteolysis** and flavor is often oversimplified — students must understand **amino acid catabolism** as a separate downstream process
- **Terroir** is often treated as mystical rather than microbiological — linking **cave microbiome** to **PDO** helps ground the concept in science
- Students frequently confuse **acidification** (bacterial) with **acid coagulation** (direct acid addition), missing the microbial role
