# Cheese Science — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Cheese: Chemistry, Physics and Microbiology** (4th edition, Fox, Guinee, Cogan, McSweeney, 2017) — The comprehensive reference work. Four volumes covering all aspects from milk chemistry to final product. Excellent for deep dives into specific topics. Can be dense; use as reference rather than linear reading. Available through most university libraries.

- **Fundamentals of Cheese Science** (Fox et al., 2017, 2nd edition) — Streamlined textbook version of the above. Better paced for students, covers the essentials without overwhelming detail. Good balance of microbiology, chemistry, and engineering. Intermediate-level appropriate.

- **The Science of Cheese** (Michael H. Tunick, Oxford University Press, 2014) — Accessible scientific overview written for non-specialists. Strong on microbiology and aging chemistry. Good supplementary reading for context. Less technical than Fox.

- **UC Davis Food Science and Technology Department** — https://foodscience.ucdavis.edu/ — Course materials, research publications, and short courses. The dairy science program is one of the strongest globally.

- **University of Wisconsin-Madison Center for Dairy Research** — https://cdr.wisc.edu/ — Industry-leading research center. Excellent technical bulletins, fact sheets, and online resources. Strong on production science and quality control.

- **Cornell University Dairy Foods Extension** — https://cals.cornell.edu/dairy-foods — Practical guides, fact sheets, and videos. Good for connecting science to practice.

## Textbooks by Topic

- **Microbiology**: *Food Microbiology: Fundamentals and Frontiers* (Doyle & Buchanan, 2020) — Chapter on fermented dairy products
- **Biochemistry**: *Biochemistry of Foods* (Eskin & Shahidi, 2013) — Sections on milk protein and lipid chemistry
- **Sensory**: *Sensory Evaluation of Food: Principles and Practices* (Lawless & Heymann, 2010) — Methods applicable to cheese evaluation

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Cheese and archaeology** — Ancient proteins preserved in pottery: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature23821
- **Cheese microbiomes as model ecosystems** — Wolfe lab publications on community assembly
- **Cheese terroir and climate change** — Studies on how warming affects milk composition and cave conditions
- **Cheese waste valorization** — Using whey and cheese byproducts for bioactive compounds, bioplastics
- **Cheese and the human microbiome** — Probiotic effects, fermented food consumption and gut health
- **Artisanal vs. industrial microbiology** — Comparative studies of standardization vs. diversity

## Code and Data

- **QIIME2** — https://qiime2.org — Microbiome analysis platform, tutorials include cheese rind datasets
- **R packages for sensory analysis** — SensoMineR, FactoMineR for multivariate sensory data analysis
- **Open Food Facts** — https://world.openfoodfacts.org — Crowdsourced food product database, includes many cheeses with nutritional data

## Key Papers (Landmark and Review Articles)

- Wolfe et al. (2014) "Cheese rind communities provide tractable systems for in situ and in vitro studies of microbial diversity" *Cell* — Foundational paper on cheese rinds as model ecosystems

- Dugat-Bony et al. (2016) "Overview of a surface-ripened cheese community functioning by meta-omics analyses" *PLoS ONE* — Multi-omics approach to cheese microbiology

- Yvon & Rijnen (2001) "Cheese flavour formation by amino acid catabolism" *Int. Dairy J.* — Classic review of flavor biochemistry

- McSweeney (2004) "Biochemistry of cheese ripening" *Int. J. Dairy Tech.* — Comprehensive review of aging chemistry

- Montel et al. (2014) "Traditional cheeses: rich and diverse microbiota with associated benefits" *Int. J. Food Microbiol.* — Review of artisanal cheese microbiology and terroir
