# Polymer Science — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Polymer Chemistry & Synthesis
- Chain-growth polymerization (radical, ionic, coordination)
- Step-growth polymerization (condensation, polyaddition)
- Copolymerization and polymer architecture
- Living/controlled polymerization techniques (ATRP, RAFT, anionic)
- Click chemistry and post-polymerization modification

### 2. Polymer Structure & Morphology
- Molecular weight distributions and averages
- Chain conformations (random coil, rod-like, branched)
- Crystallinity vs. amorphous regions
- Phase separation in block copolymers
- Glass transition and melting behavior

### 3. Polymer Characterization
- Size exclusion chromatography (SEC/GPC)
- Spectroscopic methods (NMR, IR, Raman)
- Thermal analysis (DSC, TGA, DMA)
- Microscopy (SEM, TEM, AFM)
- Scattering techniques (X-ray, light, neutron)

### 4. Polymer Physical Properties
- Mechanical behavior (elasticity, viscoelasticity, plasticity)
- Rheology and flow properties
- Thermal properties and transitions
- Electrical and optical properties
- Solution properties and thermodynamics

### 5. Polymer Processing & Applications
- Extrusion, molding, and film formation
- Polymer blends and composites
- Functional polymers (conducting, biomedical, smart materials)
- Sustainability and degradation
- Industrial applications

## Student Level Considerations

For **intermediate** students, assumes:
- Solid foundation in organic chemistry (reaction mechanisms, functional groups)
- Basic physical chemistry (thermodynamics, kinetics, phase equilibria)
- Some exposure to materials science or macromolecular concepts
- Comfort with mathematical descriptions (statistics, calculus)

Focus should be on:
- Connecting molecular structure to macroscopic properties
- Developing intuition for structure-property relationships
- Hands-on problem solving with real polymer systems
- Understanding characterization methods and data interpretation
- Building vocabulary and conceptual frameworks for advanced study

Avoid at this level:
- Overly detailed statistical mechanics derivations
- Advanced scaling theory without motivation
- Exhaustive catalog of all polymerization mechanisms
- Highly specialized topics (e.g., single-chain mechanics, advanced simulation methods)
