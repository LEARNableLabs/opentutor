## Domain Hooks
- **The Maryland flag paradox** (lesson 4-5) — Maryland's flag violates the "keep it simple" rule with its busy quartered design, yet ranks #1 in NAVA surveys. Why? It's distinctive, rooted in heraldic tradition, and beloved by residents. Teaches that rules have exceptions when cultural buy-in is strong.

- **Nepal's flag mathematics** (lesson 17) — Nepal's flag is the only non-rectangular national flag, defined by a 24-step geometric construction involving angle bisectors, perpendicular lines, and precise ratios. The construction sheet is a work of art. Perfect for math-inclined students.

- **The Liberian county flags disaster** (lesson 21) — Liberia's county flags are *spectacularly* bad — clipart, Comic Sans text, low-resolution images. They're a masterclass in what not to do, and they're real government flags. Use them for critique practice with humor.

## Common Failure Modes
1. **"Complexity equals sophistication"**
   - **Why students get this wrong**: Design novices associate detail with skill and care. They think elaborate seals or intricate patterns show more effort.
   - **How to correct**: Show side-by-side comparisons of simple flags (Japan, Switzerland) recognized globally vs complex flags (Wisconsin, many US states) that fail recognition tests. Emphasize that simplicity is *harder* than complexity — it requires distilling meaning to essentials. Use the "draw it from memory" test: can you sketch it after seeing it once?
