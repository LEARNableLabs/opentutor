# Research Summary: The Essay — From Montaigne to the Personal Essay

## Major Subtopics

1. **Montaigne's Innovation (1580)**
   - Invention of the essay genre with "Essais"
   - The word "essai" (to try/attempt) as a literary form
   - Self-portraiture and introspection as literary method
   - 107 essays in three books, increasingly personal and digressive

2. **Evolution of the Form**
   - Francis Bacon's English essays (more structured, aphoristic)
   - The Enlightenment essay (philosophical, argumentative)
   - Romantic personal essay (Hazlitt, Lamb, De Quincey)
   - American transcendentalist essays (Emerson, Thoreau)

3. **The Personal Essay Tradition**
   - 20th century masters (Woolf, Orwell, Baldwin, Didion)
   - Characteristics: first-person narrator, digression, reflection, intimacy
   - Distinction from academic essay, journalism, memoir
   - Contemporary forms (lyric essay, hermit crab essay, segmented)

4. **Craft Elements**
   - Voice and persona
   - Structure and digression
   - Scene-setting and exposition balance
   - The "so what" question — universal from particular
   - Ending strategies

## Pedagogical Notes from Research

- Students struggle with Montaigne's non-linear style — patience required
- Montaigne taught in both philosophy and creative writing contexts (interdisciplinary)
- Distinction from five-paragraph essay crucial for students
- Contemporary personal essay courses emphasize craft exercises, reading assignments, and writing practice
- Strong emphasis on reading widely across the tradition before attempting to write
