# The Essay — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Montaigne's Essays — Complete Texts
- **Project Gutenberg** — [Essays of Michel de Montaigne, Complete](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3600/3600-h/3600-h.htm)  
  Free complete HTML edition. Good for browsing and searching.
  
- **Internet Archive** — [Essays of Montaigne](https://archive.org/details/essaysofmontaign02mont)  
  Multiple formats (PDF, EPUB, Kindle). Downloadable for offline reading.
  
- **Early Modern Texts** — [Montaigne Book I (PDF)](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/montaigne1580book1.pdf)  
  Modernized English version—easier syntax while preserving meaning. Excellent for intermediate students encountering Montaigne for the first time.

### Scholarly Books on the Essay Form
- **Oxford Academic** — [On Essays: Montaigne to the Present](https://academic.oup.com/book/36719)  
  Scholarly collection examining the essay form across 400+ years. Strong theoretical and historical framework. For intermediate students: use introduction and selected chapters.

- **Modern Language Association** — [Approaches to Teaching Montaigne's Essays](https://www.mla.org/Publications/Bookstore/Approaches-to-Teaching-World-Literature/Approaches-to-Teaching-Montaigne-s-Essays)  
  Pedagogical guide for instructors, useful for understanding how Montaigne is taught and why certain essays are chosen. Student perspective: the annotated reading lists are valuable.

### Reference and Context
- **Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy** — [Michel de Montaigne](https://iep.utm.edu/montaigne/)  
  Comprehensive philosophical context: Montaigne's Skepticism, ethics, political thought. Situates the Essays in Renaissance philosophy.

- **Wikipedia** — [Essays (Montaigne)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essays_(Montaigne))  
  Solid overview with publication history, structure, major themes, and reception. Good starting point.

- **The Collector** — [Michel de Montaigne and the Art of the Personal Essay](https://www.thecollector.com/michel-de-montaigne-essay/)  
  Accessible article on Montaigne's innovation and influence. Well-illustrated, clear prose. Excellent for visual learners.

## Video Courses and Lectures

### Coursera — Wesleyan University
- **[Writing a Personal Essay](https://www.coursera.org/learn/personal-essay)**  
  Free to audit. Video lectures on craft, writing prompts, peer review. Part of "Memoir and Personal Essay" specialization with 16 writing assignments across four courses. Good for hands-on practice alongside this theoretical/historical curriculum.

### Workshop-Based Online Courses (paid, but valuable)
- **UCLA Extension** — [Personal Essay I](https://www.uclaextension.edu/writing/creative-writing/course/personal-essay-i-writing-x-4221)  
  Focuses on craft elements: theme, character development, voice, pacing, scene-setting, exposition. Strong on the nuts and bolts of essay construction.

- **Writers.com** — [Personal Essay Writing Courses](https://writers.com/online-personal-essay-writing-courses)  
  Various formats and levels. Established program (online since 1995). Good for students wanting structured guidance and instructor feedback.

- **Writing Workshops** — [6-Week Intro to Personal Essay with Diana Spechler](https://writingworkshops.com/products/6-week-online-intro-to-personal-essay)  
  Asynchronous online classroom with craft materials, lectures, reading assignments, writing prompts. Emphasis on contemporary personal essay.

## Critical Writing and Essay Analysis

- **Essay Daily** — [Reading Montaigne's "Of Practice"](http://www.essaydaily.org/2016/07/of-bedrock-reading-michel-de-montaignes.html)  
  Close reading of a single Montaigne essay. Models analytical approach. Essay Daily's entire archive is valuable for contemporary critical writing about the essay form.

- **National Endowment for the Humanities** — [Our Contemporary, Montaigne](https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2015/marchapril/feature/our-contemporary-montaigne-he-pioneered-the-personal-essay-and-ma)  
  Essay on Montaigne's invention of the personal essay and contemporary relevance. Covers NEH seminars for teachers, shows how Montaigne is used in educational contexts.

- **University of Chicago Magazine** — [Of Montaigne](https://mag.uchicago.edu/arts-humanities/montaigne)  
  Interview with Philippe Desan, Montaigne scholar, on teaching the Essays. Insight into how Montaigne is taught at university level.

## Anthologies and Essay Collections (not linked, but essential for deeper study)

### Recommended for Purchase or Library Access
- **Phillip Lopate, ed., *The Art of the Personal Essay: An Anthology from the Classical Era to the Present***  
  THE anthology for this topic. Spans 2,000+ years, includes Montaigne, Bacon, Hazlitt, Lamb, Woolf, Baldwin, Didion, and many more. Lopate's introductions are mini-essays on the form. Essential for seeing the full tradition.

- **John D'Agata, ed., *The Next American Essay* and *The Lost Origins of the Essay***  
  Two anthologies exploring experimental and historical essay forms. D'Agata argues for a broader definition of the essay. Controversial but generative for discussions of genre boundaries.

- **Nicole Walker and Margot Singer, eds., *Bending Genre: Essays on Creative Nonfiction***  
  Contemporary essays *about* essays and creative nonfiction craft. Great for meta-discussions.

## Contemporary Essayists to Explore (beyond the curriculum)

### Classic 20th Century
- **James Baldwin** — *Notes of a Native Son*, *The Fire Next Time*  
  Personal essay as social and political critique. Essential.

- **Joan Didion** — *Slouching Towards Bethlehem*, *The White Album*  
  Syntax as thought, cool observational stance, fragmentation.

- **Virginia Woolf** — *The Death of the Moth and Other Essays*, *The Common Reader*  
  Close observation, interior reflection, feminist critique.

- **George Orwell** — *Shooting an Elephant and Other Essays*  
  Political essays grounded in personal experience and observation.

- **E.B. White** — *Essays of E.B. White*  
  American familiar essay tradition. Conversational, wry, humane.

### Contemporary (21st Century)
- **Maggie Nelson** — *Bluets*, *The Argonauts*  
  Lyric essay, associative structure, genre-blending.

- **Leslie Jamison** — *The Empathy Exams*  
  Personal and cultural criticism intertwined.

- **Claudia Rankine** — *Citizen: An American Lyric*  
  Hybrid essay/poetry form addressing race and identity.

- **Eula Biss** — *Notes from No Man's Land*, *On Immunity*  
  Personal experience as lens for social analysis.

- **Teju Cole** — *Known and Strange Things*  
  Photography, travel, politics, art—essayistic range.

- **Jia Tolentino** — *Trick Mirror*  
  Contemporary internet culture through personal lens.

## Unexpected Connections (Rabbit Holes)

### The Essay and Philosophy
- Montaigne's Skepticism connects to ancient Pyrrhonism and contemporary epistemology
- Adorno's "The Essay as Form" (1958) — philosophical defense of the essay as thought-mode
- The essay as "thinking in public" connects to Habermas's public sphere theory

### The Essay Across Media
- **Video essays** on YouTube (ContraPoints, Folding Ideas, Every Frame a Painting) adapt essayistic techniques to visual medium
- **Podcast essays** (*This American Life*, *Serial*, *Radiolab*) use first-person, digressive, meditative structure in audio
- **The essay and the blog**: early 2000s blogs revived essayistic writing for digital age

### The Essay and Other Genres
- **Montaigne and Shakespeare**: *The Tempest* draws on "Of Cannibals," *Hamlet* shares essayistic introspection
- **The essay and the novel**: Novels-in-essays (Jenny Offill's *Dept. of Speculation*, David Shields's *Reality Hunger*)
- **The travel essay**: From Montaigne's Italy journey to contemporary travel writing (Pico Iyer, Geoff Dyer)

### Gender and the Essay
- Why have women writers excelled at the essay form? (Woolf, Sontag, Hardwick, Nelson, Jamison)
- The personal as political: feminist and queer essayists (Adrienne Rich, Audre Lorde, Maggie Nelson)

### The Essay in Translation
- How the essay form developed differently in German (*Versuch*), Spanish (*ensayo*), French (*essai*)
- Untranslatable essayists worth reading in translation: Walter Benjamin, Jorge Luis Borges, Clarice Lispector

## Tips for Resource Use

### For Students
- **Start with accessible contemporary essays** before diving into Montaigne—the form is more familiar, syntax easier
- **Read essays aloud**—you'll hear the voice and rhythms more clearly
- **Keep a "moves I notice" notebook**—when you see a craft technique (a great digression, a particular-to-universal move), write it down
- **Imitate before innovating**—try writing a paragraph in Didion's style, then Montaigne's. Imitation teaches craft.

### For Tutors
- **Pair readings**: always pair a historical essay with a contemporary one using similar techniques
- **Annotate together**: model close reading by annotating an essay's structure with the student
- **Ask "how" not "what"**: Don't ask "what is this essay about?" Ask "How does this essay move? How does the voice work?"
- **Embrace the mess**: The essay resists clean definitions. Lean into that productive confusion.
