# Cartography Research Summary

## Major Subtopics Identified

1. **Foundations of Cartography**
   - Maps as representations of reality
   - Coordinate systems and datums
   - Map projections and their properties
   - Scale and generalization

2. **Cartographic Design**
   - Visual hierarchy and composition
   - Typography and labeling
   - Color theory for maps
   - Symbolization and semiotics

3. **Thematic Mapping**
   - Choropleth maps
   - Proportional symbols
   - Dot density and heat maps
   - Multivariate visualization

4. **Map Projections (Deep Dive)**
   - Projection properties (area, shape, distance, direction)
   - Tissot's indicatrix
   - Choosing appropriate projections
   - Web Mercator and its implications

5. **Modern Digital Cartography**
   - GIS fundamentals
   - Web mapping technologies
   - Interactive and animated maps
   - Mobile cartography

6. **Critical Cartography**
   - Map interpretation and literacy
   - Bias and power in maps
   - Persuasive cartography
   - Ethical considerations

## Key Educational Resources Found

### University Courses
- **UNC Geography 370: Introduction to Cartography** (Meghan Kelly) — comprehensive syllabus covering reference and thematic mapping over 12 weeks
- **Penn State GEOG 383: Introduction to Cartography** — standard intro course structure
- **MIT OpenCourseWare GIS Tutorial** — practical exercises using ArcGIS Pro and QGIS
- **Penn State Maps and the Geospatial Revolution** — online course on making maps and analyzing geographic patterns

### Video Lectures

### Interactive Tools and Simulators
- **Projection Playground** (Observable) — compare ~100 map projections interactively
- **Projection Wizard** (projectionwizard.org) — helps select appropriate projections based on extent and distortion properties
- **NASA G.Projector** — transforms images into 200+ map projections
- **mysimulator.uk Map Projection Simulator** — visualizes Tissot indicatrices under different projections
- **Cartography Playground** (cartography-playground.gitlab.io) — explains cartographic algorithms interactively
- **Penn State Interactive Album of Map Projections 2.0** (projections.mgis.psu.edu)

## Pedagogical Insights
- Cartography sits at the intersection of art and science
- Strong visual/spatial component makes interactive tools essential
- Common progression: understand projections → design principles → thematic mapping → critical interpretation
- GIS skills often taught alongside cartography but require separate technical focus
- Map critique and interpretation as important as production skills
