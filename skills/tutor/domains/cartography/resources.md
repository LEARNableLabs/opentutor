# Cartography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks
- **Krygier & Wood (2011): *Making Maps: A Visual Guide to Map Design for GIS*** — Gold standard for design-focused cartography. Perfect for intermediate students. Covers visual hierarchy, color theory, typography, and symbolization with clear examples.
- **Slocum (2009): *Thematic Cartography and Geovisualization*** — Comprehensive technical reference. Strong on classification schemes, multivariate mapping, and animated cartography.
- **Brewer (2006): *Designing Better Maps: A Guide for GIS Users*** — Shorter, practical guide focused on design principles. Excellent for ColorBrewer methodology.
- **Kraak & Ormeling (2002): *Cartography: Visualization of Spatial Data*** — European perspective, strong on map communication theory.
- **Monmonier (2018): *How to Lie with Maps*** — Essential reading for critical cartography. Shows how design choices shape interpretation.

### Online Textbook
- **OpenALG Introduction to Cartography** — https://alg.manifoldapp.org/read/introduction-to-cartography — Free, openly licensed textbook covering fundamentals through advanced topics

### University Course Materials
- **UNC Geography 370 (Meghan Kelly)** — https://meghankelly-cartography.github.io/teaching_materials.html — Comprehensive teaching materials including syllabi, assignments, and examples for reference and thematic mapping
- **Penn State Cartography and Visualization ROAM** — https://roam.libraries.psu.edu/resource/cartography-and-visualization — Open and affordable materials for cartography courses
- **MIT OpenCourseWare GIS Tutorial** — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-str-001-geographic-information-system-gis-tutorial-january-iap-2022/pages/gis-level-1/ — Practical introduction to GIS and basic cartography principles

### Interactive Tools and Simulators

#### Map Projection Tools
- **Projection Playground (Observable)** — https://observablehq.com/@floledermann/projection-playground — Interactive tool to explore and compare ~100 map projections. Adjust projection parameters in real-time. Perfect for lessons 6-10.
- **Projection Wizard** — https://projectionwizard.org/ — Web app that recommends appropriate projections based on map extent and distortion properties. Returns PROJ and WKT strings for GIS use.
- **NASA G.Projector** — https://www.giss.nasa.gov/tools/gprojector/ — Transform images into ~200 different map projections. Download for desktop use.
- **mysimulator.uk Map Projection Simulator** — https://www.mysimulator.uk/gis/gps-map-projection/ — Visualizes coastlines and Tissot indicatrices under five classic projections (Mercator, Mollweide, Equirectangular, Azimuthal Equal-Area, Stereographic)
- **Penn State Interactive Album of Map Projections 2.0** — https://projections.mgis.psu.edu/ — Searchable database of map projections with interactive examples

#### Cartography Playgrounds
- **Cartography Playground** — https://cartography-playground.gitlab.io/ — Interactive explanations of cartographic algorithms and problems. Aimed at students wanting to deepen technical knowledge.

#### Color Tools
- **ColorBrewer 2.0** — https://colorbrewer2.org — Essential tool for choosing cartographic color schemes. Supports sequential, diverging, and qualitative palettes with colorblind-safe options.

## Unexpected Cross-Discipline Connections

### Mathematics
- **Differential Geometry** — Gaussian curvature explains why you can't flatten a sphere (fundamental theorem of surfaces)
- **Topology** — Map coloring problems (four-color theorem), network analysis
- **Complex Analysis** — Conformal mappings in complex plane relate to conformal projections

### Vision Science and Psychology
- **Perceptual Psychology** — Why we perceive some colors/sizes/shapes as "more" than others (Stevens' power law)
- **Gestalt Principles** — Figure-ground, proximity, similarity shape how we read maps
- **Color Vision Research** — Colorblind-safe design requires understanding cone cell responses

### Political Science and History
- **Decolonization Studies** — Maps as tools of empire (colonial cartography, imposed boundaries)
- **Propaganda Studies** — How authoritarian regimes use maps persuasively
- **Critical Geography** — Maps as social constructions, not neutral representations

### Art and Design
- **Typography** — Type design principles apply directly to map labeling
- **Information Design** — Edward Tufte's principles (data-ink ratio, small multiples) apply to cartography
- **Graphic Design** — Layout, composition, and visual hierarchy are shared with graphic design

### Computer Science
- **Computational Geometry** — Algorithms for generalization, buffering, polygon operations
- **Computer Graphics** — Rendering techniques, anti-aliasing, color spaces
- **Data Visualization** — D3.js, Observable, modern web-based cartography

### Environmental Science and Geography
- **Remote Sensing** — Satellite imagery provides source data for mapping
- **Spatial Statistics** — Analyzing geographic patterns, autocorrelation
- **Physical Geography** — Understanding terrain, hydrology shapes how we map them

## Controversial or Provocative Maps (for critical discussion)

- **Mercator vs. Peters Projection Debate** — Search for "west wing map projection scene" for pop culture entry point
- **Upside-Down World Maps** — Stuart McArthur's "Universal Corrective Map of the World" (1979), south on top
- **Authagraph Projection** — 2016 Good Design Award winner, rectangular equal-area projection
- **Dymaxion Map** — Buckminster Fuller's icosahedral projection with no "up"
- **Election Maps** — How choropleth maps make landslides look bigger (land doesn't vote, people do)
- **Colonial Maps** — Historical maps showing how empires carved up Africa, Asia
- **Corporate Persuasion Maps** — Real estate maps that exaggerate proximity to amenities
