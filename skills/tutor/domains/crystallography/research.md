# Crystallography — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

1. **Symmetry & Group Theory** — Point groups, space groups, symmetry operations, Hermann-Mauguin notation
2. **Crystal Systems & Lattices** — 7 crystal systems, 14 Bravais lattices, unit cells, Miller indices
3. **Diffraction Methods** — X-ray diffraction, Bragg's law, reciprocal space, Ewald sphere, structure factors
4. **Structure Determination** — Phase problem, Patterson maps, direct methods, molecular replacement
5. **Experimental Techniques** — Single crystal vs powder diffraction, synchrotron sources, cryo-crystallography
6. **Applications** — Protein crystallography, materials characterization, pharmaceutical polymorphs, minerals

## Key Sources

### Textbooks
- **"Introduction to Crystallography" by Donald E. Sands** — Excellent intermediate text, clear geometric approach
- **"Fundamentals of Crystallography" edited by Giacovazzo** — Comprehensive IUCr reference
- **"Structure Determination by X-ray Crystallography" by M.F.C. Ladd & R.A. Palmer** — Practical focus on diffraction
- **"Elements of X-Ray Diffraction" by B.D. Cullity & S.R. Stock** — Engineering perspective

### Interactive Tools & Software

### Pedagogical Approach for Intermediate Level

At the intermediate level, students should:
- Build strong geometric intuition for symmetry
- Master the 32 point groups and understand space group notation
- Understand reciprocal space conceptually and mathematically
- Learn practical diffraction experiment design
- Practice structure determination workflows
- Connect crystallography to real materials problems

The curriculum emphasizes visualization, hands-on software use, and real-world applications alongside mathematical rigor.
