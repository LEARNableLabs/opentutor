# Crystallography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **"Introduction to Crystallography" by Donald E. Sands** (Dover, 1993) — Excellent geometric approach, minimal prerequisites. Perfect for intermediate learners. Covers symmetry through space groups with clear diagrams.

- **"Fundamentals of Crystallography" edited by Carmelo Giacovazzo** (Oxford University Press, 3rd ed.) — Official IUCr textbook. Comprehensive but dense. Use selectively for reference on structure factor, phase problem, refinement.

- **"Elements of X-Ray Diffraction" by B.D. Cullity & S.R. Stock** (Pearson, 3rd ed.) — Engineering/materials focus. Excellent on powder diffraction and experimental techniques. Practical, less formal than Giacovazzo.

- **"Structure Determination by X-ray Crystallography" by M.F.C. Ladd & R.A. Palmer** (Springer) — Good balance of theory and practice. Strong on direct methods and Patterson techniques.

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 3.60** (Symmetry, Structure, and Tensor Properties of Materials) — Full lecture notes, assignments, exams. Prof. Ceder's lectures cover symmetry systematically.
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-60-symmetry-structure-and-tensor-properties-of-materials-fall-2005/

- **IUCr Online Crystallographic Teaching Pamphlets** — Free, peer-reviewed, topic-focused (5-15 pages each). Cover symmetry, space groups, diffraction, refinement. Excellent as primary lesson sources.
  - https://www.iucr.org/education/pamphlets

## Supplementary (for engagement)

  - https://www.khanacademy.org/science/chemistry

- **Crystallography Matters! (YouTube channel)** — IUCr-produced videos on applications, history, techniques
  - https://www.youtube.com/user/iucr

  - https://www.youtube.com/user/harryb5

- **Periodic Videos / Sixty Symbols** — Crystal structures of elements, quasicrystals, diffraction experiments (public-facing, motivating)
  - https://www.youtube.com/user/periodicvideos

### Interactive Tools

- **VESTA (Visualization for Electronic and STructural Analysis)** — Free, cross-platform, 3D crystal structure visualization. Essential tool. Students should install and use regularly.
  - https://jp-minerals.org/vesta/en/

- **Mercury** (Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre) — Free crystal structure visualization and analysis. Integrates with CSD. Excellent for exploring packing, symmetry, polymorphs.
  - https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/solutions/software/mercury/

- **Bilbao Crystallographic Server** — Online database and tools for space groups, symmetry operations, subgroup relationships, representations. The authoritative computational resource.
  - http://www.cryst.ehu.es/

- **Jmol / JSmol** — Open-source molecular viewer. Web-based, scriptable. Used in many educational sites.
  - http://jmol.sourceforge.net/

- **CrystalMaker** — Commercial but intuitive for building and manipulating crystal structures. 30-day trial available.
  - https://crystalmaker.com/

- **DoITPoMS (University of Cambridge)** — Interactive teaching and learning packages. Excellent modules on Miller indices, reciprocal space, Ewald sphere.
  - https://www.doitpoms.ac.uk/tlplib/crystallography3/index.php

### Code & Computational Tools

- **pymatgen** (Python Materials Genomics) — Python library for materials analysis. Symmetry analysis, structure manipulation, integration with databases.
  - https://pymatgen.org/

- **ASE (Atomic Simulation Environment)** — Python framework for atomic-scale simulations. Includes crystallography utilities.
  - https://wiki.fysik.dtu.dk/ase/

- **spglib** — Space group determination from atomic positions. C library with Python bindings.
  - https://spglib.github.io/spglib/

- **Jupyter notebooks for crystallography** — Search GitHub for "crystallography jupyter" to find interactive tutorials on symmetry, diffraction simulation, structure analysis.

### Databases

- **Cambridge Structural Database (CSD)** — 1.2+ million organic and metal-organic structures. Requires institutional access but offers free teaching subsets.
  - https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/solutions/software/csd/

- **Protein Data Bank (PDB)** — 200,000+ biological macromolecule structures. Fully open access.
  - https://www.rcsb.org/

- **Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ICSD)** — Inorganic materials. Requires subscription but many universities have access.
  - https://icsd.products.fiz-karlsruhe.de/

- **Crystallography Open Database (COD)** — Open-access repository of crystal structures (organic, inorganic, minerals). Great for teaching.
  - https://www.crystallography.net/cod/

- **American Mineralogist Crystal Structure Database** — Minerals and related compounds. Open access.
  - http://rruff.geo.arizona.edu/AMS/amcsd.php

- **Materials Project** — Computed materials properties, including crystal structures, symmetry analysis. Open access.
  - https://materialsproject.org/

### People (to look up)

- **Dorothy Crowfoot Hodgkin** — Nobel Prize 1964 for structures of penicillin, vitamin B12, insulin. Pioneering work on phase problem.
- **Max von Laue** — Nobel Prize 1914 for discovery of X-ray diffraction by crystals.
- **William Henry Bragg & William Lawrence Bragg** — Nobel Prize 1915 (father-son team) for X-ray crystal structure analysis. Bragg's law.
- **Linus Pauling** — Chemical bonding and crystal structures. "The Nature of the Chemical Bond."
- **Dan Shechtman** — Nobel Prize 2011 for discovery of quasicrystals (5-fold symmetry, non-periodic).
- **Kathleen Lonsdale** — First female Fellow of the Royal Society; structure of benzene, diamond synthesis.
- **Rosalind Franklin** — X-ray diffraction of DNA (Photo 51), viruses, coal/graphite. Underappreciated during her lifetime.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards)

- **Quasicrystals and Islamic tilings** — Medieval Islamic art uses aperiodic Penrose-like patterns centuries before Penrose. Cultural + mathematical depth.
  - https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05452

- **Crystallography in art conservation** — X-ray diffraction identifies pigments, corrosion products in historical artifacts. Getty Conservation Institute, British Museum labs.

- **Crystallography and music** — Symmetry group theory underlies musical transformations (12-tone serialism, neo-Riemannian theory). See Dmitri Tymoczko's work.

- **Protein crystallography and drug design** — Structure-based drug design uses crystallographic structures to find inhibitors. COVID-19 protease structures led to Paxlovid.

- **Crystalline nanoporous materials (MOFs, zeolites)** — Record surface areas, gas storage, catalysis. Reticular chemistry (Omar Yaghi).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal-organic_framework

- **Topological materials** — Topological insulators, Weyl semimetals have crystallographic space group symmetries that protect electronic states. Nobel Prize 2016 connection.

- **Crystallography in space** — Protein crystal growth in microgravity (ISS experiments). Mineral structures from meteorites. Astrobiology implications.

- **Historical codebreaking** — Patterson function development during WWII; Dorothy Hodgkin's work had military relevance (penicillin production).
