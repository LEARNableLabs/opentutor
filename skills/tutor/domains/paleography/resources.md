# Paleography — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks & Reference Works

- **Bernhard Bischoff, *Latin Palaeography: Antiquity and the Middle Ages*** (Cambridge, 1990)
  - Comprehensive, authoritative reference for Latin scripts from late antiquity through medieval period
  - Best for: script evolution, dating diagnostic features, regional variations
  - Level: Advanced, but essential reference for intermediate students doing dating work

- **Michelle P. Brown, *A Guide to Western Historical Scripts from Antiquity to 1600*** (British Library, 1990)
  - Visual album with extensive plates and clear explanations
  - Best for: script identification, seeing exemplars of major types
  - Level: Accessible to intermediate students

- **Raymond Clemens & Timothy Graham, *Introduction to Manuscript Studies*** (Cornell, 2007)
  - Includes paleography chapters alongside codicology, bookbinding, illumination
  - Best for: understanding manuscripts as objects, context for scripts
  - Level: Perfect for intermediate

- **Albert Derolez, *The Palaeography of Gothic Manuscript Books from the Twelfth to the Early Sixteenth Century*** (Cambridge, 2003)
  - Definitive reference for Gothic scripts with detailed classification
  - Best for: distinguishing Gothic variants, dating Gothic manuscripts
  - Level: Advanced, but invaluable for lessons 8-12

- **M.B. Parkes, *English Cursive Book Hands 1250-1500*** (Oxford, 1969)
  - Focused study of English cursive scripts with dated examples
  - Best for: understanding Secretary hand's development, English manuscripts
  - Level: Intermediate

- **Adriano Cappelli, *Dizionario di Abbreviature Latine ed Italiane*** (Milan, 1899; many reprints)
  - Standard abbreviation dictionary, thousands of forms
  - Best for: expanding unfamiliar abbreviations, systematic reference
  - Level: All levels (reference tool)
  - Online: https://www.adfontes.uzh.ch/en/ressourcen/abkuerzungen/cappelli-online

### Online Courses & Tutorials

- **The National Archives (UK) — Palaeography Tutorial**
  - https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/palaeography/
  - Interactive tutorials for English documents 1500-1800
  - Includes transcription practice with answer keys
  - Best for: Secretary hand, early modern English documents, self-paced practice

- **University of London / Institute of Historical Research — Palaeography Summer School**
  - https://www.history.ac.uk/our-research/research-training/palaeography
  - In-person courses (various levels), also has online resources
  - Best for: structured learning, expert instruction
  - Level: Beginner through advanced

- **Ad fontes (University of Zurich)**
  - https://www.adfontes.uzh.ch/en
  - Training platform for working with medieval sources
  - Includes paleography modules, abbreviation tools, practice transcriptions
  - Best for: systematic abbreviation learning, Latin texts

## Digital Manuscript Collections (with transcriptions/tools)

### Major Repositories

- **British Library Digitised Manuscripts**
  - https://www.bl.uk/manuscripts/
  - Over 1000 manuscripts fully digitized, high-resolution images
  - Browse by date, origin, language, subject
  - Best for: seeing authentic examples, comparing scripts, dating practice

- **Parker on the Web (Corpus Christi College, Cambridge)**
  - https://parker.stanford.edu/
  - Anglo-Saxon and medieval manuscripts, many with scholarly descriptions
  - Best for: Insular scripts, early English manuscripts

- **e-codices (Virtual Manuscript Library of Switzerland)**
  - https://www.e-codices.ch/en
  - Swiss manuscripts, excellent image quality, scholarly metadata
  - Best for: codicology practice, seeing manuscripts in context

- **Gallica (Bibliothèque nationale de France)**
  - https://gallica.bnf.fr/
  - Vast collection including manuscripts; search "manuscrits"
  - Best for: French manuscripts, Gothic scripts, comparative examples

- **Digital Bodleian**
  - https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/
  - Oxford manuscripts, medieval and early modern
  - Best for: diverse examples, high-quality images

### Specialized Paleography Tools

- **DigiPal — Digital Resource for Palaeography**
  - https://www.digipal.eu/
  - Searchable database of Anglo-Saxon scribal hands with detailed annotations
  - Allows searching for specific letterforms, comparing scribal practices
  - Best for: Insular scripts, learning diagnostic features, attribution practice
  - Level: Intermediate to advanced

- **IIIF Collections**
  - Many institutions use IIIF (International Image Interoperability Framework)
  - Allows side-by-side comparison of manuscripts from different libraries
  - Tools: Mirador viewer (https://projectmirador.org/)
  - Best for: comparative paleography, attribution work

## Interactive Tools

### Transcription Practice

- **Scribes of the Cairo Geniza**
  - https://www.scribesofthecairogeniza.org/
  - Crowdsourced transcription project (Hebrew/Judeo-Arabic, but principles apply)
  - Best for: seeing how transcription works as collaborative process

- **Transcribe Bentham**
  - https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/transcribe-bentham/
  - Crowdsourced transcription of Jeremy Bentham papers (18th-19th c.)
  - Best for: early modern handwriting practice

### Image Tools

- **IIIF Viewers** (Mirador, Universal Viewer)
  - Work with any IIIF-compliant manuscript repository
  - Features: zoom, brightness/contrast adjustment, side-by-side comparison
  - Best for: detailed examination, comparing manuscripts

## Code & Computational Resources

### Digital Paleography Projects

- **Transkribus**
  - https://readcoop.eu/transkribus/
  - AI-powered handwriting recognition for historical documents
  - Can train models on specific scripts
  - Best for: understanding computational approaches, large-scale transcription projects
  - Level: Requires some technical skill, but accessible

- **Kallimachos Project**
  - Computational paleography, feature extraction from manuscript images
  - Research papers available online
  - Best for: understanding how computers "read" handwriting

- **DigiPal Framework** (GitHub)
  - https://github.com/DigiPal/
  - Open-source platform for annotating and analyzing manuscript images
  - Best for: technically-inclined students interested in digital humanities

## Unexpected Connections

### Cross-Disciplinary Links

- **Typography and Font Design**
  - Modern typefaces descend from manuscript hands (Garamond ← Humanistic minuscule, Fraktur ← Gothic textualis)
  - Study: Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*
  - Why relevant: understanding letterform evolution, design principles

- **Forensic Document Examination**
  - Handwriting analysis techniques overlap with paleography
  - Study: forensic handwriting analysis methods, forgery detection
  - Why relevant: attribution methods, understanding variation vs. identity

- **Historical Linguistics**
  - Spelling variants in manuscripts reveal pronunciation changes
  - Study: Middle English orthography, dialectology
  - Why relevant: helps date and localize manuscripts, understand scribal choices

- **Book Conservation**
  - Understanding manuscript construction aids paleographical analysis
  - Study: conservation literature on parchment, ink chemistry, binding
  - Why relevant: explains damage patterns, ink fading, palimpsest recovery

- **History of Education**
  - Scribal training, literacy rates, educational institutions
  - Study: medieval schools, university manuscript production
  - Why relevant: explains script standardization, variation by context

### Wild Cards (Fascinating Tangents)

- **Graffiti and Informal Writing**
  - Walls, flyleaves, margins show non-professional hands
  - Example: Pompeii graffiti, medieval graffiti in churches
  - Why relevant: contrast with formal book hands, shows everyday literacy

- **Paleography of Non-European Scripts**
  - Chinese paleography, Arabic paleography, Hebrew paleography have parallel traditions
  - Example: Chinese bronze inscriptions → seal script → clerical script evolution
  - Why relevant: comparative perspective, universal vs. culture-specific issues

- **Decipherment of Unknown Scripts**
  - Linear B, Mayan glyphs, Rongorongo
  - Why relevant: paleography = known script/unknown hand, decipherment = unknown script, but methods overlap

## Learning Paths by Interest

### For Archival Researchers
Focus: National Archives tutorials, Secretary hand, documentary hands, 15th-17th c. English
Key resources: National Archives, DigiPal (for earlier periods), Parkes

### For Literary Scholars
Focus: Book hands, vernacular manuscripts, Middle English/Old French texts
Key resources: British Library, Parker Library, Bodleian collections

### For Art Historians
Focus: Decorated manuscripts, interaction of script and image, Humanistic books
Key resources: e-codices, British Library Illuminated Manuscripts, Gallica

### For Digital Humanists
Focus: Computational methods, Transkribus, digital archives, IIIF tools
Key resources: DigiPal framework, Transkribus, Peter Stokes's work

### For General Medieval Enthusiasts
Focus: Script diversity, manuscript culture, surprising facts
Key resources: Medieval Manuscripts Blog, Erik Kwakkel's blog, YouTube videos
