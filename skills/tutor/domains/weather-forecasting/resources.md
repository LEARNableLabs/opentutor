# Weather Forecasting — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Authoritative References
- **WMO International Cloud Atlas** (https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/) — The definitive cloud classification reference with high-quality photos, species/varieties documentation, and observation guidelines. Use this as the ground truth for cloud identification.
- **NOAA JetStream** (https://www.weather.gov/jetstream/) — Comprehensive online weather school covering clouds, pressure systems, fronts, forecasting, and severe weather. Excellent for intermediate learners; well-organized modules with graphics.
- **Met Office Learn About Weather** (https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/weather/learn-about) — UK perspective on weather education. Strong on synoptic meteorology and frontal analysis. Good complement to US-centric resources.

### Observational Training
- **CoCoRaHS Training Portal** (https://www.cocorahs.org/Content.aspx?page=training_portal) — Citizen science observer training covering precipitation measurement, cloud observation, and weather reporting. High-quality materials for building observational discipline.
- **NWS Skywarn** (https://www.weather.gov/skywarn/) — Severe weather spotter training program. Find local spotter training courses for hands-on cloud and storm structure education.

### Data Sources
- **Aviation Weather Center** (https://aviationweather.gov/) — Real-time METAR, surface analysis charts, prognostic charts, and area forecasts. Essential for observational practice.
- **Weather.gov Local Forecasts** (https://www.weather.gov/) — NWS local forecasts with Area Forecast Discussions (AFDs) — these show how professional meteorologists reason through forecasting uncertainty.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

### Videos & Visual Learning
- **NOAA SciJinks** (https://scijinks.gov/) — Engaging weather explainers with animations, videos, and games. Good for visual learners and reinforcing concepts.
- **NWS WFO Birmingham Cloud Spotter Training** (YouTube: search "NWS Birmingham cloud spotter") — Practical video training on cloud identification and severe weather features.
- **Pecos Hank** (YouTube channel) — Storm chaser who explains cloud structures and severe weather indicators with stunning footage. Great for engagement, but supplement with technical resources.
- **WMO Cloud Atlas Videos** (https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/videos.html) — Time-lapse cloud videos showing formation and evolution.

### Interactive Tools & Live Data
- **Windy.com** (https://www.windy.com/) — Beautiful real-time pressure maps, wind patterns, model forecasts, and webcams. Essential tool for practicing pressure system identification and forecasting.
- **Zoom Earth** (https://zoom.earth/) — Near-real-time satellite imagery with analysis overlays. Great for tracking cloud systems and fronts from space.
- **NOAA GOES Imagery Viewer** (https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/GOES/) — Satellite imagery (visible, infrared, water vapor) from GOES-16/17. Advanced supplement for students wanting satellite meteorology.
- **Radar (NOAA)** (https://radar.weather.gov/) — Live NEXRAD radar mosaic. Essential for nowcasting and precipitation tracking.
- **Tropical Tidbits** (https://www.tropicaltidbits.com/analysis/models/) — Model guidance viewer (GFS, NAM, ECMWF) with clean interface. Excellent for learning model interpretation.
- **College of DuPage NEXLAB** (https://weather.cod.edu/forecast/) — Comprehensive weather data aggregator with model loops, satellite, radar, and surface analysis.

### Mobile Apps
- **Windy** (iOS/Android) — Mobile version of Windy.com; great for on-the-go observation and forecast checking.
- **RadarScope** (iOS/Android, paid) — Professional-grade radar app; optional upgrade for students wanting detailed radar analysis.
- **NOAA Weather Radio** (iOS/Android) — NWS forecasts and alerts; useful for verification practice.

### Code & Data Exploration
- **Python + MetPy** (https://unidata.github.io/MetPy/) — Python library for meteorological data analysis. For students with coding background who want to plot surface maps or analyze soundings.
- **NOAA API** (https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api) — Free API for programmatic access to NWS data. Build custom forecast verification tools.
- **Iowa State Mesonet** (https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/) — Archive of surface observations, radar, and NWS products. Great for historical case studies.

## People (to look up and learn from)

### Meteorologists & Educators
- **Jeff Haby** (https://www.theweatherprediction.com/) — Prolific weather education author; extensive tutorials on forecasting, pressure patterns, and synoptic meteorology. Intermediate-friendly writing style.
- **Cliff Mass** (Weather and Climate Blog: https://cliffmass.blogspot.com/) — University of Washington atmospheric scientist; posts detailed Pacific Northwest weather forecasts with educational explanations. Great model for forecast reasoning.
- **Ryan Maue** (@RyanMaue on Twitter/X) — Meteorologist who shares weather analysis and forecasts; good example of real-time synthesis of obs + models.

### Historical Figures (context & appreciation)
- **Luke Howard (1772-1864)** — "Father of cloud classification"; developed the Latin naming system (cumulus, stratus, cirrus) still used today.
- **Vilhelm Bjerknes (1862-1951)** — Norwegian meteorologist who developed the Bergen School cyclone model and frontal theory. His work underlies modern synoptic meteorology.
- **Tor Bergeron (1891-1977)** — Bjerknes' student who refined warm front / cold front / occlusion concepts and developed the Bergeron process for precipitation formation.

### Citizen Scientists & Communities
- **CoCoRaHS Observers** (https://www.cocorahs.org/) — Community of volunteer weather observers; join to practice daily observation and learn from peers.
- **CWOP (Citizen Weather Observer Program)** (http://www.wxqa.com/) — Volunteer network contributing real-time surface observations. Good way to share and validate your observations.

## Unexpected Connections (for wild cards & rabbit holes)

### Weather in Art
- **Constable's Cloud Studies** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Constable) — 19th-century painter John Constable made scientifically accurate cloud studies. Search for "Constable cloud studies" to see cumulus, stratocumulus, and cumulonimbus rendered by a master observer.
- **Meteorology in Poetry** — Percy Bysshe Shelley's "The Cloud" anthropomorphizes cloud life cycles. Use as a literary hook for cloud formation concepts.

### Cross-Discipline Links
- **Sailing & Navigation** — Traditional sailors used cloud patterns and barometer readings for forecasting before radio. Read "Heavy Weather Sailing" (Adlard Coles) for pre-digital forecasting methods still used offshore.
- **Aviation** — Pilots are expert weather observers; they *must* read cloud bases, icing layers, and convection. FAA aviation weather handbooks (https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak/) teach forecasting from an airborne perspective.
- **Agriculture** — Farmers have traditional forecasting knowledge (red sky at night, halo around the moon). Explore which folk sayings align with modern meteorology and which are superstition.
- **Photography** — Cloud photography teaches attention to detail and lighting. Share work from storm chasers and cloudscape photographers (e.g., Camille Seaman's "The Big Cloud").

### Climate Science Connections
- **ENSO (El Niño / La Niña)** — How tropical Pacific sea surface temperatures affect global jet stream patterns and regional weather. Link: https://www.climate.gov/enso
- **NAO (North Atlantic Oscillation)** — How pressure differences between Iceland and Azores influence European and eastern US weather. Link: https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-variability-north-atlantic-oscillation
- **Blocking Patterns** — Persistent high-pressure ridges that stall weather systems. Connect to heat waves, droughts, and cold snaps.

### Tech & Instrumentation
- **Personal Weather Stations** — If students want to go deeper, explore Davis, Ambient Weather, or WeatherFlow stations for backyard observation.
- **Barometers** — Historical instrument design (mercury, aneroid) and modern digital sensors. Understanding the tool sharpens understanding of pressure.
- **Weather Balloons (Radiosondes)** — Twice-daily upper-air soundings provide the vertical structure models need. Explore https://www.spc.noaa.gov/exper/soundings/ for real-time soundings.

### Historical Weather Events (case studies)
- **1993 Storm of the Century** — Massive cyclone that challenged forecasting models. Great for discussing forecast uncertainty.
- **2011 Joplin Tornado** — Warning lead time and observational challenges. Links severe weather clouds to real-world impacts.
- **Ash Wednesday Storm (1962)** — East Coast extratropical cyclone; classic example of rapid intensification and coastal impacts.
- **Perfect Storm (1991)** — Documented in book/film; shows complex multi-system interactions.

## Ongoing Practice Resources

### Daily Habits
- **Morning routine**: Check local METAR (https://aviationweather.gov/metar/data), look outside, note cloud types, check pressure tendency. 3 minutes.
- **Evening forecast**: Make a 24-hour forecast for tomorrow, write it down. Verify the next day. Track accuracy over weeks.

### Community Engagement
- **Join CoCoRaHS** (https://www.cocorahs.org/) — Submit daily precipitation reports and cloud observations. Structured, ongoing practice.
- **Follow local NWS office on Twitter/X** — See real-time forecast reasoning in Area Forecast Discussions. Learn how pros handle uncertainty.

### Advanced Self-Study (for students wanting more)
- **Meteorology textbooks**:
  - "Weather Analysis" by Dusan Djuric — classic synoptic meteorology text; intermediate-to-advanced
  - "A World of Weather: Fundamentals of Meteorology" by Lee M. Grenci & Jon M. Nese — accessible intermediate text
- **AMS (American Meteorological Society) resources** (https://www.ametsoc.org/) — Professional society with educational programs, journals, and certification paths for serious students

### Fun & Motivation
- **Cloud appreciation societies** — Cloud Appreciation Society (https://cloudappreciationsociety.org/) celebrates cloud beauty and shares global observations. Join for photo galleries and cloud-loving community.
- **Weather Twitter/X** — Follow #wx, #clouds, #weather for real-time community of observers and forecasters worldwide.
