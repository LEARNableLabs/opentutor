# Mushroom Identification — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Comprehensive Field Guides
- **Mushrooms Demystified** by David Arora — the 1000-page bible of North American mushrooms; exhaustive, witty, and opinionated; best for West Coast but useful everywhere
- **North American Mushrooms: A Field Guide to Edible and Inedible Fungi** by Orson Miller and Hope Miller — scientifically rigorous, organized by spore print color, covers eastern and western species
- **Mushrooms of the Redwood Coast** by Noah Siegel and Christian Schwarz — modern, photo-rich, ecologically organized; excellent for California but demonstrates best-practice field guide design
- **National Audubon Society Field Guide to North American Mushrooms** — portable, photo-based, good for beginners; organized by visual similarity rather than taxonomy

### Online Comprehensive Resources
- **MushroomExpert.com** by Michael Kuo (https://www.mushroomexpert.com) — free, expert-authored, covers hundreds of species with detailed descriptions, ecology, and keys; excellent glossary and how-to guides
- **MyCoPortal** (https://mycoportal.org) — university consortium database of herbarium specimens; useful for verified IDs and range maps
- **Index Fungorum** (http://www.indexfungorum.org) — authoritative taxonomic database for checking current scientific names

### Regional Guides (examples)
- **Mushrooms of the Northeastern United States and Eastern Canada** by Barron
- **Mushrooms of the Rocky Mountain Region** by Denver Botanic Gardens / Cripps
- **Mushrooms of the Upper Midwest** by KUO and Methven
- **All That the Rain Promises and More** by David Arora — quirky Pacific Northwest pocket guide with foraging focus

## Supplementary Resources

### Videos and Video Channels
- **North American Mycological Association (NAMA) YouTube** (https://www.youtube.com/c/NAMYCO) — identification workshops, forays, expert talks
- **Learn Your Land** YouTube channel — field identification videos, emphasis on edible/medicinal species
- **Mushroom Identification** by Shroomery — community-contributed videos and guides (focus on active ID skills)
- **Cornell Mushroom Blog** — video field guides to common species in the Northeast

### Interactive Tools and Platforms
- **iNaturalist** (https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/47170-Fungi) — global citizen science platform; upload photos, get community ID suggestions, track observations; excellent for seeing variation within species
- **Mushroom Observer** (https://www.mushroomobserver.org) — dedicated mycology observation database; more detailed than iNaturalist, used by serious amateur and professional mycologists
- **MycoMap** (https://www.mycomap.com) — interactive range maps and phenology data for North American species
- **Fungal Diversity Survey** (https://www.fungaldiversity.org) — mapping and recording fungal diversity with regional focus options

### Smartphone Apps
- **iNaturalist app** — best for general fungi observation with AI-assisted initial ID and expert review
- **Seek by iNaturalist** — beginner-friendly, gamified version for instant (but less accurate) ID
- **Shroomify** — mushroom-specific app with dichotomous key and photo library
- **Picture Mushroom** — AI-based ID app (use with caution; verify with multiple sources)

**Note on ID apps:** AI-based identification apps are improving but NOT reliable for determining edibility. Use them as a starting point, then verify with field guides, expert consultation, and multiple sources.

### Code and Data
- **iNaturalist API** (https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/docs) — programmatic access to observation data for analysis
- **Mushroom Observer API** (https://mushroomobserver.org/api) — access specimen data and identifications
- **Fungal Trait Database** (https://www.fungaltraits.org) — ecological and functional trait data for research

### People and Communities
- **Michael Kuo** — author of MushroomExpert.com and multiple field guides; excellent writer
- **David Arora** — author of Mushrooms Demystified; influential West Coast mycologist
- **Tradd Cotter** — cultivation expert and author (Organic Mushroom Farming and Cultivation)
- **Paul Stamets** — well-known mycologist, author (Mycelium Running, Growing Gourmet and Medicinal Mushrooms)
- **Local mushroom clubs** — find your nearest NAMA-affiliated club at https://www.namyco.org/clubs.php for forays, workshops, and expert mentorship

### Toxicology and Safety
- **NAMA Toxicology Committee** (https://www.namyco.org/toxicology.php) — authoritative resource on mushroom poisoning syndromes
- **Poison Control** (1-800-222-1222 in the US) — 24/7 hotline for suspected mushroom poisoning
- **Case Studies in Mushroom Poisoning** (https://www.namyco.org/toxicology_case_registry.php) — real poisoning cases with identification lessons

### Scientific Literature (advanced)
- **Mycologia** — journal of the Mycological Society of America
- **Mycological Progress** — open-access research journal
- **Index of Fungi** — published with Mycological Research (name changes, taxonomic updates)
- **Regional monographs** — specialized treatments of single genera (e.g., Bresinsky & Besl on Amanita)

## Unexpected Connections

### Mushrooms and Art
- **"The Kingdom: Fungi" photography book** by Lynn Margulis and others — stunning mushroom photography
- **Mushroom dye workshops** — using mushrooms for natural fabric dyeing (search for local workshops or books like "The Rainbow Beneath My Feet" by Alissa Allen)
- **Carsten Höller's fungal installations** — contemporary art exploring mushrooms and psychedelics

### Mushrooms and Literature
- **"Entangled Life"** by Merlin Sheldrake — poetic, accessible science writing on fungal biology and ecology
- **"The Mushroom at the End of the World"** by Anna Tsing — anthropology of matsutake foraging and global capitalism
- **"Mycophilia"** by Eugenia Bone — memoir of a mycophile with science, history, and recipes

### Mushrooms and Climate Science
- **Fungal responses to climate change** — research on shifting ranges, phenology, and mycorrhizal network disruption (search Google Scholar for recent papers)
- **Fungi as carbon sinks** — role of mycorrhizal networks in forest carbon storage (research by Suzanne Simard and others)

### Mushrooms and Food Culture
- **"Mushroom: A Global History"** by Cynthia Bertelsen — cultural history of mushroom use worldwide
- **Wild mushroom foraging recipes** — search for regional cookbooks (e.g., "The Mushroom Hunters" by Langdon Cook)
- **Cultivated exotic mushrooms** — home cultivation of shiitake, oyster, lion's mane (see Tradd Cotter's books)

### Mushrooms and Technology
- **Mycelium as biomaterial** — mushroom-based packaging, leather alternatives, building materials (search for Ecovative Design)
- **Mycoremediation** — using fungi to break down pollutants (Paul Stamets' work on oil spill cleanup)
- **Fungal computing** — experimental research using mycelial networks for computation (search "fungal computers")

## Regional Resources (Bioregional Learning)

### Find Your Local Resources
- **NAMA club directory** (https://www.namyco.org/clubs.php) — find a local mushroom club for forays, workshops, and mentorship
- **Regional forays** — most clubs host weekly or monthly walks during mushroom season (spring and fall in most regions)
- **University herbaria** — many universities have fungal collections open to the public by appointment
- **State mycological societies** — many states have dedicated organizations (e.g., Puget Sound Mycological Society, Boston Mycological Club)

### Regional Field Guides (Prioritize These)
Always prioritize regional guides over continental ones for your area — they'll cover local species in more depth and with better ecological context.

- **Pacific Northwest:** Mushrooms of the Redwood Coast (Siegel & Schwarz), All That the Rain Promises (Arora)
- **Northeast:** Mushrooms of Northeastern North America (Barron)
- **Rocky Mountains:** Mushrooms of the Rocky Mountain Region (Cripps et al.)
- **Midwest:** Mushrooms of the Upper Midwest (Kuo & Methven)
- **Southeast:** Mushrooms of the Southeastern United States (Bessette et al.)

## How to Use These Resources

### For Lessons (in order of priority)
1. Start with **MushroomExpert.com** for clear, free explanations and photos
2. Cross-reference with your **regional field guide** for local species
3. Use **iNaturalist** to see the range of variation and geographic distribution
4. Consult **NAMA** resources for toxicology and safety questions
5. Join **Mushroom Observer** for peer review of your identifications

### For Field Work
1. Bring your **regional field guide** (paper or digital)
2. Use **iNaturalist app** to photograph and track observations
3. Take detailed notes (substrate, associates, smell, staining)
4. When home, consult **MushroomExpert.com** and cross-reference multiple sources
5. Upload observations to **Mushroom Observer** or **iNaturalist** for expert feedback

### For Going Deeper
1. Attend **local mushroom club** forays to learn from experienced foragers
2. Read **Entangled Life** (Sheldrake) for big-picture fungal ecology
3. Explore **wild cards** (fungal art, biomaterials, climate science) to sustain curiosity
4. When you hit identification limits, acknowledge them — consult microscopy resources or experts rather than guessing
