# Plasma Physics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

### Textbooks

- **Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion (Volume 1: Plasma Physics)** by Francis F. Chen
  - Classic intermediate text, clear physical explanations, excellent problem sets
  - Covers: fundamentals, single particle motion, kinetic theory, MHD, waves, instabilities
  - Good match for this curriculum's level and scope

- **Plasma Physics** by Richard Fitzpatrick (University of Texas at Austin)
  - Free online textbook: https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/plasma/Plasma/
  - Comprehensive coverage from basics to advanced topics
  - Strong mathematical rigor balanced with physical insight
  - Used extensively in curriculum resources

- **Fundamentals of Plasma Physics** by Paul Bellan (Caltech)
  - Modern intermediate text with emphasis on physical understanding
  - Excellent treatment of MHD and magnetic confinement
  - Good diagrams and real-world examples

- **The Physics of Plasmas** by T.J.M. Boyd and J.J. Sanderson
  - British perspective, thorough and rigorous
  - Strong on kinetic theory and wave-particle interactions

### Reference Materials

- **NRL Plasma Formulary**
  - https://www.nrl.navy.mil/News-Media/Publications/nrl-plasma-formulary/
  - Essential quick reference for plasma parameters, formulas, and scaling laws
  - Revised periodically; 2023 edition most recent
  - Invaluable for order-of-magnitude estimates

### Online Courses & Lecture Notes

- **MIT 22.611J/8.613J: Introduction to Plasma Physics I** (Fall 2003, 2006)
  - https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/22-611j-introduction-to-plasma-physics-i-fall-2003/
  - Lecture notes, problem sets, and exams
  - Covers fundamentals through waves and instabilities
  - Aligns well with this curriculum

- **MIT 8.333: Statistical Mechanics II (Plasma Physics segment)**
  - Kinetic theory foundations
  - Strong on distribution functions and Vlasov equation

- **University of Texas Plasma Physics Course**
  - Full online textbook by Fitzpatrick (linked above)
  - Problem sets and solutions available

### Interactive Tools & Simulations

- **PlasmaPy**
  - https://www.plasmapy.org/
  - Open-source Python library for plasma physics
  - Calculate plasma parameters, analyze particle dynamics, visualize distributions
  - Great for hands-on exploration and homework problems
  - Can be used throughout the curriculum

- **Particle trajectory simulators**
  - Online tools for E×B drift visualization
  - Magnetic mirror animations
  - Useful for lessons 6-10

- **Dispersion relation plotters**
  - Interactive tools to explore wave modes
  - Change parameters and see how ω(k) changes
  - Useful for lessons 23-24

- **Virtual tokamak tours**
  - 3D visualizations of ITER, JET, and other tokamaks
  - Useful for lesson 26

### Code & Computational Resources

- **PlasmaPy** (mentioned above) — Python library for plasma calculations
  - Installation: `pip install plasmapy`

- **BOUT++**

- **Particle-in-Cell (PIC) codes**

  - Educational versions and tutorials available online

### People to Know (Historical & Contemporary)

**Founders of the field:**
- **Irving Langmuir** — coined the term "plasma" (1928), Nobel Prize 1932
- **Hannes Alfvén** — MHD waves, frozen-in flux, Nobel Prize 1970
- **Lev Landau** — Landau damping, kinetic theory
- **Lyman Spitzer** — stellarator concept, early fusion pioneer

**Contemporary researchers:**
- **Francis F. Chen** — textbook author, UCLA plasma physicist
- **Richard Fitzpatrick** — UT Austin, author of free online textbook
- **Paul Bellan** — Caltech, textbook author and experimentalist
- Many active researchers at PPPL, MIT, IPP, and other labs

### Unexpected Connections & Cross-Disciplinary Links

- **Space weather** — solar flares and coronal mass ejections are plasma phenomena that affect Earth
  - NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center: https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/

- **Astrophysical plasmas** — pulsars, accretion disks, jets
  - Most visible matter in the universe is plasma

- **Industrial applications** — semiconductor etching, plasma displays, materials processing
  - Plasma etching is critical for microchip fabrication

- **Medical applications** — plasma sterilization, plasma medicine
  - Cold atmospheric plasma for wound healing

- **Lightning and atmospheric electricity** — Earth's ionosphere is a plasma
  - Sprites, elves, and other upper-atmospheric phenomena

- **Plasma propulsion** — ion drives for spacecraft
  - NASA's DAWN mission used ion propulsion

## Resource Notes

### Using Primary Sources
- Fitzpatrick's online textbook is free and comprehensive — use it as the backbone
- Chen's textbook for additional perspective and excellent problems
- NRL Formulary for quick lookups and parameter estimates

### Supplementing with Videos
- Use PPPL and ITER videos to anchor abstract concepts in real devices
- NASA resources for space plasma context
- YouTube animations for visual intuition (especially particle motion and Landau damping)

### Hands-On Exploration
- PlasmaPy is invaluable for intermediate students — encourage installation and experimentation
- Interactive simulations for building geometric intuition (drifts, mirrors)
- Coding assignments to compute plasma parameters, solve trajectory equations, plot dispersion relations

### Advanced Resources (for rabbit holes)
- Research papers on arXiv.org in physics.plasm-ph category
- Conference proceedings from APS-DPP
- Advanced texts: Krall & Trivelpiece, Stix, Bittencourt

## Resource Strategy by Lesson

- **Lessons 1-5**: Fitzpatrick chapters 1-2, NRL Formulary, PPPL videos for motivation
- **Lessons 6-11**: Fitzpatrick chapters 3-4, particle trajectory simulations, PlasmaPy for calculations
- **Lessons 12-17**: Fitzpatrick chapters 5-6, Chen for Landau damping treatment
- **Lessons 18-22**: Fitzpatrick chapters 7-8, Chen on MHD
- **Lessons 23-26**: Fitzpatrick chapters 9-10, ITER and PPPL for confinement context

Always supplement with PlasmaPy for hands-on calculation and exploration.
