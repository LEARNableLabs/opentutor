# Circus Arts: Aerial Silks Physics and Rigging — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **"The Backstage Handbook" by Paul Carter** — Technical theatre rigging bible, covers hardware, load calculations, safety systems. Excellent for lessons 6-11 (rigging systems). Not circus-specific but foundational engineering principles apply directly. Available from major booksellers.

- **ETCP Rigging Handbook** — Entertainment Technician Certification Program study materials. Industry-standard reference for rigging safety, load paths, hardware specifications. Essential for lessons 8-11 and 23-25. Available at https://etcprigging.org/

- **"Mechanical Design for the Stage" by Alan Hendrickson** — Advanced rigging engineering with detailed force calculations, structural design, and mechanical systems. Chapter 2 (loads and forces) and Chapter 3 (wire rope and cables) are most relevant. Intermediate level will benefit from the worked examples. Available from Routledge.

- **ESTA E1.43 Standard for Performer Flying Systems** — Defines safety requirements for aerial rigging in entertainment. Not specific to silks but establishes principles. Access through https://tsp.esta.org/

- **"Physics of the Human Body" by Irving P. Herman** — Comprehensive biomechanics textbook. Chapters on skeletal loading and muscle mechanics apply to aerial movement analysis (lessons 18-22). Available from Springer.

- **Aerial Physique blog by Emily Scherb** — Physics-focused aerial instruction with force diagrams and biomechanical analysis. Excellent supplementary material throughout curriculum. https://aerialphysique.com/

- **Dragonfly Aerial Rigging Guides** — Practical rigging education with good visual diagrams for hardware, angles, and safety systems. Accessible writing for intermediate learners. https://dragonflyaerial.com/rigging

## Supplementary Materials

### Video Lectures and Demonstrations

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 8.01 Classical Mechanics (Walter Lewin lectures)** — Foundational physics, particularly lecture 6 (Newton's laws, tension) and lecture 10 (friction). Not circus-specific but excellent for lessons 1-4. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/

- **Circus Sciences YouTube Channel** — Physics demonstrations applied to circus apparatus, including slow-motion drop analysis and force visualization. Good visual supplements for lessons 4, 15, 19-20. Search YouTube for "Circus Sciences"

- **Professional Riggers' Channels**:
  - **Stage Rigging Essentials** — practical hardware tutorials, inspection walkthroughs
  - **Theatre Safety Programs** — pre-flight check demonstrations
  - Search YouTube for specific topics like "carabiner strength test" or "bridle angle demonstration"

- **Slow-motion aerial drops** — Search YouTube for "aerial silks slow motion" to find force visualization examples. Useful for lessons 4, 19-21. Look for videos that show fabric deformation and body position changes during catches.

### Interactive Tools and Calculators

- **Rigging Load Calculator** — spreadsheet-based tool for bridle angles, load factors, safety margins. Community-developed, often found on circus rigging forums. For lessons 8-11.

- **Vector Decomposition Applets** — Physics education sites like PhET Interactive Simulations (https://phet.colorado.edu/) have vector addition/resolution tools. Excellent for lesson 8 (bridle geometry). Look for "Vector Addition" simulation.

- **Desmos Graphing Calculator** — use to plot friction vs wrap angle relationships (capstan equation), force vs angle relationships. Free at https://www.desmos.com/calculator — good for visualizing lesson 2 and lesson 8 concepts.

- **Tracker Video Analysis Software** — free tool for analyzing motion in videos (https://physlets.org/tracker/). Can be used for lesson 20 (video force estimation). Requires some learning curve but powerful for quantitative movement analysis.

- **Material Property Databases** — MatWeb (https://www.matweb.com/) for looking up tensile strength, elasticity, and other properties of fabrics and hardware materials. Lesson 12-14 applications.

### Academic Papers and Research

- **Circus Sciences Research Network** — interdisciplinary research on circus arts, including biomechanics and injury studies. Some papers accessible at https://circussciences.org/

- **"Injury Incidence in Circus Artists: A Systematic Review"** (Shrier et al., various years) — epidemiological studies of circus injuries, useful context for lessons 19-20, 22.

- **Journal of Textile Science & Engineering** — occasional papers on fabric strength, abrasion resistance, and material degradation. Advanced reading for lesson 12-14.

- **Sports Biomechanics journal** — not circus-specific but methodology transfers. Look for papers on gymnastic apparatus or climbing for similar movement patterns.

## Unexpected Connections

- **Sailing and rope work** — Nautical rigging uses identical physics (capstan equation, line tension, knot strength). Sailing resources often have better-documented force calculations. Book: "The Complete Rigger's Apprentice" by Brion Toss.

- **Rock climbing physics** — Climbing communities have extensively documented fall factors, equipment wear, and anchor geometry. Many concepts transfer directly. Look at "The Physics of Climbing" articles on climbing blogs.

- **Gymnastics biomechanics** — Apparatus work (rings, bars) shares loading patterns with aerial. Research on shoulder mechanics in gymnasts applies to aerialists. Search academic journals for "gymnastics shoulder loading."

- **Structural engineering** — How buildings handle dynamic loads (wind, earthquakes) uses similar principles to aerial rigging dynamic loading. Civil engineering textbooks on dynamic analysis are surprisingly relevant.

- **Industrial rope access** — IRATA and SPRAT certification programs for workers on ropes (building cleaning, inspection) use rigging principles identical to circus. Their training materials are excellent and highly safety-focused.

- **Parachute and paragliding physics** — Load factors, fabric behavior under tension, and dynamic impact forces. Parachute Landing Fall (PLF) technique is similar to aerial drop mechanics. Resources from skydiving communities can provide alternative perspectives on impact reduction.

- **Textile engineering for climbing gear** — Research on rope and webbing degradation from the climbing industry (UV damage, cyclic loading) directly applicable to aerial fabric. Look at research from companies like Sterling Rope or Mammut.

- **Dance and choreography theory** — While not physics-focused, understanding compositional principles helps in sequencing movements with appropriate force progressions. Laban Movement Analysis is a framework used in both dance and circus.

## Apps and Mobile Tools

- **"Knots 3D"** — while circus rigging tries to avoid knots in load paths, knowing knot strength and behavior is still essential. Visual 3D demonstrations of tying and strength impacts.

- **Physics Toolbox Suite** — smartphone sensors (accelerometer, gyroscope) can be used to measure actual forces and accelerations during movement. Advanced but possible to use in lesson 20 for real data collection.

## Historical and Cultural Context

- **"The Ordinary Acrobat" by Duncan Wall** — cultural history of circus, includes discussions of training evolution and safety culture development. Humanizes the technical material.

- **Cirque du Soleil documentary series** — behind-the-scenes looks at rigging and safety protocols in professional shows. "Fire Within" and "Worlds Away" special features show rigging processes.

## Certification Pathways (for those pursuing professional work)

- **ETCP Certified Rigger** — Arena or Theatre Rigger certifications require passing written and practical exams on rigging physics and safety.

- **IRATA Industrial Rope Access** — Levels 1-3 certification for working at height, includes rigging and rescue skills.

- **Aerial Teacher Training Programs** — Various schools offer certifications (not standardized industry-wide). Look for programs that include rigging physics and safety modules.

## Updates and Staying Current

- **ESTA Technical Standards Program Updates** — standards evolve; subscribe to updates at https://tsp.esta.org/

- **Circus Safety Conferences** — annual gatherings where practitioners share injury data, new research, and evolving best practices. Look for American Circus Educators conferences or EYCO (European Youth Circus Organization) events.

- **Equipment Recalls and Safety Notices** — follow major manufacturers (Petzl, Black Diamond, CMI, etc.) for recall announcements and safety bulletins.
