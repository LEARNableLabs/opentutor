# Research Summary — Circus Arts: Aerial Silks Physics and Rigging

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Force Mechanics and Load Distribution
- Static vs. dynamic loading on apparatus
- Tensile forces in fabric under different body positions
- Load multiplication factors during drops and dynamic movements
- Center of mass and equilibrium in aerial positions

### 2. Rigging Engineering
- Safe working load (SWL) and breaking strength
- Hardware specifications: carabiners, swivels, shackles, spans
- Rigging point geometry and angle dependencies
- Bridle systems and load sharing
- Inspection protocols and wear patterns

### 3. Fabric Material Science
- Fabric types: nylon vs. polyester properties
- Stretch characteristics and elastic deformation
- Friction coefficients and grip mechanics
- UV degradation and environmental factors
- Fabric width effects on pressure distribution

### 4. Biomechanics of Aerial Movement
- Joint loading during climbs, inversions, and drops
- Muscular force requirements for static holds
- Impact forces during catches and dynamic sequences
- Rotational mechanics in spins and wraps
- Proprioception and spatial awareness in 3D space

### 5. Safety Systems and Risk Assessment
- Redundancy in rigging systems
- Fall arrest and backup systems
- Mat placement and fall zone calculations
- Pre-flight checks and equipment retirement criteria
- Industry standards (ETCP, ESTA, circus-specific guidelines)

## Gaps in Literature

- Limited peer-reviewed research specifically on aerial silks physics
- Most knowledge is practitioner-transmitted or in circus school curricula
- Few openly-accessible rigging calculators specific to fabric apparatus
- Standardization still evolving (unlike established theatrical rigging)
- Biomechanics research is sparse compared to traditional sports
