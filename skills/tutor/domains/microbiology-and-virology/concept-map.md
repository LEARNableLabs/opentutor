# Microbiology and Virology — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

### Foundational Microbiology

1. **Microbial diversity** — the three domains (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya) and size scale
2. **Prokaryotic cell structure** — cell walls, membranes, organelles. Depends on: microbial diversity
3. **Peptidoglycan** — bacterial cell wall structure and Gram staining. Depends on: prokaryotic cell structure
4. **Bacterial motility** — flagella, chemotaxis, pili. Depends on: prokaryotic cell structure
5. **Archaeal adaptations** — unique membrane lipids and extremophile strategies. Depends on: prokaryotic cell structure
6. **Microbial metabolism** — chemotrophy, phototrophy, electron donors/acceptors. Depends on: prokaryotic cell structure
7. **Aerobic vs anaerobic respiration** — oxygen use and toxicity. Depends on: microbial metabolism
8. **Bacterial growth dynamics** — growth curves, phases, population dynamics. Depends on: microbial metabolism
9. **Biofilms** — quorum sensing, extracellular matrix. Depends on: bacterial growth dynamics
10. **Bacterial DNA replication** — circular chromosomes, bidirectional replication. Depends on: prokaryotic cell structure
11. **Operon regulation** — lac and trp operons, gene expression control. Depends on: bacterial DNA replication
12. **Horizontal gene transfer** — transformation, conjugation, transduction. Depends on: bacterial DNA replication
13. **Plasmids** — extrachromosomal DNA, antibiotic resistance. Depends on: horizontal gene transfer

### Virology Fundamentals

14. **Viral nature** — obligate intracellular parasites, living vs non-living debate. Depends on: microbial diversity
15. **Viral structure** — capsid, envelope, genome types. Depends on: viral nature
16. **Baltimore classification** — seven classes based on genome and replication strategy. Depends on: viral structure
17. **Envelope acquisition** — budding vs direct assembly. Depends on: viral structure
18. **Viral entry** — attachment, receptor binding, endocytosis. Depends on: viral structure
19. **Lytic vs lysogenic cycles** — productive infection vs integration. Depends on: viral entry, bacterial DNA replication
20. **Retroviral replication** — reverse transcriptase, integration. Depends on: viral entry, Baltimore classification
21. **RNA virus evolution** — high mutation rates, quasi-species. Depends on: retroviral replication
22. **Bacteriophages** — phage diversity, therapy, role in HGT. Depends on: lytic vs lysogenic cycles, horizontal gene transfer

### Host-Microbe Interactions

23. **Pathogenesis** — virulence factors, toxins, Koch's postulates. Depends on: microbial metabolism, bacterial growth dynamics
24. **Microbiome and symbiosis** — immune tolerance, beneficial relationships. Depends on: pathogenesis, biofilms
25. **Viral immune evasion** — antigenic variation, latency. Depends on: retroviral replication, RNA virus evolution
26. **Zoonotic transmission** — species barriers, spillover events. Depends on: viral entry, viral immune evasion
27. **Vaccination** — adaptive immunity, memory cells, vaccine types. Depends on: pathogenesis, viral immune evasion

### Applications

28. **Microbial biotechnology** — recombinant DNA, industrial applications. Depends on: plasmids, operon regulation
29. **Metagenomics** — 16S sequencing, virome analysis. Depends on: microbial diversity, bacteriophages

## Critical Dependencies

### Microbiology → Virology
- **Operon regulation** provides the conceptual foundation for understanding viral gene expression strategies
- **Horizontal gene transfer (transduction)** requires understanding both bacterial genetics and bacteriophage biology
- **Bacterial growth curves** establish the framework for understanding viral replication kinetics
- **Cell structure** knowledge is essential for understanding viral entry mechanisms and envelope acquisition

### Within Virology
- **Baltimore classification** organizes all subsequent viral concepts — it's the taxonomic backbone
- **Lytic vs lysogenic cycles** must precede any discussion of prophages, gene transfer, or viral persistence
- **Envelope acquisition** determines viral stability, transmission, and disinfection strategies
- **RNA virus evolution** explains vaccine challenges, drug resistance, and emergence of new pathogens

### Integration Points
- **Bacteriophages** bridge microbiology and virology — they transfer bacterial genes (HGT) and demonstrate viral principles
- **Biofilms and microbiome** connect individual microbial biology to community-level phenomena
- **Pathogenesis and immune evasion** require integration of microbial/viral biology with host immunology
- **Metagenomics** synthesizes taxonomy, evolution, and ecology

## Prerequisite Topics (External)

- **Cell biology** — needed for: membrane structure, endocytosis, cellular organization
- **Molecular biology** — needed for: DNA replication, transcription, translation, central dogma
- **Basic chemistry** — needed for: metabolism, pH, redox reactions
- **Organic chemistry (helpful)** — needed for: metabolic pathways, lipid structure
- **Immunology basics (helpful)** — needed for: host-microbe interactions, vaccines

## Common Bottlenecks

1. **Operon regulation** — abstract gene control mechanisms; students often struggle with negative vs positive regulation
2. **Baltimore classification** — requires mental juggling of genome type, replication strategy, and mRNA production
3. **Horizontal gene transfer** — three different mechanisms to distinguish, plus understanding their evolutionary implications
4. **Retroviral replication** — reverses the central dogma, challenges students' mental models
5. **Antigenic variation** — requires understanding both viral genetics and immunology

## Conceptual Milestones

- **After Lesson 8** — students should see microbes as diverse, adaptable organisms (not just disease agents)
- **After Lesson 13** — students should understand bacterial genetics and regulation at a mechanistic level
- **After Lesson 17** — students should be able to classify viruses and predict replication strategies
- **After Lesson 22** — students should integrate bacteriology and virology through the lens of phages
- **After Lesson 27** — students should connect molecular mechanisms to public health applications
- **After Lesson 30** — students should think systemically about microbes in ecosystems, health, and technology
