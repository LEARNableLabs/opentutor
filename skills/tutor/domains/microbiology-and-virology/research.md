# Microbiology and Virology — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### Core Microbiology
1. **Microbial Cell Structure** — prokaryotes vs eukaryotes, bacterial cell walls, membranes, cytoplasmic organization
2. **Metabolism & Growth** — energy production, biosynthesis, environmental factors, growth curves
3. **Genetics & Molecular Biology** — DNA replication, transcription, translation, gene regulation, horizontal gene transfer
4. **Microbial Diversity** — bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists, classification systems
5. **Host-Microbe Interactions** — normal microbiota, pathogenesis, immune evasion, symbiosis
6. **Applied Microbiology** — industrial applications, environmental microbiology, biotechnology

### Virology
1. **Viral Structure & Classification** — capsids, envelopes, genome types (DNA/RNA, ss/ds), Baltimore classification
2. **Viral Replication Cycles** — attachment, entry, replication, assembly, release; lytic vs lysogenic
3. **Host Range & Specificity** — receptor binding, tissue tropism, species barriers
4. **Viral Evolution** — mutation rates, recombination, reassortment, emergence of new viruses
5. **Virus-Host Interactions** — immune responses, persistence, latency, oncogenic viruses
6. **Antiviral Strategies** — vaccines, therapeutics, resistance mechanisms

### Integration Topics
- Bacteriophages and horizontal gene transfer
- Viral metagenomics and the virome
- Emerging infectious diseases
- One Health perspective

## Pedagogical Notes

**Intermediate level assumptions:**
- Students have completed introductory biology (cell biology, molecular biology basics)
- Familiar with chemistry fundamentals (organic chemistry helpful but not required)
- Can read scientific figures and interpret experimental data
- Ready for mechanistic explanations beyond descriptive overview

**Recommended approach:**
- Start with bacterial cell biology as foundation, then build to metabolism and genetics
- Introduce virology after establishing microbial fundamentals (shared molecular mechanisms)
- Use comparative approach: bacteria vs archaea, prokaryotes vs eukaryotes, DNA vs RNA viruses
- Emphasize molecular mechanisms and experimental evidence over memorization
- Connect to real-world applications: disease, biotechnology, environment
- Include historical context for major discoveries (Koch's postulates, Hershey-Chase, HIV discovery)

**Difficulty progression:**
- Begin with structural biology (concrete, visual)
- Build to metabolism (quantitative, pathway-oriented)
- Peak with genetics/regulation (abstract, multi-level control)
- Virology integrates prior concepts with new principles
- Advanced topics: evolution, metagenomics, systems-level thinking
