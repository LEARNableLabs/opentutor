# Particle Physics — Research Summary

## Major Subtopics

### 1. Foundations & Symmetries
- Symmetry principles in physics (Noether's theorem)
- Conservation laws and their quantum origins
- Poincaré and internal symmetries
- Group theory basics (U(1), SU(2), SU(3))

### 2. Quantum Field Theory Framework
- From quantum mechanics to QFT
- Fields as fundamental objects
- Creation/annihilation operators
- Feynman diagrams and perturbation theory

### 3. Gauge Theories
- Local gauge invariance
- Gauge bosons as force carriers
- Covariant derivatives and minimal coupling
- Spontaneous symmetry breaking (Higgs mechanism)

### 4. Particle Classifications
- Fermions vs bosons (spin-statistics)
- Leptons and lepton families
- Quarks and color charge
- Fundamental forces and their mediators

### 5. Electroweak Unification
- Weak interactions and parity violation
- W and Z bosons
- Electroweak mixing
- The Higgs field and mass generation

### 6. Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)
- Color charge and confinement
- Gluons and the strong force
- Asymptotic freedom
- Hadron structure

### 7. Experimental Verification & Beyond
- Particle accelerators and detectors
- Discovery timeline and evidence
- Neutrino oscillations and mass
- Open questions (hierarchy problem, dark matter, matter-antimatter asymmetry)

## Research Gaps Identified

For intermediate-level students:
- Bridge between pop-science explanations and full mathematical formalism
- Visual intuition for abstract concepts (gauge invariance, spontaneous symmetry breaking)
- Connection between Lagrangian formalism and physical predictions
- Experimental evidence linked to theoretical predictions (not just "trust the math")

## Recommended Lesson Structure

Start with symmetry principles → build QFT intuition → introduce gauge principle → classify particles → unify forces → explore experimental verification → discuss open questions.

Emphasize:
- Why symmetries dictate particle properties
- How conservation laws emerge from field theory
- Physical meaning of mathematical structures (not just formalism)
- Real experimental evidence and discovery timeline
- Connection to ongoing research (LHC results, neutrino physics)
