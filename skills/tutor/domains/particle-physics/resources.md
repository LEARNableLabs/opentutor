# Particle Physics — Resources

## Primary Sources

### Textbooks

- **Griffiths, "Introduction to Elementary Particles" (2nd ed.)** — The standard intermediate-level textbook. Clear explanations, minimal field theory prerequisites, accessible mathematics. Perfect for this level. Covers symmetries, conservation laws, Feynman diagrams, QED, electroweak theory, and QCD at appropriate depth.

- **Perkins, "Introduction to High Energy Physics" (4th ed.)** — Experimental emphasis with good coverage of detection methods and experimental results. Complements Griffiths' theoretical focus. Includes historical development and discovery stories.

- **Halzen & Martin, "Quarks and Leptons"** — More advanced than Griffiths, but excellent on Feynman rules and QCD phenomenology. Good for students wanting more calculation practice.

- **Particle Data Group, "Review of Particle Physics"** (updated annually) — Comprehensive reference for particle properties, interaction cross-sections, and review articles on every Standard Model topic. Free online: https://pdg.lbl.gov/

### University Courses

- **MIT 8.701 (Introduction to Nuclear and Particle Physics)** — OpenCourseWare with full lecture notes, problem sets, and exams. Taught at undergraduate/early graduate level. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-701-introduction-to-nuclear-and-particle-physics-fall-2020/

- **MIT 8.324 (Relativistic Quantum Field Theory II)** — More advanced, covers renormalization and advanced QFT topics. For students wanting deeper mathematical treatment. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-324-relativistic-quantum-field-theory-ii-fall-2010/

- **Leonard Susskind's Theoretical Minimum (Particle Physics)** — Video lecture series on YouTube. Very accessible, conceptual approach with minimal prerequisites. Good for building intuition before diving into formalism. https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL5F7D3DD710D57FC4

- **CERN Summer Student Lecture Programme** — Expert lectures on all Standard Model topics. Recorded and freely available. Mix of theoretical and experimental perspectives. https://cds.cern.ch/collection/Summer%20Student%20Lectures

### Review Articles

- **arXiv:hep-ph/0001283** — "The Standard Model Higgs Boson" by J.F. Gunion. Detailed review of Higgs mechanism and phenomenology.

- **arXiv:1506.05957** — "From Gauge Invariance to Charge Conservation" by O'Raifeartaigh and Straumann. Historical and conceptual development of gauge theories.

- **arXiv:1110.2253** — "The Higgs Boson and Electroweak Symmetry Breaking" review. Pre-discovery, excellent pedagogy.

- **PDG reviews** — The Particle Data Group publishes authoritative review articles on every major topic (QED, QCD, electroweak theory, neutrinos, etc.). https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/reviews/contents_sports.html

## Supplementary Resources

### Videos & Lectures

- **PBS Space Time** (YouTube channel) — High-quality pop-science explanations of Standard Model concepts. Good for conceptual overview before diving into math. Topics: Higgs field, quarks, gauge bosons, symmetry breaking. https://www.youtube.com/c/pbsspacetime

- **3Blue1Brown** — While focused on math, his group theory and linear algebra videos build essential intuition for gauge groups and representations. https://www.youtube.com/c/3blue1brown

- **Fermilab** (YouTube channel) — "Dr. Don Lincoln" explains particle physics concepts clearly. Topics on Higgs, quarks, antimatter, symmetries. https://www.youtube.com/user/fermilab

- **ATLAS and CMS experiment channels** — Real footage of LHC experiments, Higgs discovery announcement, detector construction. Makes the physics tangible. 
  - ATLAS: https://www.youtube.com/user/ATLASExperiment
  - CMS: https://www.youtube.com/user/CMSExperimentCERN

- **Sixty Symbols** (Nottingham University) — Physics professors explain concepts on camera. Informal, accessible, good for alternative explanations. https://www.youtube.com/user/sixtysymbols

### Interactive Tools & Visualizations

- **CERN's "Particle Adventure"** — Interactive journey through the Standard Model. Click-through lessons on particles, forces, and experiments. https://particleadventure.org/

- **Particle Data Group website** — Interactive particle property tables, Feynman diagram tools, decay mode searches. Essential reference. https://pdg.lbl.gov/

- **LHC@Home** — Visualize real particle collision data from CERN experiments. Citizen science project. https://lhcathome.web.cern.ch/

- **ATLAS Event Displays** — Browse real collision events from the ATLAS detector, color-coded by particle type. See Higgs candidates, top quark pairs, etc. https://atlas.cern/resources/event-displays

- **PhET Interactive Simulations** — Quantum mechanics and atomic physics sims (not particle physics directly, but foundational). https://phet.colorado.edu/

- **Feynman Diagram Generator** — Online tools to draw and compute simple Feynman diagrams. Helps build intuition for allowed processes. Multiple tools available (search "Feynman diagram calculator").

### Code & Computational Tools

- **PYTHIA** — Monte Carlo event generator for particle collisions. Industry standard for simulating LHC physics. Can run simulations locally. https://pythia.org/

- **MadGraph** — Automated calculation of cross-sections and event generation from Standard Model processes. https://launchpad.net/mg5amcnlo

- **ROOT** — Data analysis framework used by CERN experiments. Can explore real LHC data. Steep learning curve but powerful. https://root.cern/

- **Jupyter notebooks for particle physics** — Various educational notebooks demonstrating calculations, Feynman diagrams, and data analysis. Search GitHub for "particle physics jupyter" or CERN Open Data portal.

### Unexpected Connections

- **Condensed matter physics** — Superconductivity and the Meissner effect are directly analogous to the Higgs mechanism. BCS theory (1957) inspired Higgs (1964). Explore connections via "Anderson-Higgs mechanism."

- **Cosmology** — The early universe was a plasma of Standard Model particles. Baryogenesis (matter-antimatter asymmetry), Big Bang nucleosynthesis, and dark matter all connect particle physics to cosmology. See Kolb & Turner, "The Early Universe."

- **Quantum computing** — Simulating quantum field theories is a target application for quantum computers. Gauge theories are hard to simulate classically (sign problem). Follow developments in "quantum simulation of lattice gauge theories."

- **Mathematical physics** — Gauge theories connect to fiber bundles, differential geometry, and topology. Yang-Mills theory is a central object in pure math (millennium problem). See Nakahara, "Geometry, Topology and Physics."

- **Philosophy of science** — What does it mean for a theory to be "fundamental"? The Standard Model is an effective field theory. Are we finding "truth" or just better approximations? See "The Particle at the End of the Universe" by Carroll for accessible discussion.

- **Nuclear physics** — The strong force binds quarks into hadrons (particle physics) and hadrons into nuclei (nuclear physics). The transition from QCD to nuclear forces is an active research area. Explore "QCD and nuclear physics."

## Critical Datasets

- **Particle Data Group tables** — Definitive compilation of particle properties (masses, lifetimes, decay modes, cross-sections). Updated annually. Every particle physicist's reference. https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/tables/contents_tables.html

- **Higgs discovery papers** — ATLAS (arXiv:1207.7214) and CMS (arXiv:1207.7235) simultaneous announcements July 2012. Historic papers showing discovery at 5-sigma significance.

- **CERN Open Data** — TeV-scale collision data from LHC. Educational datasets suitable for learning analysis techniques. http://opendata.cern.ch/

## Learning Paths

### For visual learners
Start with CERN's Particle Adventure → PBS Space Time videos → Griffiths textbook → ATLAS event displays → Feynman diagram practice

### For mathematical learners
Start with Griffiths textbook → PDG reviews → MIT OCW problem sets → arXiv review articles → computational tools (MadGraph)

### For experimental learners
Start with Perkins textbook → CERN experiment pages → CERN Open Data → detector descriptions → Symmetry Magazine for context

### For conceptual learners
Start with Susskind lectures → Sean Carroll's Higgs book → PBS Space Time → Griffiths textbook → philosophy of science connections
