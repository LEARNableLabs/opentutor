# Competitive Rock Climbing: Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types

- **Footage-based prediction (core exercise)**: the student watches an IFSC clip, writes predictions (sequence, rests, crux, setter intent), then compares with what the athlete did. Lesson 12 predictions are saved as an answer key and reused in lesson 20.
- **Move naming and explanation**: label holds, footwork and body positions in a clip (lessons 5-9).
- **Pause-and-predict**: pause a clip before a move and predict the next one (review lessons).
- **Hand calculation**: fit the critical-force line F = CF + W'/t, equivalently F·t = CF·t + W', from 3 data points using slope and intercept. Use the pre-filled data set. Never ask for maximal hangs.
- **Scoring problems**: compute boulder and lead results from tops, zones and attempts.
- **Programme critique and design**: judge a sample training week against the principles taught. Use Track A (hangboard) or Track B (climbing-based loading) as appropriate.
- **Scenario and referral decisions**: decide whether a situation is a train-through, modify or refer case.
- **Teach-back**: explain a concept in the student's own words.

## Exercise Format Preference

**mixed**. Multiple choice for scoring rules, vocabulary and format facts. Free text (socratic) for route reading, setter intent, physiology reasoning and training design. Fall back to multiple choice for a struggling student.

## Resource Types

- Official IFSC footage and rulebook (primary for formats and route reading).
- Peer-reviewed reviews and papers for physiology (Giles 2006, Mermier 2000, Saul 2019, the critical-force literature, the 2023 IOC REDs statement).
- Practitioner sources (Lattice, Hörst) clearly labelled as practitioner knowledge.
- Wikipedia glossary as supplementary only.
- A graphing tool for the critical-force lesson.

## Difficulty Curve

- M1-M2 are low difficulty (2-3): vocabulary and formats. Footage skills build here.
- M3 rises to 4: pacing and setter intent are judgement-heavy, with the extended lessons 13-15.
- M4 is the steepest part. Lesson 19 (difficulty 5) is the hardest, because it needs algebra and the concept of W' together. Lesson 18 must include a worked example before the student attempts lesson 19.
- Lesson 22 is a gate review. Do not advance past it if the student cannot explain the critical-force model and the finger-health principles.
- Reviews fall at 9, 15, 20, 22, 27, 34 and 38, never more than 7 lessons apart. Consolidate there.

## Domain Hooks

- Watching a final and seeing the route from the setter's side.
- Predicting a result before the athlete moves, then checking it.
- A 3-point line on paper that separates sustainable effort from not.
- Why the same boulder suits one body and not another, and what that means for setting.
- The pump explained as a physiological event rather than a vague feeling.
- How the format and scoring rules change what a good strategy looks like.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Single-factor explanations** ('strong fingers decide everything'). Taught in lesson 16 only. Revisit through the reviews rather than re-teaching it elsewhere.
- **Confusing CF with maximal strength**: CF is a sustainable force and W' is a finite reserve. Use the worked example in lesson 18.
- **Algebra slips** in lesson 19: swapping slope and intercept, or mixing the F-t and impulse forms. Check units.
- **Treating the format as fixed**: IFSC rules change by season. Check the current rulebook.
- **Presenting practitioner claims as proven**. Label them.
- **Training advice drifting into medical or nutrition advice.** Stay in scope: give no diagnosis, no rehab prescription and no nutrition prescription. Refer to a qualified professional.
- **Adolescents or new trainers copying hangboard protocols.** Direct them to Track B and to hangboard clearance.
- **Any drift towards body-weight or body-composition targets.** This is a hard rule. Do not set, praise or imply one, even when asked. Redirect to the REDs and welfare material and to a qualified professional.

## Vocabulary

- **Use freely**: boulder, lead, top, zone, attempt, hold, crimp, sloper, pinch, pocket, volume, heel hook, toe hook, flag, dyno, crux, beta, isolation, on-sight.
- **Define carefully**: critical force (CF), W', impulse, occlusion, oxygenation, intermittent contraction, setter intent, observation period, coordination move, REDs, low energy availability, Track A and Track B.
- **Avoid or use with caution**: 'tendon strength' claims, 'ideal weight', 'lean', 'cutting', and any phrase that frames body mass as a performance lever. Use 'diagnose' and 'prescribe' only to say these are out of scope.
