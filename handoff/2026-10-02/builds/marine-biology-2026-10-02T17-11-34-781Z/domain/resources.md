## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Herring, *The Biology of the Deep Ocean* (Oxford University Press, 2002)**: Readable survey of zonation, light, bioluminescence, pressure and food supply. Best single book for Modules 1-3.
- **Van Dover, *The Ecology of Deep-Sea Hydrothermal Vents* (Princeton University Press, 2000)**: Core reference for Module 4 (vent chemistry, symbiosis, succession, dispersal).
- **Gage & Tyler, *Deep-Sea Biology: A Natural History of Organisms at the Deep-Sea Floor* (Cambridge University Press, 1991)**: Classic text on abyssal plains, sediment communities and deep-sea diversity (Module 5). Older, so check its numbers against newer sources.
- **Smithsonian Ocean Portal, deep-sea pages**: https://ocean.si.edu. Accessible, curated overviews with specimen photos.
- **NOAA Ocean Exploration**: https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov. Expedition logs, educational materials and the Okeanos Explorer archive. Good for 'where does the evidence come from' lessons.
- **MBARI (Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute)**: https://www.mbari.org. Midwater ecology, marine snow, whale falls and ROV research.
- **Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution**: https://www.whoi.edu. Vent discovery history, Alvin and deep-submergence science.
- **Census of Marine Life**: https://www.coml.org. Decade-long global biodiversity survey; useful for the 'what we know and don't know' framing.

### Key papers (real, citable)

- Orr, J.C. et al. (2005), 'Anthropogenic ocean acidification over the twenty-first century and its impact on calcifying organisms', *Nature*. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04095. Use in the climate-stressor lesson.
- Levin, S.A. (1992), 'The Problem of Pattern and Scale in Ecology', *Ecology*. https://doi.org/10.2307/1941447. Optional framing for the biodiversity lesson.
- Corliss, J.B. et al. (1979), 'Submarine thermal springs on the Galápagos Rift', *Science*. The 1977 vent discovery.
- Cavanaugh, C.M. et al. (1981), 'Prokaryotic cells in the hydrothermal vent tube worm *Riftia pachyptila*', *Science*. The first evidence of chemosynthetic symbiosis.
- Yancey, P.H. et al. (2014), 'Marine fish may be biochemically constrained from inhabiting the deepest ocean depths', *PNAS*. Source for the TMAO/piezolyte and fish depth-limit lessons.
- Jamieson, A.J. et al. (2017), 'Bioaccumulation of persistent organic pollutants in the deepest ocean fauna', *Nature Ecology & Evolution*. Pollutants in hadal amphipods.
- Haddock, S.H.D., Moline, M.A. & Case, J.F. (2010), 'Bioluminescence in the sea', *Annual Review of Marine Science*. The standard review for the bioluminescence lesson.

### Lesson 1: Where does the 'deep sea' actually begin, and why is it the largest habitat on Earth?
- ▶ [The otherworldly creatures in the ocean's deepest depths - Lidia Lins (TED-Ed)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U69LIr0OrNc) — It starts from the fact that about 60% of the ocean is cold and dark, so the deep sea stops being a strange corner of the planet and becomes most of it.
- ▶ [Ocean DEPTH Comparison (3D Animation) - MetaBallStudios](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q5C7sqVe2Vg) — A 3D drop from the surface to the hadal trenches, with skyscrapers and mountains for scale, shows how thin the sunlit layer is compared with everything below it.
- 📄 [Depth zones of the ocean (World Ocean Review)](https://worldoceanreview.com/en/depth-zones-of-the-ocean/) — It explains the Greek roots of each zone's name, so 'abyssopelagic' (bottomless) and 'hadopelagic' (underworld) become easy to remember.
- 📄 [Ocean Twilight Zone (WHOI Know Your Ocean)](https://www.whoi.edu/know-your-ocean/ocean-topics/ocean-life/ocean-twilight-zone/) — Woods Hole scientists explain why the 200–1,000 m layer just below the boundary may hold more fish than the rest of the ocean combined.

### Lesson 2: How quickly does sunlight die as you go down, and what does 'dark' mean at 1,000 m?
- ▶ [How does Ocean depth impact colour? (Ocean Generation)](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LCPD1FJcbdA) — In under a minute you see red, orange and yellow drop out of the spectrum one by one until only blue is left.
- 📄 [How far does light travel in the ocean? (NOAA Ocean Exploration)](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/facts/light-distributed.html) — Light can be detected down to 1,000 m but is almost useless below 200 m, and that gap is the twilight zone this lesson is about.
- 📄 [Why are so many deep-sea animals red? (NOAA Ocean Exploration)](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/facts/red-color.html) — Red animals are invisible at depth because no red light reaches them to reflect, which makes light absorption a survival strategy.
- ▶ [Ocean Twilight Zone: the Great Layer of Life (WHOI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GcZempOu7pk) — A quick WHOI short arguing that the dim zone where sunlight fades may be where most of Earth's animals live.

### Lesson 3: What would 400 atmospheres do to your body, and why doesn't it crush a jellyfish?
- ▶ [Deep sea physics: Water pressure and the incredible shrinking head (MBARI)](https://youtube.com/watch?pp=0gcJCfwAo7VqN5tD&v=ud7w4nHgS0E) — MBARI sends a foam head down on a robot sub and brings back a shrunken one, which shows clearly that pressure crushes gas spaces and leaves water alone.
- 📄 [How does pressure change with ocean depth? (NOAA Ocean Service)](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/pressure.html) — It gives the 1 atm per 10 m rule, then shows sperm whales getting around it with collapsible lungs and flexible ribs.
- 📄 [The incredible reason deep-sea animals don't implode under crushing ocean pressure (Discover Wildlife)](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/marine-animals/why-deep-sea-animals-dont-implode) — It answers the jellyfish question directly: no air pockets, cell membranes that stay runny like olive oil, and bodies made of jelly instead of swim bladders.
- 📄 [Sea creatures' fishy scent protects them from deep-sea high pressures (Science News Explores)](https://snexplores.org/?p=189679) — The 'fishy' smell comes from TMAO, a molecule that keeps proteins working under pressure and may explain why no fish has been found below about 8,400 m.

### Lesson 4: Why is there oxygen at 4,000 m but almost none at 600 m?
- ▶ [The Thermohaline Circulation (NASA)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1uuqvu--6Yg) — NASA's animation follows cold, dense water sinking near Greenland and spreading along the seafloor, which is how oxygen from the surface gets to 4,000 m.
- 📄 [What is the global ocean conveyor belt? (NOAA Ocean Service)](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/conveyor.html) — A parcel of water can take about 1,000 years to go around the conveyor, so the deep water carries oxygen it picked up at the surface centuries ago.
- 📄 [Breathing underwater: MBARI device measures the respiration of deep-sea animals](https://www.mbari.org/?p=86211) — Monterey Bay's oxygen minimum zone near 700 m is growing, and scientists are watching shrimp move upward to escape it.
- ▶ [Exploring an Invisible World in the Ocean | Microbes in Oxygen Minimum Zones (Schmidt Ocean Institute)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ui3c9pC6P7s) — An expedition video about the microbes that use up mid-depth oxygen as they break down sinking food, and that keep living after the oxygen runs out.

### Lesson 5: Review: How do we know any of this when so little of the deep seafloor has been seen?

- 📄 [Humans still haven't seen 99.999% of the deep seafloor (NPR via WLRN)](https://www.wlrn.org/npr-breaking-news/2025-05-07/humans-still-havent-seen-99-999-of-the-deep-seafloor) — Researchers tallied about 43,000 dives using ROVs, crewed subs and landers since 1958, and nearly all of them were near just five countries.
- ▶ [Dive Deeper: Alvin Takes You There (WHOI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NrahF3opykM) — You ride along in Alvin, the crewed submersible behind many famous deep-sea discoveries, which makes the comparison between people and robots easy to picture.
- 📄 [Deep Sea Collaboration: ROV Hercules and AUV Sentry Team Up in American Samoa (Ocean Exploration Trust)](https://nautiluslive.org/blog/2024/09/03/deep-sea-collaboration-rov-hercules-and-auv-sentry-team-american-samoa) — A real expedition where a free-swimming AUV maps the seafloor so a tethered ROV knows where to go and look.

### Lesson 6: Why do most animals in the open deep ocean make their own light?
- ▶ [The weird, wonderful world of bioluminescence - Edith Widder (TED-Ed)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lKeDBpkrDUA) — Edith Widder, one of the first people to film deep-sea light shows, shows animals using light to hunt, lure prey and set off 'burglar alarms'.
- 📄 [New study shows that three quarters of deep-sea animals make their own light (MBARI)](https://www.mbari.org/?p=86796) — After counting 350,000 animals in ROV footage, scientists found that 97–99.7% of jellies glow but only about half of fish do.
- 📑 [Quantification of bioluminescence from the surface to the deep sea demonstrates its predominance as an ecological trait (Martini & Haddock 2017, Scientific Reports)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5379559/) — The open-access paper behind the 76% figure has readable depth-by-depth charts, so you can see the evidence yourself.

### Lesson 7: How do you hide where there is nowhere to hide, and why does a barreleye have a see-through head?
- ▶ [Macropinna microstoma: A deep-sea fish with a transparent head and tubular eyes (MBARI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RM9o4VnfHJU) — The scientists who solved the barreleye puzzle show how its glowing green eyes swivel inside a clear shield, so it can look up for shadows and then forward at its food.
- 📄 [Octopuses and squids can switch camouflage mode to stay invisible in the twilight zone](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/phenomena/2011/11/10/octopuses-and-squids-can-switch-camouflage-mode-to-stay-invisible-in-the-twilight-zone/) — Some squid switch from glass-clear to dark red in moments when a searchlight hits them, which shows the trade-off between transparency and pigment better than any diagram.
- 📄 [Ultra-black skin allows some fish to lurk unseen](https://today.duke.edu/2020/07/ultra-black-skin-allows-some-fish-lurk-unseen) — Sixteen fish species have skin that absorbs more than 99.5% of light, and engineers want to copy it for solar panels and telescopes.
- 📄 [How does depth affect the color of marine animals? (NOAA Ocean Exploration)](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/facts/animal-color.html) — Red light never reaches the deep, so the reddest animals down there are the ones you can't see.

### Lesson 8: What stops a fish's proteins from being crushed at 7,000 m, and why can't fish reach the deepest trenches?
- ▶ [Snailfish breaks record, becomes deepest fish ever filmed and captured](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IXZ-ftJGDCg) — A small, almost cute snailfish swimming calmly at 8,336 m, right at the edge of the predicted limit for fish, is a good reason to ask why nothing goes deeper.
- 📄 [How deep can fish go? (The Naked Scientists)](https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/170978) — The molecule that makes fish smell fishy is the one that protects their proteins at depth, and it also puts a hard floor on where fish can live.
- 📑 [Marine fish may be biochemically constrained from inhabiting the deepest ocean depths (Yancey et al., PNAS 2014)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3970477) — This is the open-access paper behind the 8,200 m figure, and the TMAO-versus-depth graph alone shows you how the limit was predicted.
- 📑 [On the Success of the Hadal Snailfishes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7671157/) — A readable review of why soft, scaleless, jelly-like snailfish took over the trenches, from TMAO to pressure-tolerant enzymes and membranes.

### Lesson 10: How do you find a mate when you may meet another of your species once a year?

### Lesson 12: If no sunlight reaches the seafloor, where does dinner come from?

### Lesson 13: How does the ocean move carbon from the sky to the seafloor?

### Lesson 29: Review: Pick a habitat (vent, whale fall, seamount, trench or abyssal plain) and build its energy budget
- ▶ [Deep-sea blizzard: Today's forecast calls for flurries of marine snow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RAO-dfLXszI) — The slow fall of 'snow' you watch here is the main food supply for the abyssal plain and much of the deep sea, so it is the first line of almost every energy budget.
- 📄 [What is a whale fall? (NOAA Ocean Service)](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/whale-fall.html) — One carcass can drop decades' worth of food in a single day and feed scavengers, then worms, then sulfide-loving microbes, which makes it the best contrast to slow marine snow.
- ▶ [Return to the Davidson Seamount Whale Fall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8e0Mw_25ojg) — A whale fall on a seamount puts two habitats in one shot, so you can ask which energy source is feeding which animals.
- 📄 [Marine Snow: The Ocean's Endless Snowfall Explained](https://oceana.org/blog/marine-snow-the-oceans-endless-snowfall-explained/) — A quick primer on what the flakes are made of and how a surface bloom can bring years of food to the seafloor in a few weeks, the numbers you need for a budget.

### Lesson 31: What does a bottom trawl leave behind on a seamount?
