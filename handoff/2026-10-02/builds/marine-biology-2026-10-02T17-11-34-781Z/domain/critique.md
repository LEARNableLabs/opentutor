## Overall
This is a strong, coherent plan. The environment → constraints → solutions → energy → places → people arc is sound. Misconceptions are handled well, and the uncertainty framing is good. The difficulty curve is mostly honest. I found no blocking defects, but there are several fixable issues.

## Coverage
- **Exit criteria mismatch.** The plan's draft exit criteria say 'at least five adaptations'. The JSON says 'at least four'. Pick one. The JSON lists six adaptation lessons, so five is achievable.
- **Missing content in lessons.** Several promised items have no home in the lesson concepts. These are the Cavanaugh 1981 symbiosis evidence, Kleiber's law (only listed as a rabbit hole), the 'dark oxygen' debate (named in the teaching notes for lesson 30 but absent from lesson 30's concepts), and Taq polymerase and biotech (a Taq lesson reference is also unassigned, 'Lesson 19 or 30').
- **Thin treatment.** Sound, pressure and temperature as sensory constraints are absent. Mechanosensing and the lateral line are not mentioned. Gelatinous zooplankton and midwater ecology (siphonophores, jellies) get almost no dedicated coverage. Deep-sea fisheries beyond trawling, and cable and oil/gas impacts, are not covered. Treat these as minor.

## Sequencing
- **Lesson 19 is difficulty 5 with a single lesson.** It carries redox, the electron donors and acceptors, chemoautotrophy and the indirect dependence on oxygen. The plan itself says 'may split'. Splitting it in advance, or moving the 'indirect dependence on surface O2' piece to lesson 24, would be safer. I'd recommend adding a short redox primer or a worked analogy exercise in lesson 18.
- **Lesson 8 depth-limit claim.** It states an '8,200–8,400 m fish depth limit' as fact in the concepts. The teaching notes say to present it as a hypothesis. The lesson 8 concept wording should say 'proposed limit'.
- **Lesson 5 is an exploration review before the content it reviews.** The plan says this module ends with a review that also covers exploration methods. In practice that is a new topic (exploration) taught inside a review lesson at difficulty 2. Reviews should consolidate, not introduce. Either make it a mini-lesson or move exploration earlier in the module.
- **Lesson 15 (whale falls) before chemosynthesis.** It is justified as a bridge, but its sulfophilic stage is explained before students have met chemosynthesis. State explicitly that it is a preview, and revisit it in lesson 24.

## Breadth vs Depth
- Module 4 has 7 lessons, but seeps, brine pools and AOM are packed into one lesson (21), and AOM is hard. Lessons 22 and 23 are also dense.
- Module 6 puts mining, trawling, climate, pollution, governance and the capstone in 6 lessons, which is workable but tight for a structured debate.

## Resources
- **Heavy Wikipedia reliance.** Most lessons list only Wikipedia pages. Many lessons (13, 14, 16, 17, 21, 22, 25, 31 and others) have no primary or institutional source. The plan's own strategy prioritises MBARI, NOAA, SOI, WHOI and the Deep-Sea Guide, and the lessons do not follow it.
- **Citation mismatch.** The plan lists Smith & Baco 2003 (whale falls), Martini & Haddock 2017, Danovaro 2008, and Jamieson 2019. In JSON, lesson 33 cites doi 10.1098/rsos.180667, a Royal Society Open Science DOI. The plan says Jamieson et al. 2019 and the resource list says Jamieson et al. 2017 (Nature Ecology & Evolution). Verify the DOI and the year, and reconcile 2017 and 2019. Lesson 15 has no Smith & Baco citation, and lesson 18's Corliss DOI should be verified.
- **Possible wrong DOI use.** Lesson 28 uses 10.1016/j.cub.2007.11.056 for Danovaro et al., with the Levin 1992 DOI 10.2307/1941447. Verify both. Levin 1992 is also a weak fit for a student reading, so keep it optional.
- **Lesson 1 and lesson 12 cite Ramirez-Llodra et al. 2010 (a dense review)** for an intermediate student. Mark it as abstract-only, as the plan says.
- **Check URLs.** Resources should be link-checked, as the plan states. Pages such as the Wikipedia 'High Seas Treaty' title should be confirmed to exist under that name.
- There is little video resource linking in the lessons themselves, despite 'real footage is the data'. Specific MBARI/NOAA video URLs should be assigned for lessons 6, 7, 14, 15 and 20.

## Exercises
- Strategy is good and domain-appropriate. However, no exercises are attached per lesson in the JSON, and the lesson types (mini-lesson, question, real-world, resource-drop) do not map to the stated exercise types. The structured debate, design-a-creature, and the profile-interpretation exercises need to be assigned to specific lessons.
- Lesson 10 is a resource-drop on mate-finding but is marked difficulty 3 with mostly Wikipedia material. This is fine, but check that there is an exercise.
- The Orr et al. 2005 paper is in lesson 32, as the plan says.

## Bias
- Module 6 is value-laden. The mining debate lesson 30 should present industry and battery-supply arguments (land-mining impacts, the energy transition) fairly. As written, the concepts lean toward harm ('sediment plumes and loss of habitat'). Add a land-mining comparison and uncertainty.
- The plan relies heavily on US and Western institutions (MBARI, NOAA, WHOI). Consider adding non-Western sources such as JAMSTEC, as the Izu-Ogasawara snailfish and Japanese expeditions are relevant to lessons 8 and 27.
- The 'dark oxygen' item should be taught as disputed, as the notes say, and lesson 30's concepts should include it.

## Pedagogy
- The plan says every lesson ends with 'Where does this organism's energy ultimately come from?'. This is a strong thread. Lessons on non-organism topics (4, 5, 34) will need adapted wording.
- The 'one back-of-envelope estimate per lesson' rule is good, but there are no specified estimates for many lessons.
- The 3–5 minute lesson length is very short for the difficulty 4–5 lessons (19, 20, 21). Allow a longer slot or the split.
- The 7-week pacing claim ('in line with the 1% growth rule') is unclear and should be dropped or explained.

## teacher.md
- The teaching notes are clearly domain-specific, with named misconceptions, verification flags and level adjustments, so teacher.md should not be generic. It should carry the misconception list, the 'facts to handle with care' list and the constraint-first move. Note the one conflict: the notes say Corliss 1979 and Danovaro 2008 are stretch reading, while lesson 18 uses Corliss as a main resource. Reconcile.

## Verdict
No major structural problem. The fixes are mainly about reconciling mismatches and tightening resources. I recommend one light revision pass, but it is not blocking.
