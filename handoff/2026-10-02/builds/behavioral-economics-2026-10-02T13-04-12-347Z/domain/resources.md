# Behavioral Economics — Resources

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Erik Angner, *A Course in Behavioral Economics* (3rd ed., Palgrave)** — main textbook spine; intermediate level, with exercises.
- **Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011)** — heuristics, biases, prospect theory, System 1/System 2, written by a field founder.
- **Richard Thaler, *Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics* (2015)** — intellectual history of the field, mental accounting, endowment effect.
- **Thaler & Sunstein, *Nudge: The Final Edition* (2021)** — choice architecture, defaults, libertarian paternalism.
- **Tversky & Kahneman (1974), "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases," *Science* 185** — anchoring, availability, representativeness. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124
- **Kahneman & Tversky (1979), "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk," *Econometrica* 47** — value function, loss aversion, probability weighting. https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185
- **Tversky & Kahneman (1981), "The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice," *Science* 211** — Asian disease problem. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.7455683
- **Thaler (1999), "Mental Accounting Matters," *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making* 12** — mental accounting.
- **Laibson (1997), "Golden Eggs and Hyperbolic Discounting," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 112** — beta-delta present bias. https://doi.org/10.1162/003355397555253
- **Fehr & Gächter (2000), "Cooperation and Punishment in Public Goods Experiments," *American Economic Review* 90** — costly punishment.
- **Fehr & Schmidt (1999), "A Theory of Fairness, Competition, and Cooperation," *QJE* 114** — inequity-aversion utility. https://doi.org/10.1162/003355399556151
- **Gneezy & Rustichini (2000), "A Fine Is a Price," *Journal of Legal Studies* 29** — incentives crowding out motivation.
- **Madrian & Shea (2001), "The Power of Suggestion: Inertia in 401(k) Participation and Savings Behavior," *QJE* 116** — defaults. https://doi.org/10.1162/003355301753265543
- **Thaler & Benartzi (2004), "Save More Tomorrow," *Journal of Political Economy* 112** — commitment and present bias in policy.
- **Simon (1955), "A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice," *QJE* 69** — bounded rationality.
- **DellaVigna & Malmendier (2006), "Paying Not to Go to the Gym," *American Economic Review* 96** — field evidence of overestimated future self-control.
- **Mertens et al. (2022), "The effectiveness of nudging: A meta-analysis of choice architecture interventions across behavioral domains," *PNAS* 119** — and the critical reply by Maier et al. (2022), "No evidence for nudging after adjusting for publication bias," *PNAS* 119. Use together for the replication/effect-size lesson.
- **DellaVigna & Linos (2022), "RCTs to Scale: Comprehensive Evidence from Two Nudge Units," *Econometrica* 90** — nudge effects in the field are smaller than in academic papers.

## Supplementary (for engagement)

- **Videos** — Dan Ariely's and Kahneman's talks on TED (ted.com) for hooks; Veritasium and Vox explainers on loss aversion and anchoring; Yale/Stanford open lectures on behavioral economics when available on YouTube.
- **Courses** — MIT OpenCourseWare and Coursera behavioral-economics offerings (search ocw.mit.edu and coursera.org for current courses).
- **Interactive tools** — Run the classic experiments on the student: Linda problem, Asian disease, Allais choices, ultimatum game. Gorilla.sc and jsPsych (jspsych.org) host simple experiment demos.
- **Code** — Massimiliano Pozzi, python_julia_structural_behavioral_economics: https://github.com/MassimilianoPozzi/python_julia_structural_behavioral_economics (optional, beyond course scope); econ-ai-guide/resources: https://github.com/econ-ai-guide/resources
- **Websites** — Behavioral Economics Guide (behavioraleconomics.com); Behavioural Insights Team (bi.team) for applied nudge case studies.
- **People** — Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, Richard Thaler, Herbert Simon, David Laibson, Ernst Fehr, Uri Gneezy, Stefano DellaVigna, Cass Sunstein, Alvin Roth.
- **Unexpected connections** — cab drivers' daily income targets (Camerer et al. 1997), organ-donation defaults across countries (Johnson & Goldstein 2003, *Science*), and the equity premium puzzle (Benartzi & Thaler 1995 on myopic loss aversion).

## Caution Notes

- Some well-known studies are contested: ego depletion, social priming, and several findings in Ariely's *Predictably Irrational* (including a retracted 2012 honesty paper). Use them as case studies in evidence quality, not as settled facts.
- Verify any citation before presenting it as fact; references in this list are to real, widely cited works, but check page and volume details against the source.
