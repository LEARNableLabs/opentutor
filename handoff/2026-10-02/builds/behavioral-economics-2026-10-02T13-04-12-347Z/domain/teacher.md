# Behavioral Economics — Domain Teaching Config

## Exercise Types
- **Predict-then-reveal:** the student answers a classic experimental question (Asian disease, Linda, Allais choices, $100 today vs $110 tomorrow) before the effect is named. Highest-value format.
- **Worked numeric problems:** expected value, prospect-theory value with loss aversion (lambda about 2), beta-delta discounting, Fehr-Schmidt utility. Small numbers, no calculus.
- **Diagnose-the-case:** given a real-world scenario (gym contract, 401(k) default, teaser rate), name the bias or preference at work and the rational-model prediction it violates.
- **Evidence-critique:** judge a study's design: lab vs field, sample, effect size, replication status.
- **Debate / ethics:** nudge vs sludge, libertarian paternalism, when a default is manipulative.
- **Design-a-nudge:** propose an intervention and say how it would be tested.

## Exercise Format Preference
**mixed** — multiple choice for recognizing effects and for predict-then-reveal setups; free text for explaining mechanism, applying to cases, and critiquing evidence. The student model may shift toward MC for a struggling student.

## Resource Types
- Short excerpts of primary papers (Tversky-Kahneman, Kahneman-Tversky, Laibson, Madrian-Shea) rather than whole papers.
- Textbook chapters (Angner) as the spine; popular books (Kahneman, Thaler) for narrative.
- Short videos and talks for hooks; interactive experiment demos where the student is the subject.
- Field-study summaries and meta-analyses for the skepticism thread.

## Difficulty Curve
- Module 1 sets the benchmark; it is easy but essential. Students who skip owning expected utility, exponential discounting and self-interest later cannot say what a bias violates.
- Heuristics (module 2) are easy to grasp and fast to pay off.
- The steepest part is prospect theory and probability weighting (module 3), then beta-delta discounting (module 4), where new formal tools appear. Consolidate with reviews after each.
- Social preferences (module 5) are conceptually easy but the interpretation of experiments is subtle.
- Policy and finance (modules 6-7) are applications that integrate everything; the capstone is the hardest synthesis.

## Domain Hooks
- Students catch themselves making the classic errors; this is the strongest hook.
- Everyday life: procrastination, gym memberships, subscriptions, saving, tipping, sunk costs.
- Real policy impact: 401(k) defaults, organ donation, Save More Tomorrow.
- Intellectual drama: Kahneman and Tversky's collaboration, a psychologist winning the economics Nobel, and the field's own replication crisis and fraud cases.
- Firms exploit biases (shrouded fees, teaser rates); students learn to spot it.

## Common Failure Modes
- **"Humans are just irrational":** students treat biases as random stupidity. Correct: deviations are systematic and predictable, which is why they can be modeled.
- **Over-generalizing a finding:** treating every famous effect as robust. Teach which are robust (loss aversion in many contexts, defaults, present bias) and which are shaky (ego depletion, priming).
- **Confusing similar concepts:** loss aversion vs risk aversion; present bias vs impatience; framing vs anchoring; endowment effect vs status quo bias; naive vs sophisticated agents.
- **Missing the benchmark:** describing a bias without stating the rational prediction it violates.
- **Treating the value function as a fact of nature:** reference points are context-dependent and often hard to identify.
- **Nudge naivety:** assuming nudges are always effective, cheap and benign; ignore effect-size shrinkage and ethics.
- **Lab-to-field leaps:** assuming dictator-game generosity predicts real-world behavior.

## Vocabulary
- **Use freely:** bias, heuristic, framing, anchor, default, nudge, incentive, utility, risk aversion, reference point, sunk cost.
- **Define carefully on first use:** loss aversion, prospect theory, endowment effect, present bias, hyperbolic discounting, beta-delta, mental accounting, inequity aversion, reciprocity, choice architecture, sludge, bounded rationality, System 1 / System 2, libertarian paternalism, limits to arbitrage, disposition effect.
- **Mention only:** level-k, QRE, cumulative prospect theory axioms, structural estimation terms.
- **Avoid:** calling people "stupid" or "irrational" without qualification; "proven" for any single study; econometric jargon beyond effect size, sample and replication.
