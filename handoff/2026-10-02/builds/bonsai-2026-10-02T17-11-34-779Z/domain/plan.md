## Scope
**In scope:**
- Applied tree physiology for container growers: water transport, energy budget, apical dominance, roots and mycorrhizae, CODIT, dormancy.
- Substrate science and watering, nutrition, light, microclimate and winter protection.
- Core techniques: pruning, pinching and defoliation, wiring, trunk thickening, deadwood, grafting and air-layering.
- Six species groups: elm/zelkova, juniper, maple, pine, ficus and other tropicals, azalea and flowering/fruiting trees.
- Pests, disease, IPM and chemical safety.
- Biosecurity and ethical, legal sourcing.
- Design principles, styles, display, and traditions: Japanese bonsai, Chinese penjing, Vietnamese hòn non bộ, Korean bunjae, Western naturalistic.

**Out of scope:**
- Suiseki connoisseurship (mentioned only in display).
- Competitive exhibition judging.
- Commercial production.
- Advanced plant molecular biology.
- Ethics of supply chains beyond legality and biosecurity, as the Critic advised.

**Student and topic level:** both intermediate. The student has general biology and some gardening exposure but no formal horticulture.

## Rationale for the order
- **Physiology first.** M2 and M3 teach mechanisms before any technique, so every technique in M4 can be justified by a mechanism.
- **Gate at L20.** The gate sits at the end of M3 and covers all of M2 and M3. L20 gates M4: L21 is not delivered until it is passed.
- **Species after technique.** Species lessons come after the techniques because each profile is the general techniques with species-specific timing.
- **Aesthetics after craft.** Aesthetics comes once the student can actually carry out a design. L3 gives early vocabulary so that M4 has words for what it shapes.
- **Health and sourcing late.** These come late so that biosecurity and IPM land when the student is ready to buy and treat trees.
- **Capstone last.** It synthesizes everything.

## Gate definition (single source of truth: plan, teaching-notes.md and L20 JSON must match verbatim)
**Format.** Five scenario cards, each with a photo description, a care history and a season. The student diagnoses each one.

**Scoring.** Each scenario is scored 0–3, one point per criterion:
- (a) correct symptom reading;
- (b) names the underlying mechanism;
- (c) prescribes a correct action with correct timing.

The maximum score is 15.

**Pass.** At least 10 out of 15 AND no core scenario scored 0.

**Core scenarios:**
- **A. Root rot.** A juniper in a dense organic mix, watered daily: yellowing and browning from the inside, sour-smelling roots. Mechanism: air-filled porosity, root hypoxia and rot (L9, L13, L15). The yellowing is a deliberate red herring for chlorosis (L16).
- **B. Post-repot collapse.** A maple that was hard-root-pruned and heavily top-pruned in the same spring, then wilts in early summer. Mechanism: energy budget and root:shoot imbalance (L6, L9, L17).
- **C. Bare interior.** A tree with a bare interior and all its growth at the branch tips after years of letting the tips run. Mechanism: apical dominance and back-budding failure, made worse by shade (L7, L18).

**Secondary scenarios:**
- **D. Wound dieback.** A large flush cut with a raised, sunken dead strip extending below it, plus rot. Mechanism: CODIT (L10).
- **E. Overwintered indoors.** A deciduous maple overwintered in a heated living room, with weak, leggy, late spring growth. Mechanism: dormancy, chilling requirement and winter protection (L11, L19).

**L20 JSON concepts (exact):** ["air-filled porosity and root rot", "energy budget after repotting", "apical dominance and back-budding", "CODIT wound response", "dormancy and chilling requirement"].

**Fail handling:**
- Write BLOCK on M4.
- Write REVISIT on the mechanism of every scenario scored 1 or lower.
- Teach the targeted re-lesson, then retake with fresh scenarios testing the same five mechanisms.
- At most two retakes. After that, the teacher re-sequences the relevant M2 or M3 lessons.

## Pedagogical decisions
- **Mechanism-first.** Every technique lesson opens with the physiological reason it works and cites the L-number where that mechanism was taught.
- **Hemisphere-anchored timing.** L1 intake records hemisphere, climate zone, indoor/outdoor space, and the trees the student owns. All timing is then given relative to the tree's phenology (bud swell, hardening off, leaf drop) plus the local month, never as fixed calendar months.
- **Out-of-season lessons.** If a lesson's technique is out of season for the student, the teacher teaches it as planning ('do this at bud swell, around <local month>') and never encourages out-of-season work on a live tree.
- **Safety is content.** Tool safety (L4), lime sulfur (L26) and chemical handling (L46) are taught, not footnoted.
- **Misconception schedule.** Each misconception is introduced, then revisited. Revisit lessons list it in JSON concepts as 'misconception: <text>' so the teacher knows to test it.
  - 'bonsai are a dwarf species': L1 → L29, L48
  - 'heavy pruning makes a tree stronger': L6 → L21, L25
  - 'a small pot keeps the tree small': L9 → L17
  - 'trees heal their wounds' and 'wound sealant stops rot': L10 → L26
  - 'indoor bonsai thrive on a windowsill': L2 → L18, L34
  - 'water on a fixed schedule': L15 → L20 scenario A
  - 'bring deciduous trees indoors to protect them in winter': L11 → L19, L31
- **Consolidation spacing.**
  - Never more than 4 consecutive lessons at difficulty 3 or above.
  - Never more than 7 lessons between consolidation points. The consolidation points are L8, L12, L20, L24, L28, L32, L36, L42, L43, L49 and L50.
  - Before the difficulty-5 capstone (L51), the student has passed through difficulty-4 lessons at L10, L20, L26, L27, L33, L43 and L50. L50 is a direct planning scaffold for it.
- **Bias.** Penjing (L40) gets its own lesson with Chinese-practitioner sources. Saikei is a Japanese tradition and does not count toward non-Japanese coverage. L41 must say plainly: 'English-language sources on Korean bunjae are thin; treat this section as an introduction, not an authority.'

## Resource strategy
**Rules (the builder must enforce all of them):**
1. Every lesson has at least 2 resources, and at least one must be a non-Wikipedia source from the assignment table.
2. Wikipedia is for definitions only and is never the sole resource.
3. Drop tangential definition pages (Perched water table, Field capacity, Microclimate, Chlorosis) unless they are paired with a bonsai-specific or extension source on the same point.
4. Generic Grafting and Layering pages are replaced in L27 by bonsai-specific sources.
5. **Bonsai Mirai pairing rule.** A Bonsai Mirai link never appears alone. It is always paired, in the same lesson, with a written source on the same technique (a Bonsai Tonight post or a book chapter), because some Mirai content is paywalled.
6. Books are cited via an Open Library or publisher URL in the JSON. resources.md gives the chapter or section.
7. The builder checks that every URL resolves before writing it.

## Exercise strategy
**Default format: mixed.**
- Multiple choice for vocabulary and identification: L1–L4, L14, L37, L44.
- Socratic free text everywhere else.
- Diagnosis scenarios use the same 0–3 rubric as the gate: symptom, mechanism, action and timing.

**Exercise types:**
- Scenario diagnosis (M2, M3, M7)
- Timing decisions ('your tree is at stage X; what do you do now and what waits?')
- Design critique from photo descriptions (M6)
- Teach-backs: L28, L36, L43, L50, L51

**L36 species calendar template.** A table, one row per month, with month labels adjusted to the student's hemisphere. Columns: Month | Growth phase | Watering | Feeding | Pruning/pinching | Wiring | Repotting | Winter/heat protection | Pest watch. The student fills one calendar per species they own, or for elm plus one other species if they own none.

**Model answers.** Every scenario and teach-back has a model answer in teaching-notes.md.

## Pacing
- 51 lessons, about 3–5 minutes each. At 5 per week this takes about 10–11 weeks.
- Difficulty rises gently through M1, has a first hump at M2/M3 that ends in the d4 gate, plateaus at 3–4 through M4 and M5, eases in M6 and M7, then ramps to d5 in the capstone.
- Live-tree work is planned, never forced. If the student's season does not fit a technique, it is planned and practiced on paper or on cheap nursery stock.
