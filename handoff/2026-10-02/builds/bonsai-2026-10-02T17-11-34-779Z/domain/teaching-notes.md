# Bonsai — Teaching Notes

## Approach

Teach every technique as applied physiology. A rule (water when the soil surface dries, wire at 45 degrees, prune after flowering) should always arrive with the mechanism behind it (transpiration, the energy budget, CODIT, flower-bud set). This topic is hands-on and visual. Use photos and the student's own tree wherever possible, and ask the student to observe something real (soil moisture, bud swell, leaf colour) before they reason about it. Aesthetics come after health. A beautiful tree that is dying is not a bonsai.

## Gate definition (single source of truth — L20)

GATE: diagnose 5 written cases (overwatering/root rot, underwatering, indoor light starvation, chlorosis from high pH, winter root damage); for each give the cause, the M2 mechanism and an M3 fix; pass = cause and mechanism correct in at least 4 of 5 cases and no prescription that would kill the tree; fail = reteach the mapped lessons before M4

What to reteach for each missed case:
- Overwatering/root rot → L9, L13, L15
- Underwatering → L5, L15
- Indoor light starvation → L2, L6, L18
- Chlorosis from high pH → L16
- Winter root damage → L11, L19

Write each case with the species, the season, the symptoms and a short care history. Mix the species: at least one deciduous tree, one conifer and one tropical.

## Species calendar template (L36)

The student fills in one row per month with these columns: growth stage (dormant, bud swell, flush, hardening, autumn, leaf drop) | watering notes | feeding (none, light or full) | allowed techniques (repot, prune, pinch, wire, defoliate, decandle) | protection needs (shade, wind, frost) | what to watch for (pests, disease). They then adjust the month boundaries for their own climate zone and cite one source for each species-specific claim.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **Bonsai are a dwarf species** — the word on the label misleads beginners. Show a nursery juniper next to a styled juniper of the same species (L1).
2. **Bonsai are houseplants** — gift-shop marketing suggests they are. Explain that temperate trees need outdoor light and a cold dormancy, and only tropicals tolerate indoor life (L2, L34).
3. **Water on a schedule** — fixed schedules feel safe. Ask the student to predict how evaporation differs in July and November (L15).
4. **Trees heal like skin** — damaged tissue is walled off, never replaced. Use CODIT (L10, L12).
5. **Ordinary potting soil is fine** — peat-heavy mixes hold too much water in shallow pots. Use the perched-water demonstration with a sponge (L13).
6. **More work means faster progress** — stacking stresses drains the tree's energy reserves. Apply one major stress per year (L24, L28).
7. **Pinch juniper tips** — it was traditional advice, but it browns foliage. Cut back with scissors instead (L30).
8. **Bonsai is only Japanese** — penjing came first. Cover the traditions in L40–L41 and revisit in L42.

## Level Adjustments

The student is intermediate: general biology, some gardening, no formal horticulture. Use correct terms (xylem, auxin, CEC, PPFD) but define each one the first time it appears. Leave out molecular signalling detail. Use numbers only where they change decisions: wire gauge as a fraction of branch thickness, rough minimum temperatures for tropicals, and the fact that PPFD indoors is far lower than outdoors. For a struggling student, use multiple choice for identification questions and keep free-text answers for diagnosis and planning.

## Chemical and tool safety

L4 (tools), L26 (lime sulfur: gloves, eye protection, ventilation, keep it off live tissue) and L46 (IPM) must each include explicit safety steps. Always tell the student to follow the product label and local regulations. Never recommend a specific pesticide dose.

## Source notes

- L41: English-language sources on hòn non bộ and bunjae are thin. Say so plainly, and present claims about those traditions with appropriate hedging.
- L47: cite Lovett et al. 2016 (https://doi.org/10.1890/15-1176) for the general impact of nonnative forest pests in the US and how they arrive with live plants. Back the specific Anoplophora chinensis bonsai-import story with a separate source, such as the Wikipedia article and the plant-health agency references it cites.
- L38: the MC-curves paper (Ziatdinov et al. 2013) is an optional bonus on the geometry of aesthetic curves. It is not evidence about bonsai design.
- Before giving species-specific timings, check them against more than one source. Timings vary with climate.
