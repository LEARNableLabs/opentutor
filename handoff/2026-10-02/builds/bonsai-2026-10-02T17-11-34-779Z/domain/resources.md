# Bonsai: Resources

Note: Per-lesson primary sources are assigned in the curriculum plan table (curriculum.json mirrors it). This file lists the pool they draw from. Books are cited by title and author only. Check any title and edition before you buy.

## Primary Sources (for lesson content)

- **Taiz & Zeiger, _Plant Physiology and Development_ (Sinauer/Oxford)**: the standard plant physiology text. Companion site: https://www.plantphys.net/. Use it for M2: water transport, carbohydrate budget, auxin and apical dominance, dormancy.
- **Shigo, _A New Tree Biology_ (Shigo and Trees, Associates)**: the original CODIT work, for L10 and wound and deadwood decisions.
- **Shigo & Marx, _Compartmentalization of Decay in Trees_, USDA Forest Service Agriculture Information Bulletin 405 (1977)**: the short primary-source version of CODIT.
- **Brady & Weil, _The Nature and Properties of Soils_**: porosity, particle size, cation exchange and pH, for L13, L14 and L16. Perched water in shallow pots is also covered in container-horticulture literature.
- **Lovett, Weiss, Liebhold et al. (2016), "Nonnative forest insects and pathogens in the United States: Impacts and policy options," _Ecological Applications_**: the primary source for L47 (biosecurity). https://doi.org/10.1890/15-1176
- **USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map**: https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/. Use it for L11 and L19 (hardiness and winter protection). Students outside the US should find their national equivalent.
- **US EPA, Safe Pest Control**: https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol. Use it for L44 (IPM and chemical safety). The product label is the legal authority.

## Bonsai Books (technique, design, species)

- **John Naka, _Bonsai Techniques I_ and _II_**: classic technique and design reasoning.
- **David De Groot, _Principles of Bonsai Design_**: design principles for L3 and the design lessons.
- **Deborah Koreshoff, _Bonsai: Its Art, Science, History and Philosophy_**: a broad overview that includes some plant science.
- **Michael Hagedorn, _Bonsai Heresy_**: a science-minded challenge to bonsai folklore. It fits the misconception revisits.
- **Harry Tomlinson, _The Complete Book of Bonsai_**: a general reference with good photographs.

## Collections and Museums (traditions and display)

- **US National Arboretum, National Bonsai & Penjing Museum**: https://www.usna.usda.gov/. Japanese, Chinese and American collections. Good for the traditions lessons (L40, L41).

## Supplementary (for engagement)

- **Videos**: YouTube channels from the people above (Crataegus Bonsai, Bonsai Mirai, Walter Pall's channel, Bonsai Empire). Always paired with a written primary source for any factual claim.
- **Tools**: the USDA zone map for the winter lessons, a basic PAR or lux phone-meter app for L18, and a kitchen scale and sieve for L13 and L14 particle-size exercises.
- **People to look up**: Alex Shigo (tree biology), John Naka, Masahiko Kimura, Walter Pall, Michael Hagedorn, Ryan Neil.
- **Unexpected connections**: Vietnamese hòn non bộ, Korean bunjae and Chinese penjing as parallel traditions; fractal branching and phyllotaxis; the mathematics of curve aesthetics; the biosecurity history of plant trade.

## Lesson by lesson

Found by web search; every link was checked.

### Lesson 1: What turns an ordinary tree into a bonsai?
- ▶ [What is Bonsai? (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=neb5UGYMM9M) — It comes from the biggest bonsai teaching site, and it shows that the 'tiny tree' is made by pruning, potting and patience, not by its seed.
- ▶ [What Makes Bonsai So Mysterious? (History of Simple Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D6ENasnXyr0) — It tells the history of how a Chinese and Japanese art of potted trees became a worldwide obsession, which shows bonsai as an art form and not a plant product.
- 📄 [Why Are Bonsai Trees So Small?](https://www.scienceabc.com/nature/why-are-bonsai-trees-so-small) — It says plainly that bonsai are 'ordinary trees and shrubs, not hereditary dwarfs', and that the same tree in open ground could grow 15 to 30 m tall.
- 🕹 [USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (interactive)](https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/) — Type in your ZIP code to find your own climate zone, which is the first fact you need before choosing a tree that can live outside where you are.

### Lesson 2: Why do most first bonsai die indoors?
- ▶ [Indoor Bonsai 'All You Need To Know' (Herons Bonsai)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDxtzNixs1Q) — Peter Chan, a nurseryman with decades of experience, sorts the few species that cope indoors from the many that don't, and shows you round his own indoor collection.
- 📄 [What actually kills an outdoor tree when it is kept indoors? (Bonsai Mirai forum)](https://forum.bonsaimirai.com/t/what-actually-kills-an-outdoor-tree-when-it-is-kept-indoors/8251) — Practitioners explain the slow death step by step: indoor light is too dim for photosynthesis, the tree misses its dormancy, and it uses up its reserves until it fails, often by the third season.
- 📄 [Indoor Bonsai (Bonsai4me)](https://bonsai4me.com/indoor-bonsai/) — It explains plainly that every bonsai is an outdoor tree, and that a temperate tree denied its winter dormancy can grow itself to death.
- 📄 [Indoor Light Reality: Why Plants Need More Light Than You Think](https://www.plantlovers.eu/blogs/care-and-maintenance/indoor-light-reality-why-plants-need-more-light-than-you-think-especially-in-winter) — Its lux figures are a shock: a bright windowsill gives a small fraction of outdoor sun, and the light falls off sharply only a metre or two back into the room.

### Lesson 3: How do you read a bonsai from roots to apex?
- ▶ [Bonsai Design Principles for Beginners (Bonsai Heirloom)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q16C2FMPWLg) — It walks through the guidelines that make a tree look old and convincing, which gives you a checklist to use on any bonsai you see.
- ▶ [How to create a Nebari (surface roots) by Michael Hagedorn (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RiKdolsY4ww) — A well-known American bonsai artist shows why a strong spread of surface roots is the first thing an experienced eye looks at, and how one is built.
- 📄 [How to create a good Bonsai trunk (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.bonsaiempire.com/basics/styling/trunk) — Once you know about taper and reverse taper, you will start spotting trunks that look like telephone poles everywhere.
- 📄 [Bonsai pots (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.bonsaiempire.com/basics/choosing-a-bonsai-pot) — It opens with the line 'a tree is a tree, and a pot is only a pot' and shows how pot shape and colour can echo the bark, leaves or flowers.

### Lesson 4: Which tools do you actually need, and how do you use them without hurting yourself or the tree?
- ▶ [Bonsai Tools - All You Need To Know (Herons Bonsai)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=00uz-wCZPdo) — A veteran nurseryman sorts the tools you really need from the expensive extras that beginners buy too early.
- ▶ [Bonsai Tools Basics: Concave Branch Cutter VS Knob Cutter (Wazakura Japan)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qfx_0LHm5UY) — A side-by-side demonstration makes clear why one cutter leaves a flat hollow and the other a deep scoop, and when each wound heals better.
- 📄 [Tips for using basic bonsai tools (Bonsai Tonight)](https://bonsaitonight.com/2016/12/23/tips-using-basic-bonsai-tools/) — A working professional gives small, specific tips you won't find in a catalogue, such as cutting thick wire in the notch and not at the tips, and using the blunt end of the concave cutter for big branches.
- 📄 [Garden Myth Busters: Using Bleach to Disinfect Tools](https://mgnv.org/garden-myth-busters/garden-myth-busters-using-bleach-to-disinfect-tools/) — It tests the standard advice to dip tools in bleach and finds it rusts your expensive steel, which makes cleaning your tools a question of evidence and not habit.

### Lesson 5: How does water climb from root to leaf without a pump?
- ▶ [How Trees Bend the Laws of Physics (Veritasium)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BickMFHAZR0) — A suction pump can't lift water more than about 10 m, yet trees lift it over 100 m, and Veritasium shows how negative pressure makes that possible.
- ▶ [Transpiration and water transport in trees: the cohesion-tension theory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UHmw02DDB3A) — A focused walk-through that follows the chain from stomata to xylem to roots, which is the lesson's main sequence.
- 📄 [Cohesion-Tension Theory (LibreTexts General Botany)](https://bio.libretexts.org/Courses/Norco_College/BIO_5%3A_General_Botany_(Friedrich_Finnern)/12%3A_Transport/12.06%3A_Cohesion-Tension_Theory) — A free, open textbook page you can come back to whenever you need to know why a tree in a dry bonsai pot wilts within hours.
- 📑 [Passive water ascent in a tall, scalable synthetic tree](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6959229/) — Engineers built an artificial tree that lifts water with no pump at all, which is striking proof that the physics in the videos really works.

### Lesson 6: Where does a tree's energy come from, and where does it go?
- ▶ [Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration: Crash Course Botany #5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WzOrF5W4l3Q) — Crash Course presents photosynthesis and respiration as income and spending, which is the energy-budget idea this lesson is built on.
- 📄 [Distinguishing vigorous growth from healthy growth (Bonsai Tonight)](https://bonsaitonight.com/2016/12/20/distinguishing-vigorous-growth-healthy-growth-bonsai/) — Side-by-side photos teach you to read shoot length, leaf size and bud size to judge whether a tree has the energy for wiring or heavy pruning.
- 📄 [How much to prune?](https://gardenlore.substack.com/p/how-much-to-prune) — It explains why the arborists' rule of 'no more than 25 to 30 percent a year' exists: every cut removes stored carbohydrate as well as future leaf area.

### Lesson 7: Why does the top of a tree grow fastest?
- ▶ [Bonsai techniques: Backbudding in Bonsai by Heike van Gunst](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ourS8-9W6fM) — You watch a bonsai professional make a tree grow new buds on old wood, which shows that the tree's vigour can be moved around on purpose.
- ▶ [Bonsai techniques: Backbudding on Pine Bonsai by Koji Hiramatsu](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mBM4pLF_dIQ) — Pines are known for refusing to back-bud, so seeing a Japanese professional get around that shows how stubborn apical dominance can be.
- 📄 [Maintenance Pruning part two: Pinching Out Deciduous Trees (Bonsai4me)](https://bonsai4me.com/?p=2422) — Its diagrams circle the exact buds that wake up once you pinch the tip, so the auxin idea becomes something you can do with your fingers.
- 📑 [A Growing Stem Inhibits Bud Outgrowth – The Overlooked Theory of Apical Dominance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5671643/) — This open-access paper questions the simple textbook story that 'auxin from the tip suppresses buds', so you see that the science is still being argued.

### Lesson 8: Can you explain a wilting, weak, top-heavy tree using water, energy and hormones?
- ▶ [How Trees Bend the Laws of Physics (Veritasium)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BickMFHAZR0) — Trees pull water up through very strong negative pressure, and once you see how fragile that water column is, a wilting tree makes sense.
- 📄 [Distinguishing vigorous growth from healthy growth (Bonsai Tonight)](https://bonsaitonight.com/2016/12/20/distinguishing-vigorous-growth-healthy-growth-bonsai/) — It explains why a tree with good colour can still be too weak to work on, which is the energy-budget idea seen from the bench.
- 📄 [Pruning Myths (Linda Chalker-Scott handout)](https://gardenshow.com/docs/librariesprovider108/2020-speaker-handouts/linda-chalker-scott-pruning-myths.pdf) — A university horticulturist looks at popular pruning beliefs one by one, which is a good way to test the idea that cutting a tree back makes it stronger right away.

### Lesson 9: What is happening underground where you can't see it?
- ▶ [The secret language of trees – Camille Defrenne and Suzanne Simard (TED-Ed)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V4m9SefyRjg) — A short animation of the fungal network that trades sugar for nutrients under every healthy tree, presented by the researcher behind the 'wood wide web' idea.
- ▶ [Radish germination timelapse (root hair close-up)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e2zVeUPxBU4) — Root hairs are usually invisible, and this time-lapse shows how fast a fuzz of them appears behind the root tip.
- 📄 [Why Does Over-Watering Kill Plants? (ScienceABC)](https://www.scienceabc.com/nature/why-does-over-watering-kill-plants) — Oxygen moves through water about 10,000 times more slowly than through air, which explains how a soaked tree can drown and die of thirst.
- 📄 [All About Akadama (Bonsai Tonight)](https://bonsaitonight.com/2016/07/15/all-about-akadama/) — It shows why bonsai growers pay for gritty baked clay instead of potting soil: the air gaps are the point.

### Lesson 10: Why can't a tree heal its wounds?
- ▶ [How Trees Compartmentalize Decay (CODIT Explained Using a Large Oak)](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Dh4pn2KkLaI) — On a real oak, an arborist shows that the tree walls off rot instead of repairing it, and the short makes that point on its own.
- 📄 [Woods Whys: How Do Trees Heal Wounds on Trunks and Branches? (Northern Woodlands)](https://northernwoodlands.org/articles/article/woods-whys-how-trees-heal) — A clear, short read on the 'seal, don't heal' idea: a chemical reaction zone first, then wound wood that grows over the damage.
- 📑 [A Pictorial Primer for Proper Pruning (Alex Shigo)](https://shigo.davey.com/shigo_publications/2125/publication_file/a-pictorial-primer-for-proper-pruning.pdf) — Drawings by the man who named the branch collar show exactly where to cut and why a flush cut opens the tree to decay.
- 📄 [Pruning Sealants (University of Arizona Extension)](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/attachment/PruningSealants.pdf) — An extension publication on whether you should paint a cut, a debate that bonsai forums still argue about.

### Lesson 11: Why do trees need winter?
- ▶ [Bonsai and Winter Dormancy: Dormancy is not optional](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Ntjxn_Dgw8) — The title makes the point: keeping a temperate bonsai warm indoors to protect it slowly kills it.
- 📄 [Understanding Dormancy and Chilling (University of Minnesota Extension)](https://blog-fruit-vegetable-ipm.extension.umn.edu/2024/11/understanding-dormancy-and-chilling.html) — Trees keep count of cold hours before they will wake, which protects them from a false spring.
- 📄 [Winter care for Bonsai (Bonsai Empire)](https://bonsaiempire.com/basics/bonsai-care/overwintering) — It sorts trees into tropical, deciduous and conifer, and warns that roots in a pot face cold from every side, including underneath.
- 📄 [Fact Sheet FS528 (Rutgers Cooperative Extension)](https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs528/) — An extension fact sheet on keeping container plants alive through winter, where root hardiness is the weak point.

### Lesson 12: Roots, wounds and winter: can you predict what will go wrong?
- ▶ [CODIT & MICROS by Dr. Alex Shigo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o6U-6uTAm-o) — Alex Shigo, the scientist who proved that trees don't heal like skin, explains it himself, which makes this a primary source for the misconception revisit.
- 📑 [Wound Dressings: Results of Studies Over 13 Years (Shigo & Shortle, 1983)](https://auf.isa-arbor.com/content/9/12/317) — 275 wounded trees were cut open over 13 years and no dressing stopped decay; the individual tree mattered more than any product.
- 📄 [Winter Care for Bonsai (Bonsai Tonight)](https://bonsaitonight.com/?p=22880) — It names wind, not cold, as the biggest winter danger, which is a good test of whether you can tie hardiness back to water loss.
- 📄 [Why Is My Bonsai Tree Dying? Here's How to Bring It Back to Life (Bonsai Direct)](https://www.bonsaidirect.co.uk/blog/bonsai-tree-care/why-is-my-bonsai-tree-dying-heres-how-to-bring-it-back-to-life/) — Work from symptoms back to causes such as soggy soil starving roots of oxygen, and check whether you can predict the problem before reading the answer.

### Lesson 13: Why does bonsai soil look like gravel?
- ▶ [Perched Water Table: don't add rocks to your plant pots](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mNTjSLfwRDw) — It shows why a layer of gravel at the bottom of a pot makes waterlogging worse, which most people find hard to believe until they see it.
- ▶ [BONSAI SOIL vs POTTING MIX: Does it make a difference?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LO3J-CDojBo) — It tests the idea that ordinary potting soil is fine for bonsai by growing trees in both, so you can see the answer instead of just being told.
- 📄 [An Introduction to Bonsai Soils (Bonsai4me)](https://bonsai4me.com/an-introduction-to-bonsai-soils/) — It turns 'use a gritty mix' into exact particle sizes (2–3 mm, 4–5 mm, 6–7 mm for pines) and explains why sieving out the dust matters as much as the material you choose.

### Lesson 14: Akadama, pumice, lava, organics: what does each one actually do?
- ▶ [Bonsai substrate, Bjorn Bjorholm](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iCDPvzoNtC4) — Bjorholm spent six years as an apprentice in Japan, and here he explains in plain terms why each part of the mix is there.
- 📄 [To Akadama, or not to Akadama? (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.bonsaiempire.com/blog/akadama-soil) — It looks honestly at whether the expensive Japanese clay is worth it, given that it breaks down within about two years.
- 📄 [Bonsai soil, recommended substrate mixtures (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.bonsaiempire.com/basics/bonsai-care/bonsai-soil) — It gives the role of each ingredient and ready mixes for different kinds of tree, like 50/25/25 for deciduous trees and equal thirds for pines.
- ▶ [Why we don't use Akadama](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sItSk-ywkUk) — A grower makes the case against the 'standard' ingredient, which pushes you to think about what each component actually does.

### Lesson 15: How do you know when to water?
- ▶ [Watering Bonsai trees (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ljc3WDWsVRY) — A short lesson from one of the best-known bonsai teaching channels that shows how to read the tree and the soil instead of following the calendar.
- ▶ [How To Water Your Bonsai In The Summer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1K1V-ecbg6c) — Summer heat is when watering on a fixed schedule kills trees, and this shows how much a tree's needs change with the weather.
- 📄 [Watering Bonsai; how to water your trees (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.bonsaiempire.com/basics/bonsai-care/watering) — It lists everything that changes how fast a pot dries (sun, wind, pot size, soil, season), so 'every Tuesday' falls apart.

### Lesson 16: What does a tree eat?
- ▶ [Understanding Bonsai Fertilizer (N, P, K, Mg, Ca, Fe, S)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-QBjl8ct6_E) — It covers the nutrients beyond the three numbers on the bag, including the magnesium and iron that keep leaves green.
- 📄 [The secret of bonsai fertilisation, the power of NPK (Mistral Bonsai)](https://www.mistralbonsai.com/en/the-secret-of-bonsai-fertilisation-the-power-of-npk/) — It is written by a working bonsai nursery and connects each nutrient to something you can see on the tree, ending with how to fix iron chlorosis.
- 📄 [Soil Basics Part II: Chemical Properties of Soil (UMass Extension)](https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/vegetable/fact-sheets/soil-basics-part-ii-chemical-properties-of-soil) — A clear university explainer of cation-exchange capacity and pH that shows why a soil can hold nutrients or let them wash away.
- 📄 [Iron Chlorosis: Symptoms, Causes and Solutions (Utah State Extension)](https://extension.usu.edu/news_sections/gardening/iron-chlorosis-symptoms-causes-and-solutions) — It explains the puzzle that yellow leaves often grow in soil full of iron, because high pH locks the iron away from the roots.

### Lesson 17: When and how should you repot?
- ▶ [Bonsai Repotting Made Easy (Herons Bonsai)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VAtNcgfaQdM) — Peter Chan, who founded Britain's best-known bonsai nursery, makes a job most beginners dread look simple.
- ▶ [Repotting theory and Bonsai substrate explained (Bjorn Bjorholm)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oju3bwiICgE) — It explains why you repot, including how soil breaks down over time, so timing and root pruning stop feeling like arbitrary rules.
- 📄 [Repotting a Trident Maple: Securing the Tree in the Pot (Bonsai Tonight)](https://bonsaitonight.com/2009/12/18/repotting-a-trident-maple-securing-the-tree-in-the-pot/) — Step-by-step photos of the wiring that keeps a tree from rocking in its pot and tearing the new roots.
- 📄 [Repotting and Root-Pruning Bonsai, Part 1 (Bonsai4me)](https://bonsai4me.com/repotting-and-root-pruning-bonsai-part-1/) — It explains how much root you can safely cut and why the season, judged by bud swell, decides how well the tree recovers.

### Lesson 18: How much sun does a bonsai need, and can grow lights replace it?
- 📄 [Where to place your Bonsai tree (Bonsai Empire)](https://www.bonsaiempire.com/basics/bonsai-care/position) — Its warning that light drops sharply just a few feet from a window explains why so many indoor trees slowly die.
- ▶ [Growing Bonsai Indoors Under LED Light](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BxH1fG4W380) — It shows a real indoor setup that works, which makes 'can grow lights replace the sun?' concrete.
- 📄 [Important Considerations for Providing Supplemental Light to Indoor Plants (Iowa State Extension)](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-indoor-plants-under-supplemental-lights/important-considerations-providing-supplemental-light-indoor-plants) — University guidance that replaces lux and watts with the measures plants actually respond to, so you can compare grow lights on the right numbers.
- 📑 [Lighting Plants Indoors (Michigan State University Floriculture)](https://www.canr.msu.edu/floriculture/uploads/files/Lighting%20plants%20indoors.pdf) — Written by plant-lighting researchers, it explains PPFD and how much light a plant gets over a day, so you can tell how far a windowsill is from full sun.

### Lesson 19: How do you get a tree through winter where you live?
- ▶ [Overwintering Bonsai trees (Bonsai Empire, with Ryan Neil)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hAd-Gw6Gevs) — Ryan Neil goes through it one temperature band at a time, so you can tell exactly when your own tree needs more than a sheltered bench.
- ▶ [Winter Protection of Bonsai (What to when it gets cold) – Growing Bonsai by Jelle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OyHxk51YuB4) — A hobbyist shows his own low-cost setup as the frost arrives, which proves you don't need a greenhouse to keep trees alive.
- 📄 [How do I overwinter my bonsai plant? – Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/gardens-gardening/your-garden/help-for-the-home-gardener/advice-tips-resources/gardening-help-faqs/question/537/how-do-i-overwinter-my-bonsai-plant) — A botanical garden's horticulturists put cold frames, unheated garages and heeling in side by side on one short page, so you can choose what fits your space.
- 📄 [Winter Care For Bonsai – Bonsai4me](https://bonsai4me.com/winter-care-for-bonsai/) — It explains why a 'hardy' tree can still die in a pot: its whole root system sits above ground, where it freezes much faster than roots in the soil.

### Lesson 20: Can you diagnose five sick trees?
- ▶ [Why Your Bonsai Is Dying (And How to Save It) – The Bonsai Barn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xnDCgRn4l7Y) — A fast tour of the usual killers that works as a warm-up before you try the five cases.
- 📄 [How to tell when something is wrong with your bonsai – Bonsai Tonight](https://bonsaitonight.com/2016/12/16/tell-something-wrong-bonsai/) — Jonas Dupuich teaches a diagnostic habit: first decide if the problem sits on the leaf surface or comes from inside the tree, which is half of the gate task.
- 📄 [What is Iron Chlorosis and What Causes it? – Utah State University Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/forestry/trees-cities-towns/tree-care/causes-iron-chlorosis) — It shows why a tree can starve for iron in soil full of iron: above about pH 7 the iron stays locked in the soil.
- 📄 [Indoor Bonsai – Bonsai Empire](https://bonsaiempire.com/tree-species/indoor-bonsai) — It shows how much weaker window light is than outdoor light, which explains why indoor trees decline slowly instead of dying all at once.

### Lesson 21: Why prune at all?
- ▶ [How to Prune a Bonsai tree – Bonsai Empire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=672Ua75bW-s) — A short, clear demo that separates the regular trim that keeps a tree's shape from the big cuts that build its structure.
- ▶ [Natural-target-pruning (ala Alex Shigo) vs close-cut Pruning on pine sapling – Bob Seymour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tFd0z8EDSZo) — A side-by-side experiment lets you see whether leaving the branch collar really changes how a wound closes.
- 📄 [Compartmentalization of decay in trees (CODIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compartmentalization_of_decay_in_trees) — Shigo found that trees don't heal wounds, they wall them off, and that changed where everyone makes their cuts.
- 📄 [Timing cutback to support development goals – Bonsai Tonight](https://bonsaitonight.com/2019/10/25/bonsai-development-series-18-timing-cutback-to-support-development-goals/) — The same cut can thicken a trunk or refine a branch depending on the season, and this article shows how to time it to the result you want.

### Lesson 22: When do pinching and defoliation help, and when do they hurt?
