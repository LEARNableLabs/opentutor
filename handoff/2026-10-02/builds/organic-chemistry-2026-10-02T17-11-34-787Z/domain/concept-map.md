# Organic Chemistry — Reaction Mechanisms — Concept Map

## Core Concepts (in learning order)

1. **Curved-arrow bookkeeping** — arrows move electron pairs (or single electrons, fishhooks) from a source to a sink; atoms and net charge are conserved at every step. (L1)
2. **Donor–acceptor orbital picture** — nucleophile HOMO (lone pair, π, σ) into electrophile LUMO (π*, σ*, empty p). Depends on: 1 (L2)
3. **pKa as compass** — sets which species can exist under given conditions, leaving-group ability and proton bookkeeping. Depends on: 1 (L3)
4. **Energy surfaces** — TS vs intermediate, rate-determining step, ΔG‡. Depends on: 1 (L4)
5. **Hammond postulate** — TS resembles the nearer-in-energy species; reactivity–selectivity. Depends on: 4 (L5)
6. **Reactive-intermediate stability** — carbocations, carbanions, radicals; hyperconjugation and resonance. Depends on: 2, 3 (L6)
7. **SN2** — backside attack into σ*, inversion. Depends on: 2, 4 (L8)
8. **SN1 and ion pairs** — ionisation-limited, partial racemisation. Depends on: 4, 6 (L9)
9. **Nucleophilicity scales** — Swain–Scott n for SN2 at methyl; Mayr N/s_N/E for carbocations and π-electrophiles only. Depends on: 3, 7, 8 (L10, revisited L29)

## Dependencies

- **SN2 (7) and E2 (10)** both need the σ* acceptor picture (2). E2 antiperiplanarity is the same orbital argument as SN2 backside attack.
- **Curtin–Hammett (12)** needs energy surfaces (4) and a concrete conformational case. The E2 chair cases (10) supply one.
- **Rearrangements, NGP and bridged ions (13–15)** are all carbocation-stability arguments (6). NGP is intramolecular SN2 (7) leading to a bridged ion, which is exactly the bromonium logic of 15.
- **Carbonyl chemistry (17–22)** rests on pKa (3) for every proton transfer. Conjugate addition (22) reuses the Mayr E scale (9) in its validated domain.
- **SNAr (24)** is carbonyl addition–elimination (17, 20) transplanted to an arene. Benzyne is an elimination (11).
- **Radical selectivity (25)** is the Hammond postulate (5) applied to H-abstraction. Radical clocks (26) are the first quantitative competition experiment and set up trapping (27).
- **Woodward–Hoffmann (30)** generalises the FMO arguments of 28–29. Vitamin D (L44) needs both electrocyclic (L43) and [1,n] shift logic.
- **Cross-coupling (34)** applies elementary steps (33) and uses SN2-like oxidative addition pathways (7) and pKa-driven base roles (3).
- **Evidence lessons (35–38)** use energy surfaces (4), Hammond (5) and substituent effects (23), plus specific earlier mechanisms as case studies: benzyne (24), radical clocks (26), Suzuki transmetalation (34).

## Prerequisite Topics

- **Stereochemistry and conformational analysis** — needed for SN2, E2, Curtin–Hammett, alkene additions, [3,3] chair TS (concepts 7, 10, 12, 16, 31)
- **Kinetics and thermodynamics (ΔG, ΔG‡, rate laws)** — needed for 4, 5, 12, 21, 35, 37
- **Basic MO theory** — needed for 2, 28–31
- **Aromaticity / Hückel 4n+2** — needed for 23–24 and as the analogy behind the aromatic TS view of W–H (30)
- **Oxidation states** — needed for 32–34
- **NMR/IR basics** — needed to interpret labeling and KIE data (35–36)
